## Overview

B2B prospecting often stalls because sellers describe product functions while customers evaluate revenue, cost, cash flow, and risk. The PSS5 value-selling approach treats this as a translation problem. The seller first translates a visible operating issue into business impact, then connects the proposed capability to that impact through a clear and testable mechanism.

## Diagnose the language gap

More lists, calls, and product materials do not automatically create relevance. An economic buyer thinks about return and risk. A user buyer thinks about workload and ease of use. A technical buyer thinks about security, compliance, and integration. A useful proposal gives each stakeholder the appropriate view of the same underlying value logic.

## Construct the value logic

Begin with an operating issue that could be measured: revenue leakage, avoidable cost, constrained cash flow, or risk exposure. Map the solution capability to the process producing that issue. Describe why the process should change and how the change would be observed. For a complex account, organize this as a profit improvement proposal. For a smaller account, use a one-page view of input, benefit, and net effect.

Do not hide assumptions. A value model is useful when the customer can inspect and revise it. Use customer data where available and conservative hypotheses where it is not. The proposal becomes stronger as assumptions are replaced by confirmed information.

## Extract signals from public data

Annual reports can reveal strategic direction, management priorities, cost pressure, margin changes, and operating risk. Recruitment pages can reveal missing skills or expansion plans. Industry discussions can reveal process bottlenecks. These public signals are not a final diagnosis. They are evidence for choosing a relevant opening question.

Convert each signal into an if-then value hypothesis. If a capability changes a specific process, then a related revenue, cost, cash-flow, or risk outcome may improve. Keep the language conditional until the customer confirms the underlying facts.

## Adapt to the buying stage

In early contact, use one concise value hook to earn a conversation. During discovery, define the business impact, decision criteria, and evidence required. During solution design, connect functions to operating outcomes. During negotiation, use the agreed value logic to place price and implementation choices in context. Value selling is therefore a continuous operating discipline rather than a final presentation.

## Field workflow

Select a target account and read its latest public business material. Mark the operating indicators related to the solution. Write an if-then hypothesis for each indicator. Combine the strongest hypotheses into a one-page value summary. Use the summary to request a short discussion with an informed stakeholder. After the discussion, replace assumptions with customer-confirmed information and deepen the profit logic.

## Takeaway

Effective prospecting is not simply more activity. It is the disciplined conversion of public business signals into relevant customer questions, then into a transparent business case. Public data provides the opening clues, consultative questioning establishes the facts, and the value model gives the decision chain a common language for evaluating the proposal.
