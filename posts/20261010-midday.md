# When Customer Decisions Move: Redrawing the Key-Account Decision Map

A recent public report described how Sanofi is handing the definition of a next-generation product to its Shanghai team. The detail matters far beyond one company. For anyone selling into large accounts, the story signals something practical: the map of who decides is being redrawn, and the old relationships may no longer point to the people who matter.

Decision rights follow business weight. When a multinational moves product definition from headquarters to a local team, the customer's center of gravity shifts, and sales resources, coverage and information flow must follow. This guide covers what changes, how to re-scan the decision chain, and what teams can do this week.

## Why decision rights migrate

The source frames the shift as a business-weight story. Public reporting cited there indicates China already participates deeply in over 90 percent of Sanofi's synchronized development projects, about one third of future group projects are planned for China, and Shanghai will become its largest translational medicine center in China. That weight redistributes resources, standards and responsibility.

For sales, at least three things change.

- The decision venue moves. Review meetings travel from an overseas headquarters to a China team. Travel costs may fall, but new local review steps appear, and none of them can be skipped.
- Information freshness improves. Requirements that used to arrive paraphrased through headquarters channels can now be obtained first-hand from the China region, which lowers the cost of validation.
- The competitive field widens. Competitors who already know China's R&D and procurement processes gain a seat at the same table. Incumbent advantages have to be re-proven.

The takeaway is not that every account will follow the same path. It is that decision geography is not fixed, and sales coverage built on last quarter's assumptions can quietly stop matching reality.

## Where decision power goes, relationship power must follow

Who really decides inside a customer organization is rarely written on the org chart. When a multinational moves new-product definition from an overseas headquarters to Shanghai, the old relationship map in a salesperson's hands can lose its value overnight. The source assigns this kind of judgment to the relationship capability within PSS5: replace personal feeling with decision information, and confirm where your time should go.

For context, PSS5 is a five-capability model summarized by Yan Ke Xu Hanbo from more than 20 years of key-account sales practice. It consists of value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability and negotiation capability. This topic sits in the relationship capability, which builds customer relationships through professional ability, decision information and credible action, with the emphasis on understanding and covering the customer's decision chain.

### A story about who is actually embracing you

Consider a familiar pattern. A supplier has spent years cultivating a headquarters technical advisor. That advisor shaped the standards and signed off on the shortlist. Then the customer moves product definition to a local team. The advisor still exists, but the local R&D and procurement leads now control the review criteria. The salesperson's warm relationship remains warm, yet it no longer covers the new decision point. Nothing dramatic happened. The map simply stopped matching the territory.

## Redrawing the decision chain with PRTABCD

PRTABCD is a decision-information scanning tool inside the relationship capability. It uses seven information categories to expose intelligence blind spots.

- P: past information
- R: requirements
- T: timing and key milestones
- A: advocates, the people in the customer organization who embrace you
- B: budget
- C: competitors and alternatives
- D: the decision chain

After decision rights migrate, every item needs to be re-marked with what is known, what is unknown, the information source and the next question. Do not carry over last quarter's table.

Two letters need priority review. A asks who embraces you inside the customer; after reorganization, that contact may have changed roles or allegiance. D describes the multi-person decision process. When definition moves to Shanghai, D must be re-answered locally: who raises requirements, sets standards, approves. Each answer needs fresh evidence.

A natural objection follows: internal moves at the customer are their business, not mine. The connection is the window. A newly formed review team has not yet locked its criteria, and the supplier list is not final. Industry insight delivered during this period is most likely to be heard. Wait until the list is fixed, and you are responding to rules someone else wrote.

The scanning exercise fits on one page. Give each role a row and each of the seven information categories a column, then fill the cells. The point is to make the gaps visible before they become lost deals.

## A compact comparison: old map versus new map

| Dimension | Old map | New map |
|---|---|---|
| Decision venue | Overseas headquarters | China-based team, with added local review steps |
| Information source | Relayed through headquarters channels | First-hand from the China region, lower validation cost |
| Key contacts | Headquarters technical advisors | Local R&D, procurement and review leads |
| Competitive position | Incumbent advantage assumed | Must be re-proven against rivals who know local processes |
| Risk | Resources aimed at the wrong decision point | Window of opportunity closes once criteria and lists harden |

## Three actions to start this week

- Pull every active key-account project and re-label each role on the decision chain with name, position and latest movement. Focus on customers with organizational changes in the past six months.
- Build a backup for each advocate. Establish at least one more contact at the same level who is willing to tell the truth, so a single information channel cannot break.
- Prepare three validation questions for the next visit. Examples include who is drafting the new review criteria, when they take effect, and which steps require overseas countersignature.

## Practical checklist

- Re-scan all seven PRTABCD categories for each active account, not just the two you remember.
- Record for every category: known, unknown, source and next question.
- Prioritize A and D, because advocates and decision chains change fastest after reorganizations.
- Confirm whether budget authority has moved, and to whom.
- Identify which review steps are new and who owns them.
- Map each competitor's local relationships without assuming incumbency protects you.
- Verify claims with customer behavior evidence: who asks detailed questions, who requests proposals, who pushes internal approval.
- Set a review cadence so the map is refreshed whenever the customer's organization changes.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does it mean for key-account sales when a multinational moves product definition from overseas to China?**

It means the decision chain's geographic center is moving. Previously, multinationals took orders in China while approval sat abroad. When Sanofi hands a next product's definition to Shanghai, budget, standards and supplier influence shift local. A salesperson maintaining only the China handler may miss real approval power, and must re-scan veto holders.

**Q2: How does the relationship capability in PSS5 differ from relationship building over meals?**

Relationship capability means building trust through professional ability, decision information and credible action. A meal creates goodwill but not decision information: who holds budget, sets standards, carries responsibility. With product definition now in China, sales must cover the new chain with verifiable insight so insiders share information.

**Q3: How should the seven PRTABCD items be used when decision rights migrate?**

PRTABCD covers seven intelligence categories: past information, requirements, timing, advocates, budget, competitors and decision chain. When decision weight shifts to China, old intelligence fails: budget authority, advocates and review steps change. Re-record known, unknown, source and next question per item, then advance.

**Q4: What is the most common mistake during a decision-chain migration?**

The common mistake is fighting a new battle with an old map. Old relationships at an overseas headquarters do not cover new China-based decision points. Do not treat migration as rumor and delay. Judge by customer behavior evidence: who probes for details, asks for proposals, pushes internal approval.

**Q5: What should the first step of a visit look like with such a customer?**

First redraw the decision chain, not hand over a proposal. List roles: China user department, local procurement, Asia-Pacific management, overseas headquarters. Confirm whether each raises requirements, sets standards or gives final approval. Answer A and D first, then allocate visit time to decision participants.

**Q6: How does this connect to the value capability in PSS5?**

The connection is direct: only significant China business weight brings new-product definition to Shanghai. Your value inside a key account determines your position in the decision chain. If the China team only orders, the supplier stays at execution. Explain in profit language how you improve operations and you earn a seat at definition.

**Q7: At which stage of the sales funnel must the decision chain be verified?**

Under the system capability in PSS5, the five sales stages are contact, requirements, solution, negotiation and closing. Each stage should be judged with customer behavior evidence. Decision-chain verification should begin at the requirements stage: when the customer confirms the problem, the value and the ownership of responsibility, you must know who carries that responsibility. Discovering at the solution stage that the review committee sits in another country can invalidate earlier progress. The dual-perspective funnel is used to check the customer's seven-stage purchasing process in parallel, so both sides can tell whether they are in sync.

**Q8: How should a sales leader guide a team through a decision-rights migration?**

Three moves help. Make the decision-chain profile a mandatory update, so any organizational change triggers a PRTABCD re-scan. Define wolf-pack roles clearly: who covers China, who covers headquarters. Use the ASK model to check attitude, skills and knowledge. Update speed determines project speed.
