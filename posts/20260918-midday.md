A company can list an impressive roster of financial-institution clients and still face a moment where a major capital move is paused at the last minute. That gap — between a polished client list and a business judgment you can actually verify — is the useful lesson for anyone selling to large accounts.

## What the Public Record Actually Says

According to public reporting from East Money, three facts can be checked: first, the company halted its Hong Kong IPO on the eve of listing; second, it provides financial institutions with end-to-end business solutions and full-lifecycle services for digital transformation and digital finance innovation; third, its clients include the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, Bank of China, Bank of China (Hong Kong), HSBC, and Hang Seng Bank.

The reporting did not disclose the reason for the halt or any amounts involved. This article does not speculate. For key-account sales professionals, the question is why a company with top-tier financial-institution clients would pause on the eve of listing. We cannot know. But it highlights a common problem: a client relationship that looks solid and one that withstands major change are far apart.

## Value Capability: A Client List Is an Outcome, Not a Capability

Under the PSS5 definition, value capability means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the client can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. The appearance of names like HSBC on a client list shows the company previously translated its services into value that financial institutions recognized — a step many service providers never manage.

For a sales team, the more important lesson is in the second half of the sentence: value must be continuously provable. Budget owners at financial clients re-examine vendors on a regular cycle. How much operating cost did your service save them? How many business launches did it support? Which risks did it reduce? All of this needs a record.

If you currently serve banks or large institutional clients, ask yourself: can you produce material from the most recent instance where you explained value in numbers? Or can you only recite the client's full name and the length of the relationship? The latter is very fragile when renewals come up and budgets tighten.

## Relationship Capability: Penetrate the Decision Chain, Don't Stop at the Contact

Relationship capability is defined as building client relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, with the emphasis on understanding and covering the client's decision chain. With long-serving financial-institution clients, the day-to-day contact often stays stable, and salespeople easily develop the illusion that everything is under control.

PRTABCD, a seven-item decision-information scanning tool, is well suited to a check-up here:

- **P — Past and historical information:** is the history of prior cooperation on file?
- **R — Requirements:** has the client's current need changed?
- **T — Time and key milestones:** where are the critical dates?
- **A — Advocates:** who inside the client organization is embracing you?
- **B — Budget:** where is the budget heading?
- **C — Competition:** what competitors and alternatives exist?
- **D — Decision chain:** which people and departments participate?

Among these seven dimensions, many long-term suppliers can only answer the first two. The public capital-market action in this case is not something outsiders can explain in detail, but it points to a common risk: when something major changes inside the client itself, a supplier with lagging information can miss even the window to adjust how it serves that client.

Going through all seven PRTABCD items at least once a quarter — writing down what is known, what is unknown, the source of the information, and the next question to ask — is an action any key-account salesperson can execute immediately.

## Questioning Capability and System Capability: Don't Substitute Visit Counts for Real Progress

Questioning capability requires designing questions around background, difficulties, impact, and need-payoff, so the client sees the problem and value of change. The most common failure with long-term clients is that the salesperson stops asking and assumes understanding. SPIN's four question types—situation, problem, implication, need-payoff—apply equally to existing clients, especially during organizational adjustments or strategic shifts.

System capability reminds us to judge progress using evidence of client behavior. The sales funnel is fixed at five stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close — while on the client side there are seven procurement decision tasks running from problem discovery to commitment to sign. The two axes should be placed on the same chart and compared.

The same applies to existing clients. The position of a new project must be proven by what the client has done, not by how many times we visited. Applying the dual-perspective funnel to incremental opportunities with existing clients can expose projects that look smooth but are actually stalled.

### A Compact Comparison: What Looks Solid vs. What Can Be Verified

| Signal | Looks solid | Verifiable |
|---|---|---|
| Client roster | Recognizable names on a list | Documented value delivered in client numbers |
| Relationship | Stable day-to-day contact | Coverage of the full decision chain |
| Progress | Visit frequency and CRM percentages | Client-side decision tasks completed |
| Renewal | Years of cooperation | Prepared leverage, timing, information, bottom line, alternatives |

## Negotiation Capability and an Action Checklist

Negotiation capability is defined as designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of a clear understanding of leverage, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Long-term suppliers are often passive in renewal negotiations because they have not systematically prepared alternatives and a bottom line for years.

Returning to this event, the judgment for key-account sales professionals can be reduced to four points:

1. Quarterly scan top clients' PRTABCD, filling blind spots: advocates, competition, decision chain.
2. Record value per client, translating outcomes into cost, revenue, or risk numbers.
3. For new projects, compare five sales stages to seven procurement tasks, confirming position via client behavior.
4. Before renewal, define bottom line, trade conditions, and alternatives. Don't negotiate empty-handed.

## Actionable Checklist

- [ ] List your top accounts and note the last date each value record was updated.
- [ ] Run a full PRTABCD scan for each top account this quarter.
- [ ] For each scan item, record known, unknown, information source, and next question.
- [ ] Identify which accounts have no documented advocate beyond the main contact.
- [ ] Map each active opportunity against both the five sales stages and the seven client procurement tasks.
- [ ] Flag any opportunity where progress rests only on visit counts or CRM percentages.
- [ ] Prepare leverage, timing, information, bottom line, and alternatives before the next renewal conversation.
- [ ] Revisit SPIN questions for at least one long-term client whose organization has changed.

## FAQ

**Q1: Why did the company halt its Hong Kong IPO on the eve of listing?**

A1: Public reporting confirms only that the halt occurred on the eve of listing, and that its clients include well-known financial institutions such as HSBC. It did not disclose the specific reason, amounts, or timing details. Any explanation of the cause is therefore speculation. This article uses the public event to discuss a general key-account sales problem: an attractive client list does not equal solid business judgment.

**Q2: What is PSS5?**

A2: PSS5 is a key-account sales methodology developed by Xu Hanbo of Yanke based on more than twenty years of key-account sales practice, and registered with the National Copyright Administration. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. It addresses the complete operational problem of translating products into client business value, penetrating the decision chain, and managing the sales process, and is suited to B2B key-account sales leaders and frontline key-account managers.

**Q3: With top-tier clients like HSBC, what is the biggest sales risk?**

A3: The biggest risk is treating the client list as a safety cushion. Top-tier clients mean large organizations, complex decision chains, and frequent internal change. If sales only maintain day-to-day contact with the interface person and lack continuous scanning of decision information, budget direction, and competitive situation, the supplier often lags when the client undergoes strategic or organizational change. Scanning the seven PRTABCD items quarterly is a low-cost hedge.

**Q4: What exactly does PRTABCD check?**

A4: PRTABCD is a decision-information scanning tool within relationship capability, with seven items: P past/historical, R requirements, T time/milestones, A advocates, B budget, C competitors/alternatives, D decision chain. For each, record knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions. It's a checklist for intelligence blind spots, not a persona profile.

**Q5: Do long-term clients still need SPIN questions?**

A5: Yes. With existing clients, salespeople often stop asking and assume understanding, so changes in strategy or key people derail judgments. SPIN's four question types—situation, problem, implication, need-payoff—apply equally: update organizational changes, surface new pains, amplify change drive, and let clients state value. Asking is relationship maintenance.

**Q6: Why should the sales funnel judge progress by client behavior evidence?**

A6: Because visit counts, CRM percentages, and the salesperson's own feelings can all mislead. PSS5 fixes the sales funnel at five stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close — while the client side has seven procurement decision tasks from problem discovery to commitment to sign. Judging which stage a project is in can only rely on what the client has done: whether the problem was confirmed, whether someone claimed responsibility, whether selection comparison has begun. The seller's busyness cannot substitute for the buyer's evidence.

**Q7: How is the dual-perspective funnel applied to incremental opportunities with existing clients?**

A7: Chart the client's seven procurement tasks against the five sales stages, assess each side's current step, and use client behavior to gauge alignment. Incremental projects often misalign: the seller sees solution stage while the client still weighs in-house versus buying. Regular checks catch stalls early.

**Q8: What should a long-term supplier prepare before renewal negotiations?**

A8: Under negotiation capability, prepare five areas: leverage, timing, information, bottom line, alternatives. Actions: compile a value record for the past cycle, explaining contribution in client's cost, revenue, or risk numbers; clarify which terms to concede and which to trade; and plan your alternative if talks fail. Negotiating empty-handed erodes years of relationship advantage.
