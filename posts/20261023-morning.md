Competition can make customers more careful, not more rushed. When an industry is described as fiercely competitive and a leader says he fears wasting every day, the useful sales lesson is not to push harder. It is to make every meeting count against the customer's current purchasing task. This guide turns that signal into a practical key-account sales routine. It does not claim to describe Unitree Technology's internal sales process, customer policy, or results. It uses one public judgment about competitive pressure and time waste as a prompt for better evidence-based selling.

## Why competitive urgency changes the sales conversation

When customers face competitive pressure, their time budget tightens. Decision makers inside the customer organization ask more risk questions. A seller who only presents product features adds information but not decision confidence. A seller who can translate urgency into verifiable business impact earns attention. The task is to align each conversation with the procurement task the customer must complete now: clarify why change is needed, who owns the decision, how value will be verified, and what happens if the project stalls.

## The five PSS5 dimensions in practice

The framework has five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Facing competitive urgency, a sales leader can ask five questions: Why must the customer change now? Who is pushing? Are we asking the right questions? Where is the project? What if no deal is reached? The five questions map to the five capabilities. They do not all need to be solved in one meeting, but each meeting should leave customer-side evidence.

### Value capability: turn urgency into the customer's business case

Value capability requires translating features into changes in revenue, cost, cash flow, and risk that the customer can calculate. Under competitive pressure, customers care more about whether results will appear quickly. A value case should name the business metric that will change, who will verify it, and what customer data is needed. Write the proposal as an investment case, not only a product configuration. If the customer cannot see the return, fix the value case before discussing price.

### Relationship capability: use PRTABCD to close information gaps

Relationship capability must rest on decision-chain information. PRTABCD checks seven items: P for past, R for need, T for timing, A for Arm or the person embracing you, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, and D for Decision, the multi-person, multi-department decision chain rather than one final approver. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. This shows whether the seller lacks information or lacks action.

### Questioning capability: help the customer articulate the reason to change

Questioning capability advances through the four SPIN question types. Situation questions establish how the customer works now. Problem questions surface bottlenecks. Implication questions help the customer see the cost of delay. Need-payoff questions let the customer state the benefit of solving the problem. Under time pressure, ask: Which step wastes the most time? Whose work does it hold up? What will be lost if the delay continues? Which metric will improve first after resolution? Connected in sequence, these questions help the customer form the reason to change; the seller does not need to conclude for them.

### System capability: judge stage by customer behavior evidence

This dimension places the sales process inside a dual-view funnel: five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages. The sales stages are contact, need, solution, negotiation, and close. The customer stages run from problem discovery, value judgment, confirmation that a solution exists, confirmation of responsibility, build-or-buy, solution and vendor selection, to commitment and signing. The two axes are many-to-many, not one-to-one. To judge progress, look for customer actions: providing data, arranging key people, confirming evaluation criteria, and entering budget and terms discussions. Visit counts and verbal warmth cannot replace such evidence.

### Negotiation capability: convert urgency into conditional exchange

Negotiation capability centers on power, time, information, and alternatives. The more urgent the customer seems, the more precisely the seller should ask where the urgency comes from: a competitive window, an internal review, or a budget cycle. Prepare with three actions:

- List exchangeable conditions on both sides, including scope, delivery cadence, payment, service response, and risk sharing.
- Write down your BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, with an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record.
- Phrase concessions as conditional statements: adjust price, scope, or timing only when the customer makes a corresponding commitment.

This keeps urgency from becoming a one-sided price cut and keeps negotiation from being reduced to price alone.

|---|---|---|
| Value | Why is this worth investment now? | Metric, baseline, verifier, data source |
| Relationship | Who is embracing us and who decides? | Known and unknown PRTABCD items plus next question |
| Questioning | What makes change necessary? | Customer-stated impact and expected return |
| System | Where is the purchase? | Customer actions, not seller activity |
| Negotiation | What if we cannot agree? | Exchangeable conditions, BATNA, concession triggers |

## A four-row battle sheet for the next account

The public signal offers limited information, but it is enough to remind sales leaders that customer sensitivity to time is rising and progress cannot rely on self-assessment. Using the five PSS5 dimensions, build a four-row battle sheet:

1. Decision task and behavior evidence: state the customer's current purchasing task and cite what the customer has actually done.
2. Value account: show the gain or loss the customer can calculate.
3. Relationship blind spots: use PRTABCD to mark unknowns and the next question.
4. Negotiation conditions: list exchangeable resources and the BATNA.

Every row needs customer-side evidence. A row that contains only a seller action is a gap to close before the next meeting.

## What this means for sellers in highly competitive industries

This guide does not infer Unitree's internal practices. The transferable point is for sales teams facing enterprise customers in high-competition industries: translate competitive pressure into the customer's decision risk, and avoid turning urgency into chasing. Customers buy verifiable outcomes. Seller effort must become evidence the customer can see. One meeting can accomplish three things: confirm the customer's current purchasing stage, find the person or group embracing the change, and agree on the next verifiable action. When all three happen, the urgency in the public signal has been connected to the customer's decision chain.

A4: Look for customer behavior: data provided, key people arranged, evaluation criteria confirmed, and budget or terms discussed. The five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages are many-to-many. Visit frequency and verbal goodwill are not evidence of stage movement.
