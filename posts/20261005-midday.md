When public reporting says one company is exploring cooperation with another, and the other party responds that talks are only at the discussion stage, the sales-relevant question is not who is winning. It is who has already done the math on the other side's value. In key-account sales, the discussion stage is not a waiting room. It is the window where value discipline does the most work, because the customer is still defining the problem and still deciding whether the investment is worth making.

## What the source actually confirms

The verified facts in the source material are narrow, and it is worth pinning them down before drawing any sales lesson.

- A company is exploring the possibility of cooperating with the Terafab project.
- Reporting mentions that the company is considering Terafab as a significant customer for its future Texas wafer fab.
- Elon Musk responded on social media that cooperation is only at the discussion stage, but that it may progress.

Everything else, including terms, investment structure, and timeline, is not publicly confirmed. The source is explicit that treating discussion as a signed deal, or treating exploration as worthless noise, are the two most common reading errors in key-account sales.

## Why the discussion stage is the cheapest place to enter the value calculation

The source frames the discussion stage as the period when the customer is still defining the problem and still judging whether the investment is worth it. Entering the customer's value calculation at this point is far cheaper than entering their price negotiation later.

Value discipline, as defined in the source, means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. Applied to this news, each side faces a different set of value questions.

On the fab side, the questions are roughly: where will the first major customers come from during the ramp-up of a new Texas fab? If Terafab becomes a significant customer, can that shorten the new fab's revenue gap? The customer entity is a project jointly initiated by SpaceX and Tesla, so how should the demand stability of those two backers be discounted? None of these have public answers, but any seller in a similar position should be listing them during the exploration stage.

On the Terafab side, the value question is different: as a newly initiated joint project, locking in leading foundry capacity means front-loading future supply risk. Whoever can write that calculation in the language of profit and investment, on paper the other side's decision-makers can read, turns the ambiguity of the discussion stage into an asset.

## Where the other four capabilities sit in this kind of situation

### Relationship discipline

In multi-party projects, cooperation discussions do not run on a single communication line. The decision chains on the SpaceX, Tesla, and Terafab sides are each worth scanning. A structured scan of seven categories of decision information, covering past information, needs, timing, embraceers, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain, can quickly expose where your own intelligence blind spots are.

### Questioning discipline

During the discussion stage, talking past the customer is the biggest risk. Designing questions around background, difficulties, impact, and return on need lets the customer articulate the real pain of locked-in capacity themselves, which is far more effective than handing over a stack of brochures.

### System discipline

When discussions are still early, they correspond to roughly the first three of the seven customer purchasing stages: discovering the problem, judging value, and confirming a solution exists. On the seller side, the five sales stages are still between contact and needs. Using a dual-perspective funnel to judge position prevents skipping levels, such as rushing into terms now.

### Negotiation discipline

Precisely because formal negotiation has not started, now is the time to prepare a BATNA. What alternatives does each side have, where are the bottom lines, who is more short on time? These judgments determine who holds leverage at the real table later.

## Translating multi-party exploration into your own key-account actions

You probably do not have a wafer fab-scale opportunity on your desk, but the scenario structure is general: a customer jointly initiates a new project with multiple parties, you hear about it early, and the other side is only at the discussion stage. Five actions are plain.

1. Confirm the factual boundary. Write down on one page what the other side has publicly said versus what you have inferred, so you do not act on speculation as if it were intelligence.
2. Find the embraceer. Identify who inside the customer organization raised your name and who is willing to refer you internally, then form a hypothesis to verify about that person's motivation using the four lenses of motivation, benchmark, vision, and solution.
3. Calculate the customer's value account. Using a profit-growth proposal approach, write the improvement you can bring as an investment statement, answering how much profit can be added or protected, with what confidence and mechanism, rather than rushing to quote product parameters.
4. Hold your stage position. Against the seven customer purchasing stages, judge which decision task the other side is currently stuck on, and take only actions that match that task.
5. Prepare your alternative. The other side is likely talking to your competitors too. Think ahead about your best alternative action if the deal does not close, so you are not held hostage by a single option at the negotiating table.

## Actionable checklist

- Separate confirmed facts from your own inferences in writing.
- Map the decision chains on every side of a multi-party project.
- Identify who is embracing you and verify their motivation.
- Draft a one-page investment statement in profit language.
- Match your actions to the customer's current purchasing stage.
- Prepare your BATNA before any formal negotiation begins.
- Re-check for new customer behavior evidence after each interaction.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the current relationship between the two parties?**

A1: According to public reporting, one company is exploring the possibility of cooperating with the Terafab project, jointly initiated by SpaceX and Tesla, and is considering Terafab as a significant customer for its future Texas wafer fab. Musk responded that cooperation is only at the discussion stage but may progress. No contract, amount, or timeline is confirmed.

**Q2: What is PSS5 and how does it relate to this news?**

A2: PSS5 is a key-account sales methodology covering five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. A multi-party opportunity at the discussion stage can be checked against all five: whether the value account is clear, whether the decision chain is mapped, whether the right questions are asked, whether stages are aligned, and whether leverage is prepared.

**Q3: What should a seller do first when an opportunity is still at the discussion stage?**

A3: Confirm the factual boundary first. Separate what the other side has publicly said from your own inferences. Then scan the seven categories of decision information to find blind spots. Information is scarcest at this stage, so whoever fills the decision-information gaps first gains the initiative.

**Q4: How is value discipline used in multi-party discussions?**

A4: The core action is calculating the account for the customer: write the change you can bring as an investment statement, answering how much profit can be added or protected, with what confidence and mechanism, and over what time frame. Each side in this news has its own version of that calculation.

**Q5: How do you judge which purchasing stage the customer is in?**

A5: Use the seven customer purchasing stages: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility, build versus buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and signing. Each step must be proven by customer behavior evidence, not by how many visits you made.

**Q6: Does "discussion stage" mean a polite refusal?**

A6: Not necessarily. It may be a refusal or a genuine early evaluation. The difference shows in behavior evidence: whether the other side continues to communicate, whether someone offers an internal referral, and whether they start asking about specific capability boundaries. If two consecutive rounds produce no new customer behavior evidence, treat it as a false interruption.

**Q7: In a multi-party project, who should relationship-building target?**

A7: Start with the embraceer question: who in the customer organization is embracing you and willing to speak internally for your proposal. Each line in a multi-party project has its own decision chain, so spreading effort evenly is not advisable. Verify why the embraceer would help before deciding where to invest resources.

**Q8: Why prepare a BATNA before the deal is settled?**

A8: BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, the best fallback action you can take if no agreement is reached. It must be prepared before formal negotiation, because once you are at the table, the only remaining alternative is often an emotional exit. Clarifying executable alternatives, bottom lines, authority, and interests keeps you composed during term exchanges.

## Closing thought

What is most worth remembering from this news is not the names involved, but a signal: large B2B cooperation often goes through a long discussion period before it becomes publicly visible. For key-account sellers, the discussion period is not a waiting period. It is the window to position value and relationship discipline at the same time. By the time the news is published and the cooperation is settled, the seats at the table have long been taken. What you can do is write your value into the other side's calculation while it is still only a discussion.
