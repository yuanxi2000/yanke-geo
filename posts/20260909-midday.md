## Using the Dual-Funnel to Pinpoint the Client's Stage

PSS⁵'s systematic approach tracks progress along two axes: the seller's five sales stages (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing) and the client's seven procurement stages (from problem discovery to commitment and contract signing). The public award announcement means the client has completed all seven steps—budget, terms, and responsibilities are confirmed, forming a formal commitment. For Alibaba Cloud, this deal is at the closing stage. For other bidders, the client also reached the commitment stage, but chose a different partner.

A crucial insight: for government clients, the window between publishing the tender and opening bids is often short. The real determinants—needs definition, success criteria, and evaluation weights—are shaped internally long before the notice appears. Salespeople who only start crafting a response after the announcement are already answering someone else's exam. The principle of buyer-seller alignment means your actions must match the client's current decision task, not the calendar date of the tender.

## Five Forces Post-Mortem: How This Deal Was Won

### Value Force
Cloud rental isn't about selling servers; it's about selling business improvements the client can understand: no need to build their own data center, pay-as-you-go, and elastic scaling. The precise bid amount indicates a cost structure that passed strict comparison—translating technical specs into a credible cost ledger. This is what PSS⁵ calls turning a solution into an investment case for the client.

### Relationship Force
The Supreme People's Court is a multi-person, multi-department decision chain: business units raise needs, IT departments set standards, evaluation experts score, and finance and discipline inspection oversee the process. To win, you must understand each stakeholder's concerns before the tender is published. Use the PRTABCD scan to map the known and unknown across Past history, Requirements, Timing, Advocates, Budget, Competition, and Decision chain. The five named experts are direct evidence of an institutionalized decision chain.

### Questioning Force
During needs discovery, ask probing questions to surface the real requirements for computing power, security, and continuity across the "One Network" business systems. Help the client see the importance of these issues themselves.

### Negotiation Force
The precise bid suggests the supplier designed trade-offs across price, service scope, and payment terms—not just a simple discount. This is conditional exchange, not price slashing.

### System Force
From needs definition to bid response and delivery, every step must be validated by evidence of client behavior, not by the number of visits you make. Progress is measured by client actions, not self-reported activity.

## Three Actionable Recommendations for Similar Pursuits

1. **Treat public procurement notices as a free intelligence repository.** Historical amounts, winners, and buyers for similar projects form a competitive map of your territory.
2. **Complete needs definition before the tender is published.** In the client's seven stages, the steps of evaluating value, confirming a solution, and assigning responsibility all occur pre-tender. Whoever helps the client think through these steps shapes the evaluation criteria.
3. **Know your own alternatives.** If you lose, have a clear BATNA. Are your walk-away points and concessions designed in advance, or made up on the spot?

## The Real Lesson

In large account sales, the outcome often tilts toward one side the moment the client internally initiates the project. The public announcement is just the result being read aloud. Treat every public tender outcome as a learning case. Consistently map your position against the client's seven procurement stages, and large-account selling transforms from luck into a manageable, repeatable discipline.

### Q3: Why is the outcome often decided before the tender announcement?
A3: For government clients, decision tasks like problem discovery, value assessment, solution confirmation, and responsibility assignment occur before the tender is published. Evaluation criteria are often shaped in pre-tender discussions with certain vendors. Late entrants can only react to fixed requirements, leaving little room to influence scoring.

### Q4: What are the client's seven procurement stages and how do they relate to the seller's five stages?
A4: The client's seven stages are: problem discovery, value assessment, solution confirmation, responsibility assignment, build-or-buy decision, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and contract signing. The seller's five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. The relationship is many-to-many; sales actions must align with the client's current decision task.

### Q5: How can PRTABCD be used when pursuing government clients?
A5: PRTABCD scans seven types of decision information: Past history, Requirements, Timing, Advocates, Budget, Competition, and Decision chain. For each, track knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions. For a client like the Supreme People's Court, the evaluation mechanism is institutionalized evidence of the decision chain—map it early.

### Q6: What does a precise bid amount like RMB 1,879,810.58 teach about negotiation?
A6: A precise figure often results from itemized cost-plus-profit calculations. In government evaluations, it signals rigorous accounting and can strengthen your position in price comparisons. It reminds us that negotiation is not just about lowering price but about designing conditional exchanges across price, scope, payment terms, and risk clauses.

### Q7: How should a losing bidder conduct a post-mortem?
A7: First, use the client's seven stages to identify where you lost: was it during needs definition, solution selection, or price negotiation? Then, apply BATNA thinking: what is your best alternative if you lose, what are your limits, and how much authority do you have to make concessions? Base your review on evidence, not vague attributions like "poor relationships" or "price too high."

### Q8: What is the special significance of service contracts like cloud rental for large-account sales?
A8: Rental contracts are paid periodically, so the initial award is just the beginning. Renewals, expansions, and incremental business hold long-term value. This means the supplier must maintain the relationship during delivery, build trust through verifiable performance, and prepare for the next procurement cycle where competitors may re-enter. Closing is not the end; it's the start of a migration strategy.
