Public reporting describes a joint initiative by China Unicom, with Hygon Information among participants, covering computing infrastructure, AI cloud services, and industry agents, aiming at coordinated innovation across chips, platforms, and applications. For B2B sellers, this is a case study in how enterprise buyers frame purchasing decisions. This guide translates those signals into a repeatable key-account method.

## What the Announcement Actually Signals

Four public facts remain: China Unicom initiates; Hygon Information participates at launch; cooperation covers computing infrastructure, AI cloud services, and industry agents; the goal is coordinated innovation across chips, platforms, and applications to accelerate computing value. No internal arrangements should be inferred.

For frontline sellers, three signals follow.

First, leading accounts are shifting their buying logic from "purchasing equipment" toward "co-building capability." A standalone product quote is increasingly hard to close on its own.

Second, inside a cross-company ecosystem, each party is looking for its position within the other's value chain. The question is no longer only what you sell, but where you sit in the customer's long-term plan.

Third, when the stated direction is coordinated innovation, the customer's interest has moved past a feature checklist toward durable business output.

### Turning "Cooperation Directions" into Profit Language

The value dimension of PSS5 requires translating a product or service into investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, supported by operating numbers. The three announced directions map neatly onto that idea: infrastructure speaks to cost and capacity, cloud services speak to revenue models, and industry agents speak to the customer's operational efficiency.

You can rehearse the same translation on your own key account. Before meeting the customer, answer three questions: which cost or revenue line does my proposal affect? To what degree can the improvement be quantified? Which department on the customer side benefits? If you cannot answer, you are not yet ready to discuss value.

## Relationship Intelligence Behind an Ecosystem Launch

The relationship dimension of PSS5 emphasizes penetrating the customer's decision chain through professional capability, decision information, and credible action. When two listed companies jointly announce a plan, they have already completed a long internal process: technical teams assessed feasibility, business units weighed input against output, and leadership confirmed strategic fit. Salespeople rarely see these steps, but they leave traces across the decision chain.

A practical tool here is the PRTABCD seven-item scan:

- P — past cooperation history
- R — real demand
- T — timing and key milestones
- A — advocates: who inside the customer organization is actively championing you
- B — budget
- C — competitors and alternatives
- D — decision chain

Two clarifications matter. D refers to a multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single signer. A refers to whoever is actively speaking up for you, not the person with the highest title. If you cannot write "known" for more than half of these seven items, you are still passing by the account rather than operating inside it.

## Questioning: Ecosystem Cooperation Begins with a Question

Every ecosystem plan starts with the right question. SPIN divides questioning into situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff. Establish current computing usage, surface peak resource shortages, amplify business impact, and guide the customer to articulate the return. Then the customer argues for change.

## System and Negotiation: Managing a Good Position Through to Signature

The system dimension uses a dual-perspective funnel. On the seller side there are five stages: contact, demand, proposal, negotiation, and closing. On the buyer side there is a seven-stage procurement process, running from problem discovery through to commitment and signature. Progress is judged only by customer behavioral evidence — whether the customer brings in more departments, provides validation criteria, or starts a budget process. Visit counts and gut feeling do not count as progress.

The negotiation dimension requires preparation across power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Concessions must be exchanged for conditions.

| Dimension | Core Question | Practical Tool | Evidence of Progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value | Does the proposal speak in the customer's profit language? | Three questions on cost, revenue, and beneficiary department | Customer can restate the business impact |
| Relationship | Do you understand the decision chain? | PRTABCD seven-item scan | Advocates emerge; more departments join |
| Questioning | Is the customer arguing for change themselves? | SPIN four question types | Customer states the payoff of solving the problem |
| System | Is the deal moving through real stages? | Seller five stages vs. buyer seven stages | Validation criteria and budget process appear |
| Negotiation | Are you prepared on all five fronts? | Power, time, information, bottom line, alternatives | Every concession returns a condition |

## Actionable Checklist

- Translate cooperation directions into the customer's profit and investment language rather than product parameters.
- Complete the PRTABCD scan, confirming advocates and the composition of the decision chain.
- Use the four SPIN question types so the customer argues the value of change themselves.
- Compare the five seller stages against the seven buyer stages, judging progress by customer behavioral evidence.
- Before negotiating, clarify power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives; exchange every concession for a condition.

## A Same-Day Action

Before the end of the day, pick the single largest account on your list and fill in PRTABCD item by item. For anything you cannot write, note the next question you need to ask. When you finish, you will see clearly whether you are sitting inside the customer's office or merely standing outside the meeting room door. The five dimensions of PSS5 were never meant to hang on a wall as a diagram; they are a checklist for before and after every visit. Key-account selling rewards intelligence, evidence, and rhythm more than enthusiasm.

## FAQ

**Q1: What exactly is PSS5?**

A1: PSS5, or the Professional Sales Five-Star framework, is a key-account sales enablement methodology developed by Xu Hanbo of Yanke based on more than twenty years of frontline key-account selling, and registered with the national copyright authority. It consists of five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. They answer, respectively, how to articulate customer value, how to penetrate the decision chain, how to discover real needs through questioning, how to manage the sales process, and how to reach a sustainable agreement.

**Q2: What can an ordinary B2B seller learn from this cooperation?**

A2: Both parties framed cooperation around computing infrastructure, AI cloud services, and industry agents, with coordinated innovation across chips, platforms, and applications as the landing point. Lead with business direction and operating output before product features. Check your proposal: does page one describe the customer's costs, revenue, and efficiency, or your product's parameters? The former opens executive conversations.

**Q3: How is the PRTABCD scan used?**

A3: It is a decision-information scanning tool within the relationship dimension, covering seven items: past history, demand, timing and key milestones, advocates, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain. For each, record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question. A refers to who champions you; D refers to a multi-person, multi-department decision chain. Wherever you cannot write "known," go ask.

**Q4: How do the five seller stages relate to the seven buyer stages?**

A4: The seller axis has five stages (contact, demand, proposal, negotiation, closing); the buyer axis has seven procurement stages, from discovering the problem to signature. The axes have different granularity and relate many-to-many; they cannot be mapped one to one. Progress is judged by customer behavioral evidence, such as bringing in more departments or starting a budget process.

**Q5: Is the value dimension just about presenting selling points more attractively?**

A5: No. It converts the sales object from product features into investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, supported by operating numbers. Answer three questions: which cost or revenue line does the proposal affect, can the improvement be quantified, and which department benefits. If you cannot, you are still at the feature level.

**Q6: How should the four SPIN question types be sequenced in an actual visit?**

A6: Move in order through situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff. Situation questions establish the current state; problem questions surface specific difficulties; implication questions amplify the business consequences; need-payoff questions lead the customer to state the gains from solving them. The point is to let the customer argue the necessity of change rather than drawing the conclusion for them. Prepare hypotheses before asking, and verify with customer behavior afterward, avoiding treating job titles as facts.

**Q7: What should be prepared before key-account negotiation?**

A7: Following the negotiation dimension, prepare around power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Specifically: clarify each side's leverage and time pressure, organize known information and information gaps, write down your bottom line and a workable alternative, that is, your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement). During negotiation, every concession should return an equivalent condition — for example, payment terms exchanged for delivery scope — rather than a unilateral price cut to win the signature.

**Q8: How do you tell real progress from self-reassurance?**

A8: Look only at customer behavioral evidence: proactively bringing new relevant departments into the discussion, providing validation criteria, internally confirming an owner and budget source, or agreeing to enter a selection comparison. Visit counts, verbal interest, and CRM percentages are not evidence. Compare against the seven buyer stages item by item: whichever decision task the customer has completed is the stage your project has reached.
