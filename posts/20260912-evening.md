A public disclosure by a precision-components manufacturer offers a compact lesson in how to talk about value to a large customer. The company told institutional investors that it supplies core precision components for a next-generation device to an important customer, covering hinge structural parts, screen support plates, ultra-thin vapor chambers, and flanged ultra-thin stainless-steel battery housing modules. It stated that the per-device value is several times that of a bar-type phone, and that folded-screen projects are progressing smoothly, with mass-production ramp-up and customer delivery proceeding on plan in the third and fourth quarters.

For anyone selling to key accounts, this passage is worth reading line by line. It does not stop at a product list. It names the customer type, locates the product inside the customer's finished device, and then quantifies value with a single comparison. That sequence is the practical core of what PSS5 calls value power.

## What the disclosure actually does

The statement follows three moves. First, it anchors the customer and the product position: an important customer, core precision components. Second, it uses a familiar benchmark: several times a bar-type phone. Third, it attaches a delivery rhythm: mass-production ramp-up and customer delivery in the third and fourth quarters.

Notice the language. It says value amount, not unit price. A value amount answers how much measurable increment the supplier creates for the customer. A unit price answers how much the supplier charges. The first speaks from the customer's ledger; the second speaks from the supplier's price list. That difference often separates a sales argument that can appear in an institutional research note from one that stays in a quotation email.

### Why the benchmark matters

The phrase several times a bar-type phone works because it borrows a reference the listener already knows. The customer, and the investor reading the note, already has a mental number for a bar-type phone. The comparison lands on a figure the audience already believes. A value claim without a benchmark asks the listener to trust a new number. A value claim with a benchmark asks the listener to compare two numbers they already understand.

## Three actions to copy from this sample

1. Anchor the customer and the product position before discussing value. State the customer's business scenario, then where your deliverable sits inside it, and only then the value calculation.
2. Use a comparison baseline to make value perceptible. Find an existing reference the customer already accepts: the current practice, the previous generation, or an internal cost figure.
3. Write the delivery rhythm into the value statement. A value claim without a time frame is a static table. A value claim with a time frame is a commitment.

## Value power is not the only lens

The same event also rewards a relationship lens. The supplier could disclose which components go into which models and when ramp-up is scheduled. That kind of information does not arrive on a supplier's desk by itself. It implies coverage of the customer's decision chain.

Using the PRTABCD scan, at least four items are visible here: past cooperation history (P), customer needs (R), time nodes (T), and the decision chain (D). The D item deserves attention. Large-customer procurement is a multi-person, multi-department process. A supplier needs an arm inside the customer organization, someone willing to speak internally and pass information along. You can write the best value statement in the world, but if it never reaches the person who signs off, it remains a private exercise.

## Use questioning power to surface the value opportunity

A per-device value several times higher than a bar-type phone is not an opportunity that waits to be found. It is asked into existence. Imagine visiting that end customer years earlier. Using SPIN-style questions, you would first ask about the situation: what form factor does the next-generation device aim for? Then the problem: under a bar-type form factor, which heat-dissipation and structural-strength issues remain unsolved? Then the implication: how would those issues raise device cost or delay launch? Finally the need-payoff: if the hinge and vapor chamber were solved in one step, how much would the launch window and margin improve?

When the customer states the implication and the payoff themselves, the argument carries more weight than any repetition of product features. Questioning power is the discipline of letting the customer see the problem and the value of change.

## System power and negotiation power: moving from opportunity to agreement

Once the opportunity is confirmed, system power keeps the process from drifting. The sales five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. The customer procurement seven stages run from problem discovery to commitment and signing. Placed on one dual-view funnel, the two axes have different granularity and a many-to-many relationship. Customer behavior evidence tells you whether you are synchronized. If the customer is still comparing build versus buy and you rush to price, you are ahead of the frequency. If the customer has entered supplier selection and you are still confirming needs, you are behind. A mass-production ramp-up and delivery rhythm only runs smoothly when buyer and seller share the same cadence.

At the negotiation stage, negotiation power designs condition exchanges around power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. A value amount several times higher than a bar-type phone is itself a reason for the customer to stay at the table.

## Actionable checklist

- Rewrite the opening of your most important proposal: customer business scenario first, your position second, value calculation third.
- Find one baseline the customer already believes, and express your value as a comparison against it.
- Attach a time frame to every value claim, including when the improvement becomes measurable.
- Identify the arm inside the customer organization, the person who speaks for you internally.
- Map the decision chain: how many gates exist, and who guards each one.
- Use SPIN questions to let the customer articulate the implication and the payoff.
- Check synchronization between your sales stage and the customer's procurement stage using behavior evidence.
- Prepare a profit improvement proposal that answers how much profit is added or protected, through what mechanism, and within what time frame.
- Review the PRTABCD scan and mark which item you cannot answer; make that your next visit's required question.

## FAQ

### What does value power mean in PSS5?

Value power means shifting the sales object from product features to an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. The test is simple: after hearing your statement, can the customer calculate a verifiable improvement using their own ledger? A feature list, however detailed, is not a value-power statement.

### What is the single most transferable point in this disclosure?

The phrase several times a bar-type phone. It anchors the customer and the component position first, then borrows a familiar benchmark, then adds a delivery rhythm. Locate, benchmark, rhythm: anyone can copy this sequence into a customer proposal.

### How is value power different from a quotation?

A quotation answers how much you charge and stands on the supplier's price list. Value power answers how much profit the purchase adds or protects and stands on the customer's ledger. The same product looks like an investment and a return in value language, and like a cost in price language. The negotiation position differs accordingly.

### What is a PIP, and how does it relate to value power?

A PIP, or profit improvement proposal, writes the operational improvement for the customer as an investment case. It answers how much profit is added or protected, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. It is the delivery vehicle for value power, not a product quotation. The time-bound ramp-up and delivery rhythm in the disclosure resembles this style of writing.

### How is PRTABCD used in customer relationship management?

PRTABCD scans seven categories of decision information for blind spots: P for past history, R for needs, T for time nodes, A for arm, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, and D for the decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. It helps reveal who should receive the value statement, who speaks for you inside the customer organization, and which information is still missing.

### What role does the arm play?

The arm answers who inside the customer organization embraces you: the person who recognizes your value and is willing to pass information or speak internally. This person may not be the decision maker, but is often the entry point for information into the decision chain. Key-account sales should identify and develop the arm, while remembering that the arm is not the decision chain itself. Sign-off usually remains a multi-person, multi-department process.

### Why look at the sales five stages and the customer procurement seven stages together?

The sales five stages describe the seller's tasks: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. The customer procurement seven stages describe the buyer's decision tasks from problem discovery to commitment and signing. The two axes have different granularity and a many-to-many relationship. Placed on one dual-view funnel, customer behavior evidence shows whether the two sides are synchronized, which helps avoid advancing too early or lagging behind the customer's process.

### What can a frontline salesperson take away today?

Three things. First, rewrite the opening of your most important proposal: customer business scenario and your position first, value calculation second. Second, find an old benchmark the customer already believes for your value equation. Third, add an explicit time rhythm to value realization, and set the PRTABCD item you cannot answer as the required question for your next visit.
