Compute capacity is not what customers pay for. They pay for the business improvement it produces.

## What the Report Actually Said

A Cailianshe report republished by East Money on September 12, 2026, notes two shifts: upstream toward standardized manufacturing, downstream toward utility-style compute priced by token. Yang Chunlei of Hebei Yindan Digital Technology said compute itself does not produce value; only with scenarios, data, and algorithms does it release value.

This statement deserves attention from key-account salespeople because it names something obvious that is often ignored. Customers will not pay for capability. They will pay for the operational improvement that capability brings.

## Why This Matters to Buyers and Sellers

"Compute sold like water and electricity" becomes a value restructuring: compute shifts from heavy asset purchase to service billed by usage. The decision chain shortens, budget categories change, and evaluation shifts to output per unit of spending.

Any company selling complex solutions to large accounts will face a similar moment. The more standardized and utility-like a product becomes, the less customers care about what you have, and the more they care about what changes on their books after they use it. This is exactly the problem that the value capability within the PSS5 five-capability framework is designed to solve: converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value in business numbers.

The statement also carries a signal that is not friendly to sellers. Compute itself is treated by customers as a valueless intermediate good. Value must be released jointly by scenarios, data, and algorithms. This means the customer's mental accounting works like this:

- Compute is a cost item: billed by token or usage, every unit is an expense.
- Scenarios determine output: without a specific business scenario, the spending is pure expenditure.
- Data and algorithms determine the multiplier: the same compute, used well or poorly, can produce very different output.
- Therefore, the key decision-makers in purchasing are shifting from technical departments to business and finance departments that are accountable for operating results.

## Three Actions to Translate This Statement Using Value Capability

PSS5 defines value capability as translating a product or service into the customer's profit, investment, and business value. Against this public report, three immediately usable actions can be extracted.

### 1. Find the person who does the math

If compute is treated like water and electricity, the approval authority for usage moves down to the workshop that consumes the water. You need to ask yourself: among my key accounts, who is accountable for the business result of this spending? Is their performance metric revenue, cost, turnover, or delivery speed? If you cannot find this person, no amount of technical recognition will move the contract forward.

### 2. Replace the feature list with an input-output account

When customers buy compute, they are buying the return from the scenario that runs on that compute. The salesperson's job is to write the improvement the solution brings to the customer's business as an investment statement: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the mechanism for capturing and delivering it is, and over what time frame it will be realized. The corresponding tool in PSS5 is the PIP (Profit Improvement Proposal). Its essence is an investment statement, not a product quotation.

### 3. Monetize every link

Monetization works like this: identify the customer operating links the solution can affect, quantify the improvement, and convert the result into financial language such as revenue, cost, profit, payback period, or opportunity cost. Note that all numbers must come from definitions and data the customer recognizes. Beautiful percentages invented by the salesperson will only destroy trust.

## Calibrating the Other Four Capabilities

When value capability changes, the other four capabilities must move with it.

In relationship capability, re-scan the seven PRTABCD items: past, needs, timing, who embraces you, budget shift, competitors, and the new decision chain.

In questioning capability, use the four SPIN question types to help the customer calculate the gap between three situations: not using compute, using less compute, and using compute well. Let the customer see the problem clearly.

In system capability, use the five sales stages of contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing to compare against the customer's seven purchasing stages, and judge whether buyer and seller are synchronized. If the customer is still at the "judging value" step, rushing to push a solution is skipping a level.

In negotiation capability, usage-based billing naturally creates a price reference point. Prepare your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement) and concession conditions in advance to avoid falling into a single price comparison.

## A Compact Comparison: Old Purchase Model vs. Utility Model

| Dimension | Heavy Asset Purchase | Utility-Style Service |
|---|---|---|
| Buying process | Project approval, tendering, acceptance | Usage billing, switch on or off |
| Budget category | Capital expenditure | Operating expenditure |
| Evaluation standard | Scale of construction | Output per unit of spending |
| Key decision-maker | Technical department | Business and finance |
| Sales focus | Product features and parameters | Investment, return, and profit improvement |
| Value proof | Technical recognition | Business numbers the customer recognizes |

## Actionable Checklist for This Week

- Pick one key account currently in negotiation and write down the specific person accountable for the business result of this spending, along with their performance metric.
- Take the top few features you most want to promote in your current solution and translate each one into the customer's financial language: revenue, cost, profit, or payback period. Confirm the number definitions with the customer.
- Use PRTABCD to list what is known, what is unknown, and what the next question is. Focus on filling in the budget category and the new roles in the decision chain.
- Prepare one PIP-style investment statement for the account, covering profit increase or protection, the capture mechanism, and the time frame.
- Identify which SPIN questions you will use to help the customer compare the cost of inaction against the cost of action.
- Check whether the customer is still at the value-judgment stage before you push a solution or quote.

**Q4: What is a PIP (Profit Improvement Proposal)?**

A4: PIP stands for Profit Improvement Proposal. It writes the improvement the solution brings to the customer's business as an investment statement: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the capture and mechanism is, and over what time frame it will be realized. It is not a product quotation. It is a value proof document for decision-makers who do the math.

**Q5: How do you monetize solution value?**

A5: Monetization has three steps: first identify the customer operating links the solution can affect, then quantify the improvement, and finally convert the result into financial language such as revenue, cost, profit, payback period, or opportunity cost. The key constraint is that all numbers must come from definitions and data the customer recognizes. Percentages and amounts estimated by the salesperson without confirmation will only damage trust.

**Q6: After the compute purchasing model changes, how should relationship capability be handled?**

A6: Re-scan decision information using the seven PRTABCD items: past, needs, timing, who embraces you, budget shift, competition, and the new decision chain. Expand coverage to business results.

**Q7: The customer says "compute is too expensive." How should sales respond?**

A7: Do not rush to cut price. Use SPIN questions to clarify use, find waste, calculate impact, and let the customer state the benefit. Then offer a conditional solution and price.

**Q8: Under usage-based billing, how do you judge progress in the sales funnel?**

A8: Use the five sales stages of system capability in PSS5 (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing) against the customer's seven purchasing stages, and judge position by customer behavioral evidence, such as whether the customer acknowledges the problem, whether someone claims responsibility, and whether they have entered selection comparison. Avoid skipping levels: when the customer is still at the value-judgment stage, pushing a solution and quote will only be seen as premature pressure. The correct action is to calculate the account together first.
