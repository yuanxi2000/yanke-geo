Equipment vendors often treat a single pilot as proof of product-market fit, but the harder question is whether the sales motion repeats across customer organizations. A recent industry report noted a robotics vendor's machines entered PCB production lines at scale, covering board loading/unloading, cleaning load/unload, and inspection handling, with customers including major PCB manufacturers. The pattern suggests its sales actions cleared the bar inside several different customer organizations.

Batch rollout is never luck. It is the result of five capabilities passing simultaneously across multiple customers: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Using the PSS5 framework to break this down shows where a given project is stuck.

## Value Capability: Translating Robots into Production-Line Business Numbers

Board loading and unloading, cleaning load/unload, and inspection handling are labor-intensive, cycle-time-sensitive steps in PCB factories. To sell the first production line, an equipment vendor must translate product parameters such as robot speed and stability into business language the customer understands: how much labor is saved, how much breakage is reduced, how much yield improves, how quickly the investment pays back.

This is the definition of value capability in PSS5: shifting the sales object from product features to the customer's understandable investment, return, and profit improvement, and explaining value with business numbers. In practice, this means writing a PIP (Profit Improvement Proposal) that frames the solution's impact on the customer's operations as an investment case, answering how much profit can be added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame. A PIP is not a product quotation. If what you show the PCB factory's procurement and manufacturing departments is still a parameter sheet plus a price, you have already lost on value capability to a competitor who speaks the language of profit.

## Relationship Capability: Batch Rollout Means the Decision Chain Was Penetrated Repeatedly

Introducing robots into a PCB factory involves manufacturing, equipment, process, procurement, finance, and sometimes environmental and safety departments. Being able to enter a group of manufacturers like these suggests the supplier identified and covered the decision chain in each one, rather than winning over a single enthusiastic engineer. The point of relationship capability is never entertainment or drinking; it is building trust through professional competence, decision information, and credible action.

The PRTABCD seven-item scan identifies intelligence blind spots: P past, R requirements, T timing, A Arm (embracer), B budget, C competitors, D Decision (multi-person chain). For each, record knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions. A is not leverage or Approver; D is not a single decision-maker. Pilot-to-batch success depends on clarifying these, especially embracer, budget line, and veto holders.

## Questioning and System Capability: Pilots Most Often Die from Misaligned Advancement

Pilots are a high-risk stage in equipment sales. The customer may say "let's try it first," while internally the seven stages of customer purchasing may not be complete: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. Each step must be proven by customer behavioral evidence, not replaced by seller actions. On the sales side, the corresponding five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. PSS5 places these two axes on the same map as a dual-perspective funnel, judging whether buyer and seller are synchronized, leading, lagging, or misaligned.

Misalignment: customer compares build versus buy (step 5) while you push contract terms (stage 4). Evidence: repeated requests for test data. Advancing out of order stalls the project. Use SPIN questions to let the customer see hidden costs and line-stoppage risks of manual loading, not unilateral pitches.

## Negotiation Capability: The Leverage Structure from One Customer to Many Is Completely Different

When the first customer closes, the supplier has little leverage and often concedes margin for a case study. After batch rollout at multiple PCB manufacturers, new customers cite peers to push down price, but the supplier can use delivery records to prove risk is controllable. Negotiation capability designs concessions and condition exchanges based on power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives to secure a sustainable agreement.

Before closing, write down your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement), clarify executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. After closing, use a migration strategy: expand value through supplements, upgrades, or integration. For complex projects, use a wolf-pack approach with team coordination.

## A Practical Checklist for Your Own Deals

- Check the dual-perspective funnel: for each key account you are following, which of the seven customer purchasing stages is the buyer in, and what is the evidence? If you cannot write down customer behavioral evidence, treat it as not advanced.
- Run a PRTABCD intelligence inventory: of the seven items, how many can you fill in as known? For each unknown, what is the next question, whom will you ask, and when?
- Rewrite your quotation as a PIP: three questions must be answered, how much profit is added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame. If you cannot answer, rework it.
- Find your Arm: who in the customer organization is embracing you? If that person cannot reach the decision chain (D), you need a plan B rather than doubling down.
- Write down your BATNA before negotiating: a negotiator without alternatives has only one path, concession.

## Comparison: Quotation Versus PIP

| Dimension | Ordinary Quotation | Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary audience | Procurement, purchasing | Operations and management |
| Core content | Product parameters and price | Investment case and profit impact |
| Key questions answered | What does it cost? | How much profit is added or protected, with what confidence, over what time frame? |
| Customer response | Price comparison | Business-case evaluation |

## FAQ

### Q1: What are the five dimensions of PSS5?

A1: PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) consists of value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability translates products into customer profit and business value; relationship capability penetrates the customer decision chain; questioning capability discovers and amplifies real needs through question design; system capability manages the process using the five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages; negotiation capability forms sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

### Q2: What is the most direct lesson for key-account sales from robots entering PCB production lines?

A2: The lesson is that batch rollout rests on a repeatable set of sales actions: translating equipment parameters into the production line's profit language, mapping the decision chain and embracer in each customer, and judging purchasing progress by customer behavioral evidence rather than visit counts. You can immediately check your own projects against these four items and fill in whatever is missing.

### Q3: What is a PIP, and how does it differ from an ordinary quotation?

A3: A PIP is a Profit Improvement Proposal. It frames the solution's operational impact as an investment case, answering: how much profit can be added or protected, what confidence and mechanism, and over what time frame. An ordinary quotation lists parameters and price for comparison; a PIP speaks to management about input and output, not discounts.

### Q4: In PRTABCD, what do A and D refer to?

A4: A is Arm, meaning who in the customer organization is embracing you, that is, who is willing to speak for your solution and pass information on your behalf. D is Decision, referring to the decision chain, emphasizing that key-account purchasing is a multi-person, multi-department process rather than a single decision-maker. Neither should be guessed literally: A is not Authority or Approver, and D is not a saboteur.

### Q5: What is the dual-perspective funnel, and why look at both axes at once?

A5: The dual-perspective funnel maps seven customer purchasing stages (discovering problem, judging value, confirming solution exists, confirming ownership, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, commitment and signing) against five sales stages (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing), using behavioral evidence to judge synchronization. Looking only at your actions risks pushing a close before the customer claims responsibility, stalling the project.

### Q6: When a pilot does not convert to batch, where is it usually stuck?

A6: Three common sticking points: internal ownership not confirmed; customer still comparing build versus buy without total cost case; embracer not senior enough for budget and decision chain. Actions: reposition the seven-stage location using behavioral evidence, add a value statement with hidden costs, and establish a second contact on the decision chain.

### Q7: What should sales do after a batch deal closes?

A7: Use a migration strategy: after one successful delivery, continue expanding customer value and share through supplements, upgrades, or integration, for example from one process step to adjacent steps, or from a single plant to other bases in the group. Note the premise is that the first delivery truly succeeded. If pilot results are questionable, solve the problem before expanding, otherwise dissatisfaction will spread to more lines and more customers.

### Q8: What specifically should be prepared for BATNA before negotiating?

A8: BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, meaning the best fallback action a negotiator can take if the two sides cannot reach agreement. Preparation should clarify five things: what the executable alternative action is, where your bottom line is, how much authority you hold, what the other side's real interests are, and that all agreements are put in writing. The side without alternatives has only one option at the table: concession.
