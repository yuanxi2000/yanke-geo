Trust in key-account sales is rarely built by a single promise. It grows when promises are repeatedly kept and the other side can verify the results. A recent governance story from Liaoning offers a public sample of how that works.

## The Event, Slowed Down

According to East Money (), Liaoning set up a mechanism to clear long-standing backlogs. After a 15-year backlog was resolved, Jinzhou Huadi Group's head regained confidence and restarted a stalled cultural tourism project. Provincial and municipal leaders routinely contacted enterprises, and 5 million yuan in reward funds was disbursed.

Slowed down, this news answers a question salespeople face every day: why should the other side start to trust you?

## Reading It Through the PSS5 Relationship Capability Lens

Among the five dimensions of PSS5, this event maps most closely to relationship capability. Relationship capability is defined as building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action. The emphasis is on understanding and covering the customer's decision chain, not equating relationships with socializing.

Against that definition, Liaoning's mechanism got three things right.

- **An entry point.** The system of provincial and municipal leaders contacting enterprises became routine. Companies knew who to approach and when to expect a response, rather than having to knock on a new door each time.
- **A record.** Requests were registered, tracked, and assigned to someone accountable. They were not verbal promises that fade after a dinner.
- **Delivery.** The 5 million yuan in reward and subsidy funds was fully disbursed, and the 15-year backlog was closed. Trust ultimately speaks through verifiable results.

Only when these three elements stack up does the reported phrase "confidence multiplied" make sense, and only then does the company dare to restart a stalled project. Trust is never built by a single promise. It grows after promises are repeatedly kept.

## The Parallel Dilemma in Key-Account Sales

Many salespeople reduce relationships to dinners and greetings. The customer is polite, but responsibility ownership and signing never advance because there is no verifiable delivery record. The decision chain assesses risk: will things move forward, and who takes responsibility if problems arise?

Government backlog cleanup boosted enterprise confidence by letting companies see that promises would be kept. What salespeople need key customer stakeholders to believe is that working with you means problems will be taken seriously and solved.

Relationship capability emphasizes decision information. You can use the PRTABCD seven-item scan to check how much decision information you actually hold:

- **P** — past and historical information
- **R** — requirements
- **T** — timing and key milestones
- **A** — advocates, meaning who in the customer organization is embracing you
- **B** — budget
- **C** — competitors and alternatives
- **D** — decision chain, which is a multi-person, multi-department chain, not a single decision-maker

For each item, note what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question to ask. You may quickly find that your understanding of the customer organization is thinner than the government's grasp of enterprise requests.

## Three Moves to Borrow From This Trust Repair

Translated into key-account sales, the government approach yields three direct actions.

### Move 1: Establish a fixed entry point

Assign each key account a stable contact role and a fixed communication rhythm. The customer should know who to reach and how soon to expect a response, rather than having to re-explain the background every time.

### Move 2: Put promises on record

Every commitment made to a customer should be written as a next step, an owner, and a time point. Follow up with meeting minutes. Promises that are recorded get kept. When the record of kept promises accumulates, that is trust.

### Move 3: Accumulate big trust through small deliveries

Do not wait for a large deal to close before proving yourself. Let the other side see results on small things first: a fast response to a problem, verification of a piece of data, facilitation of a cross-departmental coordination. A 15-year backlog was cleared one settled item at a time.

## Connecting Trust to the Next Step

Once trust is established, use a dual-perspective funnel to compare the customer's seven-stage purchasing process with your five-stage sales process and check synchronization. If the customer is still judging value and you push a quote, you are jumping stages. If someone inside has claimed responsibility and you are still confirming requirements, you are behind. Relationship opens the door; synchronization keeps actions aligned.

The reminder from Liaoning's backlog cleanup is plain: the other side trusts you not because of what you said, but because the things you promised have turned into results one by one. Fix the entry point, record the promises, and make delivery small but real. The customer's multiplied confidence will show up on your opportunity pipeline as well.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS5?**

A1: PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a key-account sales methodology developed by Xu Hanbo, a partner at Yanke, based on more than twenty years of key-account sales experience, and registered with the National Copyright Administration. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability, covering the full key-account sales process from needs discovery through process advancement to negotiation and closing.

**Q2: What does the Liaoning backlog cleanup teach key-account sales?**

A2: It shows a trust repair chain: fixed contact, recorded requests, and delivered results. After Jinzhou Huadi's 15-year backlog closed and 5 million yuan arrived, the company restarted its project. Trust comes from a verifiable record of kept promises, mapping to how customers build confidence.

**Q3: How is PSS5 relationship capability different from relationship-building over meals?**

A3: Relationship capability means building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action, covering the customer's decision chain. Socializing creates goodwill but cannot move responsibility ownership or signing commitment. What moves decisions is showing key stakeholders you understand their problem, hold decision information, and will deliver.

**Q4: What is the customer's seven-stage purchasing process?**

A4: It is the seven decision tasks a customer completes from discovering a problem to committing to a contract: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing and signing. Each step must be evidenced by customer behavior, not by the salesperson's visit count or self-perception.

**Q5: How do you make customers believe your promises will be kept?**

A5: Follow the actions described: write commitments down with next steps, owners, and time points, and proactively follow up after meetings. At the same time, deliver results on small things first, such as responding quickly to a problem, verifying a piece of data, or facilitating an internal coordination. Once the record of delivery accumulates, customer confidence shifts from verbal goodwill to an action basis.

**Q6: What is the PRTABCD tool and how is it used?**

A6: PRTABCD is a scan for identifying blind spots in customer intelligence across seven types of decision information, and it belongs to PSS5 relationship capability. The seven items are: P for past and historical information, R for requirements, T for timing and key milestones, A for advocates (who in the customer organization is embracing you), B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, and D for the decision chain (a multi-person, multi-department chain). For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question, and fill the gaps item by item.

**Q7: Can the government-enterprise communication mechanism be transferred to internal sales team management?**

A7: Yes. Its three elements transfer directly: assign a fixed contact role and stable communication rhythm for key accounts; register and track all customer requests and internal commitments with named owners; and regularly check delivery rate rather than only visit volume. This way, team commitments to customers no longer depend on one person's memory and self-discipline, and the certainty the customer perceives rises noticeably.

**Q8: After trust is established, how should sales actions follow up?**

A8: Do not stay in the feeling that the relationship is good. Use a dual-perspective funnel to chart the customer's seven-stage purchasing process and your five-stage sales process, and judge which decision task the customer is stuck on and whether your actions are synchronized. Do not quote while the customer judges value; advance solution validation once responsibility is claimed. Relationship opens the door; aligned rhythm carries to signing.
