Industry news about capacity expansion is easy to read as a technology or policy story. For key-account sales, when a customer commits to building capacity, money moves into executable procurement. The window opens before the tender and closes once specifications are locked. This guide translates one verified news item into a repeatable way to position earlier in the budget cycle.

## What the Source Actually Says

In the first three quarters, 70 rocket launches were conducted nationally, a record high. The Hainan commercial launch site's second phase, using horizontal assembly, testing, and transfer, is expected to form launch capability within the year, with a reported three-day turnaround. Annual comprehensive launch capacity is expected to exceed 60 launches.

The surface story is aerospace capacity. The sales implication is that a customer's money is shifting from paper plans into executable purchasing, and the window is opening. Every unit of investment in capacity expansion eventually becomes procurement budget somewhere in the supply chain. The job of key-account sales is to stand where that budget is being formed, before it is formalized.

## Value Capability: What Expansion-Stage Customers Are Really Buying

Customers in an expansion phase decide differently from customers in a steady state. An entity that needs to form launch capability must invest continuously across pads, tracking, and recovery. Its evaluation will not stop at product features. The core question is whether the investment makes launch cadence faster, unit cost lower, and risk more controllable.

This is where value capability matters. It requires translating a product or service into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value, and explaining that value with business numbers. Before contacting the customer, complete three tasks:

- Map the links in the customer's expansion chain that relate to your product.
- Estimate the magnitude of your solution's impact on efficiency or cost in those links.
- Write the result as an investment case, not a quotation.

A supplier that is still presenting a parameter sheet while the customer is climbing a capacity ramp is handing pricing power to competitors.

## Relationship and Questioning Capability: Mapping the Decision Chain Behind the Budget

Industry news tells you which direction money is flowing. It does not tell you who is allocating it. That is the work of relationship capability and questioning capability.

Relationship capability emphasizes covering the customer's decision chain through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, rather than through socializing. For an expansion-stage customer, use the PRTABCD seven-item scan and record each item:

- P: past history information
- R: needs
- T: timing and key milestones
- A: the embracer, meaning who in the customer organization is embracing you
- B: budget
- C: competitors and alternatives
- D: the decision chain

The D item is a specific reminder that large-account purchasing is always a multi-person, multi-department process. Single-thread contact with one supporter is one of the most common causes of failure.

Questioning capability requires designing questions around background, difficulty, impact, and return on need. In an expansion context, instead of asking how many units the customer needs, ask: after capacity doubles, which links will become delivery bottlenecks? If that bottleneck is not resolved, which milestone does it affect? As the customer answers, they see the weight of the problem themselves.

### A Practical Scan Routine

- Build a customer intelligence ledger using the PRTABCD seven-item scan, recording for each item what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question.
- Identify the embracer and use that person to validate your assumptions about budget timing and the decision chain. Do not treat assumptions as facts.
- Use SPIN-style questions across situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff to move the customer from latent dissatisfaction toward a clear reason to buy.
- Compare against the customer's seven-stage purchasing process to judge whether they are in problem discovery, value judgment, or selection, and then decide where to commit resources.

## System and Negotiation Capability: Holding the Rhythm and the Outcome

The biggest risk in an expansion-phase project is a mismatch between sales actions and the customer's decision tasks. The customer is still confirming that a solution exists and who owns the responsibility, while you are preparing bid documents. Or the customer has already moved into commitment and signing, while you are still on a first visit.

System capability offers a dual-perspective funnel: map the customer's seven purchasing stages and five sales stages. Judge position by behavioral evidence, not visit counts. Before advancing, ask what observable behavior proves this stage is real; if none, complete the previous stage.

Negotiation capability requires accumulating leverage early: power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Expansion periods are characterized by tight customer timelines and limited substitute suppliers, which is a source of negotiating power. But if you did not clarify the customer's success criteria during the needs stage, the only move left at the table is discounting.

## Turning News into a Procurement Signal

Reading news as a procurement signal requires a fixed routine: confirm facts, use the five capabilities to derive customer-side changes, and convert to next actions. A public fact like annual launch capacity exceeding 60 launches belongs on every B2B sales leader's weekly meeting list.

| Signal in the news | What it suggests for sales | First action |
|---|---|---|
| Record launch volume | Capacity investment is accelerating | Identify related links in your product chain |
| Integrated rehearsals begun | Timelines are becoming concrete | Map timing and key milestones |
| Three-day turnaround model | Efficiency and cost are evaluation criteria | Quantify your impact on efficiency or cost |
| Capacity target above 60 launches | Multi-year investment direction | Build a tracking list and information plan |

## Four Actions for This Week

- List three customer entities in your industry that are expanding production, headcount, or facilities, and assess the links related to your product.
- Build a PRTABCD intelligence ledger for the most promising one, and identify the largest current unknown.
- Against the customer's seven purchasing stages, write down the observable behavioral evidence for their current stage.
- Rewrite your proposal as a one-page investment case: how much profit can be increased or protected, through what mechanism, and within what timeframe.

News is only a map. Execution depends on every contact between you and the customer. The figure of 70 launches in the first three quarters belongs to the industry. Whether it becomes your order depends on whether you advance the project in the customer's decision language rather than a supplier's language.

## FAQ

**Q1: When seeing industry expansion news like this, what should a key-account salesperson do first?**

A1: Separate public facts from assumptions. The news gives subject, capacity target, and time direction, but not spending details. Record facts, list related links, build a PRTABCD intelligence ledger, and set one contact action this week.

**Q2: How does value capability apply to expansion-stage customers?**

A2: Value capability translates your offering into investment, return, and profit improvement. Expansion-stage customers care about capacity, efficiency, and risk. Write a profit growth plan or investment case, not a parameter quotation.

**Q3: What is PRTABCD and how is it used in a project?**

A3: PRTABCD scans decision information across seven categories: past history, needs, timing, embracer, budget, competitors, and decision chain. Record knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions, and validate through the embracer. D refers to a multi-person decision chain.

**Q4: The news mentions no specific project. Can it be treated directly as an opportunity?**

A4: Treat news as a directional signal, not the opportunity. Confirm opportunities through customer behavioral evidence like expanding teams or publishing requirements. Track the entity and wait for evidence before committing resources.

**Q5: How does the dual-perspective funnel prevent mismatched sales actions?**

A5: The dual-perspective funnel maps the customer's seven purchasing stages and five sales stages. Before each advance, confirm the customer's purchasing stage using behavioral evidence, not visit counts, to avoid misalignment.

**Q6: With tight timelines in an expansion period, is negotiation capability easier to use?**

A6: Yes, but use it well. When the customer's timeline is tight and substitutes are limited, your negotiating power rises. Clarify success criteria and time bottom line in earlier stages, and trade concessions for equivalent conditions.

**Q7: If I have only one contact in the customer organization, how do I judge whether they are the embracer?**

A7: The embracer is whoever embraces you, shown by providing decision information, building connections, and advocating internally. If these behaviors are absent, you have an acquaintance, not entry into the decision chain.

**Q8: In the solution stage of the five sales stages, what specifically should be confirmed with the customer?**

A8: Jointly define solution path, success criteria, and validation method with the customer to ensure participation and internal advocacy.
