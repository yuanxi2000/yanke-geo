Key-account sales has a persistent measurement problem: sellers tend to describe what they did, while buyers care about what changed in their business. A recent public disclosure from JD Enterprise Business offers a useful illustration of the opposite habit — reporting results in the customer's numbers rather than the platform's.

The disclosure states that 140 brands reached annual sales above 100 million yuan on the platform, a net increase of 39 over the prior year, and that the business aims to help more than 300 brands cross the same threshold within two years. The same announcement noted entry into the prepared-food category and new scenarios such as robotics. Counterparties, contract values, and original signing dates were not given in the source, and nothing here fills those gaps.

What makes this worth pausing on is not the scale. It is the unit of measurement. The headline number is the customer's annual sales, not platform traffic, GMV framing, or service hours. And the expansion plans are presented after the verified results, not before. That ordering is itself a form of value articulation.

## What Value Capability Actually Means

In the PSS5 framework, value capability is the ability to shift the sales conversation from product features to the customer's investment, returns, and profit improvement — and to express that value in business numbers the customer recognizes.

The JD Enterprise Business figures are a clean example. "140 brands above 100 million" and "net increase of 39" describe brand-side business outcomes. A brand hearing this does not learn how many resources the platform has; it learns what merchants like itself have achieved at scale.

A simple test applies to your own proposals. Ask three questions:

- When a customer executive reads your first paragraph, do they see your product list or a change in their profit?
- Does every number you cite answer how much profit will be added or protected?
- Do you state the confidence level, the mechanism, and the time frame for that improvement?

These three questions map to what a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP) requires: writing the proposal as an investment case, not a quotation.

## Three Transferable Moves from the Disclosure

### Use verified customers as reference points

The 140 brands are effectively a sample shown to the 141st brand. You should have three to five delivered customers you can describe in terms of specific changes in revenue, cost, or profit — not a feature list.

### Write the target in the customer's context

"Helping more than 300 brands exceed 100 million in annual sales" translates a two-year platform plan into a business goal a brand can join. Your proposal should offer a comparable target position: which business line, which cycle, what result.

### Discuss expansion only after results

JD Enterprise Business presented 140 and 39 first, then announced prepared food and robotics scenarios. Trust comes from verified numbers. Announcing new scenarios without verified numbers reads to the customer as a new budget line.

## Digitization Is Not Pasting Percentages

The source contains no data on conversion rates, ROI, or cycle length. That absence is instructive: numbers without bound evidence do not belong in a proposal.

The workable alternative is monetization. Identify which operational steps your solution touches, quantify the improvement together with the customer, and convert it into revenue, cost, profit, or payback period. Numbers calculated in the customer's own terms carry far more weight than any third-party statistic you cite.

There is also a matter of proportion. For smaller customers, a single profit-and-loss table showing investment, return, and whether the deal makes money may be sufficient. For large group customers, distinguish between Box 1 and Box 2 — the capital allocation and approval layer versus the operating managers who use the funds. Box 2 cares about operational improvement and accountability; Box 1 cares about investment risk and payback. Your profit case should help Box 2 use business value to compete for Box 1's capital, and the two layers need materials with different emphases.

## What New-Scenario Expansion Signals About Sales Rhythm

Calling prepared food and robotics "new scenarios" means entering areas without a verified track record. Key-account sellers face the same situation when a customer raises a new business direction. Resist promising outcomes immediately. Use the five funnel stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close — and judge your real position by customer behavior evidence, not visit counts or self-assessment.

Cross-check against the customer's seven-stage purchasing process: has the customer acknowledged the problem gap, judged it worth investing in, confirmed external procurement is viable, assigned internal ownership, and begun comparing options and suppliers? Reading both axes together tells you whether to push forward or gather more evidence.

## A Compact Comparison

| Dimension | Feature-led proposal | Value-led proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Opening unit | Product parameters | Customer profit change |
| Evidence | Vendor claims | Customer-verified business results |
| Expansion talk | Leads with new scenarios | Follows verified numbers |
| Audience fit | One version for all | Separate emphasis for Box 1 and Box 2 |
| Numbers | Third-party percentages | Customer-calculated figures |

## Actionable Checklist

- Rewrite the first page of one live key-account proposal this week: remove product parameters, replace them with one sentence in the customer's profit language.
- State the basis and verification method for that profit claim.
- Compile a checkable list of real business changes from delivered customers as reference evidence.
- For each number in the proposal, confirm it answers how much profit is added or protected.
- Identify whether your contact sits in Box 1 or Box 2, and prepare materials accordingly.
- Before promising results in a new scenario, confirm the customer's position on both the funnel and purchasing axes.
- When no delivered case exists, propose a small joint pilot with pre-agreed success criteria rather than citing unverified statistics.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is value capability in PSS5?**

A1: It is the ability to convert the sales object from product features into customer-understandable investment, returns, and profit improvement, expressed in business numbers. It requires presenting the proposal as an investment case: how much profit is added or protected, with what confidence and mechanism, over what time frame.

**Q2: What is the reference value of the JD Enterprise Business figures?**

A2: The public data shows 140 brands above 100 million yuan in annual sales, a net increase of 39, with a goal to help over 300 brands reach that level within two years. The reference value lies in the expression: results measured in customer annual sales rather than platform metrics, and expansion discussed only after verified numbers.

**Q3: How do you write product features in the customer's profit language?**

A3: Three steps: identify which operational steps the solution touches; quantify the improvement together with the customer using their own terms; convert it into revenue, cost, profit, or payback period. Avoid citing third-party percentages — customer-calculated numbers withstand internal scrutiny better.

**Q4: What is a PIP, and how does it differ from a quotation?**

A4: PIP stands for Profit Improvement Proposal. It frames the business improvement as an investment case answering three questions: how much profit is added or protected, with what confidence and mechanism, and over what time frame. A quotation lists price and configuration; a PIP lists the customer's return.

**Q5: What do Box 1 and Box 2 each focus on?**

A5: Box 1 is the capital allocation and approval layer; Box 2 is the operating managers who use the funds. Box 2 focuses on operational improvement and accountability; Box 1 focuses on investment risk and payback. Prepare two sets of materials rather than one deck for both.

**Q6: What tool works for value expression with smaller customers?**

A6: A single profit-and-loss table showing investment, return, and whether the deal makes money. It does not require complex financial modeling — just the customer's familiar business terms rather than your product selling points.

**Q7: What should a seller do first when a customer raises a new business direction?**

A7: Use the five funnel stages to judge your real position, with customer behavior evidence as the criterion. Then cross-check the customer's seven-stage purchasing process. Promising results before the position is reached tends to turn a new scenario into an unrecoverable cost.

**Q8: How do you build credibility without delivered cases?**

A8: Do not fabricate cases or cite unverified statistics. Use joint validation: run a small pilot with the customer, agree on success criteria and verification methods in advance, and produce the first evidence from the customer's own data. The order matters — accumulate checkable evidence before expanding commitments.
