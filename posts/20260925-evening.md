A large-scale robot deployment in a theme park is not about robots. It is about how a technology vendor and an operating customer translated each other's output into return. According to a September 25, 2026 report by East Money Information, AgiBot and Chimelong Group produced the world's first large-scale embodied-intelligence theme park, with over 300 robots interacting with guests. The report notes AgiBot's 20,000th general-purpose robot came off the line, and each park robot is paired with a supervising staff member.

That is a useful sample of what happens when a technical capability is placed into a real commercial setting and asked to prove itself. For key-account sales, the underlying question never changes: why should the customer pay for your proposal?

## The first lesson of value capability: product features are not customer value

PSS5 defines five capabilities: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value capability is defined as converting the object of the sale from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with operating numbers.

The same robot, measured inside a factory, is judged on cycle time, yield, and labor substitution.

The robot vendor would not have pitched servo motor specs to Chimelong. It would have pitched an upgraded park experience, topic, and operational efficiency. Chimelong's willingness to open its venue was a calculation: would a new species bring incremental visitors, extend dwell time, and keep trial-and-error costs manageable? Only when each side translates the other's output into its own language of return does a transaction find footing. That is the monetization move value capability performs: identify operating links affected, quantify improvement, and convert to revenue, cost, profit, or payback.

## From public facts to key-account battle judgments: three executable moves

Suppose your company also wants to enter a similar large cultural-tourism or chain-operations customer. Three concrete moves can be drawn from this event.

### Find the Box 2 who will validate the scenario with you

Box 1 is the funding-allocation and approval layer; Box 2 is the manager layer responsible for operations and for using the funds. A park operations lead is a typical Box 2. He has to prove to the board that this introduction can move operating metrics. The salesperson's job is to help him articulate the operating value clearly, so that he has material to fight for budget, rather than going straight at the procurement department to push on price.

### Use the seven stages of customer purchasing to judge where the customer stands

The seven stages are: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, build versus buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and signing.

### Agree on success criteria and validation methods during the solution stage

What is valuable about this kind of scenario collaboration is that the validation cycle is short and guest feedback is direct.

## How questioning capability and relationship capability divide the work in scenario projects

Value capability gets numbers straight, but depends on internal customer information. The PRTABCD scan covers seven decision items: past history, requirements, timing, advocates, budget, competitors, and decision chain. Advocates are who actively embraces your proposal; the decision chain involves multiple people and departments. Key-account sales must record for each item what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question.

Information gaps are filled by questioning. SPIN's four question types—situation, problem, implication, need-payoff—can be used: ask about visitor dwell time and spending, the experience shortfall, amplify its impact on operating targets, and lead the customer to state the return. A number the customer calculates is more credible. This is relationship capability: trust through professional ability and credible action, not socializing.

## Closing: treat every scenario deployment as an asset

A theme park as a training ground means one successful scenario delivery becomes benchmark evidence and a starting point for migration to the next deal.

## Comparison: factory setting versus park setting

| Dimension | Factory setting | Theme park setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is measured | Cycle time, yield, labor substitution | Visitor traffic, secondary spending, brand buzz |
| Who owns the operating result | Production and operations management | Park operations lead |
| Nature of the value story | Efficiency and cost improvement | Experience upgrade and revenue growth |
| Validation cycle | Longer, tied to production ramp | Shorter, with direct guest feedback |
| Function of the robot | Unchanged | Unchanged |

## Actionable checklist

- Identify the Box 2 who owns the operating metric and will validate the scenario with you.
- Determine which of the seven purchasing stages the customer has actually reached, using customer behavior evidence rather than your own visit count.
- Translate the proposal into the customer's operating links: which link it affects, how much improvement it brings, and when the investment is recovered.
- Complete a PRTABCD scan and record known, unknown, source, and next question for each of the seven items.
- Use SPIN questions to let the customer articulate the value of change themselves.
- Agree on success metrics, review cadence, and adjustment rules during the solution stage.
- After delivery, review whether the promised operating improvement materialized.
- Turn verifiable results into benchmark evidence in the customer's language before planning secondary expansion.

## FAQ

**Q1: What exactly does value capability mean in PSS5?**

A1: Value capability is the ability to convert the object of the sale from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and to explain that value with operating numbers. It asks the salesperson to stop listing parameters and instead answer three questions: which operating link the proposal affects, how much measurable improvement it brings, and when the customer's investment is recovered. PSS5 consists of five dimensions, value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation, and value capability is the starting point.

**Q2: What does the Chimelong robot deployment suggest for vendor sales?**

A2: This public event indicates that when large operating customers buy new technology, they care not about the equipment itself but about whether the scenario can drive traffic, buzz, and operational efficiency. The implication for sales is to first find out who inside the customer is responsible for the operating metric, help that person prove the value of the introduction to the budget layer using the customer's own books, and avoid going straight to procurement to talk price. The language of value must follow the customer's revenue structure.

**Q3: How do you distinguish Box 1 from Box 2 in key-account sales?**

A3: Box 1 is the funding-allocation and approval layer, which cares about investment return and risk; Box 2 is the manager layer responsible for operations and using funds, which cares about meeting business goals. The correct order is to help Box 2 get the operating value straight first, then support that person in seeking budget from Box 1. Many salespeople fail by skipping Box 2 and going straight at the approval layer, ending up with neither an internal supporter nor a clear account of the proposal's contribution.

**Q4: How do the seven purchasing stages help judge sales pace?**

A4: The seven stages are the seven decision tasks a customer completes in sequence from discovering a problem to commitment and signing: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, build versus buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and signing. Each step must be proven by customer behavior evidence, not replaced by the salesperson's own visit count. Only by judging which stage the customer is in can your actions serve the decision task in front of them, rather than pushing ahead out of sequence.

**Q5: How is the PRTABCD scan used in practice?**

A5: PRTABCD uses seven decision information types to identify intelligence blind spots: past history, requirements, timing, advocates, budget, competitors, and decision chain. Write down for each what is known, unknown, the source, and whom to ask next. Advocates are who actively embraces your proposal; decision chain is the multi-person, multi-department structure. It is an information-scanning tool within relationship capability.

**Q6: How does SPIN questioning land in scenario projects?**

A6: SPIN advances needs investigation through four question types: situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff. Applied to a scenario project: first ask about the customer's current operating data and experience status, then ask what shortfall most affects the business, then lead the other side to consider how large the impact of that shortfall is on the full-year target, and finally ask what return closing the shortfall would bring. Letting the customer state the value of change themselves is far more effective than the salesperson presenting the proposal one-sidedly.

**Q7: Why define success criteria jointly with the customer during the solution stage?**

A7: Because success criteria determine the speaking position in later negotiation and acceptance. Agreeing during the solution stage on which metrics measure the effect, how often to review, and how to adjust if targets are missed anchors subsequent discussion in evidence. At the negotiation stage, the two sides exchange facts and conditions rather than their own feelings and postures. A project without success criteria tends to produce conflicting accounts at acceptance, and the room for concession is passively enlarged.

**Q8: What should sales do after a key account closes?**

A8: The five sales stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing, and the work after closing matters just as much. After first delivery, three things should be done: review whether the proposal delivered the promised operating improvement; organize verifiable results into benchmark evidence in the customer's language; and plan secondary expansion, whether supplementary, upgrade, or integrated, based on the trust already established. One successful delivery is the starting point for the next deal, while a failed delivery should not be mechanically expanded.
