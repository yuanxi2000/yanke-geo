Technical specifications win applause. Customer value wins orders. That single line captures the hardest lesson in key-account sales: the first job is not to present what you are proud of, but to translate product facts into business facts that a customer is willing to exchange budget for.

A recent news cycle offers a clean illustration. According to reporting cited by East Money, Geely Automobile Group sold more than 290,000 vehicles in September, a year-to-date high, with seven consecutive months of both year-over-year and month-over-month growth. In the same window, the group also announced a new generation of AI charging technology, with a single-gun peak charging power of 2,250 kW, described as the fastest mass-production charging speed globally, while keeping maximum charging temperature within 65°C and improving battery cycle life by 20%.

The sales number and the technology announcement appeared in the same news story. That is not a coincidence. It is a complete demonstration of value capability: technical ability was validated by the market through purchasing behavior.

For B2B key-account sales teams, this is worth studying. Many teams do not lack good products. What they lack is the set of actions that turns a product into the customer's operating language. Geely converted technical facts — 2,250 kW, 65°C, 20% — into a sales result of seven consecutive months of dual growth. Somewhere in that chain, there must have been a perceivable value expression aimed at end users.

That chain is exactly where value capability sits within PSS5, Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales. Value capability means shifting the sales object from product features to the customer's understandable investment, return, and profit improvement, and explaining value with operating numbers.

## The Three-Step Translation from Parameters to Profit

Value capability in key-account sales is not a polished value proposition. It is three executable moves.

### Step 1: Locate the Operating Link

Find the operating link your solution actually affects. Just as charging speed affects user experience, site turnover, and operational efficiency, your solution may affect production line utilization, inventory turnover, energy cost, or delivery cycle. If you cannot locate the link, every value statement is idling.

### Step 2: Quantify the Improvement

Quantify the improvement. Hold one discipline here: calculate what can be calculated, and do not fabricate what cannot. When you do not have the customer's operating data, the correct approach is to calculate jointly with the customer using questioning capability, not to force-fit an industry average.

### Step 3: Monetize the Result

Monetize. Convert the improvement into revenue, cost, profit, payback period, or opportunity cost. When the customer's manager layer needs your numbers to persuade the funding approval layer, they need an account of input and output that holds up — not a feature list.

A compact way to remember the sequence:

- Locate the link: which specific position in the customer's operating chain does the solution affect?
- Calculate jointly: use structured questioning to obtain real data and calculate with the customer, not for the customer.
- Monetize: write the improvement into revenue, cost, profit, or payback period, so the procurement rationale becomes an investment rationale.

## Reading the Evidence Structure Behind the Sales Record

Seven consecutive months of dual growth is a strong sample of value validation. Key-account sales works the same way: the value you tell the customer cannot stay in the proposal. It must land on observable behavioral evidence from the customer.

A customer willing to hold an internal review meeting, willing to bring finance into the calculation, willing to write your solution into a budget line — these behaviors carry more weight than any verbal approval.

In the dual-perspective funnel of system capability, step 2 of the customer's seven-stage purchasing process is judging value. Whether the customer acknowledges that the problem is worth investing in directly determines whether the project lives or dies. Many projects die at the proposal stage, but the root cause is that they never passed the value judgment stage: the customer was only politely chatting.

Value capability work must therefore move earlier. Complete value confirmation during the needs stage through calculation actions, rather than discussing benefits for the first time during the proposal presentation. This is also where value capability and system capability coordinate: value confirmation relies on the former, stage advancement relies on the latter.

| Dimension | Weak approach | Strong approach |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Product features and parameters | Customer operating link and investment logic |
| Data source | Industry averages applied blindly | Joint calculation with customer data |
| Language | Functional description | Revenue, cost, profit, payback period |
| Evidence | Verbal approval | Customer behavior: review meetings, finance involvement, budget inclusion |
| Timing | Benefits first discussed at proposal | Value confirmed during needs stage |

## Three Actions for Sales Leaders

1. Write a one-page value card for each main product: the affected link, the calculation logic, and the monetization standard. Require sales to open with only this card in front of the customer.
2. Set a hard gate at the needs stage of the sales funnel: no value calculation with customer participation, no entry into the proposal stage.
3. Review every lost deal with one question: did the customer ever produce a number for this account? If not, value capability was never truly online.

Geely proved the closed loop of product value transmission through a sales result. For key-account sales teams, the question is when your customer will vote for your value proposition with their purchasing behavior. There is no shortcut — only making every link, every calculation, and every page of the account solid. Value capability is trained, not talked about.

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify the specific operating link your solution affects before discussing price.
- Prepare a one-page value card for each main product.
- Use structured questioning to obtain real customer data rather than assuming industry averages.
- Calculate jointly with the customer so the numbers survive internal scrutiny.
- Convert improvements into financial language: revenue, cost, profit, payback period, opportunity cost.
- Confirm value during the needs stage, not at the proposal presentation.
- Track customer behavior evidence: internal review meetings, finance participation, budget inclusion, assigned owners.
- Set a funnel gate: no customer-participated value calculation, no proposal stage.
- Review lost deals by asking whether the customer ever produced a number.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does value capability mean in PSS5?**

A1: Value capability is one of the five capabilities in PSS5. It means shifting the sales object from product features to the customer's understandable investment, return, and profit improvement, and explaining value with operating numbers. It emphasizes answering how much profit this investment can increase or protect, through what mechanism, and within what time frame — so the procurement rationale becomes an investment rationale.

**Q2: How does the Geely September sales event relate to B2B sales?**

A2: Public reporting indicates Geely Automobile Group sold more than 290,000 vehicles in September, a year-to-date high, with seven consecutive months of dual growth, and announced a new generation of AI charging technology in the same period. This shows that technical capability, after effective value expression, was ultimately validated by the market through purchasing behavior. B2B sales can borrow this chain: translate product facts into business value the customer is willing to exchange budget for, and validate it with customer behavior evidence.

**Q3: What is the first step in translating technical parameters into customer value?**

A3: The first step is to locate the customer operating link the solution actually affects, such as production line utilization, inventory turnover, energy cost, or delivery cycle. A common mistake is jumping from parameters directly to quotation, skipping link location. Only after confirming where the solution lands in the customer's operating chain do calculation and monetization become meaningful.

**Q4: How do you calculate value when you lack customer operating data?**

A4: The correct approach is to calculate jointly with the customer using intentional questioning, rather than force-fitting industry averages. Design questions around background, difficulty, impact, and need-payoff, guide the customer to provide key data, and calculate together. This has two benefits: the data source is reliable, and the customer participated in the calculation, making the conclusion easier to adopt and spread internally.

**Q5: What is monetization and why does it matter in key-account sales?**

A5: Monetization means identifying the customer operating link the solution can affect, quantifying the improvement, and converting the result into financial language such as revenue, cost, profit, payback period, or opportunity cost. Key-account procurement often requires the business manager layer to persuade the funding approval layer. The approval layer does not listen to feature parameters; it listens to the account of this investment. Salespeople who can write value in financial language earn a seat at the real decision table.

**Q6: How does value capability relate to the needs stage of the sales funnel?**

A6: Step 2 of the customer's seven-stage purchasing process is judging value — whether the problem is worth investing resources to solve. Value capability work must move earlier to this step: confirm value during the needs stage through calculation, so the customer acknowledges the problem is worth investing in. Many projects die at the proposal stage because the value judgment never passed; the customer was only politely chatting. Value confirmation cannot be postponed to the proposal presentation.

**Q7: How do you know the customer truly accepts your value proposition?**

A7: Look at customer behavior evidence, not verbal statements. Willingness to hold an internal review meeting, bring finance into the calculation, write the solution into a budget line, or assign an owner to push it forward — these are evidence that value has been accepted. Sales should judge progress by customer-side behavior evidence, not by visit count or self-perception. This is also why PSS5 emphasizes customer behavior evidence.

**Q8: How can sales leaders implement value capability in a team?**

A8: Start with three actions: write a one-page value card for each main product, specifying the affected link, calculation logic, and monetization standard; set a hard gate at the needs stage so no value calculation without customer participation enters the proposal stage; and review lost deals by asking whether the customer ever produced a number for the account. Use systems and processes to make value capability a standard team action, not an occasional flash of insight.
