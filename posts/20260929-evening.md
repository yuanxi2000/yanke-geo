A public expansion announcement is the same information for everyone who reads it. The difference is what a salesperson does with it. One reader forwards the link. Another turns it into a stage judgment, two procurement windows, and a profit-improvement proposal that can be carried into a first meeting.

This guide uses a specific, verifiable example: an announcement that a company plans to expand optical module capacity. The facts below come from that announcement as reported. Everything else is business analysis built on those facts, not a claim about what the company has actually done internally.

## Start with the four facts that actually carry weight

Many salespeople see expansion news and think only one thing: they are going to buy equipment. Then they mass-send product materials. In the customer's seven-stage buying process, that action is nearly useless, because the customer may still be at the problem-discovery or value-judgment stage while you are handing over a proposal and a price.

Read the announcement as checkable facts first.

- **Investment scale.** Approximately 288 million yuan for the R&D and production base, and approximately 21.5586 million yuan for the high-speed optical module R&D project. The amounts show the customer is willing to commit real money.
- **Timeline.** The base is planned for construction from November 2026 to June 2028, a duration of about 20 months. The R&D project is planned for April 2028 to June 2028, a duration of about 3 months. Two timelines mean two different procurement windows.
- **Construction approach.** The project is implemented in two parts under unified planning with phased execution. This suggests procurement will most likely happen in batches; the odds of locking the entire order in a single visit are low.
- **Technical direction.** High-speed optical modules are itemized separately, signaling that technology upgrade demand and capacity demand run in parallel. Performance and validation will appear in the evaluation criteria.

These four points can be verified directly from the source. No guessing is required. The next step is to place these facts into the dual-perspective funnel and judge where the customer stands and what you should do.

## Use the seven buying stages to judge timing

The customer's buying process is broken into seven stages: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing.

Because the expansion announcement was published publicly, the customer has already completed several early steps internally: the gap has been acknowledged, the investment has been justified, and responsibility has likely been claimed by someone. When you enter, you most likely land between stage four and stage six, that is, between build-versus-buy and selection.

That position determines your action:

- If the customer is still comparing internal build against external purchase, your job is to help them calculate total cost and risk with business numbers, not to rush a equipment price.
- If the customer has already entered selection, you should fight to participate in defining the evaluation criteria and validation method, so that the scoring sheet includes items where you are strong and competitors are weak.

On the selling side, this roughly corresponds to the needs and solution stage. Buyer and seller stay in sync only when you do not skip ahead.

### What value capability looks like in practice

Value capability means converting the sales object from product features into investment, returns, and profit improvement that the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. Applied to this expansion project, there are three concrete moves.

**Deploy resources by investment rhythm.** The base construction runs about 20 months; the R&D project runs about 3 months. Their procurement rhythms are completely different. Align your resources and delivery cycles with these two timelines and turn it into a phased supply plan. The customer can understand it, and you will not bet resources on the wrong window early.

**Write a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP).** This is not a quotation. It answers how much profit your solution can add or protect, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. For example, the impact of yield, energy consumption, and on-time delivery rate on capacity ramp-up can all be monetized.

**Use the seven-item PRTABCD scan to fill intelligence gaps.** Past history, real needs, key timing, who is embracing you (A for Arm), budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, and the next question. The announcement only tells you part of the first three; the rest must be confirmed by people.

Note the boundary: the announcement does not disclose the supplier list, the tender method, or the internal decision-makers. These unknowns must go into your intelligence checklist, not be treated as known and turned into a story. The four lenses of motivation, benchmark, vision, and solution are likewise hypotheses to be verified, not customer facts to be told back to the customer.

## Three actions for the sales manager this week

1. **Assign one account manager.** Draft the PRTABCD scan within 48 hours, confirming build-versus-buy inclination and the decision chain.
2. **Work backward from the two dates.** Use November 2026 and April 2028 to plan delivery, stocking, and early capacity locks.
3. **Draft the PIP.** List influenceable areas and numbers to confirm; bring questions, not prices.

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the public facts about this optical module expansion?**

A September 27, 2026 report says the company plans to expand optical module capacity in two parts: a base built over about 20 months from November 2026 to June 2028 for about 288 million yuan, and a roughly 3-month R&D project from April to June 2028 for about 21.5586 million yuan. Other content here is business analysis, not internal actions already taken.

**Q2: What is value capability?**

Value capability is one of five dimensions in the PSS5 framework, alongside relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability means converting the sales object from product features into investment, returns, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. In an expansion announcement scenario, value capability requires you to focus on the customer's investment account, not on your own product parameters.

**Q3: Why not quote a price immediately after seeing the expansion announcement?**

At announcement, the customer may have completed only problem discovery, value judgment, and solution confirmation, not the build-versus-buy decision or supplier selection. Quoting now reveals your hand, invites price anchoring, and risks wasted resources if they build internally. Judge the stage first, then match your action.

**Q4: What are the seven buying stages?**

They are the buyer-side stage axis in the PSS5 system capability. They describe seven decision tasks the customer completes from problem discovery to commitment and signing: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. Each step must be proven with customer behavioral evidence, not replaced by the salesperson's own visit count or feelings. Together with the five selling stages, they form a dual-perspective funnel in a many-to-many relationship.

**Q5: How do the five selling stages relate to the seven buying stages?**

The five selling stages—contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing—are the seller's axis; the seven buying stages are the buyer's decision tasks. They form a many-to-many relationship, not one-to-one. Place both on one dual-perspective funnel, judge each separately, and use customer behavioral evidence to spot synchronization, leading, lagging, or misalignment.

**Q6: How is a PIP used in an optical module expansion scenario?**

A PIP, or Profit Improvement Proposal, states the operational improvement as an investment: how much profit is added or protected, through what mechanism, and when. Here, quantify equipment yield, energy, and on-time delivery effects on ramp-up and unit cost, then translate them into profit or payback language for management.

**Q7: What information can PRTABCD help fill in from the announcement?**

PRTABCD scans seven categories: past history, needs, timing and milestones, who embraces you, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain. The announcement covers only part of needs and timing. Record known, unknown, source, and next question for each item, turning blanks into a first-visit question list rather than guesses.

**Q8: How should a supplier arrange resources for a phased expansion project?**

The two parts differ in timing: about 20 months for the base, about 3 months for the R&D project. Reverse-plan supply from both timelines, lock early capacity for urgent batches, concentrate early resources on needs and evaluation criteria, and invest commercially once selection and signing begin.
