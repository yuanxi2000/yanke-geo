Regulatory news rarely reads like a sales signal. Most sellers skim a compliance notice, decide it has nothing to do with what they sell, and move on. That habit can cost you the earliest and cheapest form of opportunity intelligence available: a public event that forces a customer to reopen a purchasing decision they had already closed.

This guide looks at one such event — a regional securities regulator asking fund managers, asset managers, and fund distributors to implement new rules on online marketing of financial products — and turns it into a repeatable field method. The notice added requirements around third-party internet platform cooperation, self-operated app development, transfer channels, and information reporting. For key-account sales, that kind of compliance action often means the customer's procurement process restarts.

A note on scope before we go further: the source material identifies one securities firm only as the subject tagged in a morning briefing. The underlying report did not describe that firm's internal actions, budget, or partners. Everything below is therefore about the compliance-triggered scenario as a category, not a claim about what any named institution has done.

## Read Regulation as an Opportunity Radar

When a new rule lands, it forces the customer to re-answer steps one through five of their own seven-stage purchasing process: Is this problem discovered? Is it worth investing in? Does a viable solution exist in the market? Who owns the responsibility? Build in-house or buy?

Each requirement maps to a decision task that may reopen:

- Third-party platform cooperation restricted → the customer may need to build its own reach capability.
- Transfer channels regulated → the customer may need to change service providers.
- Reporting requirements raised → the customer may need to upgrade data and reporting tools.

This is why compliance news belongs on your radar. It tells you in advance which accounts are about to be pushed into a buying decision.

One discipline matters here: public information only supports what public information actually says. When you write an internal battle report, do not convert a regulatory requirement into a claim about a specific customer's plans.

## Five Dimensions for Battlefield Judgment

PSS5 breaks key-account selling capability into five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Against a compliance-driven opportunity, each has a concrete move.

### Value: Translate Clauses into the Customer's Profit Language

If third-party platform cooperation is restricted, the customer's customer-acquisition cost structure has to be recalculated. Help them build the arithmetic: the difference between building reach in-house and buying it externally, across investment, return, and payback. Let the customer decide with business numbers rather than fear.

### Relationship: Map the Decision Chain Before Knocking

Compliance projects usually involve the compliance department, IT, channel operations, and sometimes finance. Use PRTABCD to scan seven pieces of decision information first, establish who owns the remediation responsibility, who holds the budget, and who signs off — then decide which door to knock on first.

### Questioning: Advance with Four Question Types

Use the four SPIN question categories. Situation questions ask how reporting currently works. Problem questions ask where existing channels are stuck. Implication questions ask what business gets dragged down if reporting falls short. Need-payoff questions let the customer articulate the value of on-time compliance themselves.

### System: Compare Your Five Stages Against Their Seven

Use the five sales stages against the customer's seven purchasing stages to locate where you are. Early in a new rule's rollout, most customers sit between discovering the problem and confirming a solution exists. Jumping straight to a proposal and quote is skipping levels. Accompany the customer through value judgment and responsibility confirmation first, and your actions stay in sync.

### Negotiation: Prepare Your BATNA Early

Compliance projects carry time pressure, and the customer's urgency is one of your sources of leverage. Prepare your BATNA in advance: your executable alternative, bottom line, and authority. Trade conditions rather than concede unilaterally to buy a timeline.

## Clear Intelligence Blind Spots with PRTABCD

The biggest risk in a compliance-triggered opportunity is charging in with stale assumptions. Before you start, fill in all seven PRTABCD items:

| Item | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| P — Past information | What history do we have with this account? |
| R — Requirement | What need is actually on the table? |
| T — Time and key milestones | What deadlines and gates govern this? |
| A — Adopter | Who inside is embracing you, and why? |
| B — Budget | Who controls the money? |
| C — Competition and alternatives | What else could the customer choose? |
| D — Decision chain | Which people and departments must jointly approve? |

Two items deserve extra scrutiny. A answers who in the customer organization is embracing you and why they are willing to help. D answers which people and departments must jointly sign off on the remediation. For every item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question to ask. Fill gaps by asking, not by guessing.

## Three Things You Can Do This Week

1. **Filter the list.** Pull out accounts directly constrained by the online marketing rules. Rank them by third-party platform dependence, self-operated app maturity, and reporting pressure. Book the top three first.
2. **Prepare ammunition.** For each account, write a one-page interpretation of the new rules' impact. Cite only the notice's actual requirements, and pair it with two or three questions you will use to confirm which purchasing stage the customer is in.
3. **Set the rhythm.** Mark each account's position on the dual-view funnel along with evidence of the next customer behavior — for example, confirmation from the responsible department or entry into co-creation of a solution. Calibrate weekly progress by customer actions, not by your own visit count.

## FAQ

**What is PSS5?**

PSS5 is a key-account sales methodology built around five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value translates solutions into the customer's profit and investment terms. Relationship penetrates the decision chain. Questioning surfaces and amplifies real needs. System manages the sales process. Negotiation pursues sustainable agreements. This article uses those five dimensions to break down compliance-triggered opportunities.

**Why is regulatory rollout a sales opportunity?**

New rules change the customer's operating constraints: previously viable channels get restricted, reporting requirements rise, existing cooperation models need adjustment. The customer must re-evaluate the gap between current state and target — which reactivates the discovery and value-judgment stages of their purchasing process. Whoever helps the customer complete that judgment and confirm the path and ownership first holds the early position in later solution and selection stages. Compliance pressure also brings explicit deadlines, giving the push a rhythm.

**How does value work with financial customers?**

Help the customer do the compliance math: the difference between building reach in-house and buying externally across investment, return, risk, and payback period; how much rework and penalty risk a reporting upgrade removes. A profit-and-loss style table lets both compliance and finance see why the spend is justified.

**What is PRTABCD used for?**

It is a seven-item decision-information scan belonging to the relationship dimension. On a compliance opportunity, A answers who is embracing you and why, and D answers who must jointly approve the remediation. Record known, unknown, source, and next question for each item.

**How do the customer's seven stages relate to the five sales stages?**

The customer's seven stages describe their decision tasks: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership, build-or-buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and signing. The five sales stages are the seller's axis: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close. The two axes are many-to-many, not one-to-one. Placing both on a single dual-view funnel and judging sync, lead, lag, or misalignment by customer behavior evidence is what keeps buyer and seller aligned.

**How should questions be designed for compliance purchases?**

Use the four SPIN categories. Situation: how do platform cooperation and reporting currently run? Problem: which links already feel strained? Implication: if reporting falls short or channels are restricted, which business metrics suffer? Need-payoff: if remediation completes on time, what does that mean for acquisition and operations? Let the customer state the value.

**How does the system dimension apply here?**

Manage the process with the dual-view funnel and judge progress by customer-side exit evidence, not visit counts, CRM percentages, or gut feel. Early on, most customers sit between discovery and solution confirmation, so focus on helping them complete value judgment and responsibility confirmation. Once they enter solution and vendor selection, co-define the path, success criteria, and validation method.

**What should negotiation prepare for a compliance-driven project?**

Prepare your BATNA — your best alternative if no agreement is reached — along with your bottom line, authority, core interests, and negotiation record. Compliance projects usually have deadlines, and customer urgency is a source of leverage, but it can also be used to push for price cuts. Trade conditions for timeline rather than conceding unilaterally.
