Leadership changes at a key account are not just industry news. They can invalidate the decision map a sales team has been using: who owns the problem, who influences the evaluation, whose commitments still hold, and which relationships remain connected to real authority. Public reporting summarized in the source material says that more than a year after the formation of the new central state-owned enterprise China Changan Automobile Group, Changan Automobile underwent an important organizational personnel adjustment, with Zhao Fei succeeding Zhu Huarong. The same public material does not disclose amounts, timelines, or subsequent division of responsibilities. For anyone serving this type of large account, the practical task is not to over-interpret the headline. It is to reassess the decision chain and relationship assets, then use client behavior evidence to confirm where the opportunity actually stands.

## Start With Decision Information, Not Speculation

Personnel change first disturbs decision information. The questions are concrete: whose position changed, whose information is still valid, which commitments need reconfirmation, and which parts of the organization now matter more or less. A relationship that depended on one contact may no longer be a reliable channel. A department that was previously peripheral may move into a key position because of the new division of labor. None of that can be assumed from the appointment alone; it has to be checked with the account.

Relationship capability, in the PSS5 sense, is not equivalent to entertainment or social frequency. It is the ability to build client relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action, with an emphasis on understanding and covering the decision chain. A leadership change is a useful test. It shows whether the team has real coverage or only a comfortable single-thread relationship.

## Use PRTABCD to Turn Unknowns Into Visible Unknowns

PRTABCD is a decision-information scan within relationship capability. Its purpose is not to produce a polished account profile. Its purpose is to expose intelligence gaps. For each of seven items, record four things: what is known, what is unknown, the source of the current information, and the next question to ask. Applied to a large account after a leadership transition, the scan can be run as follows.

- P, past: Who handled past acceptance and dispute records with us? Who still remembers the details? Who is willing to speak for us internally?
- R, requirements: What operating problems the new leadership wants to solve remain hypotheses until the client confirms them. Do not treat them as settled requirements.
- T, timing and key milestones: Approval rhythm may change during an organizational adjustment. Confirm it through client actions rather than inference.
- A, Arm: Which person or group in the client organization is leaning toward us? Has that support moved up, stayed level, or left the relevant role?
- B, budget: Has budget ownership shifted with the division of labor? If there is no evidence, write unknown instead of defaulting to the old assumption.
- C, competitors and alternatives: Have competitors already reached the new responsible executive? Have they been invited into early discussions?
- D, decision chain: In a multi-person, multi-department decision, new roles can change who signs and whose evaluation carries more weight.

### What to Capture in Each PRTABCD Entry

For every item, the account record should make the evidence status visible. A known fact, an explicit unknown, a source, and a next question are more useful than a confident narrative built on assumptions. The A and D entries usually deserve priority after a leadership change because access and authority can shift quickly, but all seven entries should be reviewed before the team treats the account plan as current.

## Do Not Outrun a Client That Has Stepped Back

Client purchasing stages and sales stages form a dual-view funnel. The two axes are many-to-many; they should not be forced into a one-to-one mapping. During an organizational adjustment, a client often steps back to the task of confirming responsibility ownership. The objective may exist, but it is not yet clear who owns resources and who is accountable for the result. If the seller keeps pushing a proposal, the seller is advancing past the client's current decision task.

Progress should be judged from client behavior evidence, not from activity volume. Useful evidence includes whether the client initiates the next conversation, brings the new responsible executive into the discussion, and is willing to state internal evaluation criteria. Visit counts and CRM stage percentages cannot replace those signals. They can describe seller activity, but they do not prove client commitment.

| Signal | What it can show | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client initiates a next meeting with a specific agenda | Active interest and a reason to continue | Does not prove budget or authority |
| New executive is added to the discussion | Decision-chain reach is improving | Participation can be informational only |
| Client shares internal evaluation criteria | The client is preparing to compare options | Criteria may still be provisional |
| Visit count or CRM stage percentage rises | Seller activity has increased | Cannot substitute for client behavior evidence |

## Three Continuity Actions for the Account Team

Teams do not need to wait for every new appointment to be finalized before taking useful action.

1. Re-rank existing contacts by influence. Mark whose information is still valid and whose channel needs reconfirmation. Write the result into the account record rather than leaving it in individual memory.
2. Use questioning capability to design background, difficulty, impact, and payoff questions. Confirm which decision task the client is currently working on before deciding how detailed the proposal should be.
3. Prepare a profit-growth proposal outline. Frame it as an investment case for the client: which operating metric it can improve, through what mechanism, and over what time horizon.

## Keep Negotiation Anchored and System Coordinates Current

Negotiation capability begins with clarity about power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action available if the parties cannot reach agreement. Preparation should specify the executable alternative, the bottom line, authority, interests, and records. When a new decision-maker takes over, old verbal conditions may not be inherited. Reconciling existing conditions into a written consensus is more stable than adding unilateral concessions in the room.

System capability places the change on the dual-view funnel coordinates. For each of the five sales stages, contact, requirements, proposal, negotiation, and close, ask where the client is and what client behavior evidence would show the next step. If the coordinate is wrong, later value explanation and condition exchange will miss the actual decision task.

## Actionable Checklist

- [ ] Rebuild the contact map by influence, not by tenure or friendliness.
- [ ] Mark each decision-information item as known, unknown, source, or next question.
- [ ] Run the full PRTABCD scan and prioritize A and D for early verification.
- [ ] Label new leadership priorities as hypotheses until the client confirms them.
- [ ] Confirm budget ownership rather than assuming the old owner still controls it.
- [ ] Check whether competitors have access to the new responsible executive.
- [ ] Identify the client's current decision task before advancing the proposal.
- [ ] Require at least one client behavior evidence point for every stage movement.
- [ ] Reconfirm old verbal conditions in writing with the new decision-maker.
- [ ] Prepare the BATNA before negotiating any revised terms.
- [ ] Review the profit-growth proposal as an investment case, not a product pitch.

## FAQ

Q1: What does the Changan Automobile adjustment mean for B2B key-account sellers?

A1: Public reporting confirms a leadership change, with Zhao Fei succeeding Zhu Huarong after an organizational personnel adjustment. For sellers, it means decision-chain information must be rechecked: who remains in position, whose division of labor changed, and whether prior verbal commitments still apply. Those points require client evidence, not inference from a headline.

Q2: What are A and D in PRTABCD?

A2: A is Arm, meaning the person or group in the client organization leaning toward the seller. D is Decision, meaning a multi-person, multi-department decision chain rather than a single decision-maker. Both should be recorded with known facts, unknowns, sources, and next questions. After a personnel change, these entries often expire first.

Q3: Why not push a proposal immediately?

A3: The client may be at the stage of confirming responsibility ownership. If the seller pushes a proposal while the client has not settled who owns resources and results, the seller is overstepping. First confirm whether the client recognizes the problem and considers investment worthwhile; then choose the proposal granularity.

Q4: Is relationship capability just more entertainment?

A4: No. In PSS5 it means building client relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action, with emphasis on understanding and covering the decision chain. During a personnel change, professional competence is shown by identifying which information has expired, which contacts remain relevant, and what to ask next.

Q5: How should the dual-view funnel be used in this situation?

A5: Place client purchasing stages and sales stages on the same map, then judge whether the two sides are synchronized, the seller is ahead, the seller is behind, or the two are misaligned. The axes are many-to-many and should not be forced into one-to-one mapping. A personnel change often moves the client back to responsibility confirmation.

Q6: Should old price commitments be renegotiated?

A6: A new decision-maker may not inherit old verbal conditions. It is safer to reconfirm existing conditions in writing while preparing a clear BATNA. The goal is to avoid unilateral concessions simply to keep the process moving.

Q7: How certain is this analysis?

A7: Only the public title and summary facts are treated as confirmed: the organizational personnel adjustment at Changan Automobile and Zhao Fei succeeding Zhu Huarong. Details on division of responsibilities, budget ownership, procurement rhythm, amounts, and timelines are not public in the source material. The action steps are general practices and should be treated as assumptions to verify against the account.
