A company that publishes an annual sales target, launches several models, prices its flagship above the market, and opens stores rapidly is running a coordinated business play. For key-account sales, the question is how product, price, and channel answer to one checkable number.

This guide uses a publicly reported case to illustrate a practical framework. The case is Zhangxue Motorcycle, as reported by East Money (). The framework is PSS5, Yanke's five-capability model for key-account sales. The goal is to translate the case into actions a B2B sales leader can inspect this week, without inventing outcomes or overstating what the public record shows.

## What the Public Record Actually Says

East Money reports Zhangxue Motorcycle set a 100,000-unit annual target, launched five models, priced the flagship at 138,000 yuan, added 50 stores in a month, and plans a 186.5-mu base with full production and tens-of-billions output value.

Read these figures as a set, not isolated announcements. They show a market-facing company tying R&D, pricing, and channel expansion to one verifiable annual number. That convergence is often what sales teams lack when explaining value.

## How PSS5 Value Capability Reads This Case

PSS5 is a five-capability framework for key-account sales proposed by Yan Ke Xu Hanbo. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. This article focuses on value capability. In this framework, value capability means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement that the customer can understand, and using business numbers to explain that value.

Applied to the Zhangxue Motorcycle case, three layers of translation become visible.

### Layer One: Product Translated into Market Coverage

Launching multiple new models at once means the company is not betting on a single product. Instead, it uses a price band to cover different budgets and use cases. This reduces the operating risk that comes from one product failing.

### Layer Two: Pricing Translated into Positioning Language

The flagship model is priced clearly above the common industry range. That number is itself a statement: the brand intends to move into a higher-value segment and uses price to tell the market who it is, rather than relying on discounts to move volume.

### Layer Three: Channel Translated into Reach Capability

Opening stores in batches at a high pace turns the first two moves into physical entry points where customers can actually see, test, and buy. Without that, product and pricing remain on paper.

All three layers converge on one number: the specific sales volume written into the annual target. This is the complete chain of value capability. Every action can answer what it contributes to the business number. Actions that cannot answer that question should not consume resources.

## Four Actions Key-Account Sales Teams Can Take Now

Reading someone else's story without translating it into your own actions is just reading. The following four checks are worth running with your team this week.

1. Give your proposal a checkable numeric target. Do not fill a page with product features. Answer instead: after the customer uses this, by how much will revenue, cost, or profit improve, and can the mechanism survive questions from the customer's finance team?

2. Check whether your quotation uses price to declare a value position. Price is not just cost plus markup. It tells the customer where your solution sits in value terms. A salesperson who only discounts is repeatedly telling the customer the solution is not worth much.

3. Fill in the reach channels. Zhangxue Motorcycle added reach capability through a very fast store-opening pace. In B2B terms, this means asking whether your solution has enough entry points for the customer's key people to actually engage with it: reference sites, pilot validation, executive exchanges. Fill whichever is missing.

4. Connect the actions with one chain. Product, price, and channel all serve the same annual sales number in the Zhangxue Motorcycle case. Your visits, proposals, quotations, and pilots should likewise form a chain pointing to a customer business metric. Cut actions that cannot be connected.

## A Common Misconception: Treating Value Capability as a Feature List

Many sales teams present solutions by moving from features to parameters to after-sales service, then asking the customer what they think. The customer's conclusion is often just one sentence: we will compare a bit more. The problem is that the presentation is all about the seller, with nothing about the customer's business account.

Value capability requires answering three questions: Which account evaluates this investment? Who owns that account? Which number can the solution change? Zhangxue can write annual sales as a checkable number because capacity, product, and channel converge on it. If your proposal promises no checkable improvement, doubt is reasonable.

## Comparison: Feature-Led Versus Value-Led Presentation

| Dimension | Feature-Led Presentation | Value-Led Presentation |

## Actionable Checklist

Use this checklist before your next major proposal or quarterly review.

- Can you state the customer's business account in one sentence?
- Can you name who owns that account inside the customer organization?
- Does your proposal identify at least one specific number it will change?
- Is the improvement mechanism explainable to a finance reviewer?
- Does your quotation use price to signal value position rather than only to compete on discount?
- Have you mapped the entry points where the customer's key people can actually engage with the solution?
- Do your visits, proposal, quotation, and pilot form one chain toward a customer metric?
- Have you removed actions that cannot answer what they contribute to that metric?
- Can the customer's finance or business lead restate the mechanism and the number after your presentation?
- If the customer asks "compared with what," do you have a value-based answer rather than a feature comparison?

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the reported facts about Zhangxue Motorcycle?**

East Money reports Zhangxue Motorcycle: 100,000-unit annual target, five new models, flagship at 138,000 yuan, 50 new stores in one month, 186.5-mu base, full production system, tens-of-billions output value. Other details are not in the bound facts.

**Q2: What is the definition of value capability in PSS5?**

In the PSS5 framework, value capability means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement that the customer can understand, and using business numbers to explain value. It requires the salesperson to first understand which business account the customer is calculating, then express the solution's impact on that account in financial language such as revenue, cost, and profit, rather than staying at the feature and parameter level.

**Q3: Why is the 138,000 yuan price a form of value language?**

The flagship price sits above the industry norm, declaring a higher-value position, screening the audience, and creating profit space. For key-account sales, a quotation also declares value position; habitual discounting says the solution is not worth much.

**Q4: What does opening 50 stores in one month correspond to in B2B sales?**

Stores are physical entry points. Opening many quickly shows reach capability matters as much as product. In B2B, equivalent entry points are reference sites, pilots, and executive exchanges. Without entry points, the customer stays in observation.

**Q5: What does setting a 100,000-unit annual target mean for business operations?**

A written annual number forces resource allocation. Capacity, product line, price band, and channel must answer what they contribute to that number. For sales teams, every visit, proposal, and quotation should answer what it contributes to a customer metric.

**Q6: What is the most common value capability mistake in sales presentations?**

The common mistake is treating value capability as a seller-side feature list. The customer cannot connect it to revenue, cost, or profit. Instead, answer three questions first: which account, who owns it, and which number can change.

**Q7: What are the five dimensions of PSS5?**

PSS5 consists of five dimensions. Value capability translates a product or service into customer profit, investment, and business value. Relationship capability penetrates the customer decision chain through professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust. Questioning capability discovers and amplifies real needs through intentional question design. System capability manages the sales process with funnels, stage judgment, and real progress. Negotiation capability forms sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives. This article focuses on value capability, which has the most direct relationship to business numbers.

**Q8: How can you tell whether your proposal presentation has value capability?**

Test each sentence: does it describe your product or a change in the customer's business account? If no customer-side profit, cost, or payback language appears, value capability is missing. Can the customer's finance lead restate which number improves and how?
