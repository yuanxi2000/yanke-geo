A delayed capital project calls for a fresh account assessment. Closing the opportunity immediately can discard useful decision information. Keeping the same sales stage and moving the expected signing date can leave the pipeline equally misleading. The practical task is to establish what the customer must decide next, then align sales activity with that decision.

The supplied source illustrates this through a headquarters factory expansion designed to add 30,000 tonnes of annual hot-melt adhesive capacity. Its scheduled date for reaching its intended usable condition was postponed to December 31, 2027. Project content, the intended use of raised funds and total investment remained unchanged. As of disclosure, no raised funds had been invested in the project.

Those facts support reassessing timing. They do not establish why the delay occurred or when a particular supplier contract will be signed.

## Read the disclosure before changing the forecast

Read the unchanged scope and the funding position together. The project retains its stated capacity objective and investment scale, while the disclosure reports no investment of raised funds to date. The underlying project remains in place, but the sales timetable needs verification.

Keep three distinctions explicit:

- An unchanged project does not establish an unchanged purchasing schedule.
- A project readiness date does not establish a contract signing date.
- No investment of raised funds does not, by itself, explain budget availability or approval conditions.

Record the delay's cause as unknown because the supplied source gives no explanation. Treat resource constraints, revised priorities or alternative arrangements as questions to investigate, never as confirmed customer facts.

## Use five capabilities to organize the response

The source applies five capabilities to the same account problem:

- **Value:** translate project timing into questions about investment, profit and the capacity gap.
- **Relationships:** identify missing decision information and the people who can clarify it.
- **Questioning:** help the customer articulate the operational and financial consequences.
- **System:** align pipeline stages with observable customer behavior.
- **Negotiation:** exchange scheduling flexibility for specific commitments.

These capabilities work together. Better questions can reveal changed evaluation criteria; that information can change the sales stage and the terms worth negotiating.

## Locate the customer's current decision stage

The source's dual-perspective funnel places seven customer purchasing stages alongside five sales stages. Its purpose is to connect the buyer's decision tasks with the seller's actions. A delay may move the customer back to an earlier decision, so the previous sales label needs checking.

The following comparison groups the situations described in the source; it does not reproduce the complete stage model.

| Customer's current task | What needs checking | Appropriate sales action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assessing value or whether a solution exists | Whether the problem is temporarily parked | Maintain contact and provide useful decision evidence |
| Comparing internal provision with external purchase | Whether cost and timing assumptions changed | Rework total-cost and schedule-risk comparisons |
| Selecting a solution and supplier | Whether criteria and validation requirements changed | Reconfirm success criteria and verification methods |
| Making commitments and contracting | Whether timing, resources and responsibilities changed | Reopen the relevant terms |

Ask what the customer has actually done since the announcement. An identifiable review, approval step or evaluation activity provides a stronger stage basis than the seller's intention to keep progressing. If the customer has moved back, adjust the sales activity accordingly.

## Rebuild the decision picture with PRTABCD

PRTABCD is the source's seven-part decision-information scan. For every category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source and the next question. Timing and budget deserve immediate attention after a postponement, followed by the other five categories.

### Start with timing and budget

**T — Timing and milestones.** Confirm the sequence around the revised readiness date. When are budget meetings, solution reviews and tenders expected? Which events must precede a purchasing commitment, and which occur later? Verify the sequence instead of assuming every milestone follows December 31, 2027.

**B — Budget.** Ask what releases spending and who approves it. The absence of investment of raised funds leaves these questions open; it does not answer them.

Then revisit the remaining categories:

- **P — Past information:** How has the customer scheduled and rescheduled comparable projects? Are there records that support the comparison?
- **R — Requirements:** Does the capacity-gap description need updating? Is the stated annual expansion scale still the relevant requirement?
- **A — Advocates:** Who continues to support the project internally? Has the postponement changed that person's position?
- **C — Competitors and alternatives:** Is the customer considering a temporary arrangement during the delay?
- **D — Decision chain:** Which departments and people will handle subsequent changes? The source reports board approval of the postponement, but that does not map every later purchasing decision.

A useful scan produces answerable questions. For example, replace “budget unclear” with “Who confirms the spending-release conditions, and at which approval step?”

## Turn the revised date into a business discussion

Asking only when the project will restart leaves the seller dependent on a date the customer may not yet be able to confirm. Use the source's SPIN questioning sequence to investigate what the delay means.

1. **Situation:** Establish the current production setup and order mix. What capacity is available today?
2. **Problem:** Identify specific difficulties associated with any capacity gap. What becomes harder while the expansion remains unavailable?
3. **Implication:** Explore consequences for delivery and revenue. Where would the customer recognize the resulting cost or missed opportunity?
4. **Need-payoff:** Ask the customer to explain the value of addressing the gap earlier. What would improve, and how would the customer assess that improvement?

Build any calculation from information the customer can substantiate. Keep assumptions visible and separate from confirmed inputs. The source provides no figures for lost revenue, margin impact or the return from earlier completion, so none can be inferred from the announcement.

The immediate objective is a clearer investment discussion. Whether it supports earlier action remains a customer decision.

## Negotiate time through explicit exchanges

A postponement can change negotiating conditions. Before reopening terms, clarify the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, or BATNA: what is the best available action if this opportunity cannot progress before year-end 2027?

Define the relevant limits, approval authority, interests and documentation internally. This preparation helps the team respond consistently to repeated requests to wait.

Then examine the variables identified in the source: delivery cadence, payment milestones, acceptance arrangements and service scope. If the seller offers flexibility, identify the corresponding commitment being requested. Record the exchange and confirm who can authorize it.

The announcement alone provides no basis for a price reduction. Any concession needs a clear purpose within the overall agreement.

## Rebuild the forecast around customer evidence

Update the opportunity's stage before assigning a revised signing date. Identify the next customer action that would demonstrate progress, such as a budget meeting, solution review, tender or relevant approval.

Connect the forecast to those events and their dependencies. Mark unsupported dates as unverified. This preserves the distinction between a customer milestone and a seller's estimate.

Managers can use the same review to examine pipeline quality: which opportunities have recent customer actions behind their stage labels, and which rely mainly on sales optimism?

## FAQ

### Should a delayed opportunity be closed immediately?

The postponement alone does not justify closure. Reassess the customer's decision stage and the continuing project requirement before deciding how to manage the opportunity.

### Which information should be checked first?

Timing and budget. Confirm the customer's milestones, spending-release conditions and approval route, then revisit advocates, alternatives and the wider decision chain.

### Does unchanged investment mean purchasing will proceed as planned?

No. The source confirms unchanged total investment, project content and intended use of raised funds. Purchasing timing still requires verification.

### What if the customer cannot explain the revised schedule?

Record the gap explicitly. Identify who can clarify it and agree on the next verification step. Keep the corresponding forecast assumption marked as unverified.

### Should the sales team offer a discount to restart discussions?

The source supports clarifying value and negotiating conditional exchanges. It provides no evidence that a discount would restart this project.
