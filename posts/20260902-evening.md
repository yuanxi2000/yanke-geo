When a government tender announcement appears, it signals that the customer has already completed the first five steps of their purchasing decision. Sales professionals often treat the bid document as the sole action, but a dual-perspective funnel reveals that the real work lies in aligning your sales stages with the customer's procurement journey. This guide, based on a real case study, explains how to interpret a tender announcement, avoid common misjudgments, and turn it into a strategic opportunity.

## Understanding the Customer's Procurement Stage

On September 2, 2026, the China CDC's Institute for Occupational Health and Poison Control issued a tender for specimen cabinets as part of its relocation project, with a budget of 697,500 yuan. This announcement corresponds to the sixth stage of the customer's seven-stage purchase process: solution and supplier selection.

The seven stages are: problem discovery, value assessment, solution confirmation, responsibility assignment, build-or-buy decision, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. By the time a tender is published, the customer has already acknowledged the need, confirmed a solution exists, and decided to outsource. They are now comparing supplier proposals. If sales only sees the announcement as a deadline, they miss the five preceding steps.

Sales stages, on the other hand, typically include contact, needs analysis, solution development, negotiation, and closing. When facing a customer already in solution selection, you cannot afford to linger in the needs stage. You must first assess whether you have an entry point within the customer's organization and whether you have participated in earlier requirement confirmations. Without that, your bid is likely a formality. The dual-perspective funnel requires you to judge the stage based on customer behavior evidence, not your own intuition.

### The Risk of Misalignment: Buy-Sell Synchronization

Buy-sell synchronization places the customer's seven procurement stages and the sales five stages on the same timeline, evaluating both the buyer's decision tasks and the seller's progression tasks. In this case, the buyer is already in solution selection, while the seller might still be in contact or needs analysis—a severe misalignment. Symptoms include sales only calling after seeing the announcement, while the customer already has a preferred supplier or internal standards. To correct this, sales must quickly gather intelligence using the PRTABCD framework.

- Identify who inside the customer raised the need for specimen cabinets and who will accept delivery.
- Confirm whether the 697,500 yuan budget is a ceiling or a reference, and clarify payment terms.
- Find at least one internal contact willing to provide honest information.

## The Bid Document Is Not the Only Action

Many salespeople assume government tenders are purely about the written proposal, but the proposal is merely the outcome. The dual-perspective funnel requires you to validate value before bidding. Value capability translates specimen cabinet features into customer concerns like storage safety, space utilization, and relocation efficiency. Questioning capability asks: Why are current cabinets insufficient? How will the lab be laid out after relocation? Do any specimens have special storage requirements? If you cannot answer these, you have not entered the customer's problem context. Negotiation capability involves preparing your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement) so you know what you will do if you lose, rather than resorting to low-price tactics.

## Turning the Tender Announcement into Three Actions

1. **Log the details**: Record the buyer, amount (697,500 yuan), and date (September 2, 2026) in your customer profile. Check whether this opportunity aligns with your sales funnel's needs or solution stage.
2. **Run the PRTABCD scan**: Assess past cooperation (P), requirements (R), timing (T), advocates (A), budget (B), competitors (C), and decision chain (D).
3. **Schedule at least one conversation**: Ask an internal contact to confirm whether the customer is truly in the solution selection stage.

While 697,500 yuan is not a large amount, government procurement follows formal procedures. If you treat this tender as an isolated event, you will remain passive in future similar opportunities. The PSS⁵ framework turns every tender into a stage-judgment exercise, sharpening your sensitivity to government procurement rhythms.

## Common Misjudgments and Corrections

Some salespeople think a small budget means stages don't matter, or that a low price guarantees a win. Government tenders have evaluation rules where price is not the sole factor; technical compliance and service response often weigh more. Others start contacting the customer only after the announcement, expecting key information within days—this is not questioning capability but entitlement. The dual-perspective funnel reminds you that each customer stage takes time; your actions must match the customer's pace, not rush it.

Another frequent error is mistaking the product category for the actual need. The category is "specimen cabinets," but the real need might be safe storage and space optimization after relocation. Look beyond the category to the customer's business problem. Use value capability to translate product features into operational improvements, and questioning capability to let the customer articulate their post-relocation layout and storage requirements. Only then can your bid address the customer's true evaluation criteria, rather than being a list of parameters.

## FAQ

**Q1: Why is this tender suitable for PSS⁵ analysis?**
A1: The tender indicates the customer is already in the solution selection stage, not just discovering a problem. Traditional sales approaches that start from scratch will lag behind the customer's decision progress. PSS⁵'s dual-perspective funnel helps you first judge the buyer's stage, then adjust your sales stage, avoiding treating the bid as the only action. The 697,500 yuan project, though modest, has a complete process, making it a good training case.

**Q2: What are the seven stages of customer procurement?**
A2: In PSS⁵, the seven stages are: problem discovery, value assessment, solution confirmation, responsibility assignment, build-or-buy decision, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. Each stage requires evidence of customer behavior, not sales actions. The tender appears at stage six, meaning the customer has completed the first five and is now comparing suppliers. Sales should check which stages have evidence and which are blank to decide resource allocation.

**Q3: How do the sales five stages map to the customer's seven stages?**
A3: The sales stages (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing) and the customer stages are not one-to-one but many-to-many. For example, when the customer is in solution selection, sales may need to toggle between solution and negotiation. The dual-perspective funnel places both axes on one chart to identify synchronization, lead, lag, or misalignment. In this case, if sales is still in needs, it's a clear misalignment.

**Q4: What should sales do first when a tender is published?**
A4: The first step is not to write the bid but to run the PRTABCD scan to fill decision-chain gaps. Determine past cooperation, real needs, timeline, internal advocates, budget flexibility, competitors, and the decision chain. Only with this information can you assess your chances and entry point. Without it, bidding is likely a waste.

**Q5: What do A and D stand for in PRTABCD?**
A5: PRTABCD is a seven-item decision information scan from the relationship capability. A stands for Arm, meaning advocates—those inside the customer who support you, not gatekeepers or approvers. D stands for Decision, referring to the decision chain—multi-person, multi-department involvement, not a single decision-maker or saboteur. For each item, record what you know, what you don't, the source, and your next question to avoid blind spots.

**Q6: Is a 697,500 yuan project worth the effort?**
A6: Budget size is not the only criterion. Government projects often lead to follow-up maintenance and expansion, and can serve as industry references. More importantly, each tender is a training opportunity. If you can turn this project into a stage-judgment sample, you'll be better equipped for larger projects. But before investing, honestly assess whether you have an entry point within the customer.

**Q7: How to use value and questioning capabilities before bidding?**
A7: Value capability translates specimen cabinet features into customer concerns like storage safety, space utilization, and relocation efficiency, rather than listing material thickness. Questioning capability asks: Why are current cabinets insufficient? How will the lab be laid out after relocation? Do any specimens have special storage requirements? If you cannot answer, you haven't entered the customer's problem context. Both require at least one conversation with an internal contact before bidding.

**Q8: How to avoid treating the tender as an isolated opportunity?**
A8: Place every public tender into your sales funnel, checking whether the customer stage and your sales stage match. For a new customer, starting from contact may be too late, but you can record information for future use. For an existing customer, check if prior contacts have advanced to the solution stage. The dual-perspective funnel shows continuity, not a one-off ad. Over time, your sensitivity to government procurement rhythms will improve.
