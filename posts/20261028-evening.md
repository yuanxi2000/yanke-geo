In its 2026 third-quarter report, Hengrui Pharma disclosed GLP-1 pipeline progress: two marketing applications for HRS-7535 tablets and one for Ruipopatide injection were accepted for review. For a key-account seller, the value of the item is not the headline. It is the exercise of translating a public milestone into the language a customer manages their business in — and knowing where to stop.

## Start with the fact boundary: accepted is not approved, and approved is not an order

The checkable facts are narrow. Acceptance means the regulator has received the filing and review continues; it is not approval, and approval is not an order. A seller who blurs those three states may gain attention briefly and lose credibility permanently.

Two mistakes recur: reading a company's public progress as a specific customer's demand, and reading regulatory acceptance as a procurement signal. The first blurs your questions; the second distorts your stage assessment.

Split the item into three buckets instead:

- Facts: two acceptances for HRS-7535 tablets and one for Ruipopatide injection, disclosed in the 2026 third-quarter report. Nothing on volume, price, approval timing or any customer's plans.
- Implications: the topic may touch a customer's regulatory tracking, production readiness, channel education or patient access.
- Unknowns: whether a given customer cares, who owns it, what it would change, and when.

## Value capability: convert pipeline progress into the customer's operating questions

Value capability translates a product or service into the customer's profit, investment and operating value. With a pharmaceutical customer, start by asking which piece of their operating work a milestone would touch — R&D scheduling, production readiness, channel education, patient access — then quantify only the part the customer will confirm. Never invent benefit numbers when you have no customer data; list them as impact items to be confirmed. Value capability is not deciding the conclusion for the customer; it is helping them calculate it.

### Turning the headline into the customer's operating language

1. Name the operating area the milestone could touch.
2. Mark each item known or unknown and keep the unknowns visible.
3. Build the value case only from customer-confirmed data; never substitute industry averages for missing numbers.
4. Prepare a one-page investment note: what the customer puts in, what might be protected or improved, and how you would verify it.

If you serve a company like Hengrui Pharma, the public milestone shows at most that a new business topic may exist. Whether it merits investment depends on whether the customer names a scenario, an owner and a verification method. Without those, the value model stays a hypothesis — and a hypothesis written as a checklist is more professional than one told as a conclusion.

## Relationship capability: use PRTABCD to find intelligence blind spots

Relationship capability penetrates the customer's decision chain with professional insight, decision information and verifiable trust. PRTABCD works as a scanning sheet: P for past and historical information; R for requirement; T for timing and key milestones; A for Arm — who inside the customer is embracing you, not a bargaining chip; B for budget; C for competitors and alternatives; D for Decision — the multi-person, multi-department chain, not one decision-maker. A public milestone is a conversation entry point; it does not prove that anyone inside the customer is embracing you.

For a single key person, state four MBVS hypotheses — motivation, benchmark, vision, solution — and check them in conversation. Nothing enters the account profile until the customer's own words or behavior confirm it.

- Known: the reporting covers Hengrui Pharma and the pipeline milestones, not a specific customer's project, budget or decision-makers.
- Unknown: who owns the related work internally, who supports or resists, where the budget sits, what the timeline is.
- Next question: one question around A, one around D; record the answers and their source in the PRTABCD sheet.

## Questioning capability: let SPIN turn news into a needs conversation

Questioning capability helps the customer see the problem and the value of change for themselves. Around this milestone: a situation question on how the customer tracks regulatory milestones for comparable products; a problem question on the trouble that lagging information causes; an implication question on which part of the operating plan slips if preparation lags; a need-payoff question on the verifiable improvement from linking milestone tracking to internal preparation. Use them in sequence, and do not jump to a solution presentation without behavioral evidence.

*Noted* is not a confirmed need. Ask which internal arrangement the milestone affects, who will change what they do, and when a decision must be made. If the customer cannot describe an impact, the problem has not been enlarged. Depth of customer disclosure, not question count, is the measure.

## System capability: judge real progress with the dual-view funnel

System capability runs a dual-view funnel: five sales stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close — read against seven buying stages — problem recognition, value judgment, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership, build versus buy, solution and vendor selection, commitment and signing. The two axes map many-to-many and must not be forced into one-to-one correspondence.

Acceptance can support problem recognition or value judgment at most; it cannot move a deal into the solution stage. Judge progress by behavior: does the customer bring a business owner in, share data, confirm who owns the next step? Read both sides at once — you may be presenting a solution while the buyer is still judging value. The funnel exists to expose misalignment, not to comfort the team with a CRM percentage.

## Negotiation capability: write down alternatives and tradeables first

Negotiation capability builds a sustainable agreement around leverage, time, information and alternatives. BATNA — the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — is the best fallback available if no deal is reached; prepare the executable alternative, walk-away line, authority, interests and records. With a pharmaceutical customer, establish what they would do if they delayed or did not buy, then discuss how scope, timing, payment and risk are exchanged. Do not manufacture urgency from an acceptance; without a customer-confirmed timeline, write no deadline.

Fill information gaps first: who knows the budget, who can approve, what the competing option is, what delay costs the customer. When information is thin, concessions buy the next price cut rather than a commitment.

## The five capabilities on one page

| Capability | What it asks you to do with this news | Evidence needed before you advance |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Translate the milestone into operating questions; quantify only confirmed data | Customer-named scenario, owner, verification method |
| Relationship | Fill PRTABCD, especially A and D; test MBVS hypotheses | Customer's own words or behavior, source noted |
| Questioning | Run SPIN in order and enlarge the problem | Customer describes a concrete impact and a decision point |
| System | Place the deal on both funnels and find the gap | Business owner engaged, data shared, next-step owner confirmed |

## Actionable checklist

- Today: compress the public facts into three sentences — the fact, the boundary, and the potential impact on the customer.
- This week: book one needs interview. Ask, do not pitch. Record the customer's exact words and behavioral evidence.
- Before every advance: check whether PRTABCD has entries for A and D. If not, ask rather than guess.
- Before every quote: write down BATNA, the walk-away line, the tradeable conditions and your authority, so you are not conceding on the spot.
- After every review: mark your position on the five sales stages and on the seven buying stages, and name the misalignment.

## FAQ

**Can a seller treat this disclosure as customer demand?**
No. The disclosure shows that Hengrui Pharma's 2026 third-quarter report mentioned GLP-1 pipeline progress and that the applications for HRS-7535 tablets and Ruipopatide injection were accepted for review. Whether a customer has demand depends on whether they acknowledge a problem, share scenarios and data, and assign an owner. The news is a conversation entry point, not a substitute for needs confirmation.

**How does value capability apply here?**
Translate accepted for review into operating areas the customer may care about — R&D scheduling, production readiness, channel education, patient access — and mark each known or unknown. Build the investment note only from customer-confirmed data. Value capability turns progress into verifiable benefit or risk rather than announcing good news on the customer's behalf.

**What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?**
A is Arm — who inside the customer organization is embracing you. D is Decision — the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. Neither can be inferred from job titles. Record known information, unknowns, sources and next questions, then verify through behavior.

**Why can't acceptance be treated as the solution stage?**
Because the two axes are many-to-many. Acceptance is a public regulatory milestone; at most it supports discussion of problem recognition or value judgment. It does not prove the customer has confirmed a solution exists, confirmed ownership, or entered vendor selection.

**How should BATNA be prepared?**
Write the executable alternative, walk-away line, authority, interests and records. Establish what the customer would do if they delayed or did not buy, then design the exchange across scope, timing, payment and risk. Without a customer-confirmed timeline, do not use acceptance to create urgency.

**What is the smallest next action?**
Compress the disclosure into three sentences — fact, boundary, potential customer impact — then book one interview, ask without pitching, and log A and D afterward. Before the next quote, write down BATNA, the walk-away line and the tradeable conditions.
