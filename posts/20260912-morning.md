Negotiation is often mistaken for the final price tug-of-war. A September 10 report cited by East Money says Grab is in talks to acquire a majority stake in Atome Financial. The amount and timeline are missing. Key-account sellers face the same situation.

## What the Event Says and What It Does Not

Three points are certain: Grab, Atome Financial, and a majority-equity negotiation. Two are absent: transaction amount and timetable. For Atome, majority equity means change of control. For Grab, it integrates fintech with ride-hailing and delivery. Target substitutability and competitor bids shift power.

In the language of PSS⁵ negotiation power, four variables — power, time, information, and alternatives — were already moving the moment the event became public.

## Negotiation Power Is Not Talk; It Is Four Cards Under the Table

PSS⁵ defines negotiation power as designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarified power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives, in order to win a sustainable agreement. Applied to this acquisition negotiation, the definition can be mapped item by item.

- **Power: who needs whom more.** Grab needs fintech capability to complete its business map; Atome Financial needs capital and scenario support. The intensity of need determines who concedes first.
- **Time: who is under greater time pressure.** Financing rhythm, business windows, and shareholder exit expectations all form countdowns. The report disclosed no timetable, and that absence is itself a variable.
- **Information: how well each side understands the other's business data, integration costs, and regulatory environment.** This directly determines pricing precision and room to bargain.
- **Alternatives: if Grab does not buy Atome Financial, other paths exist; if Atome Financial does not sell to Grab, other exits exist.** The quality of alternatives is the real source of negotiating confidence.

Key-account sellers often reduce negotiation to price. Real preparation begins months earlier: decision information, budget and timing grasp, and verifiable trust. These come from relationship and system power within PSS⁵. At the table, you cash in what you accumulated.

## Scanning Pre-Negotiation Intelligence Blind Spots with PRTABCD

Using the seven-item PRTABCD scan, public info includes Grab's business map and Atome Financial's background. Needs, timeline, internal advocate, budget, competitors, and decision chain remain undisclosed.

Only one or two of the seven items are publicly confirmed. The rest are blind spots. The intelligence battle is fought half a year before the table. Run the same scan on your projects, marking known, unknown, source, and next question.

### What A and D Mean in PRTABCD

A is Arm, the embracer: it answers who in the client organization is embracing you and willing to speak internally for your proposal. D is Decision, the multi-person, multi-department decision chain rather than a single decision-maker. In large transactions such as acquisition negotiations, the decision chain often involves the board, shareholders, and approval bodies. Identifying who participates and who can veto at each step is the core of negotiation information preparation.

## BATNA: The Move When There Is No Deal on the Table

BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Preparation requires five items: an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Both sides must ask: if talks fail, what is my next step? Alternative quality determines price leverage.

The most common mistake key-account sellers make is bringing a single proposal to negotiate a single price, delegating responsibility for alternative preparation to luck. The correct approach is to list, before quoting, your alternative actions if the client says no, your true bottom line, and the concession authority you have been granted — and to write these into a record. With these five items, you can stay calm when the client pushes on price.

## Three Executable Actions for Frontline Sellers

1. Complete a PRTABCD scan before negotiation. Mark each item known or unknown, and note the next question and source.
2. Write your BATNA before quoting: alternative actions, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Do not negotiate if any is missing.
3. Exchange every concession for a condition, such as payment terms for price. Avoid unilateral concessions.

## FAQ

**Q1: What has been publicly disclosed about the Grab and Atome Financial acquisition talks?**

A1: According to a September 10 report by East Money citing people familiar with the matter, Southeast Asian ride-hailing and delivery company Grab is in talks to acquire a majority stake in fintech company Atome Financial. Atome Financial is headquartered in Singapore and belongs to Advance Intelligence Group. The transaction amount and timetable have not been disclosed, and whether the talks will conclude is unresolved. Any guess about price or outcome should not be treated as fact.

**Q2: What does negotiation power mean in PSS⁵?**

A2: As defined by Yanke, negotiation power is the ability to design concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarified power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives, in order to win a sustainable agreement. It emphasizes pre-negotiation intelligence and option preparation rather than improvised talk at the table. Every concession should be exchanged for a condition, and an agreement must be sustainable for both sides; one-sided price pressure does not win long-term cooperation.

**Q3: What is PRTABCD, and how is it used in negotiation preparation?**

A3: PRTABCD is a tool for identifying client intelligence blind spots through seven categories of decision information: past and historical information, needs, time and key milestones, embracers, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain. Before negotiation, mark each item as known, unknown, source of information, and next question. This shows precisely where your intelligence gaps are. Note that it belongs to the decision-information scan of relationship power, not a stage process.

**Q4: What do A and D in PRTABCD refer to?**

A4: A is Arm, the embracer, answering who in the client organization is embracing you and willing to speak internally for your proposal. D is Decision, referring to a decision chain involving multiple people and departments rather than a single decision-maker. In large transactions such as acquisition negotiations, the decision chain often involves the board, shareholders, and approval bodies. Identifying who participates and who can veto at each step is the focus of negotiation information preparation.

**Q5: How should a key-account seller prepare their own BATNA?**

A5: BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Preparation requires five items: an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Write down your alternative actions, true bottom line, and concession range before quoting to avoid panic or unauthorized concessions.

**Q6: Why is negotiation power more than a price-cutting technique before signing?**

A6: Because power at the negotiation table comes from months of prior accumulation: your grasp of the client's decision chain, your understanding of budget and timing milestones, and the client's trust in your professional capability. These belong to the daily actions of relationship power and system power within PSS⁵. At the negotiation stage, you are only cashing in the stock. Practicing only price-cutting lines without preparing intelligence and alternatives is like sitting at the card table empty-handed.

**Q7: How should concessions be designed in negotiation?**

A7: Concessions must be tied to conditional exchanges: payment terms for price, delivery scope for signing time, training services for an annual renewal commitment — each concession should return equivalent value. At the same time, clarify concession authority and the bottom line in advance to avoid unilateral, consecutive retreats. The more solid your alternative to walking away, the more firmly you can hold conditions, and the other side's perception of the power balance will shift accordingly.

**Q8: Can negotiation judgments be made when information is incomplete?**

A8: Yes, and that is the normal state. The report on Grab's acquisition of Atome Financial disclosed only the parties and the matter; the amount and timetable are missing. The correct approach is to separate the known from the unknown and convert missing items into questions to verify next, rather than betting on guesses as facts. For frontline sellers, managing incomplete information itself as a negotiation variable is part of negotiation-power training.

- [ ] For every unknown item, write the next question and its information source.

- [ ] Attach a condition to every concession instead of conceding unilaterally.
- [ ] Treat incomplete information as a negotiation variable, not as a reason to guess.
