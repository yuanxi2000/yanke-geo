A storage station reaching grid-connected operation gives a sales team a concrete reason to start a conversation. It does not establish a new buying opportunity. The useful work begins with understanding what the customer must manage after connection: operating performance, safety responsibilities, maintenance and future investment.

The supplied source describes two Xinneng energy storage stations completing installation and commissioning and entering grid-connected operation. This guide uses that event to explain a practical sales approach: separate facts from assumptions, develop value with customer data and judge progress through observable buying behavior.

## Separate the public event from the sales hypothesis

Keep two distinct entries in your account notes. The factual entry records what the source supports: two stations completed installation and commissioning and entered grid-connected operation. The hypothesis entry records what remains to be established.

Possible follow-up needs include operations, monitoring, safety, spare parts or expansion. Each remains a hypothesis until customer conversations and decision information support it. The announcement does not confirm an available budget, purchase intention, supportive decision makers or expansion plans.

This distinction changes the next action. Instead of preparing a quotation because a project milestone occurred, identify which operating question the customer currently needs to resolve.

## Translate the milestone into three customer accounts

Grid connection is an engineering milestone. Commercial value depends on what happens during operation. Organize discovery around three accounts that the customer can examine with its own information.

| Account | What to examine | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operations | Availability, failure downtime, inspection hours and spare-parts turnover | Operating records and maintenance data |
| Safety | Risk exposure, emergency response and responsibility boundaries | Customer-defined responsibilities and response requirements |
| Investment | Future modifications, expansion and asset life | Customer plans and investment criteria |

These accounts organize questions; they do not establish financial benefits. Ask which measures the customer uses, what the current situation is and what improvement would matter. Use customer-provided figures to examine investment, returns and potential profit improvement.

Avoid assigning an assumed cost to downtime or claiming a return that the customer has not confirmed. Where data is unavailable, record the gap and agree on what evidence would allow the account to be evaluated.

### Open with an operating question

A neutral opening could be: 'Following grid connection, which issue currently needs the most attention: operating stability, acceptance and handover, or responsibility for ongoing maintenance?'

Let the customer choose the subject. Then establish who owns it, which measures apply and what decision needs to happen next. This keeps the discussion grounded without implying access to internal arrangements.

## Map relationships with PRTABCD

Public information is a lead for investigation. It cannot establish that someone inside the customer organization actively supports your involvement. Use PRTABCD to scan seven areas:

- **P, past information:** What relevant history is known?
- **R, requirements:** What need has the customer actually described?
- **T, timing:** Which dates and milestones affect the decision?
- **A, Arm:** Who is the internal advocate supporting your involvement?
- **B, budget:** What funding information has been confirmed?
- **C, competition:** Which competitors or alternative approaches are being considered?
- **D, decision:** Which people and departments participate in the decision chain?

For every area, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source and the next question. Keep the advocate and the decision chain distinct: identifying a supporter does not explain how several departments will reach a decision.

An unknown budget is a discovery task. An unidentified advocate is a relationship gap. Neither should be filled with an optimistic interpretation of the announcement.

## Use SPIN to explore the current operating task

Begin with the customer's situation and work toward the consequences and value of improvement. The four SPIN question types provide a useful sequence:

1. **Situation:** Who operates the stations, and which performance measures are they accountable for?
2. **Problem:** Where are operations, handover or maintenance responsibilities proving difficult to coordinate?
3. **Implication:** What happens to operations when those gaps cause downtime, rework or responsibility disputes?
4. **Need-payoff:** If that issue were resolved, what improvement would matter to the customer?

Adapt the questions to the decision at hand. A customer defining maintenance responsibilities needs a different discussion from one comparing expansion options.

The aim is to let the customer explain the effect and desired improvement in its own terms. Product details become relevant when they address the problem the customer has described.

## Track selling activity and buying decisions separately

The seller's funnel has five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation and close. It describes how the sales team organizes its work.

The customer's purchasing process contains seven decision tasks:

1. Recognize a problem.
2. Assess its value.
3. Confirm that a solution exists.
4. Confirm responsibility for addressing it.
5. Decide whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Select a solution and supplier.
7. Commit and sign.

These two views have a many-to-many relationship. Do not force each sales stage into a corresponding purchasing stage. A salesperson may be preparing a solution while the customer is still assessing value or establishing responsibility.

The connection announcement describes a completed engineering milestone. It does not reveal the customer's current position in a subsequent purchasing decision. Establish that position through questions and behavior before selecting the next sales action.

## Require customer evidence of progress

Useful evidence includes the customer's willingness to share operating data, involve the operations lead, define acceptance criteria, request a site assessment or explain budget and approval milestones.

For solution selection specifically, look for evaluation criteria, a validation method, named participants and alternatives being compared. These details show what the customer is preparing to decide.

Visit counts, quick replies and verbal praise cannot substitute for that information. If the customer welcomes an introduction but will not define the problem or confirm responsibility, continue discovery. A quotation does not resolve those missing decisions.

## Prepare alternatives before negotiating conditions

When a commercial negotiation is warranted, review bargaining power, time, information and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best action available if agreement cannot be reached.

Write down an executable alternative, your minimum acceptable conditions, decision authority, relevant interests and negotiation records. An alternative must describe an action you can actually take.

For follow-on storage services, possible negotiating variables include scope, response times, spare-parts ownership, payment milestones and risk allocation. Prepare which conditions could be exchanged and which require further authority.

Keep the value discussion tied to customer-confirmed information. Do not use speculative downtime losses or unsupported benefit figures to pressure the customer over price.

## Run a short account review with this checklist

Use the public event to structure a focused team discussion and assign concrete follow-up work:

- [ ] Record only the supported connection facts; add no unsupported amount, date or counterparty.
- [ ] Separate potential service needs from confirmed customer requirements.
- [ ] Mark the known and unknown information across all seven PRTABCD areas.
- [ ] Assign a specific SPIN question to each information gap.
- [ ] Identify the customer's current purchasing decision task.
- [ ] Specify the next evidence needed: data, acceptance criteria, participants or approval milestones.
- [ ] Assign responsibility for the next customer question.
- [ ] If negotiation is approaching, document alternatives and condition exchanges.

Afterward, review whether the discussion clarified business value, identified an advocate, explored consequences, matched the customer's decision task and prepared negotiating alternatives. Advance according to the evidence obtained.

## FAQ

### Does grid connection imply a new purchase?

No. It establishes an engineering milestone. Any subsequent need, budget or purchase intention requires separate verification.

### Why start with operating value?

The milestone creates questions about running, maintaining and investing in the assets. Exploring those questions connects the conversation to outcomes the customer can evaluate with its own data.

### What if the customer will not share figures?

Record the information gap. Clarify the operating problem, responsibility and evaluation criteria, then agree on what evidence the customer can provide. Do not fill missing figures with assumed savings.

### When should a team prepare a detailed proposal?

Use the customer's decision task and evidence to judge readiness. Evaluation criteria, validation methods, selection participants and comparison options help establish whether solution selection is underway.

### Can market commentary replace account discovery?

No. Share-price movements and sector interpretations do not establish a customer's budget, advocate, decision chain or timing. Those require customer-specific evidence.
