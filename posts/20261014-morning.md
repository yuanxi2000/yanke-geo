When a flood disrupts a supply base, delivery schedules stop, but so does the normal rhythm of customer decisions. A public event like the one reported in Thailand is not just a logistics story; it is a live demonstration of how purchasing priorities, internal ownership, and supplier comparisons can shift inside a customer account. The practical question for key-account sellers is not whether the news is sad or dramatic, but what it changes in the customer's decision process. The PSS5 framework from Yanke offers a structured way to read that shift without overstepping the facts.

## Three Facts You Can Actually Use

The source material reports that the Thailand Automotive Parts Manufacturers Association said about ten auto parts companies in the country suspended operations because of flooding. Some of those companies were not flooded themselves, but employees could not travel and logistics were blocked, so they could not deliver parts on time. Some vehicle manufacturers paused production.

For a sales professional, those three sentences matter because they move three things at once: delivery timing, internal responsibility inside the customer organization, and the order in which suppliers are compared. When delivery is interrupted in a key account, the procurement rhythm is rearranged. Agenda items that were once at the top can slide down, and items that were secondary can move up.

## Value Capability: Translate the Pause into the Customer's Business Language

Value capability means moving the sales conversation away from features and toward investment, return, and profit improvement, using the customer's own language. When a line has stopped, a price sheet is unlikely to get attention. Sit with the operations manager and put three things on one page: cost of one day of stoppage, expediting cost, and downstream commitments. Use the customer's accounting logic; the seller builds the structure.

This is where the division between Box 1 and Box 2 becomes useful. Box 2 represents managers who run operations and use the money. Box 1 represents the level that allocates and approves funds. Sales should help Box 2 articulate business value clearly so that person can take the material to Box 1 and compete for resources. Going around Box 2 to talk budget directly often stalls both conversations.

## Relationship Capability: Use PRTABCD to Scan for Intelligence Gaps

Relationship capability is about building customer relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, with a focus on understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. PRTABCD divides decision information into seven items: Past, Requirement, Timing and key milestones, Arm or embrace, Budget, Competition and alternatives, and Decision chain. A stands for Arm, meaning who inside the customer organization is embracing you. D stands for Decision, meaning a multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single decision maker.

In an event like a flood, the three items most likely to change are timing and key milestones, the embrace, and competition. Milestones can move. An embrace can disappear while the customer fights fires. A competitor may use the moment to propose an alternative. Write each of the seven items in four columns: known, unknown, source, and next question. If too many items are unknown, do not rush toward signing.

## Questioning Capability: Sequence SPIN's Four Question Types

Questioning capability is about discovering and amplifying real needs through intentional question design. SPIN gives four types: Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff. Situation confirms the current supply arrangement. Problem locates the tightest bottleneck. Implication expands that bottleneck to output and downstream commitments. Need-payoff lets the customer describe what resolution would look like. Do not skip the sequence. If the customer has not admitted a bottleneck and you jump straight to payoff questions, you will likely be treated as a pushy seller.

## System Capability: Use the Dual-View Funnel to Judge Where the Customer Stands

System capability uses a dual-view funnel made of the five sales stages and the seven customer purchasing stages as a map.

Returning to the flood story: a customer facing delivery interruption may be in problem-discovery or value-judgment.

## Negotiation Capability: Write BATNA Clearly Before Discussing Delivery

Negotiation capability is about designing concessions and conditional exchanges based on power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement—your best fallback if no agreement is reached. Preparation should specify an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. After a disruption, customers care most about delivery time and capacity. Trade delivery time, batch size, payment rhythm, and backup supply sources together, but calculate each against your own bottom line first.

## A Compact Comparison of the Five Capabilities

| Capability | Core Question | What It Changes After a Disruption |
|---|---|---|
| Value | What does this mean in the customer's operating numbers? | Turns a pause into lost output, expediting cost, and downstream commitments |
| Relationship | Who is embracing us and who decides? | Maps the decision chain and spots intelligence gaps |
| Questioning | What is really stuck, and what does it affect? | Sequences Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff questions |
| System | Where is the customer in the buying process? | Uses behavioral evidence to reposition rather than chase |
| Negotiation | What is our best alternative and what will we trade? | Prepares BATNA before exchanging delivery, volume, and payment terms |

## What to Do Today

A practical checklist for the next working day:

- Turn the public news into one customer-relevant judgment: the customer's delivery has been interrupted, so procurement priorities will be rearranged.
- Build a PRTABCD intelligence sheet with the seven items and write known, unknown, source, and next question for each.
- Fill in a business-value sheet with the customer's operations lead. The customer provides the numbers; you provide the structure.
- Mark the customer's current behavioral evidence on the dual-view funnel. Confirm it before deciding the next move.
- Before the next negotiation, write down your BATNA, bottom line, authority, interests, and records.
- End every customer conversation by confirming one action the customer will take and a completion time. If that cannot be done, your stage judgment may be too optimistic.

### Q3: What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?

In PSS5, PRTABCD is a seven-item decision-information scan, not a role profile. A is fixed as Arm, meaning who inside the customer organization is embracing you. D is fixed as Decision, meaning a multi-person, multi-department decision chain rather than a single decision maker. During a delivery interruption, the three items worth checking first are timing and key milestones, the embrace, and competition, because they are most likely to change after a disaster. Each item should record known, unknown, source, and next question.

### Q4: What exactly does system capability manage?

In PSS5, system capability uses a dual-view funnel made of the five sales stages and the seven customer purchasing stages to manage the key-account sales process. The five sales stages are contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, and close. The seven customer purchasing stages run from problem discovery to commitment and signing. The two axes are many-to-many relationships. Position should be judged by customer behavioral evidence, not replaced by visit counts or a percentage in a record. After a disruption, customer owners and timing milestones move, so repositioning matters more than chasing progress.

### Q5: How do you tell whether a customer is truly advancing or just being polite?

Use behavioral evidence.

### Q6: How is BATNA used in delivery-negotiation?

BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement—your best fallback if the two sides cannot agree. Preparation should specify an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. When a customer uses delivery time to press on price, do not simply concede. Trade delivery time, batch size, payment rhythm, and backup supply sources together. Write your alternative first, then decide which conditions can be given and to what degree. Passivity at the negotiating table is often planted during preparation.

### Q7: How does value capability apply when a customer has stopped a line?

Value capability requires replacing product features with investment, return, and profit improvement the customer understands, supported by business numbers. When a line has stopped, attention is limited. Build a sheet for them first: output loss, expediting cost, and the logic of downstream commitments. Use the customer's financial logic. This helps Box 2, managers who run operations, obtain material to compete for resources with Box 1, the allocation and approval level, so your proposal can enter the budget discussion.

### Q8: Can you analyze a situation when public information has no amounts or dates?

Yes, but stay within the boundary. This news only binds the subject and the source. It lacks specific procurement amounts, counterpart companies, and timing milestones, so those cannot be guessed. Solidify the known facts and write the unknowns into the seven PRTABCD items with source and next question noted. When information is thin, use questioning capability to fill gaps rather than imagination. Update the stage judgment only when the customer provides behavioral evidence. That kind of progress can survive a review.
