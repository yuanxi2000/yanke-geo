A project announcement is not a sales pipeline. The reported landing of Taiyi Nuclear Energy's controllable fusion stellarator research and validation base in Wuxi High-Tech Zone matters to key-account sellers because it reveals a project structure: the buyer is a multi-layer organization, the decision depends on validation, and the close depends on milestones. Treating the news as a lead to call will usually stop at the first gate.

Use the announcement to build a customer map instead. The source describes a project entity, a high-tech zone administration, possible research partners and upstream component suppliers. It also describes a design the source calls a globally first dual-mirror stellarator configuration. The sales task is to determine which layer owns technical validation, which owns project milestones, and what evidence each layer needs before it can move.

## Why This Project Needs a Different Account Approach

Ordinary opportunities often concentrate decision-making in procurement and technical functions. A controllable fusion research and validation project has wider dependencies: technical validation, site implementation, supply-chain coordination and long-run operation. The source notes that each layer has its own assessment milestones. A seller who works only the procurement contact can miss the people who move the project.

The source frames this as decision-chain coverage. The first move is not a pitch but a map. Before the next visit, write down the likely layers and the question each must answer. If you cannot name who owns validation sign-off and who owns the park's milestone requirements, the account is still a headline.

## Start With the Buyer Map

The source identifies at least four layers. Their concerns are not interchangeable, and one script sent across all four will usually stall in the first round.

| Buyer layer | Likely concern | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Project entity: R&D and engineering | Technical route, validation criteria, component reliability, delivery to test milestones | Invitation to technical exchange, defined validation standards, named sign-off path |
| Wuxi High-Tech Zone industry and space administration | Site coordination, industry clustering, milestone progress, compliance | Project milestone requirements, reporting expectations, site coordination points |
| Possible research partners | Experimental design, data quality, shared validation responsibilities | Defined validation roles, data-sharing rules, joint review points |
| Upstream component suppliers | Specification fit, schedule, substitutability, quality | Procurement scope, qualification requirements, delivery dependencies |

Treat the table as a starting hypothesis, not a verified org chart. The source says the project involves these layers; it does not provide a named decision map. Replace assumptions with customer behavior and confirmed roles.

## Use Value to Turn Specifications Into an Account the Customer Can Defend

Fusion projects have large early investment and long validation cycles. Someone inside the customer must explain why the spend is justified. Value capability means translating product features into an investment case the customer can take into an internal review.

For the project entity, the source recommends at least three accounts: construction and operating costs of the validation base; supply-chain improvement from domestic key components; and the industrial clustering effect the project may create for the park. A PIP-style profit-improvement plan can organize the discussion, but the questions should be broader than profit. What can the customer increase or protect? Where does confidence come from? In what time frame? The project entity may not decide on a profit basis, but it will still ask how the investment is explained upward.

## Relationship Work Is Decision-Chain Coverage

Relationship is not a count of contacts. It is coverage of the decision chain, built through professional insight and verifiable action. The source uses a seven-item PRTABCD scan: past, needs, timing and key milestones, Arm or champion, budget, competition and alternatives, and Decision chain. Use it as an intelligence audit: where the account is blank, write the next question that would fill the blank.

The A item is often misread. Arm asks who is actively embracing the proposal, not whether one senior leader is friendly. The D item is broader than a single buyer. Decision means the multi-person, multi-department chain that can approve, delay or redirect the project. If either item is assumed rather than evidenced, the relationship map is incomplete.

### Ask About the Decisions, Not Just the Needs

The customer is not only expressing needs; it is trying to complete decisions. The source lists tasks such as which part of the validation base to build first, which steps must be outsourced, who signs the validation result, and what the park requires at project milestones. Questions should follow those tasks, because the customer will then reveal its priorities.

SPIN can supply a question order: situation, problem, implication and need-payoff. Implication questions carry particular weight. If a component is delayed, does that affect whole-machine validation, the park's assessment milestone, or both? The answer changes the next action. One case may need a technical fix; another needs a milestone recovery plan with the project entity and site administration.

## Calibrate Progress With a Two-View Funnel

A project announcement can look like the start of a sales cycle, but the customer may already be in proposal and supplier selection. The source recommends a two-view funnel: place the customer's seven procurement stages and the seller's five sales stages on the same seven-by-five map, mark both positions, then look at the gap.

The gap is useful only when based on customer behavior evidence. Has the customer issued a technical exchange invitation, provided validation standards, or arranged a site visit? Visits and CRM percentages are not progress. When the customer says it will look again, distinguish no budget, no clear ownership, and no confirmed solution. The source calls this identifying a false stall rather than forcing a close.

## Negotiate After You Write the Alternative

Long-cycle negotiations are not only about price. The source lists delivery cadence, validation responsibility, intellectual property ownership and future expansion as variables. Before discussing terms, clarify power, time, information and alternatives. Write the BATNA: if this round fails, which project can we move to, what is the minimum fallback, and where does authority sit? Without an alternative, concessions tend to become unilateral price cuts.

Supplier leverage may come from validation capability that is difficult to replace and from the ability to support project milestones. Making those points clear is more useful than shaving points off a quotation. This is a negotiating principle from the source, not a claim about any particular supplier.

## A Practical Checklist for the Next Account Review

Use this checklist before the next visit or internal review.

- Run PRTABCD and list information gaps. For each gap, write one next question.
- Place the customer in procurement stages and the seller in sales stages. Record only customer behavior evidence.
- Prepare a one-page PIP-style investment and output note covering construction, operation and supply-chain accounts.
- List at least three decision roles. For each, write the concern and the evidence needed.
- Identify who owns validation sign-off and who owns milestone scheduling. If unclear, make that the next discovery task.
- Write the BATNA and bottom line before negotiation. Mark tradeable and non-tradeable conditions.
- Treat the five capabilities as a diagnostic, not a compulsory checklist. Fix the weakest gap first.
- Convert any vague look again response into a missing condition: budget, ownership or confirmed solution.

## How to Use the Five Capabilities in Sequence

Value and questioning are most useful early, when testing whether the account deserves investment. Relationship work and the two-view funnel matter in the middle, when the seller needs to know who is pushing, what decision is being made and whether evidence supports the claimed stage. Negotiation gains weight when terms, validation responsibility and expansion are on the table.

The five capabilities are not five boxes to tick. The source says any capability left absent for a long period can cause a stall. A useful review question is therefore not Did we use all five but Which one is currently missing, and what customer evidence would show it has been addressed?

## FAQ

Q1: What should a seller do first with this project?
A1: Draw the buyer structure first: project entity, Wuxi High-Tech Zone industry administration, possible research partners and upstream suppliers. Determine which layer owns technical validation and which owns project milestones, then decide whom to meet. Without that map, visits become low-value conversation.

Q2: Why can this not be handled like an ordinary opportunity?
A2: Decision-making is spread across technical validation, site implementation, supply-chain coordination and long-run operation. Each layer has its own assessment milestones. Working only the procurement contact can miss the people who move the project.

Q3: How does value capability work here?
A3: Translate features into accounts the customer can defend internally. The source suggests construction and operation costs, supply-chain improvement from domestic key components, and industrial clustering for the park. Each account should state the investment, the improvement and the time frame.

Q4: Does relationship capability mean meeting more people?
A4: No. It means understanding and covering the decision chain through insight and verifiable action. More contacts do not prove active support or a mapped multi-department decision chain. The A and D items are especially easy to assume.

Q5: What should the seller ask?
A5: Ask about decisions: which part of the base comes first, what must be outsourced, who signs validation results, and what the park requires at milestones. Use SPIN from situation and problem to implication and need-payoff. A delay may affect a machine milestone, a park review, or both.

Q6: How can the seller tell whether the project is really advancing?
A6: Use the two-view funnel and look for behavior evidence: technical exchange invitation, validation standards or site visit. Visits and CRM percentages do not count. When the customer says it will look again, separate no budget, no ownership and no confirmed solution.

Q7: What matters in long-cycle negotiation?
A7: Write the BATNA before discussing conditions. Clarify delivery cadence, validation responsibility, intellectual property and future expansion alongside price. Leverage may come from validation capability and milestone support. Without an alternative, concessions tend to become unilateral price cuts.

Q8: Must all five capabilities be used?
A8: They are diagnostic lenses, not mandatory steps. Value and questioning are used more early, relationship and the funnel in the middle, negotiation at terms. The real test is whether any capability is absent long enough to stall the account.
