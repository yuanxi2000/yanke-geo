Industry news is rarely valuable to a key-account salesperson because of the news itself. Its value lies in the two questions it forces you to answer again: Have the customer's purchasing priorities changed? And does my position in the customer's decision chain still hold?

## Read the Event Before You Chase the Opportunity

A news report described computing-power demand driving technology iteration, with supply-chain companies looking upstream: one planned a large investment in a silicon-photonics base; another was working on EML chips and a CPO platform; a third raised investment in an optical chip and module expansion; a fourth planned a private placement for AWG, CW laser chip, and high-density optical interconnect expansion. For a salesperson, this signals shifting budget structure and decision urgency.

Keep the factual boundary clear. The facts bound to the report are the industry trend and the public investment information above. The specific operating moves, order amounts, and cooperation details of the companies mentioned were not bound to the report. Any inference must be written as a hypothesis, not as something a given company has already done.

## Value Capability: Sell in the Language of Profit

An expansion wave means the customer's capital expenditure is concentrating upstream, and procurement KPIs may shift from squeezing price toward securing supply and matching technology. If your proposal still stops at product-parameter comparisons, you hand the conversation to the customer's purchasing department. Value capability requires translating your product or service into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value: how much your offering can help improve yield, shorten delivery cycles, or save capital expenditure.

Build a profit improvement proposal, a PIP, that frames the improvement as an investment case.

### What a PIP Must Answer

- How much profit is added or protected?
- What is the mechanism, and how confident are we?
- Over what time frame does the improvement occur?

## Relationship Capability: The Decision Chain Moves During Expansion

When a customer announces an expansion or technology upgrade, internal influence reshuffles: a new project company's general manager, a new base's plant director, or a new procurement committee can change the decision chain. Relationship capability means scanning seven items with a checklist: past history, requirements, time and key milestones, the arm or embraceer who supports you, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain. For each, record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question.

Watch the arm closely. In a new project, who is willing to speak for you matters more than the title of an old contact. The decision chain is a multi-person, multi-department structure, not a single leader's one-sentence ruling. If one key person goes silent, the whole project can stall.

## Questioning Capability: Force Hidden Needs onto the Table

Technology iteration driven by computing power has a particular feature: the customer may not fully understand the next-generation solution either. Questioning capability advances through four types of questions. Situation questions establish the customer's current line and plans. Problem questions surface bottlenecks in cost, yield, and delivery. Implication questions magnify those bottlenecks into effects on the production timeline and return on investment. Need-payoff questions lead the customer to state the value of a solution themselves.

As the customer is questioned, vague anxiety turns into a clear requirement. Only then can your proposal match the language the customer uses internally to justify a project.

## System Capability: Manage Rhythm with a Two-Perspective Funnel

The most common mistake when the market is hot is that the salesperson runs faster than the customer. System capability compares five sales stages (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close) against seven customer purchasing stages (problem discovery, value judgment, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, commitment and signing). Every step must be proven by customer behavioral evidence, not by visit counts or self-assessment.

The seven customer purchasing stages and five sales stages are a many-to-many relationship. Place them on a two-perspective funnel to judge whether buying and selling are in sync. If the customer is still judging value while you push commercial terms, you are skipping stages and pushing the customer toward a competitor.

| Cadence | Action | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily | Confirm which purchasing stage the customer is in and what behavioral evidence you received | Prevent self-deception about progress |
| Weekly | Update at least one unknown among the seven scan items | Keep the intelligence network moving |
| Monthly | Clean the funnel using customer-side exit evidence and downgrade or close false progress | Reserve resources for real opportunities |

## Negotiation Capability: A Strong Market Does Not Justify Losing Control of Concessions

When upstream capacity is tight, customers may trade long-term orders for price and payment-term advantages.

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify which of the two core questions the news changes: purchasing priorities or your position in the decision chain.
- Separate bound facts from hypotheses. Do not write unbound customer actions as if they already happened.
- Draft a PIP that states profit added or protected, mechanism, confidence, and time frame.
- Run the seven-item scan and record known, unknown, source, and next question for each.
- Find the arm in any new project structure, not just the old contact with the highest title.
- Prepare four types of questions for the customer's next-generation plan.
- Compare five sales stages against seven purchasing stages and demand behavioral evidence for each move.
- Update at least one unknown scan item weekly.
- Prepare power, time, information, bottom line, and BATNA before any negotiation.
- Exchange every concession for a condition such as volume, payment structure, or long-term commitment.

## FAQ

**Q1: What should a key-account salesperson do first after seeing an industry expansion story?**

A1: Do not call the customer immediately. First judge what the news changes inside the customer: budget structure, decision-chain personnel, purchasing priority, or technical selection criteria. Then run the seven-item scan to check known and unknown information, identify the one or two customers directly affected, and decide whether to lead with a value-based profit proposal or a questioning-based needs investigation. Avoid sending the same script to every customer.

**Q2: What do the five dimensions address?**

A2: Value capability addresses why the customer should buy, translating the product into profit, investment, and operating value. Relationship capability addresses who can decide, covering the decision chain with professional ability, decision information, and credible action. Questioning capability addresses how needs become explicit, using intentional questions. System capability addresses rhythm, comparing five sales stages with seven purchasing stages. Negotiation capability addresses how the agreement is reached, exchanging conditions around power, time, information, and alternatives.

**Q3: What do A and D mean in the seven-item scan?**

A3: A is the arm, or embraceer, answering who in the customer organization supports you and is willing to speak for your proposal internally. It is not the customer's approver or job title. D is decision, meaning a multi-person, multi-department decision chain. It emphasizes that the decision is an organizational process, not one leader's personal choice. Together they remind the salesperson to find supporters and map the full decision landscape.

**Q4: How does the customer purchasing process change during an expansion wave?**

A4: The customer may compress some justification steps, speed up the build-or-buy judgment, and create a new project company or procurement committee that makes the old decision chain obsolete. The salesperson should not rush the process but reposition using the seven purchasing stages: Has the customer confirmed a solution exists? Where does responsibility sit? Each step needs customer behavioral evidence, such as asking for a proposal or scheduling a technical validation meeting, before claiming real progress.

**Q5: What is a PIP and when should it be used?**

A5: A PIP is a profit improvement proposal that frames operational improvement as an investment case. It answers how much profit can be added or protected, what the mechanism and confidence are, and over what time frame. It is not a product quotation. It fits best when the customer enters a capital-expenditure decision and must explain input and output to an internal funding approval layer, because it directly serves the capital-allocation discussion rather than only persuading a user-department manager.

**Q6: How do you tell whether sales progress is ahead of or in sync with the customer?**

A6: Place the five sales stages and seven purchasing stages on a two-perspective funnel and judge position by customer behavioral evidence. If the customer is still discovering the problem while you discuss payment terms, you are skipping stages. If the customer is already selecting suppliers while you run needs discovery, you are behind. Being in sync means your sales action serves the decision task the customer is currently completing.

**Q7: What should negotiation focus on when the market is strong?**

A7: A strong market can lead a salesperson to overestimate their power and casually agree on price or payment terms. Negotiation preparation includes clarifying relative power, time pressure, information gaps, your bottom line, and your BATNA, with executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records written down. During expansion you have capacity and technical validation as chips. Every concession should be exchanged for an equivalent condition such as order volume, payment structure, or a long-term commitment.

**Q8: Is the news still valuable if my target customer is not named?**

A8: Yes. Industry-level news reveals demand direction and competitive intensity rather than only serving named companies. You can use it as questioning material: How does the technology iteration in the report affect your product roadmap? Is your upstream supply adequate? These questions bring unspoken customer concerns to the table. Also remind yourself to separate public facts from assumptions: unbound customer actions can only be written as judgments awaiting verification.
