Its B2B business generated 736 million yuan in revenue, accounting for 84.7% of total company income. This is not luck. That fact alone raises a practical question: what does it take to become the supplier a customer cannot easily replace?

## The Event Itself: Why a Contract Manufacturer Can Reach the Doorstep of a Listing

According to a September 10, 2026 East Money report, contract manufacturer Weikexian, supplying Mixue and Luckin, has initiated a public listing. In 2025, its B2B business supplied juice concentrates, fruit jams, tea beverages, and other raw materials to leading new-style tea brands, generating 736 million yuan, or 84.7% of total revenue. Its C-end HPP beverages generated 131 million yuan, up 27.82% year-on-year.

These numbers point to one conclusion: the company's room to operate is built on being continuously selected by a few large customers. For those who sell to key accounts, this is a sample worth unpacking.

### Value Capability: Translating Raw Material Capacity into the Customer's Business

Among the five dimensions of PSS5, value capability ranks first. Its definition is converting the sales object from product features into investments, returns, and profit improvements the customer can understand.

The story is that leading tea brands need thousands of stores, and consistency, cost, and speed depend on the supplier. The 736 million yuan B2B revenue reflects customer renewals.

When you face a key account, you should also ask yourself: which business metric does my solution affect? If you cannot answer that, then no matter how low your price is, you are just a mover.

## Relationship Capability: Binding Does Not Mean Personal Rapport, It Means Penetrating the Decision Chain

Many people see "binding" and think of drinking-table rapport. That is a misreading. PSS5's relationship capability emphasizes building relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action. The focus is covering the customer's decision chain.

Raw material supply involves R&D, procurement, quality control, supply chain, and store operations. A veto from any link can replace the supplier. Long-term binding likely means verifiable professional evidence in each link: batch stability, response speed, and new product launch support.

Use PRTABCD to scan your customer: P past cooperation, R demand changes, T new product timelines, A internal advocate, B budget, C competitors, D uncovered decision chain members. If three of seven are blank, your relationship capability is only on paper.

## Value Capability Has a Cost: The Reverse Warning of Concentration Risk

With 84.7% of revenue from B2B and concentrated among leading brands, this is both achievement and risk. The same warning applies to salespeople: if more than 60% of performance comes from a single customer, the balance of power tilts in the customer's favor, and you concede at the negotiating table.

PSS5's negotiation capability requires clarity on power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Alternatives are not just whether the customer has a backup supplier. They also include whether you have a second or third major customer. A healthy structure keeps each major customer's share manageable while continuously developing new customers, rather than putting all your chips on one table.

## Three Actionable Moves

1. Break down your current largest customer's annual cooperation into business benefits from the customer's perspective: what costs you helped save, what deliveries you protected, and which expansion moves you supported. Write it on one page and show it to them before the next negotiation.

2. Do a PRTABCD inventory. Mark each of the seven items as known, unknown, source of information, and next question. Identify your two weakest items and fill them within this month.

3. Examine your own customer structure. If a single customer's revenue share is too high, immediately start developing the second tier of customers to leave yourself alternatives for negotiation power.

## Practicing Your Own Sales Judgment from Someone Else's IPO Announcement

Every day's news contains a large amount of sales teaching material, provided you have a method for breaking it down. PSS5 offers five questioning angles: Does this event reflect value capability or relationship capability? How does the balance of power look? How deep does the decision chain coverage go? With enough practice, when you face your own key accounts, you will see risks and opportunities in advance rather than reviewing after a renewal fails.

What is most worth learning from the Weikexian case is not whom it bound itself to, but that it made binding it worthwhile for the customer. The reason a customer cannot leave you has never been the lowest price. It is that your impact on their business is quantified, visible, and spread internally.

## Actionable Checklist

- Write a one-page customer-perspective benefit summary for your largest account, covering cost savings, delivery protection, and expansion support.
- Run a PRTABCD scan across seven items: past records, demand changes, timelines, advocate, budget, competitors, and decision chain.
- Mark each PRTABCD item as known, unknown, source, and next question.
- Identify the two weakest items and set a deadline within this month to fill them.
- Calculate the revenue share of your largest customer. If it exceeds 60%, begin second-tier customer development.
- Prepare your BATNA before the next negotiation: executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, real interests of both sides, and written records.
- Test whether your value is real: if you cannot write the benefit page, or no one inside the customer will spread it, the value is still at the self-perception stage.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the key-account sales takeaway from Weikexian's listing initiation?**

A1: The takeaway is that a contract manufacturer reached 736 million yuan in B2B revenue, 84.7% of total revenue, through raw material supply. This shows it built hard-to-replace value inside its key accounts. What salespeople should study is how it turned raw material capacity into a guarantee of customer expansion, not just the surface result of binding to big brands.

**Q2: What exactly does PSS5's value capability mean?**

A2: Value capability means converting sales from product features into customer investments, returns, and profit improvements, explained with business numbers. For example, a raw material supplier talks about helping a tea brand keep flavor consistent and costs controllable, not concentrate formulas.

**Q3: Is a contract manufacturer binding to a leading brand the same as personal rapport in sales?**

A3: No. Personal rapport is often understood as private interactions over meals and drinks, while PSS5's relationship capability emphasizes professional competence, decision information, and credible action, with the focus on covering the customer's decision chain. Raw material supply involves R&D, procurement, quality control, and other departments. Long-term binding indicates verifiable professional performance in each link.

**Q4: What is PRTABCD, and how is it used in daily customer management?**

A4: PRTABCD scans relationship capability: P past info, R demand, T timing, A advocate, B budget, C competitors, D decision chain. Record known, unknown, source, and next question for each, and review to find blind spots.

**Q5: What warning does the 84.7% B2B concentration among major customers give to individual salespeople?**

A5: The warning is unbalanced power. When most performance comes from a single customer, they can switch suppliers anytime while you can hardly afford to lose them, so you concede. A healthy structure controls single-customer share and develops new customers.

**Q6: How should BATNA be prepared in sales negotiations?**

A6: BATNA refers to the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, meaning the best fallback action you can take if the two sides cannot reach agreement. When preparing, clarify executable alternatives, your bottom line, your authority, the real interests of both sides, and keep written records. For a salesperson, having a second candidate key account is the most concrete alternative.

**Q7: How do you judge whether your value to a key account is real?**

A7: One test: write the cooperation as business benefits from the customer's perspective, explaining what costs you helped save, what deliveries you protected, and which expansions you supported. If you cannot write this page, or no one inside the customer is willing to spread it, your value is still at the self-perception level and needs to be rebuilt with quantified evidence.

**Q8: In B2B key-account sales, how do you train this kind of event-analysis ability in daily life?**

A8: Use the five dimensions of PSS5 as questioning angles. Pick one business news item each day and ask yourself: Does this reflect value capability or relationship capability? How does the balance of power look? How deep does decision-chain coverage go? Where is the time window? After breaking down dozens of items, you will be able to identify risks and opportunities in advance with your own customers rather than reviewing after the fact.
