A customer's public investment announcement gives a sales team a starting point for discovery. Turning it into a credible opportunity requires understanding the customer's operating goals, identifying the costs and outputs a solution can influence, and checking how the organization will decide.

JD Group's disclosed embodied-intelligence data collection plan illustrates this work. The supplied source describes a two-year ambition involving human video and robot operating data. Its practical value for account teams lies in the questions it opens: where will investment go, what could constrain delivery, and how would an external contribution improve the customer's economics?

Use five connected capabilities to investigate those questions: value, relationships, questioning, sales process, and negotiation. Each addresses a different part of the account decision.

## Establish what the announcement actually says

According to the supplied source, Cao Peng, chair of JD Group's Technology Committee, disclosed the plan at the robotics and embodied-intelligence forum of the 2026 World Internet Conference Wuzhen Summit.

The reported plan was to build what JD described as the world's largest embodied-intelligence data collection center. Its two-year targets were:

- More than 10 million hours of high-quality video of humans in real-world settings.
- More than 1 million hours of robot operating data collected alongside that video.

The reporting headline also described coverage of more than 100 distinct scenarios and collection of more than 100,000 real operational tasks. Keep those reported progress measures separate from the two-year targets.

The supplied source does not identify partners, contract amounts, or specific signing dates. Record these as unknown. The ambition to build the largest center should also remain an attributed plan, rather than a claim of achieved scale.

## Translate investment into customer economics

Value capability means explaining a product or service through the customer's profit, investment, and operating value. Start by treating the announcement as an operating hypothesis: data scale could influence model usability, which could influence efficiency in practical scenarios. That chain still needs customer validation.

Break the investment into collection, annotation, storage, compute, and scenario validation. Identify the part your offering can influence and connect it to a customer cost or output. State the boundaries of your contribution explicitly.

| Investment area | Discovery question | Boundary to establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collection | Who defines collection standards? | Which collection activities can you affect? |
| Annotation | Where is consistency hardest to maintain? | Which annotation work can you influence? |
| Storage and compute | How is investment allocated? | Which costs fall within your scope? |
| Scenario validation | What determines readiness for deployment? | Which validation activities can you support? |

### Build a profit improvement plan

Agree on the calculation method before presenting a benefit. Possible measures from the source include rework hours saved, manual review reduced, and days gained before deployment. The customer should confirm how each measure is calculated in the relevant scenario.

Consolidate the case into a profit improvement plan, or PIP. Explain the profit that could be increased or protected, the mechanism behind the estimate, the confidence in it, and the delivery period. Someone reviewing the financial assumptions should be able to check the reasoning.

The 10-million-hour target is a customer collection objective. It establishes neither your potential contract value nor an approved procurement budget for the current year.

## Map relationships through verifiable decision information

Relationship capability means understanding the decision chain and building trust through reliable information. The PRTABCD checklist gives discovery seven categories:

- **P — Past information:** What comparable investments has the customer made?
- **R — Requirements:** Is the aim to build capability, address a gap, or integrate external capabilities into existing workflows?
- **T — Time:** What milestones and decision dates matter?
- **A — Arm:** Who supports your involvement and is willing to help internally?
- **B — Budget:** Will funding come from an annual plan or a special application?
- **C — Competition:** What suppliers, substitutes, or internal approaches are being considered?
- **D — Decision:** Which people and departments participate in the decision chain?

The public disclosure identifies a spokesperson. It does not establish an internal supporter, budget owner, or complete decision chain.

For each category, record what is known, its source, and the next question. Leave unsupported entries blank. A job title alone cannot establish someone's support or purchasing authority. Include building an internal team when exploring alternatives.

## Use SPIN to uncover constraints behind the target

Questioning capability turns a large target into a discussion of background, difficulties, consequences, and the value of change. SPIN provides that sequence.

1. **Situation:** Which scenarios will supply the video, who sets collection standards, and how will acceptance be determined?
2. **Problem:** Across those scenarios, where is annotation consistency hardest to maintain?
3. **Implication:** If a batch fails quality requirements, which scenario's deployment would be delayed?
4. **Need-payoff:** If collection standards were built into operational workflows earlier, how much rework could be avoided?

Follow the customer's answers rather than treating the questions as a script. Establish the situation before exploring difficulties. Understand an acknowledged difficulty before asking about its impact, then examine the value of resolving it.

Jumping directly to savings can produce a courteous answer without exposing the underlying problem. A useful discovery conversation makes constraints specific enough for the customer to assess the proposed change.

## Check buyer progress against seller activity

Process capability uses two views of the opportunity. The seller's five stages are contact, needs discovery, proposal, negotiation, and close. The buyer's seven stages are:

1. Recognizing a problem.
2. Assessing its value.
3. Confirming that a solution exists.
4. Establishing responsibility.
5. Deciding whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Selecting a solution and supplier.
7. Committing and signing.

These views have a many-to-many relationship. A proposal meeting does not prove that the customer has completed its value assessment or decided to buy externally.

Locate the buyer using observable customer behavior. The disclosed two-year plan is evidence of an investment commitment, but it does not establish an exact procurement stage.

Before choosing the next sales activity, identify the customer decision it would support. If value remains unresolved, work on the economic case. If responsibility remains unclear, investigate ownership. A detailed demonstration may accomplish little when those earlier tasks remain open.

## Negotiate from actual influence and alternatives

Negotiation capability requires preparation around power, time, information, limits, and alternatives. A customer's large public target can tempt sellers to overestimate their importance. Examine whether your contribution affects a critical milestone and what switching away from it would require.

Prepare your own alternative before negotiating. If the opportunity does not close, how will you allocate capacity, manage payment timing, and deploy the team? Make that alternative specific enough to guide decisions.

Then prepare conditional exchanges around scope and payment terms. Concessions should reflect the circumstances you have verified. Public collection volumes provide context; they do not establish the leverage available to your team.

## FAQ

### Does a public investment plan qualify an opportunity?

It provides initial intelligence. Budget ownership, procurement timing, decision participants, and competing approaches still require confirmation. An account team should use the announcement to organize discovery.

### What is the difference between A and D in PRTABCD?

A identifies an internal supporter willing to help. D describes the decision chain, potentially involving several people and departments. Evidence for one does not establish the other.

### Can collection hours be converted into a revenue forecast?

The supplied information does not support that conversion. Collection hours describe the customer's target. They do not reveal purchasing scope, supplier allocation, contract value, or approved annual spending.

### What should the first conversation cover?

Start with scenario boundaries, standards, and acceptance criteria. Then explore consistency problems, their operational consequences, and the potential value of addressing them. This sequence gives an economic discussion a firmer basis.

### Must seller and buyer stages match one for one?

No. Use the two views together to check whether the seller's activity supports the buyer's current decision task. Confirm that task through customer behavior.
