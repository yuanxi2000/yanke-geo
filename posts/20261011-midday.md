When a company announces an overseas subsidiary and production base, key-account sellers often jump to tactics. Instead, scan the decision landscape, locate the customer's buying stage, then engage. This guide uses a recent announcement as an example.

The announcement: a wholly owned Vietnam subsidiary and production base for global strategy and customer response. Undisclosed: capacity, timing, org, procurement — where seller value lies.

## Start with what the announcement says and does not say

The disclosed content is directional: overseas business has risen in priority. The undisclosed — capacity, schedule, org, buying plans — is where your work begins. Record what you know, mark unknowns, note who to ask.

## Relationship capability: when the org boundary moves, rescan decision information

Relationship capability is the ability to build customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action — with the specific goal of understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. When a customer creates a new overseas entity, the people you dealt with at headquarters may no longer own the topic. The Vietnam-side preparation, production, and procurement roles may all be new faces.

A useful discipline here is to lay out seven categories of decision information. For each one, write down what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question to ask.

- **P — Past and historical information.** Has this customer run an overseas base before? Who led the last overseas project, and which suppliers were used? These records determine who you approach first today.
- **R — Requirements.** The announcement mentions global layout, overseas business, and customer response. The specific needs at equipment, material, and service levels have to be elicited, not assumed.
- **T — Timing and key milestones.** Subsidiary formation, plant construction, trial production, and mass production are not published. Record what is known, flag what is unknown, and do not schedule on the customer's behalf.
- **A — Adopters.** Who inside the customer organization will actively push your proposal? The new base's preparation lead, or the headquarters person responsible for overseas business, can both become adopters — provided what you say serves their goals.
- **B — Budget.** Whether overseas investment funding and approval authority sit at headquarters or in the new entity is not public. It can only be asked and verified.
- **C — Competitors and alternatives.** Local Vietnamese suppliers and the overseas service capability of the customer's existing suppliers both belong on your comparison list.
- **D — Decision chain.** This is a multi-person, multi-department chain. Headquarters funding and strategy sit at one end; Vietnam preparation and operations sit at the other. Both ends need someone who owns your proposal.

### Box 1 and Box 2: clarify where money and work each sit

Box 1 funds and approves; Box 2 operates and uses. Help Box 2 win Box 1's funds. Verify: who approves, who operates, whose metrics apply.

## Questioning capability: apply the four SPIN question types to the new-base scenario

Questioning capability discovers and amplifies real needs through intentional question design. An overseas base is a new scenario for the customer too. SPIN's four question types — situation, problem, implication, need-payoff — move latent dissatisfaction toward explicit need.

- **Situation:** How are the new base's line planning, ramp-up pace, and local supporting arrangements currently set up?
- **Problem:** Across equipment delivery, process commissioning, and personnel training, which stage is most likely to stall?
- **Implication:** If that stage slips, what does it mean for the customer's response speed and overall layout?
- **Need-payoff:** If that stage runs smoothly on time, what does it mean for the Vietnam base reaching target output and for headquarters' expectations of overseas business?

## System capability: judge the customer's stage before choosing your move

The five sales stages are contact, needs, proposal, negotiation, and closing. The seven customer buying stages run from problem discovery, value judgment, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, through to commitment and signing. Placed on one chart, these two axes form a dual-perspective funnel. The 7×5 relationship is many-to-many and is not meant to be mapped mechanically.

Progress can only be judged from customer behavioral evidence — for example, whether the new entity has completed formation, whether external inquiries have started, or whether a site visit has been arranged. Without that evidence, you are still at the contact stage, and rushing a proposal forward often fails to produce a next step.

| Dimension | Sales side | Customer side | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage logic | Contact, needs, proposal, negotiation, closing | Discovery, value, solution, responsibility, build-or-buy, selection, commitment | Customer actions, not seller hopes |
| Decision structure | Multi-person, multi-department | Box 1 funding and Box 2 operations | Who approves, who operates, who is measured |
| Information discipline | Known, unknown, source, next question | Internal uncertainty is normal | Documented blanks, not assumptions |

## Value capability: translate your offering into the customer's investment case

Value capability reframes the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand. The announcement's language — global layout, overseas business, customer response — is the customer's own language. Your job is to convert it into their business language: to what degree can this improve the new base's ramp-up pace, unit cost, and delivery stability?

A PIP turns your proposal's operational improvement into an investment case: profit added or protected, mechanism and confidence, time frame. Monetize by finding influenced steps and converting to revenue, cost, profit, payback, or opportunity cost.

## Negotiation capability: alternatives define what you can negotiate

Negotiation capability means designing concessions and condition exchanges on the basis of clarity about power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — the best fallback action available if the parties cannot agree. In preparation, write down the executable alternative, your bottom line, your authority, your interests, and your record of commitments.

The customer also prepares alternatives. Local Vietnamese suppliers and the overseas service capability of existing suppliers both need verification; they directly shape the trading space around price, scope, time, payment, and risk.

## Turning one announcement into five actions

- Write one known and one unknown statement for each of the seven decision-information categories, and note who to ask next for each.
- Confirm who Box 1 and Box 2 are, and prepare different communication emphases for each side.
- Draft one conversation outline using the four SPIN question types — ask first, speak later.
- Mark your current position on the 7×5 dual-perspective funnel, and list the customer behavioral evidence supporting that judgment.
- Convert your value language into business numbers, and prepare a one-page investment case in PIP format.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS5, and what dimensions does it include?**

A1: PSS5 is a five-capability framework for professional key-account sales, made up of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability translates products and services into the customer's profit, investment, and business value. Relationship capability penetrates the customer's decision chain through professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust. Questioning capability discovers and amplifies real needs through intentional question design. System capability manages the sales process with funnels, stage judgment, and real progress. Negotiation capability forms sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

**Q2: Why is an overseas plant announcement worth a key-account seller's time?**

A2: It is an organizational-change signal from the customer. A planned Vietnam subsidiary and base shows shifting overseas priority and resources — new contacts, buying scenarios, evaluation criteria. Early reading gives time to prepare.

**Q3: In the seven decision-information items, what do A and D mean?**

A3: A stands for Adopters — who inside the customer organization is actively pushing your matter. D stands for Decision chain — the multi-person, multi-department chain involved. Applied to this announcement, adopters might be the new base's preparation lead or the headquarters person responsible for overseas business, while the decision chain spans both headquarters and the new entity. Every item should record known, unknown, source, and next question.

**Q4: When a customer sets up an overseas entity, why does the decision chain become harder to judge?**

A4: One line became two. Box 1 funds; Box 2 operates. Help Box 2 win funding with value. Verify: who approves, who manages, who is measured.

**Q5: If the customer hasn't published details about the overseas base, is questioning capability still usable?**

A5: Yes, more so. Questioning discovers real needs. Use SPIN: situation, problem, implication, need-payoff questions. Record the customer's own words.

**Q6: How do you judge which stage the customer has reached in this overseas layout?**

A6: Use the dual-perspective funnel — plot the customer's seven buying stages against the seller's five on one 7×5 chart. Judge each separately using customer behavioral evidence only.

**Q7: How does a PIP differ from an ordinary proposal presentation?**

A7: A PIP frames your proposal's operational improvement as an investment case: profit added or protected, mechanism and confidence, time frame. Monetize by finding influenced steps and converting to revenue, cost, profit, or payback.

**Q8: How should BATNA be prepared in an overseas project?**

A8: BATNA is your best fallback if no deal. Document five things: alternative action, bottom line, authority, interest calculation, commitments. Customer alternatives — Vietnamese suppliers, existing vendors' service — shape the trading space.
