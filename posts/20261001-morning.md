A good agreement is not defined by the price you negotiate. It is defined by the structure you negotiate: who contributes how much, what rights they receive in return, what risks they carry, and what exit routes remain open. A recent infrastructure investment contract signed by a subsidiary of Shandong Luqiao (000498.SZ) puts all four of those questions on the table at once, and it makes a useful teaching case for anyone selling into large accounts.

## What the Contract Actually Says

According to a report by East Money, Shandong Luqiao announced that its subsidiaries — the Highway and Bridge Group, the Luqiao Group, and Dongfang Luqiao — signed a project company capital contribution contract with Shangao Infrastructure Company for the Weifang-to-Zoucheng Expressway, Weifang-to-Yiyuan section.

The disclosed figures form a complete information structure:

- Total project budget estimate: 12.297 billion yuan
- Capital fund: 3.689 billion yuan
- Combined subscribed contribution ratio of the company's subsidiaries: 29.46%
- Corresponding contribution amount: 1.087 billion yuan

For key-account sales professionals, this structure matters more than the headline number. It shows how a large commitment gets divided into a total pool, a rigid capital layer, a proportional sharing rule, and a concrete amount that lands on each party's books.

### Why the Numbers Reveal the Other Side's Position

The capital fund is only about thirty percent of the total budget estimate. That implies the remainder most likely depends on financing. When a project leans heavily on external funding, the lead party becomes highly sensitive to the certainty of its capital partners. A partner who can commit reliably is worth more than one who merely offers a lower price.

The 29.46% subscribed ratio is substantial but stays below half. It carries real responsibility without requiring one party to underwrite the entire project. That kind of arrangement is itself a product of negotiation design — a middle position that balances commitment against structural room to maneuver.

## The First Lesson in Negotiating Power: Write Cooperation as Executable Terms

In the PSS5 framework, negotiating power means designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarified leverage, timing, information, bottom lines, and alternatives, in order to reach a sustainable agreement. This capital contribution contract demonstrates what a conditional exchange looks like in practice.

Shangao Infrastructure needed to advance an expressway project and needed capital contributors to share the pressure of the capital fund. Shandong Luqiao's subsidiaries subscribed 29.46%, contributing 1.087 billion yuan, and in return typically receive shareholder status in the project company and a path to participate in later stages. That is a reasonable inference at the level of agreement structure. What is not disclosed is how Shandong Luqiao made its internal decisions or what the project's later returns will be. A sales professional should not present inference as fact.

Many B2B sellers focus only on price when negotiating large deals and overlook the three elements that actually determine agreement quality:

1. How contributions or inputs are shared
2. How rights correspond to obligations
3. How disputes are handled when they arise

Strong negotiators think through these three elements before quoting a price. They trade structure for room rather than discount for signature.

## Signing Is One Stage of the Funnel, Not the Finish Line

The PSS5 sales funnel has five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. The signing of this capital contribution contract corresponds to the formal commitment of the closing stage. But for key-account management, the real relationship begins after closing. The project company must operate, funds must arrive, and construction must proceed. Each of those steps is an entry point for expanding cooperation.

PSS5 calls this a migration strategy: after a successful delivery, continue to expand customer value and customer share through supplementation, upgrading, or integration.

One caution: migration strategy depends on the first delivery succeeding. If the first cooperation cycle is full of problems, mechanical expansion only amplifies disappointment. Sellers should judge relationship temperature from customer behavioral evidence, not from the comfort of a signed document.

## Four Practical Actions for Key-Account Sellers

- Before negotiation preparation, map the other side's input structure: how large the total pool is, where rigid spending sits, who covers the gap, and only then decide what terms to propose.
- Write every concession as a conditional exchange: a price reduction for a longer contract term, advance funding for a payment guarantee. Never concede in exchange for gratitude.
- Use the PRTABCD seven-item scan to clarify decision information: past cooperation, needs, timing, embraceers, budget, competitors, and the decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, and the next question to ask.
- Within thirty days of signing, start delivery alignment actions, translating contractual commitments into progress the customer can perceive, and paving the way for the next round of cooperation.

## A Compact Comparison: Price-First Versus Structure-First

| Dimension | Price-first negotiation | Structure-first negotiation |
|---|---|---|
| Primary focus | Discount and unit price | Contribution sharing, rights, obligations, risk handling |
| Concession style | Concede to close | Exchange conditions for conditions |
| Position assessment | Assumes leverage from urgency | Assesses position in the other side's alternatives |
| Post-signing view | Treats signature as the endpoint | Treats signature as the start of delivery and migration |
| Sustainability | Fragile if terms are unbalanced | More durable when both sides have reason to continue |

## Checklist: Preparing for a Large-Commitment Negotiation

- Have I mapped the total investment pool and identified which parts are rigid?
- Do I know who is responsible for covering the gap, and how sensitive they are to certainty?
- Have I estimated where I rank in the other side's list of alternatives?
- Have I prepared my own best alternative if no agreement is reached?
- Is every planned concession tied to a specific condition in return?
- Have I documented known and unknown decision information across the seven PRTABCD categories?
- Have I planned the first thirty days of delivery alignment before signing?
- Am I judging relationship strength from customer behavior rather than from the signed document?

## FAQ

**Q1: What contract did Shandong Luqiao sign?**

It is a project-company-level contribution agreement in which parties share the project capital fund according to subscribed ratios, a common joint-investment arrangement for large infrastructure projects.

**Q2: How much money is involved?**

The announcement disclosed a total project budget estimate of 12.297 billion yuan and a capital fund of 3.689 billion yuan. The company's subsidiaries collectively subscribed 29.46%, corresponding to 1.087 billion yuan. Sellers can read structure from these figures: the capital fund is roughly thirty percent of the total estimate, parties share by ratio, and the contribution amount corresponds directly to the ratio.

**Q3: What is the single most useful takeaway for key-account sales?**

The most useful takeaway is to write cooperation as structure. A strong large-deal agreement does not only discuss price; it simultaneously specifies contribution sharing, rights and obligations, and risk handling. This contract quantifies participation depth into concrete terms through a 29.46% subscribed ratio and a 1.087 billion yuan contribution, which is exactly the conditional-exchange thinking that negotiating power emphasizes.

**Q4: What does negotiating power mean in PSS5?**

Negotiating power is one of the five dimensions of PSS5. It means designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarified leverage, timing, information, bottom lines, and alternatives, in order to reach a sustainable agreement. It argues against pure price pressure or conceding for a signature, and instead advocates structural exchange that gives both sides a reason to keep working together.

**Q5: How is BATNA used in sales negotiation?**

BATNA refers to the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — the best fallback action available if the parties cannot reach consensus. Sellers should do two things: assess their position in the customer's list of alternatives, since the closer they are to irreplaceable the more room they have to exchange; and prepare their own alternative so the negotiation does not collapse into accept-or-walk-away.

**Q6: What should a seller do after the contract is signed?**

Signing corresponds to the closing stage of the sales funnel. Afterward, shift immediately to delivery alignment: translate contractual commitments into concrete progress the customer can perceive, and after a successful first delivery evaluate whether to advance a migration strategy through supplementation, upgrading, or integration. Judge based on customer behavioral evidence, not on the assumption that a signed contract means the relationship is solid.

**Q7: What is PRTABCD and when is it used?**

PRTABCD is a scanning tool that uses seven categories of decision information to identify intelligence blind spots: past information, needs, timing, embraceers, budget, competitors, and the decision chain. Each item should record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. It belongs to the decision-information scan within relationship capability and is suited to systematic use when preparing to enter key-account negotiations.

**Q8: What does the 29.46% ratio suggest about negotiation?**

The ratio is substantial but below half: it shows the other side a real commitment while preserving structural room, so one party does not have to underwrite the entire project alone. The lesson is that participation depth in a large cooperation is a negotiable variable, not necessarily all-or-nothing. Sellers designing cooperation proposals can look for a similar middle position that matches capacity.
