A proposed minority stake in a game developer is not, on its face, a sales lesson. But the logic behind it maps cleanly onto a question every key-account seller eventually faces: is your value proposition built on assets you can influence over time, or on a relationship that a competitor can replace at the next budget cycle?

This guide uses one publicly reported corporate move as a lens for examining that question. It stays inside what the source material actually confirms, and it treats the rest as methodology rather than fact.

## What the source actually confirms

According to public reporting cited by East Money, Kingnet (Kaiying Network) intends to take an indirect equity stake in Wemade, with the stated direction of positioning itself closer to the source of the Legend IP and opening up coordination on the copyright side. The same source summary offers one background fact: after more than two decades of continuous operation, the Legend IP has developed a relatively stable user base, brand influence, and a mature commercialization ecosystem, giving it strong market recognition and commercial value in the Chinese games market.

That is the full extent of the verified information. Counterparty details beyond the two named companies, transaction amount, and timing do not appear in the verified material. This article does not speculate about any of them.

## Why this is worth a key-account seller's attention

The event shows a company-level action: rather than settling for the current-period returns that come from using a mature IP, it moves toward the source through an equity relationship, shifting the initiative for future coordination forward. That instinct — upgrading usage rights into influence rights — is structurally similar to what value capability looks like in key-account sales.

### The first lesson: usage-rights value versus source value

Value capability, as defined in the PSS5 framework, means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value in operating numbers.

Applied here, the Legend IP carries at least two layers of value for a games company. The first layer is usage-rights value: building products on two decades of user base and brand recognition, generating predictable revenue. The second layer is source value: participating in licensing cadence, derivative development, and the rules that govern how the asset is allocated in the future.

Most suppliers sell the first layer — the product or service itself. When budgets tighten, competitors quote lower, or the customer reorganizes, the first layer is the easiest to re-evaluate, because switching costs look low. The reported move points at the second layer: using an equity link to turn a coordination relationship from a contract obligation into a shared interest. The question for a seller is how much of your proposal the customer could get from a different supplier, and how much only exists because the two of you are deeply bound.

## Three tests for where your value proposition sits

Bringing source thinking back to key-account sales, value capability suggests three concrete tests.

**The replacement test.** If the customer switched to a competitor tomorrow, would they lose features, or would they lose data, processes, and validation results you built together? The former means you are selling usage rights. The latter approaches source value.

**The numbers test.** Can the profit impact of your proposal be written as a PIP — a Profit Improvement Proposal — stating how much profit is added or protected, the mechanism and confidence behind it, and the time frame? If you cannot answer all three, the value is still a feature description.

**The role test.** Is this value pitched at the using department, or can it help Box 2 — the managers who operate and deploy the funds — persuade Box 1, the layer that allocates and approves capital? Source-level value has to reach the approval layer's language.

These three tests mark the boundary of value capability: it is not about making features sound better, but about completing the translation from function to investment, return, and profit improvement, verified with operating numbers the customer accepts.

## Coordination is a relationship design, not a feeling

The public reporting only describes the intended indirect stake and the direction of copyright-side coordination. How Kingnet manages the cooperation internally, and how Wemade responds, are unverified. That gap is itself useful: when a customer says "we want deep coordination," what evidence confirms it is real?

The PSS5 approach returns to relationship capability and system capability tools. Relationship capability emphasizes building customer relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, with coverage of the decision chain rather than equating relationships with socializing. System capability requires a dual-view funnel — mapping the customer's seven procurement stages against the seller's five stages — and judging progress by customer behavioral evidence rather than visit counts or self-assessment.

In practice: run a PRTABCD scan over the customer's key decision information. P for past history, R for real needs, T for timing and key milestones, A for who is embracing you, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, D for the decision chain. If three or more of these seven cannot be written down with a known source and a next question, the relationship is probably still at the verbal-coordination stage. Rather than adding commitments, design a set of intentional questions that help the customer see the problem and the value of change, then move into joint solution construction.

## A compact comparison

| Dimension | Usage-rights value | Source value |
|---|---|---|
| What the customer buys | Product or service performance | Shared assets and rules |
| Switching cost | Low; features are replaceable | High; accumulated data and process |
| Typical evidence | Feature comparisons, quotes | Joint validation, dedicated data flows |
| Who it persuades | Using department | Approval layer (Box 1) |
| Negotiation posture | Price pressure applies | Alternatives are harder to substitute |

## Four actions to take this week

1. Re-inventory your top three accounts. For each, write what the customer would lose by switching suppliers. If you cannot write it, design one asset that accumulates — a joint validation mechanism or a dedicated data process.
2. Rewrite your current proposal as a PIP draft, answering profit impact, confidence mechanism, and time frame. Read it yourself first, then hand it to your internal champion.
3. Run a PRTABCD scan on each priority opportunity, design one next question for every blank, and schedule it into the next conversation.
4. Against the dual-view funnel, write the customer's current behavioral evidence and your current sales stage. Confirm buyer-seller alignment instead of rushing to negotiation while the customer is still evaluating value.

If you reach negotiation, prepare a BATNA — the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — in advance. Define the executable alternative, your bottom line, your authority, your interests, and your record. Source thinking shows up in negotiation this way: the more real your alternative, the harder it is for the customer to use replacement as a price lever.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does public information confirm about the Kingnet–Wemade move?**

A1: Public reporting cited by East Money states that Kingnet intends to take an indirect stake in Wemade, with the direction of positioning near the Legend IP source and opening copyright-side coordination. The same summary notes the IP's two-decade user base, brand influence, and mature commercialization ecosystem. Counterparty details, amount, and timing are not in the verified material and should not be filled in.

**Q2: What does value capability mean in PSS5?**

A2: PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, used in sales training, capability assessment, and practical coaching. Value capability is its first dimension: converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer understands, expressed in operating numbers. It tests translation ability, not feature recitation.

**Q3: How do usage-rights value and source value differ?**

A3: Usage-rights value is the current-period return from using your product, with limited loss on switching and therefore easy re-evaluation during budget talks. Source value is the shared asset between you and the customer — joint validation processes, dedicated data, jointly set rules — which is costly to replace. The IP example illustrates an upgrade from the first layer to the second.

**Q4: What is a PIP and how is it used?**

A4: A PIP, or Profit Improvement Proposal, frames your solution as an investment case answering three questions: how much profit is added or protected, the mechanism and confidence, and the time frame. It is not a price sheet. Identify the customer operations your solution affects, quantify the improvement, and translate it into revenue, cost, or profit language so the approval layer evaluates it as an investment.

**Q5: What are Box 1 and Box 2?**

A5: Box 1 is the layer that allocates and approves funds; Box 2 is the managers who operate and deploy them. Many proposals fail because only Box 2 was persuaded. Value capability requires helping Box 2 argue for Box 1's capital using business value: profit impact, confidence mechanism, time frame, and risk control.

**Q6: How does a PRTABCD scan test relationship strength?**

A6: PRTABCD covers past history, needs, timing, embracers, budget, competitors, and the decision chain. Record what is known, unknown, the information source, and the next question for each. If three or more items lack a known source, decision information is insufficient and the relationship is likely a feeling rather than evidence.

**Q7: What is the dual-view funnel?**

A7: It places the customer's seven procurement stages and the seller's five stages on one map. Buyer-seller alignment means judging the customer's current decision task from behavioral evidence and letting your actions serve that task, rather than advancing by visit count or CRM percentage.

**Q8: Where is the boundary of inference in this article?**

A8: Public reporting confirms only the intended indirect stake and the direction of copyright-side coordination. Internal management, outcomes, amount, and timing are unverified. The event is used here as an object of methodology analysis; all seller-side guidance is a PSS5-based suggestion, not a description of what the companies did or achieved.
