A 360 million yuan capital injection into two storage and transport bases is, on the surface, a story about warehouses and rail sidings. For key-account sales professionals, the more interesting question is how the parties involved compressed money, location, and policy into a single sentence of value — instead of listing features. That compression is the skill worth studying.

## The Event: 360 Million Yuan Into Two Storage Bases

According to reporting from East Money Information, the construction of a hub on the Xinjiang coal outbound transport corridor received fresh momentum when two storage and transport bases operated by Guanghui Logistics received a 360 million yuan capital injection.

The reporting indicates that one base sits adjacent to the Meihuajing station on the Taiyuan-Zhongwei-Yinchuan railway, within the Ningdong energy and chemical core zone in Ningxia, close to large downstream coal chemical and power-generation coal terminals. It is described as a key node in the company's four-base layout, radiating into the northwest and connecting to north and north-central China. It is also listed as a key project in the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region's 15th Five-Year Plan and in Yinchuan's national comprehensive freight hub reinforcement program.

These facts are not scattered. The rail station addresses transport capacity. The chemical and power terminals address demand. The key-project designations address the policy channel. The 360 million yuan addresses funding. Only when these four layers stack together does a value structure worth investing in emerge.

## The Value Capability Lens: Writing an Investment as a Profit Statement

Within the PSS5 framework, the value capability refers to converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand — and expressing that value in operating numbers.

By that standard, this capital injection is a textbook example of value articulation. It does not stop at functional descriptions like "warehouse" or "yard." Instead, it places the base inside the corridor pattern of Xinjiang coal outbound transport and translates a locational advantage into operating value: proximity to coal-consuming terminals.

Key-account sellers often do the reverse with their own customers. They lead with product parameters, follow with service commitments, and only reluctantly arrive at money. This example suggests a pause before the next visit: Which operating step does the proposal actually change? Can the improvement be expressed in revenue, cost, or profit? Would a senior executive approve it as an investment? If those questions cannot be answered, the value expression is still stuck at the feature level.

### Why a Corridor Node Is Worth Paying For

The most telling line in the reporting is that the base sits adjacent to the Meihuajing station and close to large downstream coal chemical and power coal terminals. In energy logistics, a node's value does not depend on how well it is built. It depends on whose necessary path it occupies. When you hold the corridor, the customer's cost of routing around you exceeds the cost of using you — that is real negotiating leverage.

Consider the layers:

- **Location.** The base is adjacent to a rail station, so transport capacity is secured. Barriers formed by hard constraints like this are difficult to replicate.
- **Demand.** Coal chemical and power coal terminals are next door. Whether the cargo has a buyer is answered by geography.
- **Policy.** Listing as a regional 15th Five-Year Plan key project and a Yinchuan freight hub reinforcement project adds tailwinds in approvals and resources.
- **Capital.** The 360 million yuan moves the earlier value proposition from blueprint to construction — from claim to deliverable fact.

## Three Practical Moves for Key-Account Sellers

Understanding someone else's investment logic is useful only if it rewrites your own sales behavior. These moves can be applied in this week's account review.

1. **Re-examine where your proposal is stuck in the customer's process.** Just as this project pinned the base between rail and terminal, identify the cost your solution replaces and put that into your next visit's opening.
2. **Do not lead with the quote.** Write the proposal as a profit-improvement document first: how much profit can be added or protected, what the mechanism and confidence level are, and over what time frame.
3. **Check the rhythm.** A customer's procurement journey from problem discovery to signed commitment involves seven decision tasks; a seller's journey from contact to close has five stages. Actions should follow the customer's current decision task, not your quarterly target.

## On Information Boundaries

Public reporting provides only the entity and the amount. Details about the injecting party and the pace of fund deployment are not yet verifiable, and this article deliberately does not speculate. That restraint is part of the value capability: when stating value to a customer, every sentence should have a source and every number should withstand follow-up questions. Trust grows from that rigor, not from adjectives.

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify the single operating step your proposal changes.
- Quantify the improvement in the customer's own units.
- Translate that improvement into revenue, cost, profit, or payback period.
- Confirm whether a senior executive would treat it as an investment.
- Map your current deal against the customer's seven decision tasks.
- Align your next action with the customer's current task, not your internal stage.
- Separate verified facts from inferences in every customer-facing document.
- Label unknowns explicitly rather than filling gaps with assumptions.

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the basic facts of the 360 million yuan injection into Guanghui Logistics' two storage bases?**

According to East Money Information, the injection supports hub construction on the Xinjiang coal outbound corridor. One base is adjacent to the Meihuajing station on the Taiyuan-Zhongwei-Yinchuan railway, in the Ningdong energy and chemical core zone in Ningxia, near large downstream coal chemical and power coal terminals. It is listed as a key project in the Ningxia 15th Five-Year Plan and in Yinchuan's national comprehensive freight hub reinforcement program. Details beyond this, such as the injecting party, should follow subsequent corporate disclosures.

**Q2: What does the value capability in PSS5 mean specifically?**

It is the ability to convert the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, expressed in operating numbers. It requires placing the proposal inside the customer's operating context, clarifying which step changes and by how much, so a senior executive can evaluate the purchase as an investment rather than bargain over an item.

**Q3: Why is location the hardest value lever for a storage base?**

Because in logistics, location determines the customer's switching cost. A base adjacent to a rail station and near coal terminals occupies a necessary node on the corridor. Routing around it typically costs more in capacity, time, and coordination than continuing to use it. When switching cost exceeds usage cost, pricing leverage tilts toward the node holder.

**Q4: How can a key-account seller write a product proposal as a profit statement?**

First identify which operating step the proposal affects. Then quantify the improvement. Finally convert it into financial language: revenue, cost, profit, payback period. The document should answer how much profit can be added or protected, what the mechanism and confidence are, and over what time frame. The customer then receives an investment case rather than a quote awaiting a discount.

**Q5: What can manufacturing or software sellers take from this?**

The logic transfers across industries: find where your solution sits in the customer's business flow, then prove the operating improvement of occupying that point. Energy logistics uses location; manufacturing can use yield or downtime; software can use process cycle time and error cost. The common thread is pinning value to the customer's own P&L with verifiable numbers.

**Q6: How do you know your value proposition reached the customer's senior level?**

Look for behavioral evidence: has anyone confirmed your improvement numbers in writing or in a meeting, has someone internally claimed ownership of the goal and its resources, and is the proposal linked to the budget cycle and approval process? When these are missing, the proposition is still at the handler level and has not entered the resource-allocation and approval view.

**Q7: Why look at the customer's seven decision tasks and the seller's five stages together?**

A customer completes seven decision tasks from problem discovery through value judgment, solution confirmation, ownership confirmation, build-versus-buy comparison, selection, and signed commitment. A seller moves through five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. The mapping is many-to-many. Placing both on one chart reveals whether buyer and seller are synchronized; pushing for a close while the customer is still judging value, or digging for needs after selection has begun, wastes opportunity.

**Q8: How can sellers avoid over-reading news when information is limited?**

Use only verifiable facts from public reporting, such as the entity, amount, location, and project listings. Stay silent or mark as unknown the undisclosed items like the injecting party, funding pace, and internal arrangements. Distinguish fact from inference and use hypothetical language for directional judgments rather than presenting guesses as outcomes. This rigor protects professional credibility and demonstrates trustworthiness to customers.
