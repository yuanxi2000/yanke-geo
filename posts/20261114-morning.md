## Establish the factual boundary first

Before approaching the account, separate three things: disclosed information, unanswered questions, and proposed actions. Record Gotion High-Tech as the associated company. Mark the counterparty, amount, and date as unknown. Do not infer them from the headline or summary.

Apply the same discipline to internal activity. A suggestion to review purchasing schedules must remain a suggestion; it cannot become a claim that the company has already reviewed them.

This distinction protects credibility. If a customer asks where a detail came from, you should be able to identify its source or explain that it remains a question. Unsupported specificity can undermine the discussion that follows.

## Treat communication as a timing variable

Negotiation capability means designing concessions and exchanges after clarifying power, timing, information, minimum acceptable conditions, and alternatives. The purpose is a sustainable agreement.

A communication signal calls for this perspective because terms may still be forming. Two unhelpful responses are to quote as though the outcome were settled or to wait without investigating the customer's decision process.

Instead, ask how the customer interprets the likely timing. That interpretation may affect purchasing schedules, budget availability, and willingness to sign. The salesperson's task is to understand the customer's assessment, not supply an unsupported forecast.

Useful opening questions include:

- Which purchasing or delivery decisions depend on greater clarity?
- What timing assumptions are being used internally?
- What information would allow the next decision to proceed?
- Who needs to participate in reviewing those assumptions?

Record answers as customer statements, keeping assumptions distinct from confirmed conditions.

## Turn information gaps into a working record

PRTABCD scans seven categories of customer decision information within relationship capability. For each category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source, and the next question.

In this situation, prioritize T for timing and milestones, B for budget, and C for competitors and alternatives. Ask which events matter, how budget decisions depend on them, and what other options the customer is considering.

Two further definitions need care. A means Arm: the person within the customer organization who supports your involvement. Record the person and evidence of support; do not treat support as approval authority. D means Decision: the decision chain involving multiple people and departments, rather than a single decision-maker.

The supplied material does not expand P and R. Keep their meanings unspecified here rather than inventing definitions.

### Make every unknown actionable

Prepare seven rows, one for each category. Leave unsupported entries marked unknown. Beside each gap, identify the next question and whom to ask.

For timing, a next question might be which milestone would trigger a purchasing review. For budget, ask what must happen before funds can be released. For the decision chain, identify departmental responsibilities and the next person to involve.

An incomplete record with clear questions is more useful than a complete-looking record built from guesses.

## Prepare your alternative before exchanging concessions

BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best available action if the parties cannot reach agreement. It must be executable to guide a real decision.

Before discussing terms, write down five elements:

1. **Alternative:** What can you actually do if agreement is unavailable?
2. **Limits:** What are the minimum acceptable conditions?
3. **Authority:** Who can approve a change or concession?
4. **Interests:** What does each side need from the agreement?
5. **Records:** How will proposals, conditions, and decisions be documented?

Consider whether delivery timing, scope, or payment arrangements can change, and how far each can move. Evaluate these options before committing to them. A possible adjustment becomes useful only when its limits and approval requirements are clear.

Then connect any concession to an explicit exchange. Discuss the complete package of conditions instead of allowing price to carry the whole negotiation. Without a workable alternative, the salesperson risks surrendering margin simply to obtain a signature.

## Translate uncertainty into the customer's financial language

Value capability translates product features into investment, returns, and profit improvement that the customer can evaluate. Changes in trade conditions may affect purchasing costs, inventory tied up, and delivery lead times. These are useful subjects for a customer-owned calculation.

First clarify timing, information, limits, and alternatives. Then compare the current situation with a conditional scenario. Keep the second scenario explicitly hypothetical until its assumptions are confirmed.

| Comparison item | Current-terms scenario | Changed-terms scenario |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchasing cost | Customer's current figures | Customer-supplied conditional figures |
| Inventory tied up | Current inventory assumptions | Revised customer assumptions |
| Delivery lead time | Current delivery assumptions | Conditional delivery assumptions |
| Commercial package | Proposed scope, payment, delivery | Alternative scope, payment, delivery |

Use only the customer's figures and calculation conventions. Leave missing inputs open; do not fill them with external statistics or industry averages. Explain which assumptions drive any difference. The calculation should help the customer evaluate conditions, while preserving uncertainty about whether those conditions will occur.

## Match your next action to the customer's decision progress

The customer purchasing process contains seven decision tasks, from recognizing a problem to committing to sign. The sales process has five stages: contact, needs, proposal, negotiation, and closing.

These processes have different levels of detail and a many-to-many relationship. A sales stage can involve several customer decision tasks, and a customer task can extend across sales stages. They do not require a one-to-one mapping.

An external communication signal may arrive while the customer is still assessing value or confirming that a solution exists. Verify that position through behavior. Has the customer clarified responsibility? Have evaluation criteria been agreed? What evidence shows that the next decision is ready?

Visit counts and the salesperson's preferred schedule do not answer those questions. Use the customer's demonstrated progress to choose the next action.

## Use questions to move from uncertainty to a defined need

SPIN provides four question types: situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff. Applied here, they form a practical sequence:

- **Situation:** How are purchasing and delivery currently arranged?
- **Problem:** Which part of the arrangement is most uncertain?
- **Implication:** If clarity arrives later, how would inventory, delivery, and budget be affected?
- **Need-payoff:** What could advance preparation help protect?

Let the customer articulate the consequences and value of preparation. Their answers provide a firmer basis for the next discussion than a forecast supplied by the seller.

## Actionable preparation checklist

Before the next account conversation:

- [ ] Record the associated company and mark counterparty, amount, and date unknown.
- [ ] Separate source facts, customer statements, assumptions, and recommendations.
- [ ] Prepare the seven-category information record and assign next questions.
- [ ] Arrange a discussion about the customer's timing assumptions.
- [ ] Identify support within the account and map the departmental decision chain.
- [ ] Prepare current-terms and changed-terms packages covering scope, payment, and delivery.
- [ ] Document the executable alternative, limits, authority, interests, and negotiation record.
- [ ] Compare scenarios using customer-provided figures and explicit assumptions.
- [ ] Choose the next sales action from evidence of customer decision progress.

Avoid three recurring mistakes: presenting discussions as a settled outcome, adding undisclosed details to sound informed, and exchanging price concessions without discussing reciprocal conditions.

## FAQ

### Can useful analysis start with such limited information?

Yes, as preparation guidance. The source supports identifying missing information and proposing questions, alternatives, and conditional calculations. It does not support claims about undisclosed transactions or internal company actions.

### Why begin with negotiation preparation rather than a financial model?

The immediate uncertainty concerns timing and conditions. Clarify those inputs first, then use value analysis to compare scenarios the customer recognizes. An attractive calculation cannot settle an unresolved timing assumption.

### Is an internal supporter the decision-maker?

Not necessarily. A records support within the organization. D records the wider decision chain. Keep evidence of support separate from responsibilities and approval authority.

### Must the purchasing and sales stages align exactly?

No. They describe different tasks at different levels of detail. Assess progress through customer behavior, including responsibility and evaluation criteria, rather than forcing an exact stage match.
