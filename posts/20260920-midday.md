Airbus's two Tianjin final assembly lines are a case study in how proximity, translated into customer language, changes a supplier's position. The reported strategy: local customers order in China, and Airbus produces as many ordered aircraft in China as possible. This carries lessons for every B2B sales team.

## The Reported Facts

East Money reports that the dual Tianjin lines strengthen Airbus's China supply chain and global capacity. Airbus localizes: Chinese orders are largely produced in China, expanding local capacity. Airbus cites a favorable local environment. Amounts, contracts, and dates remain unspecified.

The sales lesson is not about aviation. It is about what happens when delivery capability sits inside the customer's geographic radius. The customer's position across the seven stages of procurement shifts. From judging value to confirming a solution exists to selecting a provider, every step becomes easier to influence because the supplier is close, visible, and tangible.

## Value Capability: Translate Proximity Into Customer Numbers

Airbus did not stop at a feature description like "we have a factory in China." It wrote the message in customer language: you order in China, and the aircraft is built in China. That is what value capability looks like in practice. It converts a product or service into investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand.

The frontline implication: when meeting the operations manager, discuss shorter delivery cycles, reduced in-transit risk, and local response that helps keep orders. Compressing delivery radius into profit language realizes value capability.

## Relationship Capability: Distance Is Only the Entry Ticket

Some will say Airbus won because it built a factory next to its customers, and most companies cannot spend billions on a plant. That reading misses the point. Physical proximity is only the surface of relationship capability. The deeper definition is building customer relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, while understanding and covering the customer's decision chain.

Hard to replicate is Airbus's density of local decision information: who pushes procurement, budget sources, competitor contacts. You do not need a factory. You need a PRTABCD evidence table marking seven categories as known, unknown, source, and next question.

### The PRTABCD Decision-Information Scan

- P (Past/History): How the customer bought before, from whom, and what went wrong.
- R (Requirement): What problem this purchase round actually solves, and whose problem it is.
- T (Timing and Key Milestones): Where the budget window and go-live deadline sit.
- A (Adopter): Who inside the customer organization embraces you and will speak for you.
- B (Budget): Which line item holds the money and who approves it.
- C (Competition and Alternatives): What options the customer holds, including building in-house.
- D (Decision Chain): Who holds each vote in a multi-person, multi-department decision.

Update this table weekly. Relationship capability then has a verifiable carrier instead of a feeling maintained over dinners.

## Questioning Capability: Answer the Question the Customer Has Not Asked Out Loud

Localization works only if Airbus answered unspoken customer questions: delivery delays, problem handling, currency and tariff risk. Sales must surface these with four layers: situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff, letting customers articulate the value of nearby delivery.

## System Capability: Capacity Is the Result, Pipeline Management Is the Cause

Two final assembly lines required process capability for large orders. System capability uses a dual-view pipeline: seller stages (contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, close) and buyer procurement stages (problem discovery to signing). Position is judged by customer behavioral evidence, not visit counts or CRM percentages.

Many salespeople present solutions while the customer is still at "discovering a problem." That is buyer-seller misalignment. A good solution cannot move forward from the wrong stage. Airbus's localization layout effectively removes hesitation between "confirming a solution exists" and "solution and supplier selection." In pipeline terms, this is advancing with evidence rather than enthusiasm.

## Negotiation Capability: Nearby Delivery Changes the Power Structure

Negotiation capability forms agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives. Nearby delivery raises customer switching costs and stickiness. Define your executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Proximity is not power; complete information is.

## FAQ

**What are the key facts of the Airbus Tianjin dual final assembly line event?**
According to East Money reporting, the dual final assembly line landed in Tianjin, and Airbus is strengthening its China supply chain to support global capacity goals. The report states that the two Tianjin lines are a high-value part of Airbus's global final assembly system and that Airbus follows a localization strategy: local customers order in China, and Airbus produces as much of the ordered aircraft in China as possible to expand local capacity and serve the local market. Contract amounts, counterparties, and signing dates are not bound in the report, and this article does not speculate about them.

**What is PSS5 and which dimensions does it include?**
PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, used in sales training, capability assessment, and practical coaching. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. The fourth dimension is formally named system capability.

**I cannot build a factory next to my customer. How do I improve value capability?**
Value capability does not depend on physical distance. It depends on whether you can translate delivery capability into the customer's profit language. Start with a delivery-radius audit: list the hidden costs the customer pays for long-distance delivery, such as response waiting, in-transit risk, and coordination loss. Then convert "what if your supplier were next to you" into profit the customer can protect or add, and write it as a profit improvement proposal rather than a feature introduction.

**What does the PRTABCD seven-item scan mean and how is it used?**
PRTABCD is a decision-information scan within relationship capability. It uses seven categories to identify intelligence blind spots: P for past/history, R for requirement, T for timing and key milestones, A for adopter, B for budget, C for competition and alternatives, and D for decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. Update weekly so the relationship rests on verifiable information.

**What is the dual-view pipeline and how does it differ from an ordinary sales funnel?**
The dual-view pipeline places two stage axes on one chart. The seller side has five sales stages: contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, and close. The buyer side has seven procurement stages: discovering a problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The difference is that an ordinary funnel only tracks seller actions, while the dual-view pipeline requires customer behavioral evidence to judge position on both axes. It is a many-to-many relationship, and misalignment is corrected as it appears.

**What does Airbus's localization strategy suggest for negotiation capability?**
Negotiation capability forms sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives. When a supplier places a key delivery step next to the customer, the customer's switching cost rises and the power structure at the table shifts. But be careful: proximity does not mean the other side has no alternatives. Before each negotiation, define your executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records, and assess the strength of the customer's alternatives before designing concessions and terms.

**How are the four layers of questions used to uncover needs like nearby delivery?**
The four layers progress as follows: situation questions learn how the customer delivers and collaborates today; problem questions find the most painful step, such as slow remote response or uncertain logistics; implication questions amplify the business loss when that step fails; need-payoff questions lead the customer to say what it would mean if that step happened nearby. The value of these four steps is that the customer recognizes the problem and the value of change, rather than being persuaded by the salesperson.

**What do Box 1 and Box 2 mean in a customer organization, and whom should sales prioritize?**
Box 1 refers to the funding allocation and approval layer. Box 2 refers to the operations manager layer that uses the funds. The guidance is that sales should help Box 2 win Box 1's funding with business value, rather than bypassing Box 2 to push senior leadership directly. In a localization case like Airbus, Box 2 cares most about delivery stability and operational efficiency, while Box 1 cares about return on investment and risk. Prepare two sets of language for the two decision tasks.

**What is the most typical symptom of buyer-seller misalignment, and how is it corrected?**
The most typical symptom is a salesperson pushing for solution confirmation or pricing while the customer is still at an early stage of discovering a problem or judging value. Another is repeatedly introducing company background while the customer has already entered solution and supplier selection. The correction is to reposition the customer's procurement stage using behavioral evidence, such as whether the customer has claimed responsibility, given a budget window, or started comparing solutions, and then step the sales action back to what the current stage can accept.
