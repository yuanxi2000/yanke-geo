## Value Capability: Translate Inspection Points into Quality and Capacity

In PSS5's value capability, a seller translates a product or service into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value. If 7,000-plus inspection points are described only as *AI capability*, the customer hears a technology investment. If they are described as a higher density of quality-data collection tied to a failure-rate improvement, the customer hears an operating result. Token consumption follows the same logic: token unit price alone becomes a procurement comparison; line yield, rework, line stops, and delivery become a value discussion.

| Technical framing | Business framing in the CATL scenario | What the customer can verify |
|---|---|---|
| *AI capability* or token unit price | Quality stability reflected in the failure-rate change | Failure rate, rework, line stops, delivery risk |
| More than 7,000 inspection points | Higher density of quality-data collection | Inspection records, manual re-judgment, quality cost |
| Compute consumption | A cost line inside a larger operating equation | Total quality cost, capacity stability, delivery reliability |

### A practical translation exercise

For each technical claim, ask: Does this change the failure rate in a way the customer can see? Does it reduce rework, manual re-judgment, or line-stop risk? Does it improve capacity stability or delivery reliability? If the answer is unknown, keep it as a question. Do not invent a payback period, savings figure, or contract value; the source provides no amounts or dates.

## Relationship Capability: Find the Arm, Then Map the Decision Chain

PSS5's relationship capability emphasizes professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust through the customer's decision chain. On a production line, do not speak only to IT. Line inspection affects quality, production, equipment, procurement, and finance: quality cares about failure rate, production about line stops, equipment about availability, and finance about quality cost. Use PRTABCD to organize the unknowns: P for past information, R for requirement, T for timing and key milestones, A for Arm (the person willing to push the issue), B for budget, C for competition and alternatives, and D for the multi-person decision chain. The source gives no CATL internal roles for this scenario, so treat the role map as an interview hypothesis, not customer fact.

## Questioning Capability: Don't Let Token Consumption Hide the Line Problem

PSS5's questioning capability uses intentional questions to discover and amplify real demand. Start with the situation: how many judgment records does the line produce each day, and which steps still rely on manual re-judgment? Move to difficulties: do false positives and missed detections create rework, line stops, or customer complaints? Ask about impact: what does one line stop mean for delivery and cost? Then ask about return: if the failure rate remains lower, what value can be released in quality, capacity, and delivery? These questions put token consumption back into its cost position and bring production outcomes onto the decision table.

## System Capability: Judge Progress by Customer Behavior Evidence

PSS5's system capability uses a two-view funnel: the five sales stages (contact, demand, solution, negotiation, closing) and the seven customer procurement stages (identify the problem, judge value, confirm a solution exists, confirm responsibility, decide build or buy, select solution and supplier, commit and sign). The axes correspond many-to-many. The number of visits is not customer decision progress. On the CATL scenario, *the solution sounds good* is not enough. Stronger evidence is behavioral: the customer opens line data, arranges quality and equipment staff for interviews, agrees to a small validation, or writes the failure-rate metric into an evaluation form. Record each behavior and use it to choose the next action.

## Negotiation Capability: Put Token Unit Price Back into Total Cost

PSS5's negotiation capability requires clarity on power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives before designing concessions and condition exchanges for a sustainable agreement. In this scenario, prepare more than token unit price: quality cost, line-stop loss, validation cycle, and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Write down the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. The source gives no amounts or dates, so do not invent discounts, payback periods, or contract size. Negotiate what both sides can confirm: validation scope, data permissions, responsibility boundaries, and payment conditions.

## FAQ

Q1: What do PSS5's five capabilities examine?
A1: Value looks at customer operating results; relationship at the decision chain and trust; questioning at demand discovery; system at the fit between sales stages and procurement stages; negotiation at condition exchange and alternatives. In the CATL scenario, they map to quality value, cross-functional roles, production-line questions, customer behavior evidence, and clause boundaries.

Q2: Why not focus only on token consumption?
A2: Token consumption is a cost-side metric. The customer still has to judge production-line quality, line-stop risk, delivery stability, and total cost. The 7,000-plus inspection points and failure-rate change have operating meaning only when they connect to inspection capability and quality control.

Q3: What is the first step in value capability?
A3: Replace technical description with customer operating language. Inspection-point count is not value by itself. Ask what it changes: fewer false judgments, less rework, shorter line stops, or more stable delivery. Because the source gives no amount or date, do not invent a payback period.

Q4: Why look at the Arm and the decision chain?
A4: Line inspection touches quality, production, equipment, procurement, and finance, so one contact rarely moves the full decision. In PRTABCD, A is Arm, the person willing to push the issue; D is Decision, a multi-person process. Record known information, unknowns, and sources; do not treat one leader as the whole chain.

Q5: How should questions be designed?
A5: Use SPIN's four types: situation questions about judgment records and manual re-judgment; problem questions about rework, line stops, or complaints; implication questions about delivery and cost; and need-payoff questions about quality, capacity, and delivery value if the failure rate stays lower. Keep them tied to the line, not a feature list.

Q6: How should system and negotiation capabilities be applied?
A6: Judge progress by behavior evidence such as data access, interviews, validation, and metrics in evaluation. For negotiation, clarify BATNA, bottom line, and alternatives, then discuss validation scope, data permissions, responsibility boundaries, and payment conditions rather than inventing discounts or payback periods.
