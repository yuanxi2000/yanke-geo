Restructuring news gives a sales team a reason to reassess an account. It does not reveal which projects will survive, who now controls the budget, or whether a supplier change is underway. Those questions require customer evidence.

The supplied source reports that BHP plans to restructure its Australian iron ore business and that job cuts could expand. For key-account sellers, the practical issue is how cost reviews and organizational changes may affect purchasing priorities, decision authority and negotiating conditions.

Use that signal to investigate the customer's current decision task. Then adapt your value case, stakeholder engagement, questions and commercial proposals to what the customer can confirm.

## Establish the factual boundary

Keep three categories separate: reported developments, interpretations and matters requiring customer confirmation. In this case, the source supports the proposed restructuring and the possibility of further job cuts. It supplies no monetary amount, specific date or transaction counterparty.

A project freeze, budget reduction or supplier replacement therefore remains an open question. Record it that way. When an assumption becomes an apparent fact in an account plan, later discussions can become anchored to something the customer never confirmed.

Before approaching the account, write down what the report establishes, what it leaves unknown and which customer action could clarify each uncertainty. This gives every subsequent conversation a clear purpose.

## Turn the value proposition into an investment brief

During a cost review, customers may need to decide which commitments to retain, defer or reconsider. A product description alone leaves the budget owner to translate features into an investment decision. Prepare a one-page brief that makes that decision easier to examine.

### What the one-page brief should answer

Organize the brief around four questions:

- What operating result would this investment protect?
- What labor requirement or risk exposure could it reduce?
- How would cash expenditure be distributed?
- What measures would the customer use for acceptance?

Include the current situation, the proposed outcome, the verification method and the conditions for exiting the arrangement. Keep assumptions visible and avoid percentages that have not been validated.

The brief should help the person responsible for the budget assess the proposal internally. If the customer is still evaluating value, work through the assumptions together. If supplier selection has begun, use the same brief to address the customer's comparison criteria. The evidence required depends on the decision currently being made.

## Rebuild the decision map around current responsibilities

Organizational changes can alter who requests work, who uses its results and who carries the budget or risk. An established contact may have moved roles. Finance, procurement and operations may also have different responsibilities after restructuring.

Refresh the account map before relying on earlier relationships. The source uses EB, UB and TB as starting role categories. Each category needs named people, current responsibilities and a recent verifiable action; a title alone provides too little information.

For each relevant person, establish:

- Their latest public statement or verifiable action.
- The measures against which they are currently assessed.
- The consequences they face if the issue remains unresolved.

Where the answers are missing, record the gap. Familiarity with a contact does not establish their current authority. Professional insight, useful decision information and verifiable trust help a relationship remain relevant as responsibilities change.

## Track the customer's purchasing progress separately

The source describes seven customer purchasing stages:

1. Recognizing a problem.
2. Assessing value.
3. Confirming that a solution exists.
4. Establishing responsibility.
5. Deciding whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Selecting a solution and supplier.
7. Committing and signing.

It also describes five sales stages: contact, needs discovery, proposal, negotiation and closing. These two sequences have a many-to-many relationship. They do not require a fixed correspondence between individual stages.

A restructuring announcement could reach a customer still defining the problem or one already comparing suppliers. Determine progress from what the customer does.

| Evidence type | Examples from the source | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer behavior | Shares cost-review criteria, involves users, requests a validation plan, brings finance or legal into discussions | Evidence for assessing the customer's decision progress |
| Sales activity | Completes a visit, sends a proposal, delivers a demonstration | Work completed by the sales team |

Record both types, but use customer behavior to assess purchasing progress. Sending a proposal does not establish that the customer is ready to select a supplier.

## Ask questions that reveal purchasing priorities

Move through facts, implications and the value of taking action. This sequence gives the customer room to explain the situation before discussing a commitment.

Start with four layers of inquiry:

- Scope: Which business units and cost categories are included in the review?
- Reassessment: Which expenditures must demonstrate their value again?
- Consequences: What would a delay mean for operations, compliance or delivery?
- Justification: What result would make this worth advancing now?

Process questions can uncover evaluation criteria more effectively than an isolated request for a budget number. Ask how the expenditure is being assessed, then use the answer to decide whether to quote, what scope to propose and whom to involve in the review.

Let the customer explain the reason for change. Cost pressure alone does not establish a need for your offering, and the seller should avoid supplying a conclusion before the customer has described the underlying problem.

## Negotiate verifiable conditions and next steps

Incomplete information calls for careful preparation. Establish negotiating authority, timing, available information, limits and alternatives before offering concessions. The report does not reveal the customer's commercial limits.

A customer undergoing restructuring may request lower prices, longer payment terms or a smaller pilot. Treat these as possible requests to explore, rather than confirmed features of this account.

Prepare conditional exchanges:

- A smaller scope requires clear acceptance criteria.
- A price adjustment requires explicit delivery resources and service boundaries.
- A delay requires an agreed next review point.

Document the conditions so each party can verify what happens next. Where a larger agreement is premature, a defined evaluation step may provide the evidence needed for a later decision.

If using BATNA, understand it as the best alternative action available if agreement cannot be reached. Identify executable alternatives, limits, authority, interests and records. Use that preparation to understand choices and boundaries; it should not become threatening language directed at the customer.

## An actionable account-review checklist

Use these five steps to turn the news into an account review. Attach customer evidence to each completed item.

- [ ] Redraw the decision chain. Confirm affected business units and update named contacts, responsibilities and recent actions across the EB, UB and TB categories.
- [ ] Recalculate the value case. Prepare a one-page investment brief covering operational continuity, labor, risk exposure and cash expenditure, with verification and exit conditions.
- [ ] Reassess purchasing progress. Review the seven customer stages alongside the five sales stages. Record what the customer has actually done.
- [ ] Prepare three questions. Address cost-review priorities, timing and success criteria. Agree on the evidence the next discussion should produce.
- [ ] Prepare negotiating conditions. List possible exchanges involving scope, price, payment, delivery and risk, together with limits on concessions.

A useful review record contains observable actions: the customer supplied evaluation criteria, arranged a user review or introduced finance into the discussion. A completed visit alone leaves the purchasing question unresolved.

## FAQ

### What should a seller assess first?

Establish the factual boundary, then determine the customer's purchasing stage. A customer discovering a problem needs a different conversation from one selecting a supplier.

### Does restructuring indicate a new sales opportunity?

It may lead to paused projects, supplier changes or budget reallocation. The announcement alone cannot establish which outcome applies to a particular project.

### What if the customer cannot confirm a budget?

Ask how the expenditure will be evaluated and which results would justify proceeding. Use those criteria to shape the investment brief and any proposed quotation.

### Can existing relationships still be useful?

Yes, but verify current responsibilities and authority. Previous access does not establish who now owns the decision, uses the outcome or bears the risk.

### What is a productive next step when information is limited?

Agree on a verifiable action, such as a customer review of acceptance criteria or a validation plan. Define who participates and when the next review occurs.
