A restructuring review involving *ST Hexin put a 382 million yuan transaction on the table with a headline premium rate of 744.44%. At the same time, a subsidiary's Shanghai high-end mass spectrometry instrument industrialization project had been completed and transferred to fixed assets but was not yet fully in production, adding depreciation and expensed interest that increased fixed-cost pressure. The review question was blunt: what justifies the premium, and how will future returns actually be delivered?

That question is structurally identical to what key-account sellers face every day. When your quoted price is several times the bill-of-materials cost, the customer's internal value-judgment step is effectively auditing your premium. Whether your proposal can survive line-by-line questioning across a multi-person decision chain determines whether the deal stalls at the needs stage or reaches signature.

## The Event as a Public Value Defense

From public reporting, three facts stand out. First, the transaction was priced at 382 million yuan. Second, the premium rate reached 744.44%. Third, on the listed-company side, the Shanghai high-end mass spectrometry industrialization project had been completed in the fourth quarter of the prior year and transferred to fixed assets, but due to market conditions and the pace of commissioning it had not yet achieved full production, and the resulting depreciation and expensed interest further increased fixed-cost pressure.

The reviewing body's question was direct: on what basis does the premium stand, and on what basis will future earnings be realized? A seller's proposal faces the same scrutiny. A price that sits far above book numbers must be translated into returns the buyer can understand and verify.

## How PSS5 Value Capability Breaks Down This Review

PSS5 is a five-capability framework for key-account sales, covering value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability is defined as converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with operating numbers.

A restructuring transaction is an extreme sample of value capability. A 744.44% premium means the seller must demonstrate that the target will generate returns far beyond its book assets, which follows the same logic as writing a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP).

Four judgments follow from this event.

- A premium must be supported by a return model. Restructuring materials must show that the target's performance can be sustained. A sales proposal must likewise land improvement in revenue, cost, profit, or payback period, not stop at a feature list.
- Fixed costs are liabilities under close scrutiny. The listed company carried new depreciation and interest because the project was not fully in production, and the review naturally pressed on the commissioning schedule. When customers examine your proposal, they will press on implementation cost, switching cost, and idle cost.
- Past performance and future commitments must be stated separately. The reporting compared the target company's performance with the listed company's historical performance. That is the structure of a value statement: historical evidence belongs to history, and future returns come with confidence levels and mechanisms.
- A premium is confirmed by the buyer's decision chain, not announced by the seller. A transaction needs to go through review, and a proposal needs multi-party internal approval. Missing one link can mean rejection.

### A Compact Comparison: Restructuring Review vs. Key-Account Deal Review

| Dimension | Restructuring review | Key-account deal review |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | Why does the premium stand? | Why is your price higher than alternatives? |
| Evidence required | Future earnings model, performance sustainability | Quantified improvement, mechanism, verification method |
| Cost scrutiny | New depreciation, expensed interest, commissioning pace | Implementation cost, switching cost, idle cost |
| Decision authority | Review body and disclosure process | Customer's multi-person decision chain |
| Failure mode | Premium treated as risk | Value claim without evidence treated as risk discount |

## Three Concrete Actions for Key-Account Sellers

These judgments convert into actions you can take this week.

Action 1: Run a premium self-audit on your quote. Write down the three value questions the customer is most likely to ask, such as why you are more expensive than a substitute supplier, when the extra spend pays back, and what the customer loses by not buying. Prepare an operating-number answer for each.

Action 2: Rewrite feature descriptions in your proposal as investment statements. Each input should correspond to a quantifiable return, with the realization mechanism and verification method stated, so that your internal champion can use it to fight for budget.

Action 3: Use a dual-perspective funnel to check stage mismatch. Do not advance to the proposal and negotiation stages before the customer's value-judgment step is complete. Customer behavioral evidence says more about progress than your number of visits.

## Boundaries and Reminders

Specific performance commitment terms, the review outcome, and valuation methodology details for the target asset were not provided in the public information bound to this analysis, and this article does not speculate about them. For key-account sellers, this is itself a reminder: when key numbers are missing, the buyer will simply price the risk. Any value claim in your proposal that lacks supporting evidence will be treated as a risk discount by the decision chain.

Treat 744.44% as a mirror. Someone dared to ask for such a high premium because a complete return-and-risk structure was written out. You should prepare every quote the same way.

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify the three most likely value questions from the customer's decision chain.
- Convert every feature claim into a quantified revenue, cost, profit, or payback statement.
- Separate historical evidence from future commitments, and attach a confidence level and mechanism to each future claim.
- Map the customer's current stage using behavioral evidence, not visit count.
- List implementation cost, switching cost, and idle cost explicitly, with a milestone schedule.
- Disclose unsuitable conditions and implementation prerequisites proactively.
- Confirm which internal roles must approve the premium before you present it.

## FAQ

Q1: What does the 744.44% premium rate teach key-account sellers?

A1: It shows that whether a high price holds depends on whether the seller can write future returns in profit language the buyer accepts. Restructuring materials must demonstrate the target's sustained earning capacity. A sales proposal must likewise translate feature advantages into operating numbers such as increased revenue, reduced cost, or avoided risk, with a realization mechanism. Without that return structure, a premium is treated as risk, and even a low quote may be suspected of insufficient value.

Q2: What is the precise definition of PSS5 value capability?

A2: Value capability means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with operating numbers. It is one of the five dimensions of the PSS5 five-capability framework, alongside relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. It is not simply about pitching selling points; it requires reorganizing your proposal narrative from the customer's financial perspective.

Q3: Where are the main risk points in the *ST Hexin restructuring?

A3: From public reporting, risk concentrates in two places. First, the 382 million yuan transaction corresponds to a 744.44% premium, so the review must press on whether future returns can be delivered. Second, the listed company's subsidiary's Shanghai high-end mass spectrometry industrialization project had been completed and transferred to fixed assets but was not yet fully in production, and new depreciation and expensed interest increased fixed-cost pressure. The specific review conclusion should be based on regulatory disclosure.

Q4: When a customer says our quote is too expensive, what is the first step?

A4: Run a premium self-audit first. Write down the three value questions the customer's decision chain is most likely to raise, such as why you are more expensive than alternatives, how long the extra spend takes to pay back, and what the customer loses by not buying. Then answer each with operating numbers. If you cannot answer any of them, your value argument has a gap, and you should fill the evidence before discussing price rather than rushing to concede.

Q5: What is a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP)?

A5: A PIP writes the proposal's improvement to the customer's operations as an investment statement. It answers three questions: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the confidence level and mechanism are, and over what time frame it will be realized. It is not a product quotation sheet. The return argument in a restructuring transaction is essentially a PIP addressed to the reviewer.

Q6: What does fixed-cost pressure remind us about proposal design?

A6: Because *ST Hexin carried new depreciation and expensed interest while the industrialization project was not fully in production, it shows that from the customer's perspective, an asset that cannot produce corresponding output is a liability. When designing a proposal, actively control the customer's switching cost, idle cost, and implementation-cycle risk, and write the commissioning pace as clear milestones so the customer sees that every unit of input corresponds to a verifiable output plan.

Q7: How do you judge which purchasing stage a customer is in?

A7: Use customer behavioral evidence, not your visit count. The seven-stage customer purchasing process runs from discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, build-or-buy, proposal and supplier selection, to commitment and signing. Each step needs customer-side behavioral proof. For example, if the customer is still internally comparing build versus buy, rushing to present a proposal is skipping ahead and makes you easy to classify as an ordinary supplier.

Q8: In a high-premium scenario, what evidence materials should a seller prepare?

A8: Prepare three types of material. First, historical evidence proving that your proposal or company has produced quantifiable improvement in comparable scenarios. Second, a future return model stating the improvement magnitude, realization mechanism, and verification method. Third, a risk and boundary statement that proactively discloses unsuitable conditions and implementation prerequisites. Reviewers and customer decision chains both trust sellers who proactively disclose risk more than sellers who only talk about returns.
