A four-day negotiation between a national payer and more than a hundred pharmaceutical companies offers a useful mirror for key-account sales. The structure is familiar: one powerful buyer controls access, many sellers compete for the same slot, and the outcome determines whether a product reaches the largest payment channel. The details differ, but the underlying mechanics of negotiation power do not.

## What the Public Record Shows

According to public reporting cited by East Money, the National Healthcare Security Administration held on-site negotiations and competitive bidding for the 2026 national basic medical insurance drug list from September 5 to 8. Over a hundred companies participated, with 124 drugs outside the existing list entering the stage. Innovative drugs in oncology, rare diseases, chronic diseases, and pediatric medicines were expected to be included. The new list was expected in November.

Put the parties, the counterparty, the volume, and the dates together, and the structure is not unfamiliar to B2B key-account sales. One side holds the qualification to grant access. The other side consists of multiple sellers competing for the same seat. The result directly determines whether a product can enter the largest payment channel. Understanding this structure is more useful than memorizing any single news item.

## Power: Why the Buyer Sits Steady and the Seller Cannot

The first element of negotiation power is power itself. In this negotiation, the buyer's sources of power are clear: there is only one exit for access qualification, only one door to the national payment channel, and among the 124 drugs there are alternative options across multiple therapeutic areas. Although sellers may have product advantages, once a competitor agrees to a lower price, not signing becomes the worst outcome for the remaining seller.

The same situation appears in key-account sales. The customer's procurement amount may represent a small share of your revenue, but a high share of a competitor's or candidate supplier's expectations. The more candidate suppliers there are, the greater the customer's power. To assess the balance of power, first count how many real alternatives the customer holds, then count how many cards you hold that the customer cannot do without.

|---|---|---|

| Alternatives | Multiple therapeutic alternatives among 124 drugs | Competing against other sellers who may cut price |
| Consequence of no deal | Maintains budget and list integrity | Misses the access cycle and may fall behind |

## Time: What a Four-Day Window and a November Milestone Teach

The second element is time. The negotiation was concentrated into four days, from September 5 to September 8. The list was expected in November. Missing the window means waiting for another access cycle. Time pressure is one of the hardest currencies in negotiation. Whoever has less time has less room to concede.

The same applies to key-account projects. A customer's budget year, project approval milestones, and audit cycles are fixed dates. If a salesperson waits until the late stages of the customer's procurement process to negotiate terms, they are handing over the time advantage. The better approach is to align early with the customer's seven-stage procurement process within a dual-perspective funnel, and to complete key condition exchanges while the customer can still compare options, rather than waiting until only signing remains.

## Information: The Price Lost at the Table Is Often a Debt Owed on the Intelligence Network

The third element is information. The rules, calculation methods, and review criteria for the insurance negotiation are public or semi-public, and companies prepare extensive calculations before attending. In ordinary key-account sales, however, many salespeople have far less command of the customer's budget, decision chain, competitive landscape, and key timing.

PSS⁵ organizes decision information into the PRTABCD seven-item scan: past, needs, time, embraceers, budget, competitors, and decision chain. Mark each known or unknown, note sources, and write next questions. The embraceer—who actively supports you—is critical; without one, price talks become one-sided.

## Alternatives: Decide Beforehand What You Will Do If Talks Fail

The fourth element is the alternative. BATNA is the best fallback if no deal. Prepare by clarifying the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, other side's interests, and process record. In insurance negotiation, the buyer's alternative is the candidate drug list; the seller's is often the self-pay market or other channels. Each side's assessment determines its bottom line.

Before quoting a price in key-account sales, ask two questions: If the customer does not choose me, what else can they choose, and at what cost? If I do not close, what else can I do, and what do I lose? Only when both answers are clear do you know which step can be conceded and which step must be exchanged for conditions. Concession in negotiation is never mere compromise; it is a conditional exchange with a return.

## Four Practical Actions for Key-Account Sales

- Before quoting, take stock of power: list the number of real alternative suppliers available to the customer and the value points you offer that cannot be replaced. These two factors determine your negotiating posture.
- Write the key dates of the customer's seven-stage procurement process into the project plan. Schedule all important condition exchanges while the customer still has room to compare solutions, not at the final step.
- Use PRTABCD to scan the seven categories of decision information, especially the embraceer and budget items. Turn unknown items into questions for the next visit.
- Write your BATNA before negotiation: what the executable alternative is, where the bottom line is, what authority you have, what the other side's core interests are, and how the process will be recorded. None of the five can be omitted.

## Turning a News Event into a Negotiation Preparation Habit

The outcome will be known when the November list is published. The lesson is not any product's price but the universality of the four elements of negotiation power: power looks at alternatives, time at milestone pressure, information at intelligence coverage, and alternatives at bottom-line preparation. Before your next customer meeting, spend thirty minutes scoring yourself on these four items.

**Q2: What elements make up negotiation power in the PSS⁵ framework?**

A2: In the PSS⁵ framework, negotiation power means designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarity about power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives, in order to reach a sustainable agreement. It focuses on an agreement both sides can execute, not on pushing price to the floor or winning a one-time negotiation. The four elements support one another: power and alternatives determine your confidence, while time and information determine when and how accurately you play your cards.

**Q3: Why does the buyer usually have stronger power in centralized negotiations?**

A3: The buyer controls a single exit for access and faces multiple sellers, giving it many alternatives. Each seller faces competitors who may cut price. Assess power by counting the customer's real alternatives and your irreplaceable cards. More candidates and substitutability increase buyer power, forcing sellers to differentiate on value.

**Q4: How should key-account sales manage the time advantage in negotiation?**

A4: A customer's budget year, project approval milestones, and audit cycles are fixed dates. These dates determine when the customer must decide. Sales should identify the key milestones of the customer's seven-stage procurement process early and write them into the project plan, completing important condition exchanges while the customer still has room to compare solutions. Waiting until only signing remains hands the time advantage to the other side and leaves you accepting their terms.

**Q5: What is PRTABCD, and how is it used in negotiation preparation?**

A5: PRTABCD is a decision-information scanning tool associated with relationship capability. It contains seven items: past information, needs, time and key milestones, embraceers, budget, competitors and alternatives, and decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. Completing the seven-item scan before negotiation exposes intelligence blind spots in budget and competitive landscape, turns unknowns into questions for visits, and prevents discovering missing information only at the table.

**Q6: Why is the embraceer important in negotiation preparation?**

A6: The embraceer is the A in PRTABCD: who in the customer organization actively supports you and shares real information. Without one, you hear only polite talk and price negotiation becomes a single-point game with procurement. With one, you get early budget ranges and evaluation criteria changes, settling conditions before formal talks. Finding an embraceer is key pre-negotiation.

**Q7: How should key-account sales prepare their BATNA?**

A7: BATNA is the best fallback if no deal. Clarify five things: executable alternative, bottom line, authority, other side's interests, and process record. Write them down before conceding.

**Q8: Beyond negotiation power, what other capabilities do key-account sales need?**

A8: PSS⁵ has five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation capability. Negotiation is only one. Lost deals often stem from earlier gaps in needs confirmation and relationship coverage. Check all five.
