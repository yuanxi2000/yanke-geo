A news story about a fashion brand can teach a B2B sales team more than most internal reviews. The reported facts are simple: a company drew criticism while still recording roughly 6 billion RMB in revenue, its core members spent an average of about 14,000 RMB per person in a year, and its net profit margin reached 16.5%, well above several peers in apparel. The brand's menswear line, by contrast, had not gained traction.

Two facts sit side by side: falling public sentiment and rising revenue. Both can be true at once. The people who pay are a small group with strong identification with the brand, not the larger crowd watching from a distance. That is the first question value-based selling has to answer: where does your revenue actually come from, and why do those customers keep paying?

## The Value Signal Hidden in a Headline

The reported numbers describe a membership ledger: few customers, high spend per customer, repeat purchases. That combination forms the profit base. Key-account sales works the same way. Your top few accounts often determine the year's revenue and profit, not the length of your visit list.

Many sales teams say they value key accounts but allocate time by customer count: visit whoever is easy to schedule, spend time with whoever is warm. The result is heavy effort on people who do not buy, decide, or renew. Use the reported case as a mirror: if you tier by contribution, how many accounts are at the 14,000 RMB level, what did they contribute last year, and what have you done for them that you have not done for anyone else?

This is where PSS5's value capability begins. Value capability means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. The membership ledger is a ready-made example: a small group, high order value, repeatable purchases.

## Breaking the Case Down with the Five PSS5 Dimensions

PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, used in sales training, capability assessment, and practical coaching. It contains five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Placed against the reported case, each one maps to concrete action.

### Value: Start by Counting Who Pays

Numbers like an average 14,000 RMB per core member should be reproducible on your own account list. Rank customers by revenue and profit, and identify the top ten. If you cannot produce a comparable figure for your key accounts, you do not yet know where your business comes from.

### Relationship: Reach the Real Decision-Maker

Identify who actually places orders year after year, what motivates them, and what benchmark they use. Form hypotheses using the MBVS lenses and verify them one by one rather than guessing from job titles.

### Questioning: Turn Latent Dissatisfaction into Stated Needs

Use the four SPIN question types, moving from situation and problem to implication and need-payoff. Find out why customers stay and why they add purchases. Hidden dissatisfaction becomes a clear requirement only when it is surfaced through questions.

### System: Track Progress by Customer Behavior

Compare the five sales stages with the customer's seven buying stages. Judge where each key account stands, and record progress using customer behavior evidence rather than visit counts.

### Negotiation: Trade Concessions, Do Not Give Them Away

Hold the price floor for high-value customers. Every concession must exchange for a condition. Design sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

## Three Things an Account Manager Can Do This Week

Understanding someone else's ledger is easy. Returning to your own list is harder. These three actions need no extra budget and can be completed within a week.

1. Tier customers by contribution: sort 12-month purchase amount and gross margin, and mark the small group contributing most profit as core members.
2. Run a PRTABCD scan on key accounts: record knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions for past history, requirements, timing, embracement, budget, competition, and decision chain.
3. Write a PIP profit improvement proposal for each core customer: state how much profit you can add or protect, the mechanism, and the time frame—not a product quotation.

## The Menswear Warning: One Metric Is Not the Whole Answer

Menswear had not gained traction, reminding us that identification with a core group has boundaries. A 14,000 RMB-level buyer on product line A will not automatically accept line B. Before expanding, ask whether the new line solves the same problem for the same group and whether they will make a new purchasing decision.

For B2B sales, this is a question of timing for a migration strategy. After a successful delivery, you can expand share through add-ons, upgrades, or integration. But if the new value does not match the existing customer group, pushing hard only consumes trust. The more solid your value capability, the clearer it becomes which expansions flow with the current and which are forced against it.

- Map each key account against the five sales stages and seven buying stages.

## FAQ

**What is PSS5?**

PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, used in sales training, capability assessment, and practical coaching. It consists of value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation capabilities. Each answers a distinct question: why customers pay, how to penetrate the decision chain, how to discover real needs, how to manage the sales process, and how to reach sustainable agreements.

**What does value capability mean in PSS5?**

Value capability is the ability to convert the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and to explain that value with business numbers. It requires the seller to stop listing product parameters and instead calculate how much profit the proposal can add or protect, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. A team's value capability shows in whether it can state a verifiable figure for each key account, comparable to the reported 14,000 RMB average.

**What does the reported 14,000 RMB core-member spend suggest for key-account sales?**

It suggests that business quality depends on continued payment from a small, strongly identified group, not onlookers. The brand recorded roughly 6 billion RMB in revenue despite controversy, with a 16.5% net profit margin. For key-account sales, tier customers by revenue and profit, identify your own 14,000 RMB-level accounts, direct limited time toward them, and prove value in the language of profit rather than spreading effort evenly.

**How can a B2B company borrow from membership management?**

First, sort customers by 12-month purchase amount and gross margin to find the small group contributing most profit. Second, run a PRTABCD scan on that group to clarify decision chain, budget, and competitors. Third, write a PIP profit improvement proposal for each, replacing a product quotation with an investment case. Fourth, compare the five sales stages with the customer's seven buying stages, record progress by customer behavior evidence, and allocate resources to key accounts in proportion to their contribution.

**What is PRTABCD?**

PRTABCD is a scanning tool for identifying intelligence blind spots using seven categories of decision information. It belongs to the relationship capability in PSS5. The seven items are: past history, requirements, timing and key milestones, embracement (who in the customer organization supports you), budget, competition and alternatives, and the decision chain (the multi-person, multi-department decision process). For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question, so that judgment about the deal is not based on feeling.

**What is a PIP?**

A PIP, or Profit Improvement Proposal, is a tool that writes the proposal's impact on the customer's business as an investment case. It answers three questions: how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. A PIP is not a product quotation. For large accounts, especially purchases that require joint approval from a funding layer and an operations manager, a PIP with a clear profit calculation is more persuasive than any feature introduction.

**How do you judge whether a key account deserves focused investment?**

Look at three layers of evidence. First, financial contribution: does the account rank near the top in purchase amount and gross margin? Second, relationship evidence: is there an embracer inside the customer, and will anyone on the decision chain argue on your behalf? Third, behavior evidence: does the customer keep giving forward-moving actions at the needs, solution, and negotiation stages, such as arranging tests, providing budget information, or arranging meetings with higher levels? Only when all three hold is it worth committing your best resources.

**A brand can sell well despite controversy. What is the logic?**

Payers are a small, strongly identified group; controversy comes from non-paying onlookers. Sellers should serve paying customers and prove value with business numbers. Menswear not gaining traction shows identification has boundaries.
