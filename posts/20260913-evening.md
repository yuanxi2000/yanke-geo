# Reading a Public Procurement Win Through PSS5: A Field Guide for Professional Services

When a public procurement award notice is published, it reads like a single line of fact: one supplier won, others did not. For anyone selling professional services to institutional buyers, the more useful question is not who won, but what the buying structure actually rewarded. This guide works through one such notice — a university cost-consulting engagement — and uses it to show how the PSS5 five-capability framework can be applied to large service deals. The competitive reasoning below is an analytical reconstruction and recommendation, not a claim about what any named supplier actually did.

## What This Kind of Procurement Really Buys

The notice is a cost-consulting service for a student dormitory at a university. The university is the purchasing entity; the category is cost-consulting or cost-appraisal. The university allocates funds and approves, while an operations or capital-construction office uses the output and is accountable for review quality and schedule.

In sales terms, these are two boxes: one controls money and approval, the other manages the work and owns the outcome. The seller helps the second box translate professional value into reasons the first will approve.

Cost consulting has no physical sample a buyer can trial. At selection time, the client can rely on only three things:

- Verifiable records of comparable past projects
- The professional credentials of the proposed team
- Commitments in the service plan regarding review quality and schedule risk

If any one of these lacks evidence, a low price alone rarely earns a place on the shortlist. This is the practical meaning of the value capability in PSS5: shifting the sales object from product features to an investment, return, and profit improvement the client can understand, expressed in business numbers. For public-institution clients such as universities, that value language becomes budget control, audit-reduction effect, schedule assurance, and compliance safety.

## Reviewing the Deal Through Five Capabilities

PSS5 is composed of five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Applied to a single university engagement, each dimension sets a bar a consulting firm must clear. The following is a reconstruction of the competitive process and a set of recommendations; it does not represent the actual practices of any named company.

### Value

The bid document must state clearly how much cost risk the firm can help the school control, how the audit-reduction steps are designed, and how report milestones align with construction progress — rather than listing qualification certificates.

### Relationship

Relationships rest on competence, decision information, and credible action, with emphasis on covering the client's decision chain. University procurement involves the user department, procurement gatekeeping, and evaluation experts. Map who embraces you and who watches.

### Questioning

In pre-bid communication, design questions around background, difficulties, impact, and the return on the need. Find out whether the school fears cost overruns, schedule overruns, or audit problems most. Only then does the plan have a target.

### System

Use the dual-perspective funnel — five sales stages (contact, need, solution, negotiation, close) plus the client's seven procurement stages — as a map, judging progress by client behavioral evidence. Need confirmation and supplier shortlisting begin long before the notice; starting then means you are already out.

### Negotiation

Government procurement has statutory evaluation rules and limited room to negotiate, but service scope, staffing, and response times remain exchangeable terms. Designing clauses around power, time, information, and alternatives is what produces a sustainable agreement.

## PRTABCD: Seven Scans to Complete Before the Notice Appears

To engage this kind of project early, the PRTABCD seven-item scan within the relationship capability is a ready-made checklist:

- **P — Past:** To whom has the school awarded similar cost-consulting work before, and how was the cooperation evaluated?
- **R — Requirement:** What are the specific cost-consulting requirements for this dormitory project?
- **T — Time:** The schedule of project approval, budget-estimate approval, and tender milestones.
- **A — Advocate:** Who inside the client organization is willing to speak for you?
- **B — Budget:** The room for consulting fees within the project's budget estimate.
- **C — Competition:** Competitors and alternatives, including the possibility of in-house review or switching to another institution.
- **D — Decision chain:** Which people and departments jointly decide. D always refers to a multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single leader.

For each scan, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. Intelligence blind spots surface naturally.

Done thoroughly, the bid document becomes a tailored answer for this client and project. Evaluation experts read a targeted response — the most direct source of score differences.

| Capability | What it demands in this deal | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Cost-risk control, audit-reduction design, milestone alignment | Business numbers, not adjectives |
| Relationship | Coverage of user, procurement, and expert roles | Who advocates, who watches |
| Questioning | Identifying the client's top fear | Background, difficulty, impact, return |
| System | Early engagement before the notice | Client behavioral evidence at each stage |
| Negotiation | Exchangeable terms within statutory rules | Scope, staffing, response time |

## After the Award, the Work Has Just Begun

For the winner, delivery quality earns the next engagement. For losers, the notice prompts review: which value expression, relationship coverage, or time node was missed. After one delivery, expand through supplements, upgrades, or integration. Making the current project a benchmark earns a place in the next comparable-project record.

## Three Actions for Leaders of Professional Services Firms

1. Build a pre-project tracking mechanism for government procurement information. Watch project approval and budget-estimate approval nodes, not award notices, moving the point of entry from the procurement stage back to the stage where demand forms.
2. Score and review every bid against the five PSS5 capabilities, using client behavioral evidence rather than self-perception to judge real progress at each stage. Attribute a loss to a specific dimension.
3. Write the bid document as a statement of profit and risk improvement: how much the client can save, which risks it avoids, and why your claims should be believed. A chain of evidence persuades better than adjectives.

The award notice itself carries limited information, but it reminds every professional-services seller of one thing: large deals are never decided on the day bids are opened.

## Actionable Checklist

- Map the two boxes: who controls money and approval, who owns the outcome.
- Assemble three evidence types: comparable records, team credentials, quality and schedule commitments.
- Translate capability into the client's language: budget control, audit reduction, schedule assurance, compliance.
- Run the PRTABCD scan and log known, unknown, source, and next question for each item.
- Track approval and budget-estimate nodes, not award notices.
- Score each bid against all five capabilities before and after submission.
- After a win, plan the next expansion; after a loss, attribute it to a specific dimension.

## FAQ

**Q1: Who won the cost-consulting project described here?**
According to the award notice, the project was won by a named cost-consulting company. The notice disclosed the procurement category, purchasing entity, and evaluation expert list, but not the actual award amount. Analysis should rely on the notice text and avoid guessing figures.

**Q2: What are the five dimensions of PSS5?**
PSS5 comprises value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value translates the solution into client investment and return; relationship penetrates the decision chain; questioning surfaces and amplifies real needs; system manages the sales process with a dual-perspective funnel; negotiation reaches sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

**Q3: Why does value capability matter so much in service procurement?**
Professional services have no physical sample to trial. Evaluators rely on verifiable performance, team credentials, and risk commitments. Suppliers must convert service capability into value language the client understands — budget control, audit reduction, schedule assurance, compliance — and explain the improvement in business numbers. A bid that only lists certificates rarely wins.

**Q4: What does the PRTABCD scan cover?**
It uses seven categories of decision information to identify intelligence blind spots: past history, requirement, time and key nodes, advocates, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain. Each item records what is known, unknown, source, and next question. It is a decision-information scan within the relationship capability, not a role profile.

**Q5: Is it too late to start following up once an award notice appears?**
Usually yes. The client's seven procurement stages run from discovering a problem through confirming value, confirming a solution, confirming responsibility, deciding build-or-buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing to contract. The award notice appears at the final stage, by which time needs, budget, and evaluation criteria are already fixed. Serious sellers establish contact at earlier nodes such as project approval and budget-estimate approval.

**Q6: How do the five sales stages relate to the client's seven procurement stages?**
Together they form the dual-perspective funnel in the system capability: the seller advances through contact, need, solution, negotiation, and close, while the buyer advances through seven decision tasks ending in commitment and signing. The two axes are many-to-many and cannot be mapped mechanically one-to-one. Whether a sales action is effective depends on the client's current decision task, judged by behavioral evidence.

**Q7: What characterizes a university client's decision chain?**
University procurement typically involves the user department, the capital-construction or procurement gatekeeping department, and evaluation experts — a classic multi-person, multi-department decision chain rather than a single leader deciding alone. Sellers should understand each role's concerns: the user department cares about review quality and schedule coordination; the funding and approval side cares about budget and compliance. Building professional trust layer by layer is more reliable than a single-point approach.

**Q8: How should a loss be reviewed effectively?**
Review against all five capabilities: on value, whether the bid expressed improvement in the client's budget, audit-reduction, and schedule language; on relationship, whether every decision-chain role was covered and whether an advocate existed; on questioning, whether needs were identified early; on system, whether the point of entry was too late; on negotiation, whether clause design matched the evaluation rules. Attribute the loss to specific dimensions and actions so the next bid can improve.
