When customers can measure the resources consumed by each service, a proposal needs to explain what that spending achieves. A bundled project price may describe the bill, but it does not explain the investment. Key-account sellers need to connect the unit of consumption to operating costs, expected benefits, and the basis for recovering the investment.

The supplied source illustrates this shift through an East Money report about business-environment initiatives in Wuhan's Jiang'an district. The company at the center of the account is Landing, using a transliteration of its name in the source. The summary reports average consumption of approximately 1.2 million tokens per test and describes AI diagnostic services moving toward finer measurement and transparent settlement.

That is a useful commercial signal. It is not evidence of a purchase opportunity. The practical task is to investigate how customer accounting is changing, then build a proposal that customers can evaluate and explain internally.

## Separate the signal from the opportunity

The source does not identify a transaction counterparty, amount, or date. It does not establish what the company is purchasing, who controls a budget, or when a decision might happen.

Keep those gaps visible. A news item can justify an investigation, but it cannot substitute for customer confirmation. Record the reported change in measurement, then investigate its relevance to the account.

The reported token figure describes average resource consumption. Without further evidence, it does not establish a price per token, a contract structure, or a cost-saving outcome. Sales analysis should preserve that distinction.

The working hypothesis is narrower: as customers examine inputs per test, they may ask suppliers to explain unit economics and investment recovery before discussing a feature list. Validate that hypothesis with the customer.

## Rewrite the proposal around the customer's investment

Value capability means translating product functionality into investment, returns, and profit improvement that customers can understand through operating numbers. Monetization is the practical work behind that translation: identify the operating activity affected, quantify the proposed improvement, and express it in financial terms.

Start with the customer's accounting boundary. Establish which inputs belong in the calculation and which budget categories can carry them. Do not leave the customer to translate resource consumption into a budget request.

The source suggests three practical changes:

| Proposal element | Investment-oriented treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Price for one service | Explain input per test and the labor, review, or rework it may replace or reduce. |
| Resource consumption | Connect consumption to recognizable customer budget categories. |
| Discount request | Discuss an exchange involving scope, duration, and conditions. |

These are changes in reasoning, not permission to claim savings. Any proposed reduction needs a customer baseline and an explicit basis for the estimate. Where evidence is missing, record the assumption and the question needed to resolve it.

### Build a profit improvement proposal

A PIP, or profit improvement proposal, answers how much profit a solution could add or protect, why the mechanism is credible, and over what period the effect could occur. It goes beyond a quotation.

Structure the document around:

- The operating activity the proposal can influence.
- The current input or cost basis, with its information source.
- The proposed improvement and the mechanism behind it.
- The financial expression: revenue, cost, profit, payback period, or opportunity cost.
- The expected time horizon and unresolved assumptions.

Keep per-test investment, output from each service, and total investment recovery connected. The purpose is to make the commercial logic clear enough for a customer contact to relay internally.

## Map the decision information with PRTABCD

A public report cannot establish the relationship and decision information required to manage an account. PRTABCD provides seven categories for making those gaps explicit:

- **P — Past and historical information:** How has the customer accounted for this kind of spending?
- **R — Requirements:** What problem has the customer actually expressed?
- **T — Timing and milestones:** What budget cycles and internal review points matter?
- **A — Arm:** Who inside the organization actively supports your involvement?
- **B — Budget:** Which budget category holds the money, and who approves it?
- **C — Competitors and alternatives:** What other suppliers or approaches are being considered?
- **D — Decision:** How do multiple people and departments participate in the decision chain?

For each category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source, and the next question. Leave unsupported entries blank.

The distinction between A and D matters. An internal supporter can help you navigate the organization, but that person does not necessarily represent the full decision chain. Use the information gaps to direct the next week's account work.

## Measure progress through customer behavior

The source describes a dual-perspective funnel: five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages. The sales stages are contact, needs, proposal, negotiation, and closing. The purchasing journey runs from recognizing a problem to committing to a contract.

These perspectives have a many-to-many relationship. Do not force them into matching boxes or assume that sending a proposal means the customer has reached supplier selection.

Instead, look for behavior that supports a stage judgment:

- Sharing internal accounting criteria supports a judgment that the customer is evaluating value.
- Taking ownership of objectives and resources supports a judgment that responsibility has been accepted.
- Arranging proposal validation supports a judgment that selection activity is underway.

Visit counts and positive meeting comments do not establish those changes. Managers should ask what the customer did, what that behavior demonstrates, and which next action follows from it.

## Use SPIN questions to clarify the economics

Questioning capability helps turn a change in measurement into a customer conversation. The source uses SPIN: situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions.

A practical sequence is:

1. **Situation:** How do you currently calculate the input required for each test?
2. **Problem:** Where do differences in accounting definitions create difficulty?
3. **Implication:** How does that difficulty affect budget review, review costs, or rework?
4. **Need-payoff:** What would improve if the organization agreed on a clearer measurement basis?

Use the customer's answers to refine the investment proposal. Do not build a financial claim from the question alone.

The intended result is a customer explanation of why a change matters. That explanation can then inform internal discussions about budgets and operating priorities.

## Prepare negotiation before discussing concessions

Negotiation preparation should cover power, time, information, limits, and alternatives. A detailed consumption measure can make unit price prominent, so prepare the broader commercial terms before a price discussion narrows the conversation.

BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best available action you can take if agreement is not reached. Make that alternative executable.

Write down your limits, approval authority, interests, alternatives, and negotiation record. Identify which changes in scope, duration, or conditions could justify a concession.

A conditional concession links a change in your offer to a defined exchange. The source supports this discipline; it does not establish an appropriate discount level or contractual formula for a particular account.

## FAQ

### Can the report be treated as a qualified opportunity?

No. It provides a lead for investigation. Budget, requirements, decision participation, and timing still need customer evidence. The source does not identify a confirmed purchase.

### Does token measurement require token-based pricing?

The source does not establish that conclusion. It describes resource consumption and a shift toward finer measurement. Confirm the customer's accounting and commercial arrangements before choosing a pricing structure.

### What should change first in the quotation?

Explain investment per test, the operating activities affected, and the budget categories involved. Show the proposed mechanism for reducing or replacing labor, review, or rework without presenting unverified benefits as results.

### How do value capability and monetization differ?

Value capability sets the direction: explain the offer through customer investment and business improvement. Monetization performs the calculation by connecting operational changes to financial measures.

### What should a sales manager inspect?

Inspect decision-information gaps, customer behavior, and the investment explanation. Ask which activities could change, by how much, and over what period, with evidence for the answers.
