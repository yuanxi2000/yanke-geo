A customer increases capital in an overseas subsidiary. For a key-account sales team, the useful question is what business problems that investment may address, who must make the decisions, and what evidence would justify advancing an opportunity.

In the supplied case, a capital increase for a Hong Kong subsidiary points toward an offshore innovation and operations hub, overseas sales and service networks, and advanced research and talent acquisition. These purposes provide a starting point for investigation. They do not establish a purchasing requirement, an approved supplier opportunity, or an internal division of responsibilities.

This guide applies five sales capabilities: value, relationships, questioning, systematic execution, and negotiation. The aim is to turn a public investment signal into disciplined customer discovery while keeping facts, hypotheses, and recommendations separate.

## Start by separating evidence from interpretation

Before discussing an investment announcement with a customer, classify each statement you intend to use. This prevents a plausible interpretation from becoming an unsupported account claim.

| Category | Example from this case | Appropriate treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stated purpose | Funding supports overseas sales and service networks. | Use as the starting context. |
| Hypothesis | The Hong Kong subsidiary may take responsibility for project delivery. | Verify responsibilities with the customer. |
| Sales recommendation | Exchange faster delivery for clearer acceptance conditions. | Present as a proposed negotiating option. |

The source does not establish project counts, performance measures, or organizational assignments. Leave those fields unknown until evidence supports them. An announcement about building an overseas network also does not establish a need for exchange events or prove that a partnership is secure.

## Translate investment purposes into business value

Value capability connects a proposed product or service to the customer's profit, investment, and operating priorities. Start with the business activity receiving resources, then identify the outcome the customer may want to improve.

For this case, organize discovery around three questions:

- **Overseas sales:** What revenue does the customer want to protect or support through the investment?
- **Service networks:** Which response delays, service costs, or spare-parts movements need to become more predictable?
- **Research and talent:** What verifiable outputs would demonstrate that the research investment is worthwhile?

The offshore hub adds a delivery question: could the proposed solution reduce waiting or rework across borders? Ask this as a possibility to investigate, without implying that such problems have already been confirmed.

A useful value statement connects an operating issue, its business consequence, and a way to evaluate improvement. If the customer has not supplied measures, leave them open. Do not turn the existence of funding into an invented return-on-investment calculation.

### Turn a funding phrase into a discovery sequence

Take “overseas service network” as the starting phrase. First establish how service currently works. Then identify any difficult handoffs. Explore their effects on cost, delivery, or revenue. Finally, ask how the customer would judge a worthwhile improvement.

This sequence gives the customer room to define the problem before the salesperson proposes a solution.

## Map relationships through decision information

Relationship capability means understanding the decision chain through professional competence, useful information, and credible actions. Social contact alone does not establish decision coverage.

Use PRTABCD as a seven-part information inventory:

- **P — Past:** Relevant history and previous experience.
- **R — Requirements:** Needs that have been identified or confirmed.
- **T — Time:** Timing, deadlines, and important milestones.
- **A — Arm:** The person embracing or actively supporting your involvement.
- **B — Budget:** Available funding and the relevant budget basis.
- **C — Competition:** Competing suppliers and alternative approaches.
- **D — Decision:** The decision chain across people and departments.

The distinction between A and D matters. A supportive contact is useful, but that person does not necessarily control budget, approve a purchase, or carry responsibility for delivery.

In this case, the Hong Kong subsidiary, overseas sales team, research and talent leaders, and funding-management functions are possible participants. Treat this list as a set of hypotheses. The source does not confirm their actual responsibilities.

For every inventory item, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source, and the next question. Prioritize three uncertainties: who supports the project, who controls the relevant budget, and who is accountable for the result.

## Design questions that produce verifiable needs

Questioning capability uses deliberate questions to uncover and clarify real needs. The source applies the four SPIN categories to the investment signal:

1. **Situation:** How do overseas sales and service teams currently take on projects?
2. **Problem:** Where is cross-border delivery most likely to become difficult?
3. **Implication:** What effect do those difficulties have on revenue, cost, or delivery?
4. **Need-payoff:** What criteria would make an improvement worth the investment?

Use the customer's answers to determine the next question. If no delivery bottleneck has been established, investigate the current process before discussing its supposed consequences.

After the conversation, separate confirmed needs from general interest. A courteous response is not sufficient evidence of a requirement. Record the specific issue described, its stated consequence, and any evaluation criteria the customer provides.

When an answer remains unavailable, retain the uncertainty. An unanswered question is more useful than a confident story that later account decisions depend on.

## Use two funnels to judge progress

Systematic execution connects the seller's activities with the buyer's decision tasks. The source describes five sales stages:

1. Contact.
2. Needs discovery.
3. Solution development.
4. Negotiation.
5. Closing.

It also describes seven customer purchasing stages:

1. Recognizing a problem.
2. Assessing its value.
3. Confirming that a solution exists.
4. Confirming responsibility.
5. Choosing whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Selecting a solution and supplier.
7. Committing and signing.

These are two views of the opportunity with a many-to-many relationship. A seller's solution presentation does not establish that the buyer has reached supplier selection. Several sales activities may support one purchasing task, and one conversation may clarify several tasks.

For an investment signal like this, first determine what the customer is deciding. The customer may still be assessing value or assigning responsibility. Match the next sales action to that task.

Evidence of movement can include customer-provided timing, a budget basis, evaluation criteria, a trial scope, or confirmed responsibility. Record the evidence before updating the opportunity's position. Visit counts and vague encouragement cannot substitute for it.

## Negotiate across conditions

Negotiation capability considers power, time, information, and alternatives when forming a sustainable agreement. A capital increase signals resource allocation; it does not demonstrate willingness to accept a higher price.

Bring the relevant conditions into the same discussion:

- Price and scope.
- Delivery sequence and timing.
- Payment terms.
- Service responsibilities.
- Risk allocation.

If the customer requests faster delivery, propose clearer acceptance conditions in exchange. If the customer prefers a narrowly scoped pilot, explore a written agreement concerning subsequent expansion. These are possible negotiating proposals, not commitments established by the announcement.

Keep the distinction visible in account notes. Proposed exchanges remain proposals until the customer agrees. Funding language also does not establish that the customer has completed an internal organizational or financial reform.

## An actionable account-team checklist

Use this checklist before deciding the next account action:

- [ ] Extract the stated investment purposes without adding assumed projects.
- [ ] Group the purposes into overseas sales, service networks, and research or talent.
- [ ] Write one possible profit, cost, or delivery question for each group.
- [ ] Complete the seven PRTABCD fields, marking unknowns explicitly.
- [ ] Record the source and next verification question for each material claim.
- [ ] Prepare situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions.
- [ ] Identify the customer's current purchasing task.
- [ ] Attach customer behavior evidence to any proposed stage change.
- [ ] Prepare exchanges across commercial conditions before discussing concessions.

The immediate deliverable is an evidence-based account plan: business questions, a decision-information inventory, and a justified view of purchasing progress.

## FAQ

### Which capability should come first?

Start with value. Identify the business activity receiving investment and ask what revenue, cost, or delivery outcome the customer wants to improve. That gives relationship mapping and discovery a concrete purpose.

### Does the investment identify an available sales budget?

No. The stated purposes show where resources are directed. The budget relevant to a particular purchase, its controller, and its conditions still require confirmation.

### What counts as meaningful opportunity progress?

Look for customer behavior relevant to the current decision task: providing timing, a budget basis, evaluation criteria, trial boundaries, or responsibility information. General statements about future possibilities provide less support for advancing the stage.

### How should the team handle missing information?

Document it as unknown, identify its source if applicable, and attach a verification question. Keep organizational roles, project quantities, and performance measures provisional until confirmed.
