A public project announcement is a coordinate, not a forecast. The source examines Kuangda Technology’s disclosure that a wholly owned subsidiary plans to invest in a high-performance electronic glass fiber materials project (Phase I). For a key-account seller, the useful response is not to draft a quote. It is to separate confirmed facts from blanks, then choose the customer decision task your next action should support.

## Start with confirmed facts

Separate the disclosure into three buckets before any account planning: confirmed, to verify, and questions for the customer. The public source confirms only the subject and project name: Kuangda Technology’s wholly owned subsidiary plans the Phase I project. It does not provide a usable budget basis, funding arrangement, decision owner, supplier contact status, or selection progress. If the team treats those blanks as known, every later action rests on a false coordinate.

Use the public fact as a coordinate, not as a prediction. The first job is to find out where the project sits in the customer’s procurement decisions and where you can contribute something a competitor cannot easily replace.

## Locate the opportunity with seven customer decision tasks

The seven customer decision tasks are: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, assigning responsibility, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing or signing. The phrase “plans to invest” can support a working assumption that the customer has already worked through the first two tasks, problem discovery and value judgment. That remains an assumption. Later tasks require customer behavior evidence, such as a named project owner, a review schedule, or supplier comparison activity. The announcement itself supplies none of that.

On the seller side, the five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. Do not force a one-to-one match. The relationship is many-to-many: one seller stage may serve two customer tasks, and the team may need to move back to fill a gap. Evidence comes from customer behavior, not visit counts or self-assessed progress.

### Map the two timelines without forcing one-to-one matches

Put both timelines on one page. On the left, list your sales stages. On the right, list the seven customer decision tasks. In each cell, mark evidence, no evidence, or expired evidence. Buyer-seller synchronization means the next action serves the customer’s current task. Jumping ahead consumes relationship capital.

## Fill the most expensive blanks with PRTABCD

PRTABCD is a scanning sheet for the seven decision areas: Past, Requirement, Time and key milestones, Arm or champion, Budget, Competition and alternatives, and Decision chain. It is an intelligence sheet, not a proposal template. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. If you cannot write a next question, the item is still blank.

The Arm item asks who actively embraces your side and who is merely polite. The Decision chain item captures the multi-person, multi-department participation structure and who influences each link. The sheet’s main use is prioritization. When there is no champion, debating budget is meaningless. When alternatives are unclear, any quote will be used only for price comparison. Fill the item that would change your judgment first, then schedule the next visit.

## Run the same facts through five capabilities

The five capabilities are Value, Relationship, Questioning, Systems, and Negotiation. Use the same set of public facts to ask each capability a question, then work on the weakest one. If value is missing, calculate. If relationship is missing, gather decision information. If questioning is missing, design better questions.

Value means translating products and services into the customer’s profit, investment, and operating value. For this project, answer: if the line reaches stable output one day earlier, how does capital occupation and unit cost change? If yield fluctuates once, how much rework and scrap does the customer pay for? If those translations are unclear, the negotiation table is left with price alone.

Identify who uses operating numbers. Operating managers use funds; the capital allocation and approval layer controls resources. Help the operating manager calculate the account, then let that manager discuss resource allocation with the approval layer. Sales does not need to run to the front of that conversation.

Relationship capability means penetrating the decision chain through professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust. It is not entertainment. On first contact with a newly public project, bring a specific judgment and ask: what kind of delay does this project fear most, and who bears the consequence? A good question encourages the customer to explain the decision chain.

Use SPIN’s four question types: Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff. Start with project background, ask about the hardest step, enlarge the impact on investment payback, and let the customer state the payoff of a solution. The seller’s job is to ask the right question, not to supply the conclusion.

Systems capability connects stage judgment, evidence, and next action in one view. Keep the seven-task map, the five-stage map, and PRTABCD aligned, so that notes do not become orphaned observations.

Negotiation capability draws on power, time, information, and alternatives. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action available to a negotiator if no deal is reached. Prepare an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Also estimate the alternatives the other side holds. The more specific your alternative, the more measured your concessions and the less likely time pressure will push you into unilateral concessions.

## A four-action checklist

- [ ] Split the public information into confirmed, to verify, and questions requiring a face-to-face answer.
- [ ] Draw a blank PRTABCD sheet and mark the most expensive gap.
- [ ] Prepare two ask-only questions for first contact.
- [ ] Write a one-page profit-growth draft containing only numbers the customer can check.

No action needs to wait for a tender announcement.

## FAQ

**Q1: What facts can be used directly from the source?**
Only the subject and project name: Kuangda Technology’s wholly owned subsidiary plans a high-performance electronic glass fiber materials project (Phase I). Budget basis, funding details, decision makers, and selection progress are not in the public information. Treating those blanks as known sends every visit off course; labeling them lets you decide whom to ask and what to ask.

**Q2: Why can’t a public announcement determine the project stage?**
An announcement shows that the matter has reached the public level. It does not show who owns responsibility inside the customer or who will join supplier selection. Each of the seven decision tasks needs behavioral evidence, such as an assigned owner, a review arrangement, or a supplier comparison. Without that evidence, write a hypothesis and verify it in the next conversation.

**Q3: Do the seven customer tasks map one-to-one to the five sales stages?**
No. The relationship is many-to-many. One sales stage may serve two customer tasks, and the team may need to move back to fill a missed task. The test is customer behavior evidence, not visit count or self-assessed progress.

**Q4: Which PRTABCD item should be filled first?**
Fill the item that most changes your judgment. In many projects, the champion and alternatives are the most expensive gaps. If you do not know who is actively pushing your side, budget talk is empty. If you do not know what alternatives the customer holds, your quote becomes a price comparison. Each item needs known, unknown, source, and next question.

**Q5: Where should value work start for a new materials project?**
Start with numbers the customer can check: output volatility, rework and scrap, capital occupation, and time to full production. Convert those into amounts and payback, then write a profit-growth draft. Put parameters and specifications last. If the translation is unclear, price becomes the only comparison point.

**Q6: What is better than handing over materials on first contact?**
Bring a judgment and ask impact questions: what kind of delay does this project fear most, who bears the consequence, and where did similar projects stall? These questions let the customer state the difficulty and cost, and they can reveal the decision chain. Use SPIN in sequence: background, problem, implication, need-payoff.

**Q7: What alternatives should be prepared before negotiation?**
BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action if no deal is reached. Prepare an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Estimate the other side’s alternatives too. A specific alternative supports measured concessions and reduces the chance of being pushed by time pressure.

**Q8: What does relationship capability do in this kind of project?**
It penetrates the decision chain through professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust. In practice, each contact should bring a new piece of information or a new judgment, and the decision information disclosed should be recorded in PRTABCD. Know who is embracing your side, who is hesitating, and who controls budget, with a source for each.
