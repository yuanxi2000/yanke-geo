A public transaction announcement can open a useful sales investigation. It cannot reveal a customer's internal priorities, budget or decision process by itself. The practical task is to separate what the source establishes from what a sales team still needs to learn, then turn those gaps into purposeful questions.

The supplied source concerns Dongmu New Materials Group Co., Ltd. Its public-source title identifies a transaction involving the issuance of shares and payment of cash to acquire assets. The supplied material does not establish the counterparty, amount, date, internal decision progress or subsequent actions. Those gaps must remain visible.

This guide uses that limited event as a starting point for five connected capabilities: value, relationships, questioning, process management and negotiation. The questions below are investigative prompts, not claims about the company's actual circumstances.

## Start with a boundary between facts and questions

Before planning outreach, create a fact-boundary record. Include the identified organization, the transaction type supported by the source title, the unknown fields and the assumptions that must not enter the account plan as facts.

A transaction of this kind provides a setting for investigating multiple participants, funding arrangements, organizational coordination and progress through decision stages. It does not establish how this particular organization handles any of them.

Turn the event into three working questions:

- What business problem might the customer need to solve?
- Who would be willing and able to move that work forward?
- What evidence would justify the next step?

Keep possible explanations conditional. Expansion, integration and supply-chain completion are hypotheses to explore. None is a verified motive here. An unsupported amount must also stay out of the analysis: treating it as established could distort judgments about budget and transaction scale.

## Translate the transaction into business value

Value capability means expressing a proposed product or service through the customer's revenue, costs, profit, investment and operating priorities. The transaction mechanism alone does not supply a value proposition.

Ask how the relevant problem affects revenue, cost, profit, payback time or opportunity cost. If the customer confirms an expansion or integration need, explore whether resource coordination, risk sharing or greater operating certainty matters.

Different responsibilities suggest different questions:

- Business leaders: Would resolving the problem help establish capacity sooner, reduce uncertainty in external purchasing or improve delivery stability?
- Finance leaders: Would it clarify investment returns, funding arrangements or risk exposure?
- Operations leaders: Would it improve process handoffs, responsibility boundaries or exception handling?

These are possible value categories. Ask the customer which matters, how it would be measured and who would confirm the result.

## Map decision information with PRTABCD

Relationship capability combines professional insight, decision information and verifiable trust. It requires understanding how the customer organization makes progress. Social familiarity alone does not establish that understanding.

PRTABCD scans seven categories of decision information. It is neither a personality profile nor a list of individual decision-makers. For every category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source and the next question.

| Category | Information to establish | Practical next question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P: Past and history | Relevant previous arrangements and lessons | What earlier experience should guide this work? |
| R: Requirements | The underlying business problem | Is the priority growth, integration, supply-chain completion or risk control? |
| T: Time and milestones | Confirmed timing and supporting evidence | Who confirmed the next milestone, and where is it recorded? |
| A: Arm | People embracing the proposal or change | Who supports the approach and explains its value internally? |
| B: Budget | Resources, funding and approval arrangements | What resources are arranged, and what approvals remain? |
| C: Competitors and alternatives | Other choices and evaluation criteria | What alternatives are being compared, and against which criteria? |
| D: Decision | The decision chain across people and departments | Who handles technical, commercial, financial, legal and implementation decisions? |

A is fixed as Arm: it asks who within the customer organization is embracing you or your approach. D is fixed as Decision and covers a decision chain involving multiple people and departments. Neither should be reduced to a single influential contact.

## Use questions to resolve uncertainty

Questioning capability uses deliberate questions to uncover real needs and make their implications explicit. SPIN organizes this inquiry around situation, problem, implication and need-payoff questions.

### Give every question a purpose

A useful question should reveal a fact, clarify a consequence or establish a next action. Adapt these prompts to what the customer has already confirmed:

- Situation: What arrangements exist, and which documents, meetings or decision records describe the current stage?
- Problem: Where are the specific obstacles in integration, approval, funding, delivery or coordination?
- Implication: If an obstacle remains unresolved, what happens to revenue, costs, timing or customer commitments?
- Need-payoff: What result would make resolving it worthwhile, and who would verify that result?

Do not imply access to internal information you do not possess. If the current stage is unknown, establish it before discussing detailed remedies. Capture the answer's source and the follow-up it supports.

## Judge progress through buyer and seller evidence

Process capability uses stages and evidence to manage account progress. The PSS5 sales funnel has five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation and closing.

The customer purchasing process has seven stages:

1. Discover the problem.
2. Assess its value.
3. Confirm that a solution exists.
4. Confirm ownership and responsibility.
5. Decide whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Select a solution and supplier.
7. Commit and sign.

The dual-perspective funnel places these axes on a 7-by-5 grid. It distinguishes the buyer's decision task from the seller's advancement task. Their relationship is many-to-many; the stages do not require a one-to-one match.

Use customer behavior to assess whether the two sides are aligned, ahead, behind or working at cross-purposes. More visits do not establish buyer progress. Evidence may include confirmation of the problem, stated evaluation criteria, an arranged validation, participation by another department, a confirmed milestone or decision materials.

If ownership remains unresolved, identify who will take responsibility for the result. Moving directly to price can return the project to discussion. If the customer is selecting solutions and suppliers, provide a way to validate the proposal instead of relying on a feature list.

## Prepare negotiation as a series of conditional exchanges

Negotiation capability considers power, time, information and alternatives when forming a sustainable agreement. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best available action if agreement cannot be reached.

Preparation should identify executable alternatives, limits, authority, interests and records. An imagined fallback is not enough; clarify what action could actually be taken.

Break a price discussion into scope, timing, payment, risk and responsibility. This creates distinct conditions to examine and exchange:

- If internal resources are constrained, discuss phased validation.
- If timing is central, examine delivery sequence.
- If risk is central, clarify responsibility boundaries and acceptance evidence.

Before conceding, establish what the other party can offer in return. Record each exchange so that its conditions remain traceable. Use evidence the customer can verify to reduce uncertainty; verbal assurances do not provide the same basis for evaluating a commitment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a limited public event support useful sales analysis?

Yes. It can generate business, decision-information and stage questions. Its usefulness depends on preserving the boundary between the visible event and unverified internal circumstances.

### Why exclude an amount mentioned without a verified connection?

An amount not established as part of the event remains unverified. Its appearance in a summary does not make it usable evidence for budget or transaction-scale judgments.

### How does PRTABCD differ from ordinary visit notes?

Visit notes may describe activities. PRTABCD requires a structured account of decision information, including gaps, sources and next questions across seven categories.

### What demonstrates genuine opportunity progress?

Look for new customer behavior: confirmed needs, evaluation criteria, validation arrangements, departmental involvement, milestones or decision materials. Listening to another presentation may leave the opportunity at the same stage.

### Must buyer and seller stages match?

No. They describe different tasks. The seller's next action should help the customer complete its current decision task, with observable evidence of completion.
