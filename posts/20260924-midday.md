When large industrial scenarios in shipbuilding and nuclear power open up to industrial embodied intelligence, the opening of a scenario is not the same as the opening of orders. The real competition begins when vendors must translate a technology demonstration into the customer's language of profit, and when they must read a multi-layered decision chain. Shipbuilding and nuclear power are classic key-account battlegrounds: large investment, long validation cycles, and many participants. Public reporting indicates that shipbuilding and nuclear scenarios have opened prominently, that industrial embodied intelligence is entering a practical phase in Baoshan, and that universities and enterprises have shared perspectives on industrial intelligence paradigms and welding deployment experience. For B2B salespeople, this is an opportunity to put PSS5 to work.

## Value Capability: Translate a Technology Demo into Profit Language

Shipbuilding and nuclear customers care about schedules, yield, safety, and cost. Translate your equipment into reduced rework, shorter cycles, and less human exposure. Choose one quantifiable step, confirm baseline data, and jointly model improved business numbers for internal reporting.

## Relationship Capability: Penetrate the Multi-Layer Decision Chain and Find the Embrace First

Procurement involves multiple roles with different concerns. Use the PRTABCD scan: past history, requirements, timing, embrace, budget, competitors, decision chain. The embrace—who speaks for you—is critical. One internal supporter beats ten polite visits.

## Questioning Capability: Use SPIN to Surface Real Pain Points

When a scenario opens, avoid product pitches. Use SPIN: situation questions on workflow, problem questions on rework, hiring, safety; implication questions on delivery and cost; need-payoff questions so the customer states value. Customer-voiced needs are more solid.

## System Capability: Manage Long-Cycle Projects with a Dual-Perspective Funnel

Sales cycles span years, so system capability manages progress. Align the five sales stages (contact, requirements, solution, negotiation, closing) with the seven customer procurement stages. Judge by behavioral evidence, like data sharing or cross-department meetings, not visit counts.

### Stage-by-Stage Actions in the Dual-Perspective Funnel

- Contact stage: obtain a commitment from the process or equipment department for continued communication through scenario-opening events.
- Requirements stage: obtain workstation baseline data and confirm the problem and ownership.
- Solution stage: jointly define the pilot scope and success criteria with the customer.
- Negotiation stage: design condition exchanges around price, validation cycle, payment, and risk.
- Closing stage: secure formal commitment and implementation handover.

## Negotiation Capability: Prepare Alternatives Early for Long-Cycle Projects

Negotiation is not about last-minute price cuts. For shipbuilding and nuclear customers, plan your best alternative early: alternative customers, scenarios, and models. Identify their power sources—safety records or delivery commitments—and exchange conditions for concessions.

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify contactable key people on the customer side from the event and secure a next conversation commitment.
- Build a customer file using the seven-item PRTABCD scan, recording what is known, what is unknown, information sources, and the next question.
- Design a SPIN question list for the first deep visit.

- Jointly model the improved business numbers and draft a profit growth plan.

- Prepare an executable BATNA before entering negotiation.
- Exchange a condition for every concession.

## FAQ

### Q1: What is PSS5 and which dimensions does it include?

PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a key-account sales framework developed by Xu Hanbo, a partner at Yanke, and registered with the National Copyright Administration. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. The five dimensions answer, respectively: how to translate a product into customer profit, how to penetrate the decision chain, how to surface real needs, how to manage the sales process, and how to reach a sustainable agreement.

### Q2: Why are shipbuilding and nuclear customers difficult to win?

Their procurement decisions involve the workshop floor, process, equipment, safety, and investment approval departments, with many participants, high validation requirements, and cycles measured in years. No matter how good the technology is, if it stays at the demonstration level and cannot become the customer's internal business numbers and ownership, it will not enter the formal procurement process. Winning these customers requires all five capabilities at once, not a single-point breakthrough.

### Q3: What does embrace mean in PRTABCD?

In the seven-item PRTABCD scan, A stands for Arm, or embrace, and answers the question: who in the customer organization is embracing you, willing to speak for you, and willing to provide internal information or introductions. It is not a job title or an approval authority. In key-account sales, identifying and cultivating an embrace is one of the most important actions of relationship capability.

### Q4: How is the dual-perspective funnel used?

The dual-perspective funnel aligns seven customer procurement stages with five sales stages (contact, requirements, solution, negotiation, closing). Judge progress by behavioral evidence, like data sharing or working groups, not visits. Adjust actions to avoid misalignment.

### Q5: What is the practical action of value capability?

The practical action of value capability is monetization: identify the customer operating steps your solution can affect, such as welding rework rate, labor cost in high-risk positions, and inspection time; confirm baseline data together with the customer; jointly model the improvement range; and finally convert it into financial language such as revenue, cost, profit, or payback period. The written document is a PIP profit growth plan, answering how much profit can be added or protected, what the grasp and mechanism are, and when it will be realized.

### Q6: How is SPIN used when promoting new technology?

When promoting new technology such as embodied intelligence, customers often cannot articulate their needs clearly. The four SPIN question types advance in order: situation questions to understand the current workflow, problem questions to find specific pain around rework, hiring, and safety, implication questions to convert those pains into impact on delivery and cost, and need-payoff questions to let the customer state the value of change themselves. The order cannot be scrambled; jumping straight to need-payoff will look like a sales pitch.

### Q7: How is BATNA prepared in long-cycle project negotiation?

BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best alternative action you can take when the two sides cannot reach agreement. With shipbuilding and nuclear customers, preparation includes clarifying an executable alternative customer or scenario, determining your bottom line, confirming signing authority, listing the real interests of both sides, and keeping records. The more solid the alternative, the more composed you are at the negotiating table, and the less likely you are to make a loss-making concession for a single deal.

### Q8: What should sales do in the first week after a scenario-opening event?

In the first week: identify key contacts from the event and secure a next conversation; build a PRTABCD customer file noting knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions; design a SPIN question list. Let the customer state problems first.
