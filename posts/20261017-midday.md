Public news that Zhao Fei has been promoted to chairman of Changan Automobile matters to key-account sellers for one direct reason: the account's key-person list and the order in which the opportunity moves must be reconfirmed. The appointment is public. How the new decision chain will operate is not. Continuing to use the old map is more dangerous than having no map, because it creates false confidence.

## The Decision Chain Moves Before the Order

In a group account like Changan Automobile, a project usually crosses business units, procurement, finance, and senior management. When the top layer changes, the first thing that changes is the route a project takes upward and who is willing to speak for it in critical forums. A scheduled review may slip. A middle manager who previously nodded may move to wait-and-see. Someone else may rush to advance an issue they want to own. Any of these can happen; sellers need information to confirm which is true.

In PSS5, this is first a relationship-capability problem. Relationship capability is about understanding and covering the decision chain, not about the frequency of entertainment. When the top of the chain changes, information held by people below changes in value. A former connector may still be in place but may no longer be able to reach the new decision maker.

### Scan the Blind Spots with PRTABCD

Use PRTABCD here as an intelligence scan under the PSS5 method. It scans decision information, not personality profiles. Rebuild the whole table after a top-level change rather than editing one or two cells. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question.

- P, Past: What similar investments did the account make in the past two years, and what was stopped or continued, and why?
- R, Requirement: The new team may read priorities differently, so listen to the requirement again.
- A, Arm: Who in the customer organization embraces you? The position may be vacant after a personnel change.
- B, Budget: Has budget ownership or the approval rule moved with the division of labor?
- C, Competitor: Is a competitor already contacting the new management layer?
- D, Decision: What is the multi-person, multi-department path, rather than a single decision maker?
- T, Time: Before the new team's rhythm is clear, which dates are hard constraints?

After filling the scan, two blanks are common: A has no clear person, and D has only one layer. Treat those as the priority homework for the next two weeks. That work also separates a seller who is genuinely present in the account from one who is only repeating old contact history.

| Re-check | Old-map question | Better question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsor | Who used to speak for us? | Who can reach the new decision circle now, and what evidence shows it? |
| Decision path | Which executive signs? | Which business, finance, procurement, and senior roles shape the path, and in what order? |
| Progress | How many meetings have we had? | Which customer-side decision task has moved, and what behavior proves it? |

## Ask Before You Present

With newly appointed management, rushing to introduce the product is the easiest action and often the least useful. Using the SPIN sequence described in the source's PSS5 method, ask in order: situation, problem, implication, and need payoff. The four question types do different jobs.

- Situation: Under the new division of labor, which approvals have changed pace?
- Problem: Is current work stuck on resources, standards, or ownership?
- Implication: If the blockage continues, what does it mean for a goal such as globalized operations?
- Need payoff: If the issue is solved, what measurable improvement does the accountable person receive?

Do not skip from situation to payoff; customers read that as pitching. The output is the new decision circle's own ranking of concerns, not a product list.

## Translate Five Leadership Priorities into Business Language

The public report describes five leadership roles: setting strategy, allocating resources, promoting coordination, controlling risk, and strengthening incentives. Use them as five conversation entries, not slogans. Value capability requires translating product features into investment, return, and profit improvement that the customer can understand. Each entry must land on a customer action.

- Setting strategy: Which capabilities will be built internally, and which need external supply?
- Allocating resources: Where resources tilt decides what accelerates and what waits.
- Promoting coordination: Cross-unit work often needs common tools and data definitions.
- Controlling risk: Compliance, quality, and supply stability can move from a bonus to an entry requirement.
- Strengthening incentives: Where incentives point, frontline managers' behavior follows.

Then run a self-check: how many of the five does your current proposal match? The parts that do not match are likely the parts the customer will not pay for now. Value is not a thicker proposal; it is a clearer account of what the customer receives.

## Locate the Opportunity on the Two-Axis Funnel

PSS5 system capability uses a two-axis funnel: five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages. The sales stages are contact, needs, proposal, negotiation, and close. The customer stages run from problem discovery and value judgment through commitment and signing, with seven decision tasks. The two axes are many-to-many; do not align them mechanically.

In the Changan case, a common mismatch is that the seller is still polishing proposal materials while the customer has moved back to confirming responsibility, because the new division has not decided who owns the goal. More proposal detail is then low-yield. Help the customer move ownership forward. Judge only by customer behavior: has someone invited a discussion of goals, or started asking about post-implementation acceptance criteria? Visit counts and a seller's own sense of momentum are not evidence.

## Trade Concessions for Conditions

Two misjudgments are common when a new decision layer arrives: discount out of fear of losing the deal, or freeze out of fear of change. Negotiation capability means designing concessions and exchanges after clarifying power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. In this phase, the new circle's attention is scarcer than price room.

If the customer raises a price or scope adjustment, exchange it for conditions: a named owner, review milestones, and acceptance criteria. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement; prepare it internally by writing down an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. It is internal preparation, not a threat to say aloud.

## A Two-Week Action Checklist

- Rebuild PRTABCD and mark A and D blanks; write one next question for each.
- List old contacts, current roles, and new decision-circle access; ask old contacts for information, not favors.
- Schedule one business conversation about the new division; use SPIN only, with no proposal.
- Reclassify active projects by the customer purchasing seven stages; mark seller-only activity as unverified.
- Map your proposal to the five leadership priorities; park mismatches.
- Prepare BATNA internally and draft a condition exchange in case price is raised.
- Confirm hard time constraints and review dates; record the source.
- Keep a log of customer-side behavior evidence.

## Common Traps

Treating a top-level change as a relationship reset and restarting everything wastes existing trust. Treating it as no change keeps old contacts and old reporting scripts. Treating the five public leadership roles as already implemented turns a speech into a proposal. Using the phrase we have worked together for a long time as progress evidence substitutes seller perspective for customer fact. The public appointment and the unknown operation of the new chain must be connected by questions and scanning, not speculation.

## FAQ

Q1: What should a supplier do first after the customer's top leader changes?

A1: Redraw the decision chain before changing the proposal. Rebuild PRTABCD, then focus on A and D. For each blank, write the source and the next question.

Q2: Why is D not simply the decision maker?

A2: D means the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. Business, finance, procurement, and senior roles each shape the path. Locking onto one person concentrates risk.

Q3: Should old relationships be maintained?

A3: Yes, but with a new use. Old contacts can help you learn what the new team cares about, who is authorized, and what has been re-queued. Use them for information, not entertainment frequency.

Q4: What should question capability ask in a transition?

A4: Use SPIN in order: situation, problem, implication, and need payoff. Do not jump to payoff. The result should be the customer's ranking of concerns.

Q5: How can I tell whether progress is real?

A5: Look for customer-side behavior: someone discusses goals, assigns responsibility, compares build versus buy, or asks about acceptance criteria. Visits and praise are not evidence.

Q6: Should I cut price during a personnel change?

A6: Not as a first move. Trade any price or scope concession for a named owner, review milestones, and acceptance criteria. Keep BATNA as internal preparation.

Q7: Which PSS5 dimension should be fixed first?

A7: Fix the dimension where evidence is missing. After a top-level change, relationship information usually comes first: who is the new arm, and who must the decision pass through? Then use questions, value, system, and negotiation as needed.
