A key account entering a new category is not a simpler version of its existing business. The product may share a formula logic, a production base, or a channel, but the buying committee still has to decide whether the new line deserves money, attention, and reputational cover. Salespeople often answer that question with a process story. The buyer is asking for an investment case.

## Separate what can be checked from what must be discovered

The public report behind this guide confirms the subject is Tongrentang and the broader phenomenon that Chinese medicine enterprises are collectively moving into alcohol. At product level, it describes a formula built around classic-name compatibility ideas and a 35-degree light-aroma pure-grain base liquor. It does not name the partner, amount, or signing date. This guide does not fill those blanks.

Everything about who inside the customer is pushing the project, how budget travels, or where review meetings stall is a hypothesis to test. Keep facts and assumptions separate. That discipline makes the conversation credible.

## Turn the formula story into three accounts

Value capability means translating a product into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value. For a new category, the internal debate usually reduces to three accounts: new revenue, shared-resource savings, and risk to the master brand. A supplier that only says the process is related stops at the product layer; the listener has no decision basis.

| Account | Buyer question | What to bring | Weak substitute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | Will the new category create new customers and repeat purchase? | Trial shipment and return rhythm | Industry averages |
| Cost | Which duplicate investments disappear when formula research, production scheduling, and channel resources are shared? | Itemized savings | General synergy claims |
| Risk | How will the new category affect master-brand perception? | Observation indicators and stop-loss conditions | Post-launch PR |

Write these three into a one-page investment note. It is more useful than a product brochure. PSS5 calls this motion PIP, the profit improvement plan: it answers how much profit the customer can add or protect, where the gain comes from, and within what cycle it should appear. The reported product parameters—classic-formula compatibility thinking and a 35-degree light-aroma pure-grain base liquor—point to a mass-drinking occasion rather than a gift-box occasion. The numbers should follow sell-through logic, not gift-box logic.

## Ask in the order the buyer is deciding

Questioning capability is the intentional design of questions to discover and expand real demand. SPIN gives a practical sequence: situation questions to understand the existing product and channel structure; problem questions to expose formula conversion, taste stability, and compliance-claim bottlenecks; implication questions to show how those bottlenecks affect revenue and brand; and need-payoff questions that let the customer describe the solved state.

### Sequence beats volume

The order matters more than the number. If the customer is still debating whether to enter the category, asking about taste details makes you sound eager to sell. If the customer is already selecting a partner, asking background questions wastes the meeting. The test is simple: what task did the customer's last sentence address, and does your next question follow that task?

## Find the embracer and map the decision chain

Relationship capability is not entertainment. It is the use of professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust to move through the buying chain. PRTABCD organizes account intelligence into seven categories: Past, Requirement, Time, Arm (the embracer), Budget, Competition and alternatives, and Decision chain.

On a new-category project, A is often missing: who inside the customer is actively pushing the initiative? Without that person, even a complete proposal has no internal engine. D is also not one signer. A new product launch can involve brand, R&D, production, channel, and legal review. If any part lacks information, progress can stop in a review meeting.

- Mark each of the seven items as known, unknown, source, and next question.
- Prioritize unknown Arm and Decision chain items before negotiating price.
- Treat a multi-department review as evidence of D, not as a delay.

These notes are closer to reality than a stage percentage in CRM.

## Put seven buyer stages and five seller stages on one map

The seven buyer stages describe the customer's decision tasks from problem discovery to commitment. The five seller stages are contact, demand, solution, negotiation, and closing. The two axes are many-to-many; they do not line up mechanically. If the customer is still judging value and you are preparing a quote, you are jumping ahead. If the customer is already selecting and you are still asking background questions, you are wasting the opportunity.

Judge position by behavior evidence. Has the customer changed internal data? Arranged a cross-department meeting? Put the investment into a budget line? These are stronger signals than visit count or self-reported progress. PSS5 consists of five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Stage judgment is one part of that system; used alone, it distorts.

## Negotiate conditions before price

Negotiation capability means designing concessions and exchanges after clarifying power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. In a cross-category partnership, the other side often holds channel slots and launch resources; your side may hold formula capability and stable supply. Put both sets of chips on the table first; then price has room.

BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Prepare the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. Sequence concessions by scope, time, and price. Every concession should buy a verifiable commitment—trial-store count, first-order rhythm, or a joint-promotion resource slot. A concession traded for a verbal promise tends to come back during execution.

## A checklist for the next account review

- In the first three sentences, state your understanding of the customer's new business; do not repeat public-report language.
- Replace the product introduction with a one-page investment note covering input, return, validation indicators, and review cycle.
- Fill the seven PRTABCD items and mark each as known, unknown, source, and next question.
- If more than half the items are unknown, do not move to price.
- Match the customer's current task to the seven buyer stages before choosing your next action.
- Write BATNA and concession order before the negotiation.
- Tie each concession to a verifiable customer commitment.
- Record behavior evidence for stage movement, not visit counts.

## FAQ

**What does the public report confirm?** It confirms Tongrentang as the subject and the wider phenomenon of Chinese medicine enterprises entering alcohol. It describes the product's classic-formula compatibility approach, a light-aroma pure-grain base liquor, and the 35-degree parameter. Partner, amount, and signing date are absent and should remain blank.

**Why analyze this through value capability?** Because a cross-category launch succeeds or fails on whether the customer can treat it as an investment. Value capability translates product features into profit, investment, and operating value, so the analysis centers on revenue, shared-resource cost, and master-brand risk.

**How should questions be sequenced?** Situation, problem, implication, need-payoff. First understand product and channel structure, then expose conversion, stability, and compliance bottlenecks, then enlarge the impact, then let the customer describe the solved state.

**What are A and D in PRTABCD?** A is Arm, the embracer who actively pushes the initiative inside the customer. D is Decision chain, the multi-person, multi-department path—not one signer.

**How do the two stage maps work together?** They describe buyer tasks and seller actions on separate axes. Use customer behavior evidence to locate the buyer stage, then choose the seller action. Do not force one-to-one alignment.

**What comes first in negotiation?** Scope and responsibilities, then timing, then price. Prepare BATNA, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. Each concession should exchange for a verifiable commitment.

**What is one small action for tomorrow?** Refill a live cross-category or new-product account against the seven PRTABCD items. Mark known, unknown, source, and next question. The gap is often not price authority but embracer and decision-chain information.
