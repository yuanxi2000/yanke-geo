Capacity investment is an early buying signal: equipment, tooling, materials, engineering needs come later, but spend is published. RMB 1.007 billion two-year robotics precision metal components base signals; ask what disclosure enables.

## What the announcement states, and where it stops

Disclosed: RMB1.007bn two-year robotics metal components base, funded by RMB220m over-raised and RMB787m own/self-raised. After-tax IRR 17.34%, static payback 7.12 years; mid-2026 revenue RMB589m, parent net RMB73.1m.

What is absent matters equally. There are no named suppliers, no defined procurement packages, no named selection owner, and no statement of who holds approval authority. The announcement records a company-level investment decision. It does not describe the state of any specific purchasing task, and it should not be read as though it does.

## Begin with the customer's own arithmetic

The most useful line is not the investment total. It is that the customer has published an evaluation standard for the project in investment language: return and payback. Once that is on the record, a supplier leading with product parameters is arguing outside the customer's own table.

The value capability calls for exactly this translation: express the product or service in the customer's terms of profit, investment, and operating value. On a new precision metal components base, the customer cares whether the line reaches planned output on schedule, where unit cost lands, and how quickly invested capital returns. Every quotation should map to one of those three.

### Turning the customer's numbers into an opening statement

A three-part statement works as an opener. Restate the calculation basis the customer published. State which cost or output items your proposal affects. Ask the customer to confirm who owns the assumptions. It promises nothing; it lets the customer judge, with their own numbers, whether the conversation is worth continuing.

IRR 17.34% and 7.12-year payback are customer figures under its assumptions; no supplier can commit. Explain cost/output lines; never present customer's calculation as yours.

Prepare three sets of numbers before the first technical meeting:

- The published investment and return basis: RMB 1.007 billion total investment, 17.34% after-tax IRR, 7.12-year after-tax static payback period.
- The current operating scale: RMB 589 million in revenue and RMB 73.1 million in net profit for the mid-2026 period cited in the source.
- The cost or output items your proposal can affect, and the assumption behind each one that only the customer can confirm.

## Read the stage from behaviour, not pipeline percentages

Purchasing has seven stages; sales five—not parallel but many-to-many. Announcement confirms corporate investment approval, not whether viable solution, responsibility, or build-versus-buy exists.

Progress can only be judged from customer behaviour: an invitation to a technical exchange, a request for capacity and lead-time documentation, a site visit, commercial terms placed on the agenda. Visit counts, verbal reassurances, and stage percentages in a tracking system cannot substitute for those.

Over a two-year build, demand is released in batches rather than all at once. Watch four categories of signal:

- Technical contact: the customer initiates discussions with process or equipment engineers.
- Validation requirements: the customer asks for samples, parameters, or line-fit documentation.
- Delivery conditions: the customer starts discussing lead times, acceptance standards, and ramp-up cadence.
- Commercial actions: price, payment, and contract terms enter the agenda.

## Map the decision landscape with PRTABCD

PRTABCD: record known, unknown, source, next question. Announcement fills T (timing/milestones) and part B (budget): RMB1.007bn total, RMB220m over-raised and RMB787m own/self-raised.

A is Arm, the embracer in project, process, or equipment functions. D is Decision, the multi-department chain through technical, purchasing, finance. Separate known from unknown: component selection, contract approval, planned-output responsibility need verification.

## Design the first round of questions

SPIN moves a needs investigation through situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions, taking a customer from unspoken dissatisfaction to an explicit requirement. A new base suits the sequence: confirm the target and cadence, find the bottleneck, amplify what the bottleneck costs, then let the customer state the payoff of solving it.

- When is the base scheduled to move into equipment installation and commissioning, and what is the target output cadence?
- Which existing process or supply step is most likely to slow ramp-up?
- If that step slips, what happens to output and unit cost?
- Once it is resolved, how far can ramp-up time and per-unit cost improve?

The order should not be rearranged. Asking about implication before the situation is clear reads as exaggeration; asking about payoff before implication gives the customer no reason to answer precisely.

## Negotiation: time, bottom line, alternatives

The negotiation capability asks for concessions and condition exchanges to be designed against an explicit reading of power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. A two-year construction period means hard milestones for delivery, acceptance, and ramp-up, so a supplier's delivery capability is itself leverage.

BATNA: best no-deal fallback. Prepare alternatives, authority. Customer can switch suppliers; you can place capacity elsewhere. One sheet trades concessions. Use published figures/quoted terms, not undisclosed budget.

## Action checklist

- Build the one-page reconciliation sheet: published investment and return basis, the lines your proposal touches, and the assumptions the customer must confirm.
- Complete a PRTABCD sheet, with A and D rows stating known, unknown, source, and next question.
- Place the account on the dual-view funnel, marking its position in both the seven-stage purchasing process and the five-stage sales process.
- Write the behavioural evidence that justifies each position — what the customer has actually done, not what was said.
- Prepare the four SPIN questions in order and decide which answer triggers which next action.
- Draft the three-part opening statement so it restates the customer's basis without promising the customer's numbers.
- Assign an owner to each of the four demand signals: technical, validation, delivery, commercial.
- Write the BATNA table before the first commercial meeting.
- Keep a cannot-conclude list — supplier selection, internal advocates, budget split — and turn each entry into a question.

## FAQ

**What is the practical value of this announcement?** It moves the purchasing window forward onto the table. A RMB 1.007 billion investment over two years means equipment, tooling, materials, and engineering services will be needed at different points. A seller can plan contact timing around that, but who owns selection and how budget is split still has to be confirmed through questions and behavioural evidence.

**Why read it through the value capability?** Because the customer published an investment calculation: 17.34% after-tax IRR and a 7.12-year after-tax static payback period. If a seller's language maps to the assumptions behind those figures, the conversation sits inside the customer's own evaluation table. Product parameters alone sit outside it.

**How does the dual-view funnel apply?** It places the seven purchasing stages and five sales stages on one map. The announcement shows the corporate investment decision; the seller must still determine which purchasing task is live — whether a solution is confirmed, whether responsibility is assigned, whether build-versus-buy has been compared. Each judgement needs behavioural evidence.

**What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?** A is fixed as Arm, the embracer — who in the customer organisation is pulling for you. D is fixed as Decision — the multi-person, multi-department chain. On a new base, embracers may sit in the project team or process function while the decision chain runs across technical, purchasing, and finance.

**Can a seller commit to the 17.34% figure?** No. It is a project-level after-tax IRR calculated from assumptions about investment, capacity, price, and cost. The seller can describe which cost or output items a proposal affects and ask the project or finance owner to confirm the assumptions. Claiming the customer's number as an outcome invites a dispute later.

**What can this announcement not support?** It cannot support the conclusion that a specific procurement has started, that a supplier has been chosen, or that a particular department is driving a selection. It records a company-level investment decision and a funding plan. Selection ownership, contract approval, and budget allocation remain unverified.
