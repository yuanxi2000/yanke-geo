An order disclosure gives a sales team something concrete to examine. It does not, by itself, establish a secure customer relationship, a winning solution or dependable delivery. Those conclusions require separate evidence.

The verified source concerns Montage Technology and its disclosed outstanding orders for interconnect chips. It does not establish transaction counterparties, specific dates or internal decision processes. The useful sales lesson is therefore a method: separate what the customer has demonstrably done from what the team still needs to verify.

For sales leaders, an order is evidence of customer behavior; delivery tests whether commitments are fulfilled. The following guide turns that distinction into a practical review process using the five capabilities of PSS5.

## Start with a boundary around the facts

Before discussing opportunity maturity, write down the disclosed entity and order fact. Keep every unsupported detail in an explicit unknown category. Do not supply a buyer's identity, assume budget approval or reconstruct internal meetings from the existence of an order.

For each opportunity, maintain three evidence columns:

- **Customer action:** What has the customer actually confirmed or committed to?
- **Original evidence:** What record supports that interpretation?
- **Next decision question:** What information must be verified before the stage assessment changes?

An opportunity with all three columns empty should not enter the forecast. A more advanced stage assessment requires both customer behavior evidence and a named owner. Enthusiasm about an announcement cannot substitute for either.

Record the customer's original wording, when it was recorded and the setting in which the confirmation occurred. A statement about scope supports a scope judgment. It should not silently become evidence that budget, acceptance responsibility and delivery timing are also settled.

## Check both sides of the buying process

The systems capability uses two perspectives: five seller stages and seven customer purchasing stages. These describe different tasks, so they should not be forced into a one-to-one mapping.

The seller stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation and closing. The customer stages are:

1. Recognizing a problem.
2. Assessing its value.
3. Confirming that a solution exists.
4. Establishing responsibility.
5. Deciding whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Selecting a solution and supplier.
7. Making commitments and signing.

Together, these form a seven-by-five review grid. Its purpose is to test whether the seller's activity serves the customer's current decision task. A proposal presentation is a seller action; it does not prove that the customer has assigned responsibility for the outcome.

For the Montage Technology disclosure, one possible hypothesis is that the purchasing process has reached a point requiring delivery commitments. That remains an analytical hypothesis, not a statement about the company's internal actions.

### Ask for the behavior that supports the stage

In your own accounts, ask who confirmed the scope, who accepted responsibility for the result and who established time and budget boundaries. Then check whether commitments, acceptance criteria and responsibilities are documented. Advance the assessment only as far as those records support.

## Give each capability a distinct job

The five capabilities provide complementary questions. Keeping their purposes distinct helps turn a broad account discussion into verifiable work.

| Capability | Review question | Evidence or action to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Systems | Does our activity match the customer's purchasing task? | A stage assessment supported by customer behavior |
| Value | What business result does the purchase support? | An agreed calculation and validation owner |
| Relationships | How does the decision chain work? | Sourced information about participants and responsibilities |
| Questioning | Which consequential unknown needs an answer? | A focused question tied to the current decision |
| Negotiation | Which conditions still require agreement? | Written alternatives, limits, authority and exchange terms |

Use the table to assign work. Each question should lead to an observable action, such as obtaining written scope confirmation or agreeing who will validate a value calculation.

## Translate the order into business value

An order indicates a transaction or delivery arrangement. Value analysis asks how that arrangement connects to the customer's revenue, costs, profit or investment. Product functionality alone does not answer that question.

Ask the account team to document four things:

- Which revenue or cost item the purchase affects.
- How the proposed improvement will be measured.
- Who will validate the calculation.
- When the result can be reviewed.

Use customer-provided data, public contract terms or a calculation sheet confirmed by both parties. Do not add unsupported percentages, payback periods, monetary amounts or industry statistics to make the case appear complete.

When data is missing, agree on measurement definitions, the person responsible for obtaining the data and the review timing. The immediate task is to establish a credible basis for calculation.

If the customer focuses on unit price, broaden the discussion to total cost, delivery risk and operating results. Ask which cost matters most and who can verify an improvement. A joint calculation is useful only when the customer confirms its assumptions.

## Map the decision chain and question the gaps

A list of senior contacts is only a starting point for relationship analysis. PRTABCD organizes seven categories of decision information:

- **P — Past:** Relevant history and previous experience.
- **R — Requirements:** The customer's needs.
- **T — Time:** Timing and key milestones.
- **A — Arm:** Who within the customer organization embraces or supports your involvement.
- **B — Budget:** Budget information and boundaries.
- **C — Competition:** Competitors and alternative approaches.
- **D — Decision:** The decision chain across people and departments.

For every category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source and the next question. A job title does not establish a person's actual role in this purchase.

Keep A and D separate. Arm concerns support within the organization; Decision concerns how the decision involves multiple participants. Neither should be inferred from access to one influential contact.

Use SPIN questions—situation, problem, implication and need-payoff—to explore the gaps. Begin with the business problem the purchase must solve. Explore which departments would be affected by a delivery delay. Establish who will sign off on success criteria.

Select questions that fit the current purchasing task. A focused discussion should clarify the decision without turning into a pressured sequence of interrogations.

## Prepare for negotiation after the order

An order disclosure does not establish that negotiation is finished. Delivery scope, acceptance standards, payment milestones and risk allocation may still require exchanges of conditions.

Before a negotiation, write down the executable alternatives, minimum acceptable terms, decision authority and records needed. Review the balance of power, available time, information and alternatives. These considerations help the team identify which commitments it can make.

BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best available action if agreement cannot be reached. It should describe an executable option, not a threat aimed at the customer.

Prepare the sequence of exchanges involving scope, payment, timing and risk. Where a price concession is considered, identify the corresponding condition to seek. Unclear authority makes it easier to concede price simply to keep the process moving.

## Run an actionable weekly checklist

Keep the weekly review centered on four items: the customer's current purchasing stage, supporting behavior evidence, consequential PRTABCD gaps and the written confirmation sought next week. Give each action a named owner and a date.

- [ ] State the verified order fact and separate all assumptions.
- [ ] Record who confirmed scope, budget boundaries or acceptance responsibility, including wording and timing.
- [ ] Check that seller activity matches the customer's current decision task.
- [ ] Identify high-impact gaps in the decision information.
- [ ] Use confirmed data for value calculations; assign responsibility for missing inputs.
- [ ] Document alternatives, limits, authority and proposed negotiation exchanges.
- [ ] Assign the next written confirmation to a person and deadline.

Where evidence is absent, lower the assessment or keep the item pending verification. Review the records rather than relying on visit counts or the salesperson's confidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an outstanding order prove the opportunity is mature?

It establishes an order-related fact. It does not independently verify budget decisions, decision-chain coverage or acceptance responsibility. Check customer behavior against the purchasing stages before changing the assessment.

### What should a sales leader inspect first?

Start with the boundary of the public disclosure. Then inspect the evidence behind scope, responsibilities and acceptance criteria. Keep unsupported interpretations visible as questions.

### What if the customer will discuss only price?

Ask which total-cost or operating issue matters most, then agree on data and validation responsibility. If discussing a concession, connect it to scope, payment or timing conditions.

### Can missing value data be replaced with estimates?

Do not present unsupported estimates as established value. Agree on the measurement basis and obtain customer-provided or jointly confirmed inputs before judging the size of the benefit.

### How should an incomplete account record affect the forecast?

An entirely empty evidence record should not enter the forecast. More optimistic assessments require customer behavior evidence, ownership and dated actions to resolve material unknowns.
