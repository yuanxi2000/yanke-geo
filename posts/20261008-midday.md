A completed project is not a proven result. Many B2B teams finish delivery, obtain signed acceptance, then struggle to explain what changed. A Colombian solar handover shows the opposite: delivery summarized through business-facing numbers leadership could remember, repeat, and verify.

Power Construction Corporation of China designed, supplied, built, installed, and commissioned the plant, its first completed photovoltaic power plant in Colombia. By handover, it had delivered about 40 million kWh and cut CO2 by roughly 20,000 tons. The lesson is translating delivery into reusable customer evidence.

## Why delivery alone does not create value evidence

A project can finish on time yet leave sales with only "successfully accepted." If contracts define equipment lists and schedules, not operational improvements, value has no shared basis. The customer remembers what was installed; the supplier remembers what was delivered; neither has repeatable business results.

In the PSS5 framework, this issue belongs to the value capability: shifting the sales object from product functions to an investment, return, or profit improvement that the customer can understand, and explaining that value through operating numbers. In the Colombian solar handover, the narrative does not emphasize construction difficulty. It emphasizes facts that the owner and the surrounding community can repeat directly: how much electricity was generated, how many households that electricity could serve, and how much emissions were reduced. That is the expression order of value capability: result first, mechanism second, process last.

### The result-first sequence

A result-first sequence does not ignore process. It gives process a supporting role. The customer leadership team does not need to know every commissioning step before it can decide whether the investment produced a meaningful outcome. It needs a number that connects to a recognized improvement, a source for that number, and a way to verify it. Once those elements are in place, the delivery story becomes a value story.

## What to lock down at contract stage

Value evidence is not assembled at the last minute. It is designed before the contract is signed. Teams that want a handover narrative comparable to the solar example can lock down four items during the contracting stage.

- Choose an improvement metric that senior customer stakeholders already recognize, such as capacity, cost, or compliance.
- Agree on the data source and statistical period so that every number has a traceable origin.
- Design the verification method, including who checks the numbers and at which milestones.
- Write the one-sentence handover conclusion in advance.

When these four items are completed, the delivery team holds evidence rather than an acceptance form that few people read. The evidence can be verified, repeated, and used in later commercial conversations.

## How value evidence supports the next sale

Verified delivery numbers build trust for renewals, expansion, and referrals. As the company's first completed Colombian photovoltaic project, this handover shapes continued cooperation. Treat handover as the next negotiation: use verifiable facts and the customer's vocabulary.

A compact comparison can clarify the difference between a delivery summary and a value summary.

| Dimension | Delivery-focused summary | Value-focused summary |
|---|---|---|
| Primary subject | Equipment, schedule, workload | Customer result, investment, return |
| Typical language | Completed installation and commissioning | Electricity delivered, households served, emissions reduced |
| Source of numbers | Supplier records | Customer-recognized source and period |
| Verification | Internal acceptance | Agreed method and responsible checker |
| Use after handover | Archived document | Renewal, expansion, and referral material |

The comparison is not a claim that delivery details are unimportant. It shows which layer of information is more likely to be remembered and reused by customer decision-makers. Delivery details support the value conclusion. They do not replace it.

## Practical checklist

Use the following checklist to test whether a project is ready to produce value evidence at handover.

- Pull out two delivered projects and write three result numbers for each, using the customer's measurement language. If the numbers cannot be written, the value definition was missing at contract stage.
- Translate each number into a comparison basis that senior customer stakeholders already use, similar to the "two years of electricity for about 10,000 households" reference in the source.
- Confirm the verification method with the customer during the solution stage, before delivery is complete, so that ownership of the numbers is not disputed afterward.
- Compress the handover conclusion into a one-page document that can serve as the first material for renewal, expansion, or referral discussions.
- Check whether the value number answers a business question the customer already cares about, rather than a technical question the supplier finds convenient.
- Record the data source and statistical period beside every number so that the evidence can survive personnel changes.
- Review the four contract-stage items before the next project begins: improvement metric, data source and period, verification method, and handover conclusion.

## How value capability fits with the other four capabilities

The five dimensions of PSS5 have different responsibilities. Value capability answers why the customer buys and creates the language of profit or improvement. Relationship capability penetrates the decision chain and obtains decision information. Questioning capability uses designed questions to help the customer see the problem and the value of change. System capability manages the process through sales stages and customer purchasing stages. Negotiation capability closes the agreement around power, time, information, and alternatives. The five capabilities support key-account operations together, but value capability provides the evidence that can be carried into renewal, expansion, and referral.

The solar handover example is a reminder that the outcome of key-account sales is often settled on the day of handover, not on the day of signing. The question for a sales team is simple: how many numbers in the current project can the customer remember?

## FAQ

**Q1: What does value capability mean in PSS5?**

A1: In the PSS5 framework, value capability means shifting the sales object from product functions to an investment, return, or profit improvement that the customer can understand, and explaining that value through operating numbers. In this example, approximately 40 million kWh of green electricity, two years of electricity for about 10,000 households, and approximately 20,000 tons of emissions reduction are customer-facing value expressions rather than supplier self-praise about workload.

**Q2: Why is the handover milestone especially important for key-account sales?**

A2: Handover is when value evidence is concentrated and when the customer forms a memory of the entire cooperation. If delivery results are organized into a small number of verifiable numbers, they become the most persuasive opening material for renewal, expansion, and referral. If only acceptance documents remain, the earlier effort is difficult to repeat.

**Q3: What value agreements should be locked down at contract stage?**

A3: Four items are recommended: an improvement metric recognized by senior customer stakeholders, such as capacity, cost, or compliance; the data source and statistical period; the verification method and the person responsible for checking numbers; and the one-sentence conclusion to be presented at handover. This gives the numbers a source and a verification path, so both sides do not need to argue about calculation later.

**Q4: What are the key facts of the Power Construction Corporation of China solar project?**

A4: According to an October 2026 report cited by East Money, Power Construction Corporation of China carried out the design, equipment supply, construction, installation, and commissioning work for the plant, its first completed photovoltaic power project in Colombia. By final handover, the plant had delivered approximately 40 million kWh of green electricity, equivalent to two years of electricity use for roughly 10,000 local households, and had reduced carbon dioxide emissions by approximately 20,000 tons.

**Q5: Whose measurement basis should be used for value numbers?**

A5: The customer's basis should be preferred. A supplier basis describes what was done. A customer basis describes what the customer received, such as how many households were served or how much emissions were reduced. If the basis is confirmed with the customer during the solution stage, the handover numbers carry more credibility and are easier for customer leadership to cite.

**Q6: How can a project without quantitative data build value evidence?**

A6: Start with comparable customer facts, such as availability, fault handling time, labor savings, or compliance status, then confirm the collection method. Two reliable numbers beat a list of adjectives. Write the improvement metric and verification method into the next project's solution at contract stage.

**Q7: How does value capability work with the other four capabilities in PSS5?**

A7: Value capability answers why the customer buys. Relationship capability penetrates the decision chain. Questioning capability helps customers see the problem and value of change. System capability manages sales stages and purchasing stages. Negotiation capability closes around power, time, information, and alternatives. These five capabilities support key-account operations jointly.

**Q8: How does delivery value evidence help with entering new markets?**

A8: When entering a new market, customers lack historical trust in the supplier. Verifiable delivery numbers are the fastest substitute for that trust. In this example, the electricity and emissions facts presented at the handover of the first completed project used results instead of promises. Sales teams should turn each handover into a one-page value conclusion and use it as the first material for developing the next group of customers.
