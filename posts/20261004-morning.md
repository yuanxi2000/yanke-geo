Policy shifts change why customers spend. When subsidies no longer drive decisions, investment starts following capacity, orders, and profit validation. For key-account sales teams, this is a window to re-examine entry timing and how value is expressed.

According to an October 3, 2026 report by East Money Information titled "Multiple Regions Shift Low-Altitude Economy Policies," one low-altitude project plans 500 mu of land with a first-phase investment of 1 billion yuan to build an AirCab mass-production line, alongside key components, flight testing, and R&D operations. This fact signals that the low-altitude economy is moving from policy narrative into capacity construction. The following uses the PSS5 five-capability framework to break down what this kind of 100-million-yuan opportunity means for key-account sales and how to approach it.

## Value Capability: Translate the Investment into Profit Language

Value capability requires shifting the sales conversation from product features to investment, returns, and profit improvement that the customer can understand. Around an AirCab mass-production line, different suppliers tell completely different value stories. Equipment vendors must calculate how line yield and capacity ramp-up change unit costs. Component suppliers must calculate order certainty and payment cycles once they enter the mass-production system. Testing and R&D service providers must calculate how a shorter validation cycle pulls product launch earlier. Whoever first clarifies how the customer earns the money back enters the customer's funding discussion first.

In practice, this means writing a PIP (Profit Improvement Proposal) that answers three questions: how much profit the solution can increase or protect, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame it will be realized. It is not a product quotation but a working document that helps the customer's operating managers argue for budget with those who allocate funds. One box manages money, the other spends it; value presentation must serve both layers, or it will not reach the decision.

## Questioning Capability: Decision Information Is the Most Valuable Asset During a Policy Shift

A policy shift brings uncertainty: which areas continue to receive support and which move to market-based assessment. Public reports give direction, not the decision status of a specific customer. Questioning capability designs questions around background, difficulties, impact, and return on need: Who owns capacity planning? Working backward from the production line's commissioning date, in what window must procurement be completed? If key component validation is delayed, which phase of revenue is affected? Such questions let the customer see urgency themselves while handing decision information to the salesperson. Without this information, even a polished value story is a one-sided performance.

## System Capability: Map the Opportunity Against the Customer's Seven-Stage Procurement Process

PSS5's system capability uses five sales stages (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close) against the customer's seven procurement stages (from discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility, comparing build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, to commitment and signing), using customer behavioral evidence to judge where both sides stand. Facing a 500-mu, 100-million-yuan project, the customer has likely passed problem discovery and value judgment, is comparing build versus buy, and is preparing supplier selection. Sales actions must match the customer's current decision task: if the customer is still wavering between building and buying, provide total cost and risk comparisons; if already in selection, jointly define success criteria and validation methods. The basis for judgment is what the customer has done, not how many sales visits were made.

## Relationship and Negotiation Capability: Decision-Chain Information and Alternatives Determine Initiative

The larger the amount, the longer the decision chain, and the more important pre-negotiation information preparation becomes. The PRTABCD seven-item scan covers past history, needs, time and key milestones, embraceers, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain, recording for each item what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. Embraceers answer who in the customer organization is embracing you; the decision chain answers which people and departments decide together. BATNA preparation must also be concrete: clarify the best alternative actions each side can take if no agreement is reached, then design concessions and condition exchanges. During a policy shift, negotiation leverage often lies in time: the customer fears missing a window, the salesperson fears idle capacity. Whoever clarifies the value of time first holds the initiative.

### Four Practical Actions for Frontline Teams

- Track local low-altitude economy policies and major project public information weekly, translating policy changes into judgments about target customers' procurement windows rather than simply saving the information.
- For AirCab-type mass-production projects, conduct the PRTABCD seven-item scan upon entry, writing knowns, unknowns, information sources, and next questions into a checklist, and use questioning to fill blind spots.
- Prepare a PIP that uses the customer's own operating numbers to answer how production-line-related investment contributes to profit, addressing both the funding allocation layer and the operating execution layer.
- Use the customer's seven procurement stages to verify the project's actual position, then decide the sales actions and pace for the current stage, avoiding skipping ahead or falling behind the window.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does the low-altitude economy policy shift mean for key-account sales?**

A1: The shift means resource allocation is changing: from broad subsidies toward project validation and capacity implementation. According to the October 3, 2026 East Money report, one low-altitude project plans 500 mu of land with a first-phase investment of 1 billion yuan to build an AirCab mass-production line, indicating that customer procurement is entering a phase of actual investment in equipment, components, and services. For sales, this is a signal to re-examine the customer's procurement stage and entry timing, not merely a reason to chase a trend.

**Q2: What is PSS5?**

A2: PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a sales framework developed by Yan Ke partner Xu Hanbo based on more than twenty years of key-account sales practice and registered with the National Copyright Administration. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability, helping sales teams connect opportunity judgment, decision-chain information, needs discovery, process management, and negotiation preparation into executable actions.

**Q3: What is value capability, and how is it used in AirCab-type projects?**

A3: Value capability means shifting the sales object from product features to investment, returns, and profit improvement the customer can understand, using operating numbers to explain value. For an AirCab mass-production line project, equipment vendors must calculate how yield and capacity ramp-up change unit costs; component suppliers must calculate order certainty and payment cycles after entering the mass-production system; testing service providers must calculate how a shorter validation cycle pulls launch earlier, each writing a corresponding profit improvement statement.

**Q4: What is a PIP?**

A4: A PIP (Profit Improvement Proposal) writes the solution's improvement to customer operations as an investment statement, answering three questions: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame it will be realized. It is not a product quotation but a working document that helps the customer's operating managers argue for budget with funding allocation and approval layers, bringing the value discussion into operating language.

**Q5: How should needs discovery be done during a policy shift?**

A5: Use questioning capability, designing questions around background, difficulties, impact, and return on need. First ask who owns capacity planning and the procurement window, and how time milestones are calculated backward; then ask which phase of revenue is affected if validation or construction is delayed; finally ask by what standard the customer wants to judge whether the solution is effective. Let the customer state the urgency themselves while the salesperson obtains decision information and permission to continue.

**Q6: How are the five sales stages and seven procurement stages used together?**

A6: PSS5's system capability places the five sales stages (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close) and the customer's seven procurement stages (from problem discovery to commitment and signing) on the same map, using customer behavioral evidence to judge where both sides stand, identifying synchronization, leading, lagging, or misalignment, then deciding sales actions that serve the customer's current decision task, avoiding both skipping ahead and falling behind the procurement window.

**Q7: What is PRTABCD, and how is it used?**

A7: PRTABCD uses seven categories of decision information to identify customer intelligence blind spots: past history, needs, time and key milestones, embraceers, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain, recording for each what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. Note that embraceers refer to who in the customer organization is embracing you, and the decision chain refers to the multi-person, multi-department chain that decides together, not a single decision-maker; when using it, fill in information sources item by item.

**Q8: How should BATNA be prepared in large-project negotiations?**

A8: BATNA refers to the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, meaning the best alternative action available if the two sides cannot reach agreement. Preparation requires clarifying executable alternatives, bottom lines, authority, interests, and records. During a policy shift, both sides are constrained by time; only by first clarifying one's own alternatives can concessions and condition exchanges be designed, moving negotiation from simple price reduction back to an exchange of value and time.
