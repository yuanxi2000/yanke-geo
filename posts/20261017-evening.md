An approval certificate looks like a regulatory event. For a key-account seller, it is better read as a customer signal. The source article uses Huasen Pharmaceutical's approval of two specifications of orlistat capsules as an exercise. The certificate belongs to the customer; the seller's task is to anticipate the portfolio, channel, and people changes that may follow. If you cannot answer three questions - which resources the customer may move, how the product line may be arranged, and whose targets may be increased - congratulations are only congratulations.

## Start with the Customer's Operating Equation

Value capability begins by turning the approval into the customer's business language. The source notes that the public rationale points in a clear direction: enrich the metabolic-disease product pipeline and provide a basis for expanding retail pharmacies, pharmaceutical e-commerce, and other out-of-hospital markets. The seller should continue the calculation: how the two specifications divide roles, which out-of-hospital channels enter first, and where stocking and promotion money comes from.

Write a one-page PIP - a profit improvement plan. It is not a quotation; it is a document for the customer's internal business case. It should answer how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and over what time frame. When customer data is unavailable, state assumptions and verification items. Do not present an industry average as the customer's number.

- Which buyer group and shelf does each new specification map to?
- Which operating step does our proposal affect, and can the improvement be expressed as revenue, cost, or payback?
- Who inside the customer may receive added targets, and what drives that person to push procurement?

## Turn Congratulations into Four Kinds of Questions

SPIN organizes needs discovery into situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions. When a customer receives a new product, a vague offer of support returns the information burden to the customer. Ask in sequence:

- Situation: How far does out-of-hospital coverage reach now, and how will the two specifications divide roles?
- Problem: If price bands and display compete in the same channel, where will the friction appear?
- Implication: If stocking rhythm is misaligned, what happens to inventory, payment terms, and promotion spending?
- Need-payoff: If specification roles and channel timing are clarified first, what can be saved or gained?

## Map the Relationship Beyond One Contact

PRTABCD is a seven-item scan of decision information: past information, need, timing and key milestones, arm or champion, budget, competition and alternatives, and decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. This converts assumptions into verification. A is Arm, the person or group embracing you. D is Decision, a multi-person, multi-department chain, not a single decision maker.

MBVS adds four hypotheses to test with a key contact: motivation, benchmark, vision, and solution. All four remain hypotheses. Job titles and model-based inference should not be written as customer facts. The person you think should push the purchase is exactly the person to verify in a real conversation.

## Locate the Account on a Two-View Funnel

Customer purchase has seven stages: problem recognition; value judgment; confirmation that a solution exists; confirmation of ownership; build or buy; solution and supplier selection; commitment and signing. Sales has five stages: contact; needs; solution; negotiation; close. Put both axes on one map and compare them.

### A Two-View Comparison

| View | Stages | What the approval changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer decision | Problem recognition; value judgment; confirmation a solution exists; ownership; build or buy; solution and supplier selection; commitment and signing | It may increase certainty on the confirmation-a-solution-exists side, but buying, ownership, and timing remain customer decisions. |
| Sales motion | Contact; needs; solution; negotiation; close | The seller still has to match each action to the customer's current decision task and look for behavioral evidence. |

The two axes are many-to-many, not one-to-one. For the Huasen example, the approval may raise the customer's certainty that a solution exists, but whether to outsource, who owns the result, and when to sign are still customer decision tasks. Judge progress only by customer behavior: a clear next-step date, an internal responsible person brought into the same discussion, and a request for verifiable materials. Visit counts and CRM percentages are not substitutes. The source's three closing moves are for identifying false stalls and earning the next real step, not for pressure scripts.

## Prepare Alternatives Before Concessions

BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best action you can take if no deal is reached. Prepare by writing the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. If the customer has a new product and is comparing suppliers, a specific alternative gives you room to exchange a concession for scope, timing, payment, or risk terms. Price does not have to be the only concession.

## Turn the Five Capabilities into One Battle Sheet

Bring the work into one page: value capability captures the customer's operating account; questioning lists the SPIN questions; relationship fills PRTABCD gaps; system marks the account's place on the seven-by-five map; negotiation prepares alternatives and exchange conditions. The five dimensions are useful only when each leaves a checkable trace. A filled sheet turns a news item into an executable action.

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify the portfolio, channel, and target changes the approval may trigger.
- Draft a one-page PIP with mechanism, profit effect, time frame, assumptions, and verification items.
- Prepare situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions for the two specifications.
- Fill PRTABCD with known, unknown, source, and next question.
- Test MBVS hypotheses in a real conversation; do not treat titles as facts.
- Place the account on the customer-seven and sales-five map; name the customer's current decision task.
- Collect behavioral evidence: next-step date, internal owner, and verifiable material request.
- Prepare BATNA and the terms you would trade for a concession.
- Convert the above into a one-page battle sheet with actions and evidence.

## FAQ

Q1: Why analyze a drug registration certificate?

A1: The certificate is the customer's qualification progress. The seller's opening is the resource action that follows: portfolio, channel, and targets. The source article, citing an East Money report, says Huasen Pharmaceutical's approval of two orlistat capsule specifications was described publicly as enriching the metabolic-disease pipeline and providing a basis for retail pharmacy, pharmaceutical e-commerce, and other out-of-hospital expansion. The seller's conversation should connect to those purchase and investment judgments.

Q2: What is the first value-capability step?

A2: Translate the approval into the customer's operating language: which operating step is affected, what revenue or cost may change, and what payback period is plausible. The first artifact can be a one-page PIP covering profit added or protected, mechanism, and time frame. Feature lists do not do that work.

Q3: How should SPIN be used after a new product arrives?

A3: Move from situation to problem, implication, and need-payoff. Confirm channel coverage and specification roles; look for price-band, display, or buyer-group conflict in the same channel; make the inventory, payment-term, and promotion effects of misaligned stocking explicit; then let the customer state what clear division and timing would save or gain. The sequence helps move from an implicit concern to a defined need.

Q4: What is the difference between A and D in PRTABCD?

A4: A is Arm, the person or group embracing your proposal and willing to speak for it. D is Decision, the multi-person, multi-department chain, not one decision maker. They are not interchangeable. The scan also covers past information, need, timing and key milestones, budget, and competition or alternatives.

Q5: How do the seven customer stages and five sales stages work together?

A5: They are a many-to-many comparison, not a fixed step-by-step match. The customer decides when a problem is recognized, when value is judged, when a solution is confirmed, who owns the result, whether to build or buy, which supplier is selected, and when to sign. The seller's contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close actions should be chosen against that current customer task.

Q6: How can a seller tell whether progress is real?

A6: Look for customer behavior: a clear next-step time, an internal owner and actual user in the same discussion, a request for verifiable materials, or access to budget and decision-chain stakeholders. Visit frequency and CRM percentages do not replace those signals. Closing moves should identify false stalls and secure the next real step, not force a script.

Q7: How should BATNA be prepared?

A7: Write the best executable alternative if no agreement is reached, plus your bottom line, authority, interests, and record. With a new product and multiple supplier comparisons, a concrete alternative lets a concession be exchanged for scope, timing, payment, or risk terms. That keeps price from becoming the only variable.
