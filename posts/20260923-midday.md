China–ASEAN green cooperation is often reported as energy news. For sales teams, it is better read as a map of cross-border key-account projects: who buys, why they buy, where the money comes from, and how the decision chain moves. PSS5 turns those four questions into concrete scanning actions.

## What the Reported Projects Signal

Public reporting describes a set of projects involving China Energy Engineering Group Gezhouba and its affiliated enterprises: the Upper Cisokan pumped-storage project in Indonesia, the Daday River hydropower station in Cambodia, the Tengeh Reservoir floating solar project in Singapore, and the Gia Lai wind project in Vietnam. These span four countries and several technology types—pumped storage, hydropower, floating solar, and wind—and share one purpose: adding clean electricity supply locally.

For a sales perspective, the signal is direct: cross-border clean energy megaprojects are a real, recurring market, not a concept. The reporting does not disclose contract values or signing dates, so the discussion below stays at the level of method and does not invent figures.

## Value Capability: Translating a Power Plant into the Host Country's Business Case

When a host-country buyer purchases a pumped-storage plant or a floating solar array, they are not looking at equipment parameters. They are looking at operational improvement: Is power supply stable? Is the tariff acceptable? How much employment and industrial pull does it create? Can emissions commitments be met? How long until capital is recovered?

The seller's job is to translate engineering capability into the language of profit and business numbers that a host country's finance and energy ministries can sign off on. Pumped storage maps to grid peak-shaving capacity; floating solar maps to generation growth under land constraints. Whoever helps the customer settle that account first upgrades the proposal from a quotation to an investment case.

### A Practical Translation Exercise

- Identify which operational processes the solution touches.
- Quantify the improvement in operational terms.
- Convert that into revenue, cost, profit, or payback language.
- Test whether the result reads like an investment case rather than a product quote.

## Relationship and Questioning Capability: Penetrating a Multi-Layer Decision Chain

The hard part of relationship capability in cross-border energy projects is that the customer is not one company. It is a decision chain woven from the host country's energy ministry, grid company, financiers, and local partners.

Use the PRTABCD seven-item scan as a table to fill in item by item:

- P: past project record
- R: requirement priorities
- T: financing windows and grid-connection milestones
- A: advocates—who inside the customer organization speaks for you
- B: budget sources
- C: competitors and local alternatives
- D: the multi-department participants on the decision chain

A and D deserve extra effort. A tells you who is embracing you; D tells you who actually decides. Questioning capability is then used to test assumptions. Design four question types around background, difficulties, impact, and required return, so the customer states the cost of the power gap themselves rather than the seller drawing the conclusion for them.

## System Capability: Managing Very Long Cycles with a Dual-View Funnel

Cross-border power stations often take years from first contact to signature. The sales funnel manages the seller's progress through five stages: contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, and closing. The customer side advances through seven decision tasks: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, deciding build-or-buy, selecting solution and supplier, and committing and signing.

Place both axes on the same dual-view funnel and judge position by customer behavioral evidence. If the host country has formed a dedicated working group, responsibility is moving. If a feasibility study has started, the customer has reached the stage of confirming a solution exists. No matter how busy the seller is, if the customer's task has not advanced, quoting out of sequence is a mistake.

## Negotiation Capability and an Action Checklist

Negotiation capability in these projects revolves around power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives. Buyers usually have several international contractors to choose from—that is the buyer's BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Sellers should prepare their own executable alternatives, bottom lines, and authority in advance, to avoid being forced into unilateral concessions on exchange rates, payment terms, or localization ratios.

Condense the analysis above into three actionable items:

1. After every cross-border customer contact, update the PRTABCD intelligence table, highlighting uncovered advocates and decision-chain departments.
2. At the solution stage, do not hand over a quotation first. Define success criteria jointly and turn value into business numbers.
3. Before negotiation, write out your alternatives, bottom lines, and authorized scope. Concessions must be exchanged for conditions.

China–ASEAN green transition will continue to release project opportunities. Those opportunities belong to teams that do their intelligence, value, process, and negotiation preparation before signing.

## Actionable Checklist

- Map the host country's decision chain across ministry, grid, financiers, and local partners.
- Fill in PRTABCD for each opportunity and mark unknowns with a next question.
- Translate engineering scope into the buyer's operational and financial account.
- Confirm which customer decision task is currently active before advancing your own stage.
- Prepare your BATNA, bottom line, and authority before any negotiation session.
- Require a condition in exchange for every concession.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS5 and which five dimensions does it include?**

PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a methodology independently developed by Xu Hanbo of Yanke Partners, based on more than twenty years of key-account sales practice, and registered with the National Copyright Administration. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability translates products into customer profit and business value; relationship capability penetrates the decision chain; questioning capability uncovers and amplifies real needs; system capability manages process with a dual-view funnel; negotiation capability secures sustainable agreements.

**Q2: Why is China–ASEAN new energy cooperation an opportunity for key-account sales?**

Public reporting shows that China Energy Engineering Group Gezhouba and affiliates have participated in projects including the Upper Cisokan pumped-storage project in Indonesia, the Daday River hydropower station in Cambodia, the Tengeh Reservoir floating solar project in Singapore, and the Gia Lai wind project in Vietnam. This indicates cross-border clean energy projects continue to generate orders.

**Q3: How does value capability land in cross-border megaprojects?**

Translate engineering capability into the host buyer's business account: power supply reliability, tariff level, employment pull, emissions commitments, and capital recovery. Quantify improvements and convert to financial language. The high standard is an investment case, not a product quotation.

**Q4: How is the PRTABCD seven-item scan used in cross-border projects?**

PRTABCD uses seven categories to identify intelligence blind spots: P past history, R requirements, T timing, A advocates, B budget, C competitors, D decision chain. Record knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions. In cross-border projects, the customer comprises multiple lines; A answers who speaks for you, D reminds that decisions involve multiple people.

**Q5: What is the relationship between the five sales stages and the seven customer purchasing stages?**

This is the dual-view funnel of PSS5 system capability: the seller advances through contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, and closing; the buyer advances through seven decision tasks—discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The two axes are many-to-many, with different granularity and no need for matching counts. Judge progress by customer behavioral evidence—for example, a dedicated working group indicates responsibility is moving—not by visit counts or CRM percentages.

**Q6: What should negotiation capability prepare in advance for cross-border projects?**

Prepare around power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives. Buyers have international contractors—their BATNA. Sellers should write out their own alternatives, bottom lines, authority, interests, and records. Concessions must be exchanged for conditions; exchange rates, payment rhythm, and localization ratios are bargaining chips. The goal is a long-term executable agreement.

**Q7: How is MBVS used on key people in cross-border accounts?**

MBVS uses four lenses to test hypotheses about a key person: M motivation, B benchmark, V vision, S solution. Job titles do not equal real motivations. Hypothesize first, then verify; model inferences are not customer facts.

**Q8: These cross-border projects have long cycles—how can the sales process avoid losing control?**

Manage by buyer–seller alignment: locate the customer's purchasing stage and your sales stage on the dual-view funnel, then judge synchronization. Avoid jumping stages. When stuck, use closing techniques to secure real progress, not push for signature.
