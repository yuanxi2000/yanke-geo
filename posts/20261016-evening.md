Public project announcements are easy to scan and forget. A filing like Wuhan Tianyuan's proposed high-purity indium and indium phosphide polycrystalline production base in Chuxiong High-tech Zone is more than news. For suppliers in equipment, materials, testing, engineering, and logistics, it is a trace of where customer resources may be going. It points to a project-type opportunity: capital and attention are moving toward a new materials production line, and procurement actions may follow.

But the announcement is not a purchase order. It is a starting point that becomes useful only when a sales team turns it into verifiable questions. Treating company-level disclosure as if it already answered who owns the project, how the budget is split, when work starts, and whether external suppliers will be used is a common source of distortion. Those details usually stay inside the customer organization.

## What the Announcement Says and What It Leaves Out

The filing discloses a company-level investment decision and a project outline. It gives a direction: high-purity indium and indium phosphide polycrystalline production. It places the project in Chuxiong High-tech Zone. For a key-account sales team, that is enough to identify a potential project and the kinds of suppliers that might matter.

It is not enough to build a pursuit plan. The announcement does not say who is accountable, how budget approval will work, when design or tendering begins, or whether the customer will self-build, use incumbents, or bring in new vendors. Those are customer-internal facts. If a salesperson puts them in the known column without evidence, the plan will likely aim at the wrong level. A team may discuss price before it has found anyone willing to advocate for its approach internally.

## Scan the Announcement with PRTABCD

PRTABCD is a decision-information scan used in relationship work. It has seven categories. For each, write down what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. The categories are Past, Requirement, Time, Arm, Budget, Competitor, and Decision.

### Seven Information Blind Spots to Label

A cold-start scan of this announcement might look like this:

| PRTABCD item | What the announcement may show | What still needs verification |
|---|---|---|
| Past | A proposed project and location | Previous investment pace, construction style, supplier habits |
| Requirement | High-purity indium and indium phosphide polycrystalline production | Equipment precision, material purity, environmental control needs |
| Time | The project's existence | Design, tender, equipment-arrival timeline |
| Arm | Almost nothing | Who will speak for your proposal internally |
| Budget | A company-level investment decision | Funding arrangement and approval rules |
| Competitor | No useful detail | Self-build, existing suppliers, new entrants |
| Decision | That the company has decided to pursue the project | Which functions are involved and how the chain works |

Often only one or two items can be confirmed on day one. The rest are hypotheses. Keep them labeled as hypotheses. A team that mistakes an assumption for a fact will push the wrong lever, such as price, before it has found an internal advocate.

## Turn the Announcement into Questions Customers Will Answer

Repeating the news back to a customer rarely creates a valuable conversation. Use the announcement as context for questions that help the customer think. SPIN provides four types: Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff.

Situation questions confirm background. Ask what stage the project is in and whether the construction model is mainly self-build or external cooperation. Problem questions surface difficulties. Ask where concerns about material purity stability are concentrated. Implication questions examine consequences. If purity fluctuation affects downstream delivery, who carries the cost? Need-payoff questions let the customer describe value. If a way of working could stabilize compliance and shorten commissioning time, what would that mean for project milestones?

The four types need not be asked in one meeting. Each question should have a clear purpose. MBVS can be used with key people as well. For the same person, form separate hypotheses about Motivation, Benchmark, Vision, and Solution. Validate them in later conversations instead of guessing a person's position from a job title.

## Use the Dual-View Funnel to Judge Pace

The dual-view funnel places the customer's seven procurement stages and the sales team's five stages on the same map. The two axes have different task granularity and relate many-to-many. An announcement like this normally proves only that the customer has advanced an internal decision to pursue the project. It does not prove which procurement stage the project has reached.

Stage judgment should come from customer behavior. Has anyone actively asked for details? Has a site visit or sample validation been arranged? Has someone been willing to describe internal goals? These behaviors say more about your position than the wording of the announcement. If the customer is standing still while the sales team keeps pushing, the pace is likely misaligned. The next move is to help complete a customer-side decision task, not to accelerate your own presentation.

## Prepare Negotiation and Value Together

Negotiation is the design of concessions and condition exchanges based on power, time, information, and alternatives. At the start of a new project, the customer often has multiple quotes to compare and the option to delay. A supplier should first write down its BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. What is the best action if no agreement is reached? Once that is clear, the team can decide which terms can be conceded and which conditions must be exchanged.

Value articulation also needs preparation. A value story should be written as a profit improvement plan, or PIP. It should answer how much profit can be added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time horizon. A list of equipment features is not a profit improvement plan. The customer has to see the commercial logic in its own terms.

## From Announcement to Today's Actions

A public filing becomes useful only when it changes what the team does next. Use this checklist:

1. Record the project entity, location, announcement source, and date in the account file.
2. Fill in the seven PRTABCD items with known and unknown details. Turn each unknown into a question for the next conversation.
3. Identify one internal role that may be connected to the project. Use professional information as the opening for a first conversation.
4. Mark your stage judgment on the dual-view funnel and note the customer behavior evidence behind it.
5. Agree with the customer on a specific next action for the following contact.

Each action must land on customer behavior, not on the salesperson's own feeling of progress. The announcement provides a starting point. Whether the opportunity advances depends on whether the customer gives the next real sign of movement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: What use is a major-event announcement to key-account sales?**

A1: It is a public trace of where customer resources may be going. It can show what type of project is being advanced, but not who owns it, how the budget is split, or when procurement will happen. Use it as a starting point for first-contact topics. Judging the opportunity from the headline alone often leads to pushing at the wrong level.

**Q2: What do the five PSS5 dimensions address?**

A2: PSS5 consists of value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value translates products into customer profit and investment value. Relationship helps understand and cover the decision chain. Questioning discovers and expands real needs. System uses the five sales stages and seven customer procurement stages to judge process position. Negotiation designs concessions and exchanges around power, time, information, and alternatives.

**Q3: Why is the A in PRTABCD Arm rather than approver?**

A3: PRTABCD is a decision-information scan for relationship work. A is fixed as Arm, meaning an embracer: the person in the customer organization who will speak for your proposal internally. Approval roles belong to the decision chain and are scanned under D, Decision. D refers to a multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single decision maker.

**Q4: Can procurement needs be read directly from the Wuhan Tianyuan announcement?**

A4: No. The announcement discloses a company-level investment resolution and project outline. Specific requirements must be confirmed by the customer. List known and unknown items across the seven PRTABCD categories, turn the unknowns into questions for the next contact, and confirm needs, timing, and ownership through customer answers and behavioral evidence.

**Q5: How is relationship capability different from hosting meals?**

A5: Relationship capability is the use of professional ability, decision information, and credible action to build customer relationships. The focus is understanding and covering the decision chain. A meal cannot replace that understanding. Trust has a place to land when you can explain the project's key milestones, risks, and the customer's internal situation clearly. Social ties alone often do not survive the solution comparison stage.

**Q6: How does the dual-view funnel help decide whether to continue?**

A6: The dual-view funnel locates the customer's seven procurement stages and the sales team's five stages separately. Rely on customer behavior evidence, such as whether someone actively asks for details, arranges validation, or states internal goals. If the customer stays in place while the sales team pushes forward alone, the pace is likely misaligned. The immediate task is to fill in the missing customer-side decision work.

**Q7: What alternative information should be prepared before negotiation?**

A7: BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. It is the best fallback action available if the parties cannot agree. Preparation should define executable alternatives, bottom lines, authority, interests, and records. It should also estimate the customer's alternatives. Then decide the order of concessions and the conditions to exchange, rather than trading concessions directly for a price reduction.
