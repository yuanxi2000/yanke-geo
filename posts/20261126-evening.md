A new production line can change a customer's options without changing the customer's readiness to buy. For key-account sales teams, that distinction matters: additional supply creates a reason to revisit an opportunity, but it does not establish technical suitability, financial value, or purchasing commitment.

The supplied source reports that a diamond chip-substrate project began production in Beijing in 2026. It presents the launch as a potential shift in supply expectations for applications such as high-performance computing and electric-vehicle thermal management. More stable supply and better cost competitiveness are possibilities to investigate, rather than established outcomes.

The durable sales lesson is to connect external developments with the customer's actual decision process. Identify what has changed, determine which purchasing task it affects, and obtain evidence before advancing the opportunity.

## Read the market signal without overstating it

A production announcement can help reopen a discussion previously stalled by concerns about capacity or manufacturing consistency. It cannot, by itself, answer those concerns. Starting production does not demonstrate that a supplier can meet a particular customer's volume, quality, cost, or delivery requirements.

Use the announcement to frame specific questions:

- Was limited substrate availability preventing the customer from evaluating a solution?
- Does the customer now want to investigate an additional source?
- What evidence would establish acceptable supply reliability?
- Would another supplier change the customer's selection process or budget allocation?

This shifts the conversation from announcing availability to understanding a decision. A customer may move from asking whether a solution exists to asking which supplier can deliver it reliably at an acceptable total cost. That movement must be confirmed through customer behavior.

## Manage the opportunity through two perspectives

The source describes a dual-perspective funnel within PSS5. The seller tracks five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. The buyer works through seven purchasing tasks:

1. Recognize the problem.
2. Assess its value or importance.
3. Confirm that a solution exists.
4. Establish responsibility for addressing it.
5. Decide whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Select a solution and supplier.
7. Commit and sign.

These perspectives help expose a common mismatch: the seller records activity while the buyer still has unfinished decision work. A meeting, proposal, or encouraging conversation may be useful, but it does not establish that the customer has completed a purchasing task.

The source identifies two buyer stages as especially relevant to the reported production launch.

| Buyer stage | What the supply development may change | Appropriate sales response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm that a solution exists | Capacity concerns may become easier to investigate | Provide a capacity ramp plan and quality-control standards for evaluation |
| Select a solution and supplier | An additional source may alter supplier comparisons or second-source plans | Update competitive and budget intelligence; establish the customer's evaluation requirements |

The table is a diagnostic aid, not proof that every account has advanced. Customers can respond differently to the same external event.

### Let customer evidence determine the next step

Ask what the customer has actually done since learning about the supply change. Relevant evidence may include sample-test feedback, a small trial-production order, or a formal request for quotation. Each indicates a different kind of engagement; none should automatically be treated as a signed commitment.

Match the evidence to the claimed stage. If the customer is comparing suppliers, address comparison criteria and qualification requirements. If responsibility remains unresolved, help clarify ownership before pressing for a commercial decision.

Relationships still matter. Their usefulness depends partly on timing: an executive visit aimed at establishing initial trust may contribute little when the immediate task is a detailed supplier comparison.

## Translate capacity into a customer business case

Availability describes the supplier. A value proposition explains what the customer could gain. The source recommends translating technical and supply advantages into financial terms through total cost of ownership, or TCO.

Begin with the customer's existing situation and compare it with the proposed alternative. Depending on the application, relevant questions include:

- Could improved thermal performance reduce cooling energy or maintenance costs?
- Could a more reliable supply reduce disruption-related losses?
- Could manufacturing consistency reduce unit costs?
- Could the proposed approach shorten development work?

These are hypotheses for joint evaluation. The source provides no measured savings, performance improvements, or customer results, so none should be presented as established benefits of the reported launch.

Work with the customer's finance or operations team to identify the baseline, assumptions, and evidence needed. Separate technical feasibility from financial attractiveness: a material may perform adequately without producing a compelling economic case for a particular application.

The source calls the resulting financial argument a PIP, or profit improvement plan. Its purpose is to express a proposed change in the customer's operating language. Record which inputs are supported and which remain assumptions. Where uncertainty remains, keep it visible instead of turning a possible saving into a promised return.

## Refresh account intelligence before committing resources

The source uses PRTABCD as an account-intelligence scanning tool. For this supply event, it emphasizes Competitor and Budget, alongside Arm, referring to internal supporters, and Decision, referring to the decision chain.

Use those elements to investigate four practical issues:

- Competition: Is the customer reconsidering existing suppliers, or only gathering information?
- Budget: Is funding available for evaluation or an additional source, and who controls it?
- Internal support: Who has a reason to support greater supply stability?
- Decision chain: Who evaluates performance, approves spending, and authorizes a supplier change?

The source suggests that procurement leaders or technology leaders may be important participants. Treat those roles as starting points for investigation, not a universal buying committee. Establish who owns the relevant delivery, cost, or technical objective in this account.

Implication questions, drawn from SPIN questioning, can help explore the consequences of dependence on a single source. Ask how a disruption would affect the customer's operations and how the customer currently manages that exposure. Use the answers to understand the problem rather than assuming that a second source is automatically valuable.

## Make pipeline progression and negotiation evidence-based

A supply announcement can generate attention without creating a qualified opportunity. Review existing accounts at the feasibility-confirmation stage and beyond, then update the record according to what has actually changed.

Distinguish seller activity from customer progress. Sending a quotation is an activity. Receiving a formal request with defined requirements provides evidence about the customer's evaluation. A verbal expression of interest should not carry the same weight as documented testing or an order.

If expected evidence does not arrive, revisit the underlying need. The customer may still have unresolved questions about value, ownership, or feasibility. Returning to those questions is more useful than preserving an optimistic stage label.

Price pressure also requires preparation. The source recommends BATNA analysis: identify the best alternative to a negotiated agreement and use it to clarify acceptable terms. Assess the customer's alternatives as well, including potential switching costs and quality risks.

Discuss process control, yield, or supply reliability only to the extent that evidence supports the claim. The source highlights these as relevant differentiators, but it supplies no comparative performance data that would establish superiority.

## An actionable account-review checklist

Use this checklist when an external supply development affects an active opportunity:

- [ ] Identify the buyer's current purchasing task.
- [ ] Record the customer behavior supporting that assessment.
- [ ] Establish whether the new supply information changes an existing concern.
- [ ] Obtain capacity and quality information relevant to that concern.
- [ ] Refresh competitor, budget, supporter, and decision-chain intelligence.
- [ ] Agree on the evidence needed for the next decision.
- [ ] Develop a TCO comparison with finance or operations.
- [ ] Separate measured inputs from assumptions in the business case.
- [ ] Review negotiation alternatives before discussing concessions.
- [ ] Revisit opportunities whose recorded stage lacks supporting evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a production launch justify advancing an opportunity?

No. It justifies checking whether the customer's options or concerns have changed. Advance the opportunity when customer behavior supports the next stage.

### What does systems capability mean in this guide?

It means managing the sales process through clear stages, customer purchasing tasks, and evidence of progress. Its practical purpose is to keep sales actions aligned with the decision the customer needs to make.

### Can strong relationships compensate for incomplete purchasing work?

Relationships can help establish access and understanding. They do not resolve an unproven value case or unclear responsibility by themselves. Identify the unfinished purchasing task and address it directly.

### How should a team respond when savings cannot yet be quantified?

Document the proposed benefit as a hypothesis. Agree with the customer on the baseline and information required to evaluate it. Avoid assigning financial value before the supporting inputs exist.
