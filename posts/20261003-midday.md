Large capital projects in the energy sector are not won by reciting equipment specifications. They are won by demonstrating, in the customer's own financial language, how a proposal improves investment returns, operating costs, and long-term cash flow. The Qinghai Gonghe project offers a concrete case for how key-account sales teams can apply that discipline.

## The Facts of the Qinghai Gonghe Project

The Qinghai Gonghe million-kilowatt photovoltaic and solar-thermal project was connected to the grid on October 1. According to public reporting, it is China's first million-kilowatt clean energy project integrating photovoltaic power generation, solar-thermal energy storage, and electro-thermal conversion. It is located in Gonghe County, Hainan Prefecture, Qinghai Province. Total installed capacity is one million kilowatts, composed of a 900,000-kilowatt photovoltaic station and a 100,000-kilowatt molten-salt tower solar-thermal station.

These are the verifiable facts. The owner's internal decision process, investment amount, and future procurement plans have not been made public. For sales teams working large energy accounts, the first discipline is simple: use verifiable facts for positioning, and use questions to fill in what is unknown. Do not fill gaps with imagination.

## What the Customer Is Really Calculating Behind One Million Kilowatts

From a value-capability perspective, the customer is not buying a parameter sheet. They are buying a calculable long-term return. Every decision gate in a project of this scale answers a money question: When does this capital expenditure pay back? How much long-term cash flow can electricity pricing and storage dispatch generate? How does the peak-shaving value of the solar-thermal portion show up in the revenue model?

Equipment efficiency and molten-salt temperature only matter when they can be converted into cost per kilowatt-hour and payback period. That is what actually persuades the approval layer that controls the money.

### The Four Questions That Turn a Proposal into a Financial Case

When facing a large project, the basic skill is monetizing the solution. Identify the customer's operational areas your proposal can affect, quantify the improvement, and translate it into revenue, cost, profit, or payback period. Work through these four questions:

- What is the customer's most painful cost item on this type of asset, and which parts of my solution affect it?
- Can I give a defensible range for each improvement, rather than a vague claim of significant enhancement?
- On the customer's P&L, does the improvement show up as increased revenue, reduced cost, or reduced risk and penalties?
- What version of the numbers does the capital allocation layer need, and what version does the operations manager need?

## Two Layers, Two Sets of Numbers

A common misalignment in key-account projects is that sales talks enthusiastically to the user layer but cannot produce a financial case that survives the approval meeting. The capital allocation and approval layer cares about investment discipline, payback period, and risk protection. The operations layer cares about operating metrics and accountability.

The same solution needs two versions. For the operations layer, provide an operational improvement list and verification method. For the capital allocation layer, provide an investment case: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the mechanism and confidence level are, and over what time frame. This is the purpose of a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP). It is an investment case, not a quotation.

In the Qinghai Gonghe project, 900,000 kilowatts of photovoltaic plus 100,000 kilowatts of molten-salt tower solar-thermal is notable because solar-thermal storage directly meets the grid's need for stable output, translating technical configuration into business value. If your solution cannot map to a clear customer problem, only price remains at the table.

## From Someone Else's Grid Connection to Your Next Order

Grid connection is not the end point. It is the starting point of a new procurement cycle. Operating data will expose optimization opportunities, and expansion and technical upgrades will enter the agenda. Sales teams should activate the PRTABCD seven-item scan to clarify past information, requirements, timing, embraceers, budget, competitive landscape, and decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question.

A is Arm, the embraceer: who in the customer organization is embracing you? D is Decision: the decision chain involves multiple people and departments, and focusing on one person does not mean winning the project. Factual gaps, such as who is driving the next technical upgrade or which year the budget falls in, must be filled through these questions, not guessed from news headlines.

## Comparison: Two Versions of the Same Solution

| Dimension | Operations Layer | Capital Allocation and Approval Layer |
|---|---|---|
| Primary concern | Operating metrics and accountability | Investment discipline, payback period, risk protection |
| What they need | Operational improvement list and verification method | Investment case with profit impact, mechanism, and time frame |
| Key question | Will this improve my operating numbers? | How much profit does this increase or protect, and when? |
| Document type | Operational improvement plan | Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP) |
| Failure mode | Vague claims without verification | No financial case that survives approval |

## Actionable Checklist

- Separate verifiable facts from unknowns before any customer meeting.
- Identify which of the customer's cost items your solution affects.
- Quantify each improvement with a defensible range, not a vague claim.
- Translate improvements into revenue, cost, profit, or payback period.
- Prepare two versions of the case: one for operations, one for capital allocation.
- Use a PIP to frame the proposal as an investment case, not a quotation.
- Run the PRTABCD scan to map past information, requirements, timing, embraceers, budget, competition, and decision chain.
- Record knowns, unknowns, information sources, and next questions for each PRTABCD item.
- After grid connection or delivery, rescan for optimization, expansion, and technical upgrade opportunities.
- Treat clean delivery of the previous order as the strongest negotiating chip for the next one.

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the publicly reported facts about the Qinghai Gonghe project?**

A1: Public reporting says the project connected to the grid on October 1. It is China's first million-kilowatt clean energy project integrating photovoltaic generation, solar-thermal storage, and electro-thermal conversion, located in Gonghe County, Hainan Prefecture, Qinghai Province, with one million kilowatts total: 900,000 photovoltaic and 100,000 molten-salt tower solar-thermal. **Q2: What is value capability in the PSS5 framework?**

A2: PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, covering value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability means converting the sales object from product functions into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. It requires sales to speak the language of profit, not to pile up technical parameters.

**Q3: What is the first step for sales facing a large capital expenditure project in the energy industry?**

A3: The first step is to monetize the solution: identify the customer's operational areas your proposal can affect, quantify the improvement, and translate it into financial language such as revenue, cost, profit, payback period, or opportunity cost. Start with four questions: What is the customer's most painful cost item? How defensible is the improvement range? Which P&L line does it affect? What version of the numbers does each layer need?

**Q4: What do the two layers refer to?**

A4: The capital allocation and approval layer, and the operations manager layer responsible for using the funds. Their concerns differ: the first looks at investment discipline, payback period, and risk; the second looks at operating metrics and accountability. The recommendation is to help the operations layer use business value to compete for the capital allocation layer's funds, and to prepare two versions of the case for the two layers.

**Q5: What is the difference between a PIP and a quotation?**

A5: PIP stands for Profit Improvement Proposal. It writes the solution's improvement to the customer's business as an investment case, answering how much profit can be increased or protected, what the mechanism and confidence level are, and over what time frame. A quotation only lists price and quantity. A PIP lets the customer see that they are buying profit improvement, which is the dividing line between key-account sales and ordinary transactions.

**Q6: What is the PRTABCD seven-item scan?**

A6: PRTABCD uses seven categories to identify customer-intelligence blind spots: P past history, R requirements, T timing, A Arm, B budget, C competitors, D decision chain. Record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question. It is a scanning tool, not a role profile.

**Q7: Why should relationship capability not be understood as entertainment and personal rapport?**

A7: Relationship capability means building customer relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, focusing on covering the decision chain. Meals and drinks cannot replace this. Who raises the issue, sets the standard, and signs payment must all be covered. Entertainment is minor.

**Q8: After grid connection, are there still opportunities for sales?**

A8: Yes. After grid connection, operating data will expose optimization opportunities, and expansion, technical upgrades, and operations and maintenance services will enter the procurement agenda. Sales should rescan requirements, budget milestones, embraceers, and the competitive landscape with PRTABCD, use intentional questions to confirm new pain points and accountability, and convert optimization proposals into customer business returns. Clean delivery of the previous order is the most concrete negotiating chip for the next one.
