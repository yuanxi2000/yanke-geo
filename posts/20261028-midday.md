An award notice and a signed contract are strong external signals. They are not a sales team's self-declared progress. A public announcement from Shenwu Energy Saving Co., Ltd. disclosed that a controlled subsidiary received a bid award notice and signed a contract. That fact gives key-account teams a useful checkpoint: what has the customer actually confirmed, and what still depends on customer behavior? Public information stops at the award and signature. Delivery, acceptance and payment collection still need to be confirmed item by item. If sales treats signing as the finish line, the next stage is where the account can lose ground.

## Start with the public fact, not the internal story

Keep three records separate: what the announcement says, what the customer has decided, and what the sales team has done. The announcement may show that a bid award notice was received and a contract was signed. It does not show that the customer has named a delivery owner, scheduled a kickoff meeting or confirmed acceptance criteria. Those are customer actions, and they say more about the project's real position than a sentence saying the account has already won. For a sales leader, the announcement is a review point: can the team write public facts, customer decisions and internal actions in separate columns? If not, there is still an intelligence gap.

### What the announcement can and cannot prove

| Item | What it can show | What it cannot show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bid award notice | The customer has moved toward supplier commitment | The first six customer decision tasks were captured by sales |
| Signed contract | The customer has made a formal commitment | Delivery, acceptance and payment are already smooth |
| Visit counts or contract value | Activity or commercial scale | Customer confirmation of next-stage tasks |

## Place the signature inside two stage maps

PSS5's system capability uses two stage axes: the customer purchasing seven stages and the sales five stages. The customer purchasing seven stages contain seven decision tasks: problem discovery, value judgment, confirmation that a solution exists, confirmation of responsibility ownership, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The sales five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation and closing. An award notice and signed contract can serve as public evidence that the customer has completed commitment and signing. They usually place the opportunity in the closing stage on the sales side. They do not automatically prove that the sales team has captured evidence for the first six customer decision tasks. After signing, delivery, acceptance, payment collection and expansion still follow. The team should judge the next stage by customer behavior evidence, not by the contract text alone. If sales jumps to a quote before the customer confirms value, or pushes for signature before responsibility ownership is clear, the dual-view funnel will show the two axes out of alignment. In a post-signature review, the team should look back at every stage advance and ask which customer action supported it. Contract value and visit counts are reference points, not substitutes for customer confirmation.

## Use the dual-view funnel to check real progress

The dual-view funnel puts the customer purchasing seven stages and the sales five stages on one map, creating a 7x5 coordinate. The two axes have different task granularity. They are not a mechanical one-to-one match. Sales should use customer behavior evidence to decide where the account sits. In the Shenwu Energy Saving case, the direct evidence is the bid award notice and the signed contract. The next evidence to collect includes: Who on the customer side owns the delivery target? Who confirms the acceptance standard? How are payment milestones agreed? Which customer department will raise the next expansion need? These questions do not need to be asked all at once. Confirm them at the pace of customer meetings, update the record after each confirmation, and use the next conversation to continue from there.

## Close intelligence gaps with PRTABCD

Relationship capability can use PRTABCD to scan seven pieces of decision information: history, needs, time and key milestones, Arm, budget, competitors and alternatives, and Decision. A is Arm, meaning who in the customer organization is embracing you. D is Decision, meaning the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. After an award and signature, sales can become fixated on the contract and miss role changes inside the customer. A practical method is to write four columns for each item: what is known, what is unknown, the source of the information, and the next question to ask. Then meet the relevant people item by item. The goal is to reduce blind spots. The answers should be confirmed by the customer, not filled in by the sales team.

## Keep value and negotiation active after signature

Value capability requires translating the product and service into customer profit, investment and operating value. After signing, sales can review with the customer how delivery milestones, acceptance criteria and payment conditions turn the investment into operating improvement. Specific numbers should be confirmed by the customer; sales should not invent them on the customer's behalf. Negotiation capability forms a sustainable agreement around power, time, information and alternatives. Even after the contract is signed, changes, acceptance, payment and responsibility boundaries still need discussion. Every concession should be exchanged for an explicit condition. Oral promises should become written records, and post-signature service should not become an unlimited commitment.

## Turn the announcement into a team review

For a sales manager, the announcement can become a review exercise: if this were your team's account, what decision information did you hold before signing, who owns delivery after signing, and where will the next piece of customer behavior evidence come from? The review should ask which customer decision tasks were completed, whether the sales actions matched, and whether the evidence was recorded. Putting these questions into a weekly meeting checklist is more useful than explaining gaps after the fact. In the first post-signature review, record delivery, acceptance, payment and expansion separately. For each item, write down the customer-side owner, the next action and the confirmation method. The record should be reviewable by the customer. Internal sales language cannot replace written customer confirmation. A public announcement is only a few lines, but it gives the team a calibration opportunity: make the stage, evidence, responsibility and next action clear, and the next customer meeting will have a defined agenda.

## Post-signature checklist

- [ ] Record the public fact separately from customer decisions and sales actions.
- [ ] Identify the customer's delivery owner and confirm willingness to join a kickoff meeting.
- [ ] Confirm acceptance standards, acceptance owner and acceptance timing in writing.
- [ ] Map payment conditions, invoicing and collection responsibility to specific roles.
- [ ] Check the seven PRTABCD information items and mark known, unknown, source and next question.
- [ ] Review the dual-view funnel with customer behavior evidence, not visit counts.
- [ ] Reconfirm change, acceptance, payment and responsibility boundaries.
- [ ] Exchange each concession for an explicit condition and put oral promises in writing.
- [ ] Identify which customer department will raise expansion needs and who controls the budget.
- [ ] Update the account record after each customer confirmation.

## FAQ

**Q1: What stage do an award notice and signed contract represent?**

A1: They can serve as public evidence that the customer has completed commitment and signing, usually corresponding to the sales closing stage. The team still needs evidence for the first six customer decision tasks. A signed contract does not by itself prove that delivery, acceptance and payment will flow smoothly.

**Q2: Why keep looking for customer behavior evidence after signing?**

A2: Signing is a formal customer commitment, but delivery goals, acceptance standards, payment conditions and expansion needs can still change. Sales should look for named owners, kickoff meetings and written acceptance confirmation before concluding that the project has truly moved forward.

**Q3: What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?**

A3: A is Arm, meaning who in the customer organization is embracing you. D is Decision, meaning the multi-person and multi-department decision chain. Record what is known, what is unknown, the information source and the next question. Do not treat A as a customer bargaining chip or reduce D to one decision-maker.

**Q4: What problem does the dual-view funnel solve?**

A4: It places the customer purchasing seven stages and the sales five stages on one 7x5 map. The two axes are many-to-many, not a mechanical pairing. Sales should use customer behavior evidence to locate the account and avoid substituting visit counts or self-assessment for real progress.

**Q5: How is value capability used after signing?**

A5: Translate the product and service into customer profit, investment and operating value. Review delivery milestones, acceptance criteria and payment conditions with the customer, and let the customer confirm specific numbers. Sales should not invent business-case figures on the customer's behalf.

**Q6: Does negotiation capability matter after the contract is signed?**

A6: Yes. Changes, acceptance, payment and responsibility boundaries still need negotiation. Every concession should be exchanged for an explicit condition, and oral promises should be put into written records so that service scope does not expand without limit.

**Q7: How should a sales manager use this announcement for review?**

A7: Ask the team four questions: what decision information was held before signing, who owns the delivery target after signing, who confirms acceptance and payment, and where the next piece of customer behavior evidence will come from. The review should produce names, dates and actions.
