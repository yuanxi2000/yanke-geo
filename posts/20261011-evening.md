When a major customer reorganizes around overseas operations, the previous sales approach can stop matching reality. An eastmoney report on Chery notes that as of June 2026, Chery had three overseas production bases (Malaysia, Spain, Thailand), with the Rayong plant opening in April. In July, Chery took over a South African factory expected to start production around mid-2027. The report suggests that as overseas demand is met locally, the marginal effect of pulling domestic capacity may weaken.

Those are the verifiable facts. What follows is analysis and practical guidance built on them. The report does not identify who the counterparty is, how much money is involved, or the specific dates beyond what is stated. When a customer's operating center of gravity moves abroad, the way a seller explains value, maps the decision chain, and paces progress must be rearranged. PSS5, Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, is one way to organize that rearrangement.

## The Five Capabilities as a Reorganization Checklist

PSS5 covers value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation capabilities. Each changes when a customer shifts production and decision-making overseas. None can be replaced by simply reading the news more carefully.

### Value: When the Customer's Profit Model Changes, the Value Story Must Change Too

Value capability means translating a product or service into investment, returns, and profit improvements the customer understands, backed by business numbers. An export share reaching seventy percent and overseas bases coming online changes the customer's revenue structure, cost structure, and delivery radius. Calculations built around domestic production rhythms—delivery batches, inventory occupation, service response radius—may not hold overseas.

List the cost, time, and risk items the customer has added or shifted by producing locally. Ask which are customer-confirmed and which are still your assumptions. Discuss investment and returns only for confirmed items. Value capability depends on real business numbers from the customer, not seller assumptions.

## Relationship: The Decision Chain Follows Capacity, So Rescan Seven Information Blind Spots

Relationship capability builds customer relationships through professional ability, decision information, and trustworthy action. The focus is understanding and covering the customer's decision chain, not equating relationships with socializing. PRTABCD uses seven types of decision information to identify blind spots.

- P stands for past and historical information.
- R stands for requirements.
- T stands for timing and key milestones.
- A stands for Arm, meaning who is embracing you.
- B stands for budget.
- C stands for competitors and alternatives.
- D stands for the decision chain.

When overseas bases expand, the original contact may no longer cover the new business scope, and the people controlling budget and signatures may have changed. All seven items need to be reviewed again.

### P and R

Record the customer's past purchasing and usage history and the problem it actually needs to solve now. Mark the source for each item.

### T

Confirm with the customer the milestones for overseas base production, acceptance, and switching. Do not calculate these yourself.

### A and D

Identify who is embracing you and which other roles sit on the decision chain. When the business scope changes, re-verify this.

### B and C

Clarify the budget basis and alternatives, including the customer doing it themselves, switching suppliers, or not doing it for now. Answers can only come from the customer; anything unanswered stays unknown.

## Questioning: Turn Localization into Clear Requirements with Four Types of Questions

Questioning capability means discovering and amplifying real requirements through question design. SPIN's four types: situation questions clarify the overseas base's current supply and service system; problem questions surface bottlenecks after local production; implication questions translate bottlenecks into consequences like line stoppage, rework, or delivery delays; need-payoff questions let the customer state the benefits of solving the problem.

These are questions the seller should ask. They do not mean Chery already has these difficulties. Answers can only come from the customer, and anything unanswered stays unknown.

## System: Align with the Customer's Seven Buying Stages

System capability uses a dual-view funnel made of five selling stages and seven customer buying stages. The five selling stages are contact, requirements, solution, negotiation, and closing. The seven buying stages run from problem discovery, value judgment, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, to commitment and signing.

The two axes are many-to-many and do not need to correspond one-to-one. In projects like overseas capacity expansion, buyers often pause on build-or-buy and responsibility ownership. A supplier that only watches its own closing stage may rush a solution while the customer is still judging value. Judge progress by customer-side behavioral evidence, not visit counts or self-reported progress.

## Negotiation: Write Down Alternatives and Bottom Lines First

Negotiation capability means designing concessions and condition exchanges based on clear power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives. BATNA refers to the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Preparation should specify executable alternatives, bottom lines, authority, interests, and records.

Cross-border projects amplify the cost of concessions because of geographic distance, delivery cycles, and responsibility boundaries. Those concessions are harder to recover than in domestic projects. Before clarifying the customer's timing, budget basis, and responsibility ownership, do not offer a price commitment first, and do not assume the customer's negotiation rhythm for them.

## A Compact Comparison: Domestic-Focused Versus Overseas-Focused Customer

| Dimension | Customer centered on domestic capacity | Customer shifting toward overseas bases |
|---|---|---|
| Value story | Based on domestic production rhythm, delivery batches, and service radius | Must be rebuilt around local production costs, time, and risk items |
| Decision chain | Original contacts and budget owners cover the business | Contacts, budget, and signatories may have changed and need re-verification |
| Questioning | Familiar domestic bottlenecks | Local supply, service, and compliance issues must be asked, not assumed |
| Progress measure | Visit counts and internal pipeline stages | Customer-side behavioral evidence across the seven buying stages |
| Negotiation | Domestic cycles and recovery costs | Longer cycles and higher concession costs require written alternatives |

## FAQ

### Q1: What is PSS5, and why use it to analyze this news?

PSS5 is a five-capability framework for professional key-account sales, made up of value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation capabilities. The shift in export share and global operations changes the customer's operating structure and decision rhythm. A single dimension cannot cover it. The value story must follow the customer's cost structure, the decision chain must be re-verified, and the pace must align with the customer's buying tasks. Looking through all five capabilities turns the change into checkable judgments rather than a reaction to a headline.

### Q2: What can be quoted directly from this public information?

Verifiable items include Chery, eastmoney, the reported title, three overseas bases (Malaysia, Spain, Thailand) as of June 2026, the Rayong opening in April, the South African factory expected around mid-2027, and the possible weakening marginal effect on domestic capacity. Missing are the counterparty, money involved, and specific dates; analysis should preserve that absence rather than speculate.

### Q3: When a customer moves capacity overseas, what is the first step for value capability?

Re-list the customer's cost and benefit structure before discussing value. Local overseas production changes delivery batches, inventory occupation, service radius, and risk bearing. List added or shifted cost, time, and risk items, mark which are customer-confirmed and which are still your assumptions, then calculate investment and return only for confirmed items. Value capability requires business numbers that come from the customer.

### Q4: In PRTABCD, what do A and D mean?

A is Arm: who in the customer organization embraces you. D is Decision: the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. These two are most likely to become outdated when the customer reorganizes. After an overseas base comes online or regional responsibilities are redrawn, the person who embraced you may no longer cover the new scope, and the budget or signature authority may have changed. For each item, record known, unknown, information source, and next question. If uncertain, write unknown.

### Q5: What is the most common mistake in questioning capability?

Treating your own assumptions as customer facts. Sellers easily assume the customer must have local supply, service, or compliance difficulties, then jump straight to a solution. SPIN's four question types progress through situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff, letting the customer state the problem while the seller designs questions and records answers.

### Q6: How do you tell whether an overseas-related project is real progress or just noise?

Look at customer-side behavioral evidence, not your own actions. Selling stages: contact, requirements, solution, negotiation, closing. Buying stages: problem discovery, value judgment, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. Real progress shows when the customer clarifies responsibility ownership and validates solutions. Repeated meetings without customer-side change mean stalled.

### Q7: How should BATNA be understood in cross-border projects?

BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Preparation should clarify executable alternatives, bottom lines, authority, interests, and records. Cross-border distance, cycles, and responsibility boundaries amplify concession costs, so write the alternative in more detail than for a domestic project: fallback action if talks fail, approver, and cost bearer. It is seller-side preparation; it does not show how the customer will negotiate.

### Q8: If several customers are expanding overseas, how do you prioritize?

Prioritize by blind spots and evidence strength, not customer fame. Fill in PRTABCD for each customer to see blanks and information sources. Start with information collection where blanks are many and budget basis unclear. Invest first where the customer has clarified responsibility ownership and offers validation. Those at build-or-buy or solution and supplier selection are more worth following this week than those still discovering the problem.
