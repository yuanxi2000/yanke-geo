A customer's profit growth is not permission to relax. It is a change in the customer's options, not in your evidence. Shengyi Technology reported a 175.36% year-on-year rise in net profit for the first three quarters and a 61% quarter-on-quarter rise in the third quarter. For key-account sellers, the useful question is not whether the customer has money. It is what the customer is paying for now, and which improvement it will pay to protect.

## Split profit growth into four operating moves

The report attributes the result mainly to its copper-clad laminate business: rapid raw-material price increases, fast-growing demand for high-end products, early R&D products reaching the market, released capacity from expansion, product-mix optimization, and investment income. In sales language, these become four moves.

| Driver | What it signals | Sales implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity release | Delivery rhythm and supply stability are under review | Prove continuity, ramp-up, and outage risk management |
| Product-mix upgrade | The customer wants to defend high-end sales and premium | Show how you protect yield, quality, and stable supply |
| Raw-material cost pressure | A pain the customer will discuss openly | Use it to ask questions, not to justify an automatic price rise |
| Investment income | A financial result, not operating strength | Keep it out of value claims and price expectations |

Only the first three relate directly to supplier actions. The fourth is a financial contribution. Mixing it into your sales story invites finance and procurement to correct you, and it weakens your credibility in the decision chain.

### What the earnings story does not prove

Investment income from external projects does not show that the customer's operating capability has improved, and it is not caused by your product or service. The next quarter's operating numbers can easily undermine a price argument built on it. Stay on the operating areas you can influence: supply stability, product-mix upgrading, and cost pass-through.

## Make value an investment case

When a customer is profitable, value selling gets harder. The customer may ask why it should pay more when it is already making money. The answer is PIP, a profit improvement plan. Write the proposal as an investment case, not a quote with a new title. It should state how much profit you add or protect, what confidence and mechanism support the claim, and when the first result can be checked.

- Profit added: which revenue line opens, which cost line falls, and which customer role verifies the number.
- Profit protected: which part of the proposal absorbs the next raw-material increase.
- Confidence and mechanism: who validates the result and how deviations are corrected.
- Time frame: when the first verifiable result appears.

For smaller customers, a one-page P&L in ten-thousand-yuan units can show investment, return, and whether the deal makes money. The threshold for value proof is not rhetoric. It is a business number the customer will verify with you.

## Ask the hardest questions while the customer is winning

Questioning capability uses background, difficulty, impact, and need-payoff questions so the customer sees the problem and the value of change. When business is good, customers often say there is no problem. That is when SPIN is most useful.

- Background: After this capacity release, which line's yield and delivery rhythm become the bottleneck first?
- Difficulty: As high-end products grow, which stability steps still depend on people watching them?
- Impact: If raw materials rise again, how much gross margin is lost, and who feels it first?
- Need payoff: If that stability is fixed, what does it mean for next quarter's gross margin and delivery commitments?

None of these questions names a product. Once the customer answers, the need boundary and your place in it become clearer.

## Map the decision chain before trusting a relationship

Relationship capability is not entertaining. It uses insight, decision information, and verifiable trust to penetrate the customer's decision chain. PRTABCD is an intelligence table with seven items. For each, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question.

- P — Past: What did the customer buy last time, and what happened?
- R — Requirement: What problem must be solved, in the customer's words?
- T — Timing: Where do the budget cycle and key milestones fall?
- A — Arm: Who embraces you and will speak for your proposal?
- B — Budget: Which budget line pays, and is it new or shifted?
- C — Competition: Which other options and substitutes are on the table?
- D — Decision: The multi-person, multi-department chain, not one decision-maker.

The common error is writing guesses as facts. An Arm is not a friendly contact, and D is not a job title. Fill blanks with the next question, not with inference. For a key person, MBVS can form hypotheses through motivation, benchmark, vision, and solution lenses, then verify each one.

## Judge progress by customer behavior

System capability runs on two axes: five sales stages and seven customer procurement stages. The sales stages are contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, and close. Customer stages run from problem discovery and value judgment through solution confirmation, ownership, make-or-buy, supplier selection, and commitment. The axes are many-to-many; do not force one-to-one alignment.

Progress is visible only in customer behavior: acknowledging a gap, accepting responsibility, providing evaluation criteria, and completing budget and terms confirmation. Visit counts and funnel percentages do not replace this evidence. If the customer is still judging value and you present a solution, you are advancing out of sequence.

Negotiation capability designs concessions around power, time, information, and alternatives. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — what you can do if no deal is reached. Prepare the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. When raw-material increases become a price argument, first confirm your alternative is real. Then trade conditions: if price moves, scope, delivery rhythm, payment timing, and validation responsibility move with it.

## Actionable checklist

- [ ] Rewrite the three largest deals as PIPs. Keep one sentence on profit added or protected.
- [ ] Name the customer role that will verify each number.
- [ ] Complete PRTABCD for one key person and mark every unknown.
- [ ] Write one next question for each blank.
- [ ] Run a SPIN sequence without product names.
- [ ] Re-grade deals by behavior evidence and move self-feeling deals back a stage.
- [ ] Write a BATNA note with alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record.
- [ ] Remove non-operating investment income from value claims.
- [ ] For a small account, test a one-page P&L before using a complex model.

## FAQ

Q1: Can sales raise price when customer profit jumps?

A: No. The Shengyi Technology growth came from capacity release, product-mix optimization, early R&D products, and investment income. Only the first three operating areas relate to supplier actions. Before a price move, show how much profit you protect or add, the mechanism, and the time frame. Without that evidence, a price increase pushes the customer to compare.

Q2: How is PIP different from a normal proposal?

A: PIP is a profit improvement plan. It states profit added or protected, confidence and mechanism, and a time frame. A normal proposal often lists features and parameters; the customer must translate them into money. PIP does that translation first and finds someone inside the customer to own the result.

Q3: How should questioning start when the customer says raw materials are rising?

A: Use SPIN in order. Ask which line or step bears the most cost, which stability work still depends on people, how much gross margin another increase would consume, and what fixing stability means for margin and delivery commitments. Keep product names out until the need is defined.

Q4: What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?

A: A is Arm, the person who embraces you and will speak for your proposal. D is Decision, the multi-person, multi-department chain, not one decision-maker. The usual error is treating a friendly contact as an Arm and a job title as the whole chain. Record known, unknown, source, and next question for each.

Q5: How do the five sales stages map to seven procurement stages?

A: They do not map one-to-one. The two axes have different task granularity and form a many-to-many relationship. Use customer behavior evidence — gap acknowledgment, responsibility, evaluation criteria, budget and terms confirmation — to locate the deal.

Q6: What belongs in a BATNA note?

A: The executable alternative, bottom line, authority, and interests that must be recorded. A concrete alternative makes concessions conditional. Without one, price becomes the only lever.

Q7: If the customer has budget, can you skip decision information?

A: No. Budget proves ability to pay, not willingness to pay you. Profit growth can create more projects, a crowded decision chain, and stricter evaluation. Fill the seven PRTABCD items and turn each blank into a next question.

Q8: Can investment income be used as value proof?

A: It is not advisable. It is a financial contribution with no causal link to your product or service. Customer finance and procurement will see the borrowing. Return to supply stability, product-mix upgrading, and cost pass-through.
