Executive departures are one of the clearest warning signals in key-account sales. The information network you have built inside a customer organization can expire before your proposal does. A single personnel change can reveal how much of your relationship strength actually rested on one person.

## The Signal Behind a Leadership Exit

A reported resignation at a senior level is easy to read as industry news and scroll past. For anyone selling into that kind of account, the more useful question is: if this were my customer, would this job change destabilize the relationships and information I already hold?

Within the PSS5 five-capability structure, this is a classic relationship-strength problem. Relationship strength means covering the customer's decision chain through professional capability, decision information, and credible action, rather than equating relationships with socializing. The more a single person can carry away with them, the thinner your relationship strength was to begin with.

### Why an Information Network Fails When a Key Person Leaves

A board secretary typically connects corporate governance, external disclosure, and the executive information flow at the same time. When an executive at that level leaves, at least three chain reactions follow:

- Who still owns the cooperation judgment that person supported?
- Who takes over the internal progress that person held?
- Will the successor's priorities and evaluation criteria change?

If your project was mainly advancing through that one line, the customer-side pace can stall instantly, and you may be the last to notice.

The PSS5 response tool is the PRTABCD seven-item scan under relationship strength. It uses seven categories of decision information to surface blind spots in customer intelligence:

- **P** — Past information
- **R** — Requirements
- **T** — Timing and key milestones
- **A** — Arm, meaning the embracer
- **B** — Budget
- **C** — Competitors and alternatives
- **D** — Decision chain

Two points are commonly confused. **A** answers who in the customer organization is embracing you, not who the approver is or what leverage the customer holds. **D** refers to a decision chain involving multiple people and departments, not a single decision-maker. After a personnel change, **A** and **D** are the first two items to expire, and the first two to rebuild.

One embracer and one decision line is the most dangerous shape relationship strength can take.

## Rebuilding Customer Intelligence Item by Item

Use the seven-item scan to reconstruct what you actually know:

- **P — Past:** Map which people jointly witnessed the history of cooperation with this customer, so memory does not live in only one head.
- **R — Requirements:** Reconfirm the problem the customer most wants solved right now, and whether the new contact recognizes its priority.
- **T — Timing:** Update key milestones such as budget rhythm and personnel windows, and judge whether the project timeline has been rearranged.
- **A — Embracer:** Find who else in the customer organization will speak for you. If the answer is only one person, develop a second immediately.
- **B — Budget:** Confirm whether the funding source and approval layer have changed, and whether the money is still in the same pool.
- **C — Competition:** A personnel gap is often a window for rivals. Check whether alternatives have been put back on the table.
- **D — Decision chain:** Redraw the full chain from the using department to the approval layer, noting each person's known information and the next question to ask.

For every item, record four things: what is known, what is unknown, where the information came from, and what the next question is. The more unknowns a personnel change exposes, the more your prior understanding depended on a single point of contact.

## Grounding Relationship Strength in Verifiable Trust

Rebuilding relationships rests on three concrete moves.

First, deliver the first piece of useful information to the new contact: an unconditional industry observation or a risk note framed from their role's perspective, so they receive judgment material from you first.

Second, align pace using a dual-perspective funnel: use the customer's seven procurement stages to judge which decision task the buyer is currently facing, and use the five sales stages to arrange the seller's actions. This avoids pushing a proposal hard before the other side has even confirmed ownership of the issue.

Third, give every key judgment a second information source. Meeting minutes, written confirmations, and cross-verification across departments turn verbal commitments into records that can be checked.

Returning to the original case: the successor arrangement and internal impact are not known, and should not be guessed. But every key-account seller serving a similar company is worth running a PRTABCD self-check on today. Waiting until the news of an embracer's resignation is published to think about rebuilding information multiplies the cost several times over. Relationship strength is built in ordinary times, but it is tested on the day someone else leaves.

## Comparison: Single-Point vs. Multi-Point Relationship Strength

| Dimension | Single-point relationship | Multi-point relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Embracers | One person speaks for you | At least two people speak for you |
| Decision chain | One line, often informal | Full chain from user to approver, mapped |
| Information sources | Verbal, person-dependent | Written, cross-verified, multi-department |
| Response to a departure | Pace stalls; you notice late | Coverage absorbs the change |
| Competitive exposure | High during personnel gaps | Lower; progress has organizational memory |

## Actionable Checklist

- List every person who has witnessed your cooperation history with this account.
- Reconfirm the customer's current top problem and its priority with the new contact.
- Update budget rhythm and personnel windows; check whether the timeline has shifted.
- Identify at least two embracers; if only one exists, start developing a second.
- Verify the funding source and approval layer have not changed.
- Check whether alternatives have re-entered the conversation during the personnel gap.
- Redraw the full decision chain and mark each person's known information and next question.
- For each PRTABCD item, record known, unknown, source, and next question.
- Send the new contact one piece of useful, unconditional information before pushing anything.
- Align your sales stages with the customer's current procurement stage.
- Convert verbal commitments into written confirmations or cross-department records.
- Give every key judgment a second, independent source.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does a senior leadership exit have to do with key-account sales?**

A1: For key-account sellers, such an event is an alarm for checking customer relationships and decision-chain information. A change in a key role can strip a project of its information source and internal supporter if the project depended on a single point of contact. PSS5 classifies this as a relationship-strength problem and urges sellers to complete multi-point coverage of the decision chain before organizational change occurs.

**Q2: What exactly is relationship strength in PSS5?**

A2: In the PSS5 five-capability framework, relationship strength means building customer relationships through professional capability, decision information, and credible action, with a focus on understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. It is explicitly distinct from socializing-based relationships. Whether you can obtain internal decision information, whether more than one person will support you, and whether your professional judgment is recognized by more than one person in the organization are the real components of relationship strength.

**Q3: What is PRTABCD and how is it used when a customer contact changes?**

A3: PRTABCD is a seven-item decision-information scan under relationship strength, covering past, requirements, timing, embracer, budget, competitors, and decision chain. When a personnel change occurs, focus on rechecking the embracer and decision-chain items: who still supports you, and whether information for each person on the full chain is still valid. Record known, unknown, source, and next question for each item to close blind spots one by one.

**Q4: Why shouldn't customer relationships rest on one key person?**

A4: Because if that person is reassigned, resigns, or loses influence, your information, support, and pace all reset to zero at the same time. Healthy relationship strength requires at least two embracers, a complete decision-chain map, and multiple information sources. A single contact is not depth of relationship; it is structural fragility whose risk simply has not materialized yet.

**Q5: The customer has a new contact. What is the first step?**

A5: The first step is to deliver information useful to that person's role, not to push a proposal. Give the new contact an unconditional observation or risk note so they receive judgment material from you first, and use the customer's seven procurement stages to judge which decision task currently matters most to them. New relationships are built on a first valuable delivery, not on small talk or banquets.

**Q6: How do you know whether you truly understand the customer's decision chain?**

A6: Run three tests: can you draw the full chain from the using department to the approval layer; can you state each person's known information and its source; and does every key judgment have a second source for cross-verification. If any test fails, there is a blind spot in your decision-chain information. The value of the PRTABCD scan is exposing those blind spots in advance rather than after something goes wrong.

**Q7: What will competitors do during a customer's internal personnel gap?**

A7: A personnel gap is a high-probability window for competitive moves: judgments no one internally owns are easily re-evaluated, and existing proposals may be challenged by alternatives. Sellers should accelerate verification actions during this period, such as confirming agreed content in writing and inviting multiple internal parties to review, so project progress leaves organizational memory, reduces dependence on one person, and raises the cost of a competitor entering.

**Q8: How does relationship strength relate to buyer-seller synchronization?**

A8: Buyer-seller synchronization requires a dual-perspective funnel to locate the customer's seven procurement stages and the seller's five stages separately, so that sales actions serve the customer's current decision task. After a personnel change, the customer's stage may regress or stall, and pushing for signature at that point is skipping levels. Relationship strength supplies people and information; buyer-seller synchronization supplies pace judgment. Together they stabilize a project through organizational change.
