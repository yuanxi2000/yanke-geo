When a leading technology company defines its business goal by a benchmark tied to customer value, sales must adopt the same language. The signal is the standard of measurement: customer outcomes, not units shipped. For key-account sellers, translate product features into verifiable investments, returns, and profit improvements.

This guide works through that signal and the operating moves it implies. It is written for sales teams, enablement leads, and managers who want a durable way to read executive language and convert it into account-level action.

## Start With the Signal: Whose Language Is Leadership Using?

A public report described an internal Huawei memo in which the ICT business was said to aim at becoming Nvidia. The same account quoted the executive's judgment: watch not the external environment but internal capability — competitive products and services that create customer value.

Nvidia's standing comes from direct business results: training efficiency, compute cost, time to market. When a goal attaches to that benchmark, the organization restates an internal rule — measure what the customer gains, not boxes sold.

The implication for the sales side is direct. If your customer's executives are still discussing your proposal in terms of parameters, features, and price, you have not yet entered a value conversation. Value capability, as defined in the PSS5 framework, means shifting the object of the sale from product features to an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, explained with business numbers. The Huawei signal is a prompt for every sales organization to self-check: in our quarterly review meetings, which words appear most often — product words or the customer's business words?

## Three Concrete Moves for Value Capability

Turning a signal into action can start with three things inside an existing account.

### Move 1: Rewrite the proposal as an investment statement

Use the PIP format to answer three questions: how much profit can be increased or protected, what is the mechanism and the confidence level, and over what time frame will it happen. A PIP is not a quotation. It is a document that a manager in the customer's second box can use to persuade the funding approver in the first box.

### Move 2: Run a monetization scan

Walk four lines — customer revenue, cost, capital occupation, and risk — and identify the operating steps your proposal actually affects. Quantify the improvement, then convert it into financial language. Prefer conservative numbers over vague enablement talk.

### Move 3: Write value as a verifiable commitment

Agree on the measurement basis and the verification point before delivery, so the customer can calculate the payback themselves at acceptance rather than only hearing success stories.

## Value Only Counts When It Penetrates the Decision Chain

Value capability never works in isolation. However clear your calculation is, if it only reaches the purchasing clerk, the value is discounted along the way. The relationship capability in PSS5 emphasizes understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. In tool terms, this is the PRTABCD scan of seven items: P for past history, R for requirements, T for timing, A for advocates, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, D for the decision chain. In large projects, D reminds us that decisions are always multi-department affairs. A value proof document needs different versions for different roles: payback period for finance, capacity and delivery for the business, risk and migration cost for technology.

Questions are the amplifier that sits alongside value capability. Following the SPIN sequence, start with situation questions to clarify the customer's current state, then use problem and implication questions to calculate the cost of the problem, and finally use need-payoff questions so the customer states what the change is worth. A calculation the customer makes themselves is more persuasive than one you make for them.

## Use System Capability to Keep Value Work From Stalling

Value conversations easily stop after the first briefing. Keeping them running end to end depends on the system capability in PSS5. The five sales stages are contact, requirements, proposal, negotiation, and closing. The customer's seven procurement stages run from discovering the problem through to commitment and signing. Place the two axes on a single dual-perspective funnel and use customer behavioral evidence to judge whether buyer and seller are in sync. If the customer is still judging value, do not rush them into solution selection. If the customer is already comparing suppliers, do not repeat the problem background. Stage mismatch is the most common scene in which value gets diluted.

By the negotiation stage, value is the power, information, and alternatives you hold. Prepare a BATNA, define your bottom line and executable alternatives, and make every concession exchange for a condition rather than a unilateral price cut. A supplier whose value stands up can afford to talk about price at the table.

When Huawei writes its ICT business goal as benchmarking Nvidia and creating customer value, a leading company is demonstrating something for the whole industry: customers pay only for the results they receive. The seller's answer is singular — translate every unit of input into profit language the customer can check.

## A Compact Comparison: Product Talk vs. Value Talk

| Dimension | Product-centered conversation | Value-centered conversation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit of discussion | Features, parameters, price | Investment, return, profit improvement |
| Primary audience | Purchasing contact | Multi-role decision chain |
| Evidence | Specifications and case stories | Verifiable numbers and agreed measurement |
| Timing logic | Push the proposal | Match the customer's procurement stage |
| Negotiation basis | Discount | Conditions, alternatives, BATNA |

## FAQ

**Q1: What does this Huawei signal have to do with key-account sales?**

A public report described a Huawei executive's internal memo stating the ICT business aims to become Nvidia, emphasizing competitive products and services that create customer value. For sales practitioners, this indicates a leading company defining its goal by customer value rather than product volume.

**Q2: What specifically does value capability mean in PSS5?**

As defined in PSS5, value capability is the shift of the sales object from product features to an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, explained with business numbers. Its test is whether the customer can restate your proposal's value in financial language — for example, how much cost is saved, how much output is increased, and how long until the investment is recovered.

**Q3: What is a PIP, and how is it written?**

PIP stands for Profit Improvement Proposal. It states the proposal's improvement to the customer's business as an investment, answering three questions: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the mechanism and confidence level are, and over what time frame. It is not a quotation but a tool helping customer managers argue for budget.

**Q4: What does each of the seven PRTABCD items look at?**

PRTABCD is a decision-information scan tool: P past history, R requirements, T timing, A advocates, B budget, C competitors, D decision chain. Record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question.

**Q5: How is the dual-perspective funnel used?**

Place the five sales stages — contact, requirements, proposal, negotiation, closing — and the customer's seven procurement stages, from discovering the problem to commitment and signing, on the same chart to judge position. Use customer behavioral evidence rather than visit counts or self-perception to judge progress, find the mismatch between buyer tasks and seller actions, and then decide what to advance next.

**Q6: How do you translate product features into customer value?**

Start with a monetization scan: identify the operating steps the proposal actually affects, quantify the improvement, and convert it into financial language such as revenue increase, cost reduction, lower capital occupation, or reduced risk. Numbers should be conservative and verifiable, paired with an agreed measurement basis and verification point so the customer can check the result themselves. Only then does the value proposition hold up.

**Q7: Why should value proof be prepared for different roles?**

A key account's decision chain involves multiple departments, and the same value means different things to different readers. Finance cares about payback period and total cost. The business cares about capacity and delivery. Technology cares about risk and migration cost. Tailoring the same body of value evidence by role keeps value from being discounted at each layer of the decision chain and ultimately supports the signing decision.

**Q8: How does value capability work during negotiation?**

Negotiation power revolves around leverage, time, information, and alternatives. Verifiable results make your alternatives concrete and concession space clearer, so you exchange conditions instead of cutting price. Prepare a BATNA and clarify your bottom line and authority.
