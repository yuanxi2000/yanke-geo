## A Mega-Deal That Mirrors Your Biggest Deals

According to Xinhua reporting, Paramount Skydance announced on October 6 that it had completed its roughly $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, one of the largest media mergers in American history, following months of competitive bidding and legal contention.

For key-account sellers, this is more than entertainment news. A major acquisition moves through the same decision sequence as a large B2B sale: surface the gap, judge the value, confirm a solution, assign responsibility, compare alternatives, secure commitment. The parallel is structural — a public example of a complex, multi-stakeholder decision.

This guide applies a five-capability lens — value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation — using the deal's public facts to show where large-account pursuits quietly fail.

## Value Capability: Why Would a Buyer Pay That Price?

The reported price was a bet on the combined group's operating picture, not payment for a list of assets. What the acquirer reportedly presented was the merged entity's revenue structure, cost synergies, and competitive position — not a count of film-library titles. That is the essence of value capability: translating your offering into the customer's language of profit, investment, and business performance. If your proposal is only a feature list, you have downgraded an investment case into a product brochure.

To build this capability:

- Write your offer's business impact as profit language: how much profit it adds or protects, the mechanism and confidence behind the estimate, and the timeframe.
- Use the customer's own operating numbers, not generic industry benchmarks.
- Distinguish the capital-allocation layer (who releases funds) from the operational manager layer (who runs the budget daily). Equip the operational layer to argue the business value upward.

If you cannot answer "how much, by what mechanism, by when" in the customer's own numbers, the deal will eventually be decided on price — because price is the only language left.

## Relationship and Questioning: The Information Contest Behind Months of Bidding

Public reporting shows the outcome, not the private maneuvering. But months of competitive bidding almost certainly involved repeated exchange of terms on price, conditions, and regulatory risk. In your deals, that invisible phase is where relationship and questioning capability are won or lost.

Relationship capability is coverage of the customer's decision chain through professional insight, decision-relevant information, and verifiable trust. Questioning capability is deliberate question design that lets the customer see the problem and the value of change for themselves.

A practical tool is the PRTABCD seven-item scan of decision information:

- **P — Past:** the customer's history with you and this kind of initiative.
- **R — Requirement:** what they actually need, versus what they initially ask for.
- **T — Time:** the real timeline and decision windows.
- **A — Advocates:** who inside actively embraces you — which may be nobody.
- **B — Budget:** where the money sits and how it is released.
- **C — Competitor:** who else is considered and on what basis.
- **D — Decision chain:** the collective, multi-person, multi-department process — not one decision-maker you can flatter into signing.

For each item, record what is known, unknown, and who to ask next.

## Negotiation Capability: Three Lessons From the Legal Wrangling

Once a deal lands in extended legal contention, negotiation has stopped being about price alone. Effective negotiation is built around power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives — designing conditional trades toward a durable agreement. Three transferable actions stand out.

### 1. Prepare Your BATNA Before You Need It

Your best alternative to a negotiated agreement is what you do if no deal happens. The more executable your alternative, the more genuine your confidence. Specify your alternative, bottom line, authority limits, underlying interests, and agreed terms so far.

### 2. Every Concession Must Buy Something

Concede price only for shorter payment terms; narrow scope only for faster signature. Unilateral concessions do not create goodwill — they reset the anchor lower for the next round. Treat every give as one half of a trade.

### 3. Time Is Both Leverage and Risk

Months of contested bidding carry costs for both parties; whoever can afford to wait longer holds more power. Before negotiating, calculate your time floor — how long you can sustain the pursuit. The party that has not done this arithmetic concedes first.

## System Capability: Managing Honest Progress

Even the largest transaction proceeds stage by stage. System capability means managing progression with a dual-lens map: your five-stage sales funnel (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close) on one side, the customer's buying stages from problem recognition through commitment on the other. Before every next step, ask: What buying task is the customer working on right now? Does my next action serve that task, or am I trying to skip stages?

Deals out of sync do not advance no matter how many meetings you hold. Judge progress by customer behavior — acknowledging the problem, accepting ownership, entering evaluation — not by visit counts or CRM percentages. On a mismatch, step back to the customer's current decision task and redesign from there.

## The Five Capabilities at a Glance

| Capability | Core question | Signature practices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | Why buy at this price? | Profit language, customer's own numbers, arguing value upward |
| Relationship | Who carries our case inside? | Decision-chain coverage, insight, verifiable trust |
| Questioning | What do we not know? | PRTABCD scan, deliberate question design |
| System | How do we manage progress? | Dual-lens funnel, customer-behavior evidence |
| Negotiation | How do we close durably? | BATNA, conditional concessions, time analysis |

Value answers why buy; relationship and questioning answer how you advance; negotiation answers how you close; system answers how you manage. Score your largest deal on each — your weakest is your next training priority. Before your next pursuit review: rewrite the proposal's opening as a profit statement (amount, mechanism, timeframe); complete a PRTABCD scan; name at least one active advocate (if you cannot, that is the top gap); write your BATNA in executable terms; specify what each of your next three concessions buys; set your time bottom line; and replace one CRM percentage with a customer-behavior evidence point in your forecast.

## FAQ

**What is PSS5?**
PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a key-account sales methodology built on five dimensions — value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation capability — covering the full arc from building trust and uncovering needs to managing the process and reaching agreement.

**What is the most direct lesson from this acquisition?**
Large deals are won on overall strength across all five capabilities, not a final push. The acquisition reportedly took months of bidding and legal contention — by which time the value case, decision-chain intelligence, and alternatives had long shaped the outcome. Pre-signature quality of work matters more than a last-minute discount.

**What does negotiation capability mean in PSS5?**
Designing concessions and conditional exchanges on a clear read of power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives. The weight is on preparation — leverage, time pressure, knowledge, floors, and each side's best alternative — not in-the-moment rhetoric.

**What does BATNA mean?**
Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement: your best action if no deal is reached. Prepare it concretely; the more executable it is, the less you depend on the other party's yes.

**How do I use the PRTABCD scan?**
Run it across Past, Requirement, Time, Advocates, Budget, Competitor, and Decision chain, recording what is known, unknown, and your next question and source. It is meant for the contact and needs stages and should be updated repeatedly, not filled in once and archived.

**How is relationship capability different from wining and dining?**
It builds on professional competence, decision information, and credible action. Hospitality can buy dinner, but not knowledge of who holds budget, who decides, or a key person's advocacy in a review meeting. Durable relationships rest on professional value and verifiable trust.
