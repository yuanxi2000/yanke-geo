General Motors' plan to use AI to halve new-vehicle development time is a useful key-account sales case, but not because it creates an automatic AI buying signal. The source points to a narrower change: the customer may be altering how it develops vehicles, validates design changes, and runs digital-twin factories. The first task is to locate the customer's purchasing job and decide what evidence must exist before a proposal, validation plan, or commercial commitment makes sense.

A headline such as half the development cycle can easily become a reason to act. Separate the customer's operating goal from a confirmed purchase commitment. The public information in the source names General Motors as the subject, but provides no counterpart, amount, date, or verified commercial result. Real opportunities often have similar gaps. Do not fill them with a story, and do not present cycle reduction as realized profit. Record what is known, what is unknown, where it came from, and what question comes next.

## Start with the customer's job, not the AI story

An AI-led pitch flattens a specific operating problem into a generic capability story. Here, the concrete landing points are development cycle, digital-twin factory operations, and design-change validation. A better discovery sequence asks who recognizes the cycle problem, who judges whether investment is worthwhile, who confirms that an external solution can be implemented, and who owns the result. These are decision actions, not personas on an org chart.

Keep a simple record while the picture is incomplete:

- Known: the customer plans to use AI to shorten new-vehicle development time; the source ties the case to development cycle, digital-twin factory, and design-change validation.
- Unknown: buying entity, budget, timing, validation standard, and commercial owner.
- Source: public information in the supplied article, not customer confirmation.
- Next question: which validation step would prove that an external approach can work on the customer's line?

This keeps the opportunity from inflating and gives the next meeting a factual basis.

## Use a dual-perspective funnel to test real progress

PSS5's system capability places seller progression and buyer decision-making on the same map. The seller side has five stages: contact, demand, solution, negotiation, and close. The buyer side has seven: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming who owns the result, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting the solution and supplier, and committing or signing. The axes are many-to-many; do not force a row-by-row match.

| What to compare | Seller-side view | Buyer-side view | Practical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Progression | Contact, demand, solution, negotiation, close | Discover problem, judge value, confirm solution, confirm result ownership, build or buy, select solution and supplier, commit and sign | Map the account without assuming one seller stage equals one buyer stage |
| Evidence | Customer actions, not visit counts | Joint validation, shared data definitions, written criteria, named result owner | If evidence is missing, the opportunity stays where it is |
| Next move | Qualify before proposing or conceding | Clarify budget range, validation path, and signing conditions | Link every concession to a customer commitment |

Progress appears in customer behavior: R&D, manufacturing, and procurement joining validation; existing data definitions being shared; validation criteria written into review materials; a named result owner. Visit counts cannot replace these signals.

### Three evidence checks before price or commitment

Before discussing price or asking for a commitment, test three points:

1. The customer acknowledges that the development-cycle problem is worth resources.
2. The customer confirms that an external solution can pass validation and reach the production line.
3. The customer makes the result owner, budget range, and signing conditions clear.

If any one lacks evidence, the project remains where it is. Fill the gap first; then price and commitment have a firmer rhythm.

## Value capability: turn AI into the customer's investment case

Value capability means translating product functions into the customer's language of profit, investment, and operating improvement. In an AI-linked R&D project, sales can discuss how a shorter development cycle may affect capital tied up, line-validation cost, design-rework risk, and launch cadence. It must not invent a percentage, monetary amount, or payback period. Those numbers belong to the customer's data.

Write a Profit Improvement Plan, or PIP, with the customer. A useful PIP answers:

- How much profit can be increased or protected?
- What is the confidence level, and through what mechanism will improvement occur?
- Over what time frame should it be realized?

When customer data is missing, treat the case as a hypothesis. List the data source, validation method, and owner before discussing the solution. This prevents a technical capability from being presented as a profit result.

## Relationship and questioning: find the Arm, map the Decision chain

Relationship capability is not entertainment. It is coverage of the decision chain through professional insight, decision information, and credible action. PRTABCD scans seven pieces of decision information: Past, Requirement, Timing, Arm, Budget, Competition and alternatives, and Decision chain. A is Arm: the person or group that embraces the change and is willing to act. D is Decision: the multi-person, multi-department chain that decides. A is not authority or approval power, and D is not a single decision-maker.

Questioning capability can use SPIN to move from background to problem, implication, and need-payoff. Narrow the conversation around the customer's purchasing job. If an answer reveals a new owner, timing constraint, or validation standard, add it to the next scan.

Useful questions include:

- Where does your current validation of new-vehicle development time most often get stuck?
- If a design change is found only at the production line, what does that do to cost and launch timing?
- What data and which people must be present before your team judges an external solution feasible?
- If this problem is not resolved this year, what will R&D and manufacturing continue to pay?

Convert answers into decision information. Real progress appears when the customer shares data, brings key people into the room, or agrees to a next validation step.

## Negotiation: exchange conditions, not unilateral discounts

Negotiation capability rests on power, time, information, and alternatives. In an R&D digital project, price is only one condition. Scope, validation period, payment milestones, data responsibility, acceptance criteria, and risk sharing can all be exchanged. Concessions should be tied to customer commitments. A concession that buys only thanks is a discount.

Before negotiation, prepare a BATNA—the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Write down the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. This gives concessions a boundary and makes commitments executable.

## Actionable checklist for sales leaders

Use this checklist in opportunity reviews and coaching:

- [ ] Write evidence for each of the seven buyer stages. Do not report a win probability for a stage that lacks evidence.
- [ ] Rewrite each AI proposal as a PIP. Put profit and investment impact first; put the feature list second.
- [ ] Review customer behavior changes, not sales self-evaluation.
- [ ] Name the Arm, the Decision chain, the next validation event, and the result owner for every active opportunity.
- [ ] Keep a known/unknown/source/next-question record when public information is incomplete.

These actions do not add a slogan. They pull judgment back to the customer's task. When a customer changes how it develops products, sales must change how it asks, validates, and commits.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the main reminder from the GM case?**
Start with the customer's purchasing task, not your AI solution. The case shows a possible change in R&D and validation; it does not show who confirmed the problem, judged value, or accepted responsibility.

**Q2: Why can't halving the cycle be a direct closing reason?**
A shorter cycle is an operating goal, not a purchase commitment. Sales still needs evidence that the customer believes an external solution can deliver, will commit resources, has a result owner, and has clear budget and acceptance conditions.

**Q3: How should value capability be used?**
Translate AI into investment and operating improvement. Discuss cycle time, capital tied up, validation cost, rework risk, and launch cadence, then validate data definitions. Without customer data, write hypotheses; do not invent percentages, amounts, or payback.

**Q4: Whom should relationship capability find first?**
Use PRTABCD to scan decision information, then find the Arm—the person or group willing to support the change, arrange validation, and provide data. Then map the Decision chain across R&D, manufacturing, procurement, finance, and other roles.

**Q5: How do we know a project is making real progress?**
Look for customer behavior: key roles joining validation, data definitions being shared, success criteria entering review materials, and a named owner and budget range. Compare those signals with the five seller and seven buyer stages; fill missing evidence instead of jumping ahead.

**Q6: What should be prepared for negotiation?**
Prepare a BATNA with an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Then exchange price, scope, validation period, payment milestones, data responsibility, and acceptance criteria against customer commitments.

**Q7: What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?**
A is Arm, the person or group embracing you in the customer organization. D is Decision, the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. A is not authority or the approver, and D is not a single decision-maker.
