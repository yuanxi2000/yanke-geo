When a state-owned transportation group strings 19 campsites and hotel projects along a border highway, the story looks like travel news. For B2B sales teams, it reads differently: it is a public demonstration of how to turn scattered resources into an asset base that can be financed, built, and operated — and then sold into.

## What the Source Actually Establishes

According to a September 21, 2026 report from East Money, Longjiang Jiaotou Group has coordinated the layout of 19 campsite and hotel projects since 2021. These form a province-wide self-driving and RV living network, anchored by the Harbin Yunliang River Valley Camp Park as a distribution hub and supported by nodes along the G331 border highway. The network is described as an important carrier of Heilongjiang's transportation-tourism integration.

In the same period, Simao District in Pu'er, Yunnan, built a resource library of potential plots for RV living services. That library catalogued 48 plots covering more than 1,140 mu, with two key campsites already completed.

The two approaches differ, but the underlying logic is consistent: government bodies and state-owned enterprises first consolidate resources into assets that can be marketed for investment and operated, then attract service providers to enter. For B2B teams selling equipment, campsite systems, or operational services, this is a clear signal that demand is taking shape.

## Why This Is a Value-Force Lesson, Not a Travel Story

PSS5 defines value force as the ability to shift the sales conversation away from product features and toward the customer's investment, returns, and profit improvement — explained in business numbers. In campsite projects, the customer side is typically a transportation investment group, a city investment company, or a local cultural tourism platform. What they care about is never how durable your RV water and power pedestals are. They care about three questions:

- How much revenue can each square meter of this land generate?
- How many years until the investment is recovered?
- What happens in the off-season?

Whoever calculates this account for the customer first sits at the head of the solution table.

### Translating Products into a Revenue Model

The source suggests packaging water and power pedestals, RVs, and campsite management systems into a revenue-per-unit-area model rather than an equipment list. It also recommends using public layout data — such as the 19-project network — to reverse-engineer the customer group's expansion rhythm and estimate possible procurement windows over the next 12 months. Off-season occupancy, secondary spending, and government subsidy application conditions should be written into the profit calculation so that finance-side stakeholders can understand it. A one-page input-output summary in the style of a "ten-thousand-yuan profit and loss statement" can help account managers fight for budget when reporting internally.

## The Other Four Forces in Support

Value force alone is not enough. State-owned enterprise projects have long decision chains and many participants. The D item in the PRTABCD seven-item scan reminds you that the decision chain involves multiple people and departments — planning, operations, finance, and supervision each look at different accounts. The A item asks: who inside the customer organization is embracing you? If no internal person is willing to speak for you by the solution stage, the profit calculation may just be self-indulgence.

- **System force:** Use the five sales stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing — against the customer's seven procurement stages to judge whether they are still at problem discovery or already judging value. Do not hand over a solution before the customer has claimed responsibility.
- **Questioning force:** Ask less about whether they need campsite equipment. Ask more about how much accommodation revenue this land is losing each year given current traffic, so the customer calculates the cost of inaction themselves.
- **Relationship force:** Use local cultural tourism plans and traffic data to prepare reporting materials for your internal sponsor, so the person embracing you has ammunition.
- **Negotiation force:** Before entering negotiation, write down your BATNA, clarify your bottom line, what you can concede, and what conditions must be exchanged. Avoid being pushed through by a single fixed price.

## Three Actions for Key-Account Sales

When you see signals like RV camping heating up, the source recommends three things within the same week:

1. Circle the list of state-owned enterprises and government platforms in your territory that are working on transportation-tourism integration or campsite planning.
2. Rewrite your product introduction into a one-page profit-language version, replacing the feature-and-specification table.
3. Use PRTABCD to list what you know, what you do not know, and your next question for the target customer, then arrange first contact.

The window for RV camping will not wait. The customer's asset base is already on the table. It comes down to whose account reaches the customer's heart first.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the most direct signal for B2B sales when RV camping heats up?**

A1: Government and state-owned enterprises are starting to integrate land, roads, and consumption resources into campsite assets, which means demand has moved from concept to investment stage. Moves like Longjiang Jiaotou's 19 campsite and hotel projects mean procurement demand for equipment, systems, and operational services will be released. Sales teams should lock onto target customers early and prepare value proposals.

**Q2: How does PSS5's value force land in campsite projects?**

A2: Value force requires translating product features into the customer's investment, returns, and profit improvement. For campsite projects, that means packaging water and power pedestals and management systems into a revenue-per-unit-area model, calculating payback period and off-season risk, so management sees the business account rather than just equipment parameters and a quotation.

**Q3: What should decision-chain scanning focus on for state-owned cultural tourism projects?**

A3: Use the PRTABCD seven-item scan, focusing on item D, the decision chain. These projects typically involve planning, operations, finance, and supervision departments, each caring about different accounts. Record known information, unknown blind spots, and the next question item by item to judge what stage you are at and whom to confirm what with.

**Q4: What is a ten-thousand-yuan profit and loss statement, and when is it used?**

A4: It is a value tool for smaller customers or single projects, using one profit and loss statement to clarify inputs, returns, and whether the project makes money. It fits scenarios like single campsite investment or regional agent calculations. It helps the customer's internal sponsor quickly fight for budget when reporting to the group.

**Q5: How do you judge which procurement stage the customer is in?**

A5: PSS5 uses a dual-perspective funnel comparing the customer's seven procurement stages against the five sales stages. Look at behavioral evidence: if the customer is still discussing whether the campsite should be built, they are at problem discovery or value judgment. If they are already comparing suppliers, they are at selection. Seller actions must match buyer position; avoid skipping stages.

**Q6: How does questioning force show up in cultural tourism customer visits?**

A6: Advance through the four SPIN question types: first understand local traffic and campsite status, then ask where the existing accommodation capacity gap is, then guide the customer to estimate annual lost revenue, and finally confirm what closing the gap would mean for business goals. Let the customer state the cost of change themselves — far more persuasive than the salesperson saying it.

**Q7: What preparation should be done before entering business negotiation?**

A7: First, per negotiation force, write down your BATNA — the best alternative action if no deal is reached — and clarify your bottom line, what you can concede, and what conditions must be exchanged. State-owned projects often demand a single fixed price. Prepare a condition-exchange list in advance, such as payment rhythm for price space, to protect profit while reaching agreement.

**Q8: What should be done in the first week after spotting this kind of opportunity?**

A8: Do three things: circle the list of state-owned enterprises and government platforms in your territory that are laying out campsites or integrating transportation and tourism; rewrite your product into a one-page profit-language introduction; use PRTABCD to list what you know, what you do not know, and your next question for the target customer, then arrange first contact. Move fast, because early movers during the demand-formation period are more likely to enter the solution-definition stage.
