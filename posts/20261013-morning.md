When a customer announces full capacity and further investment, treat it as operating numbers, not background news. This guide uses a public case — a tool manufacturer reporting full utilization in Thailand and a planned 423 million yuan investment for a new line with 7 million DC lithium-battery power tools annually, funds committed in stages — to show how to convert a headline into a structured sales conversation. Facts come from a public financial news report; everything beyond is an open question.

## Start by Confirming What the Numbers Actually Mean

Before any positioning or proposal work, three business questions need to be answered from the customer's side.

First, which product line does the incremental capacity serve? A stated annual output implies a specific product family. Second, how will staged funding create a buying rhythm? Phased money tends to produce phased budgets and approvals. Third, which payment terms are affected by cash discipline? The report notes cash-flow pressure is manageable, suggesting cash outlays are watched and payment terms likely on the table.

This is where many sales conversations break down. When the customer is talking about full utilization, expansion, and staged capital, responding with equipment precision figures and parameter comparisons misses the point. The customer is discussing an investment and its return, not a specification sheet.

### Turning 7 Million Units into Conversational Units

The capacity figure, funding rhythm, and cash constraint each translate into a distinct conversation topic. Capacity increment raises questions about unit output, yield, and changeover efficiency — not specifications. Staged funding means budget and approval appear in batches, so align delivery and acceptance milestones. Cash constraint means payment terms and settlement conditions are explicit negotiation topics.

## Where the Five Capabilities Do Their Work

A structured framework helps assign each part of the announcement to a specific sales task. Under the PSS5 glossary definitions, the framework is composed of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability.

Division of labor: value capability converts the 423 million yuan figure into input-output language; questioning capability establishes expansion sequence and priority; relationship capability identifies the product-line owner; system capability uses sales-stage and procurement-stage models to judge the customer's position; negotiation capability prepares condition trades under staged funding.

| Dimension | What it addresses on this announcement |
|---|---|
| Value capability | Reading 423 million yuan as an investment-and-return equation |
| Questioning capability | Clarifying the sequence and priority of the expansion |
| Relationship capability | Finding the person who owns this product line |
| System capability | Judging the customer's current stage using the two stage models |
| Negotiation capability | Preparing condition trades under staged funding |

None of these dimensions produces an answer on its own. They produce better questions.

## Using a Decision-Information Grid to Expose Blind Spots

PSS5 defines a seven-category decision-information grid — commonly referred to by the acronym PRTABCD — for identifying intelligence gaps and recording, for each item, what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question to ask.

Applied to this announcement, few cells can be filled. Requirement direction is clear: a DC lithium-battery power tool line. Timing is known only as staged funding. The budget clue is the 423 million yuan figure, landing in batches. Competitors, alternatives, decision chain, and the internal champion have no public answers.

Writing these seven items into a table is more reliable than debating impressions in a meeting room. The point is not to fill the table with guesses; it is to make the gaps visible and assign a next question to each one.

## Four Actions You Can Take Immediately

- Place the customer's staged funding rhythm and your delivery and acceptance milestones on the same timeline, and mark what each tranche of money buys.
- Ask about the sequence and product-line priority of the expansion before describing what you can offer. Without a confirmed sequence, any recommendation is guesswork.
- Build a single conditions table covering payment terms, staged delivery, and acceptance standards, and mark which item can be conceded and what it should be exchanged for.
- Record “who is responsible for this line” as your next question rather than assuming support already exists.

## Write Down Your Alternative Before You Negotiate

PSS5 defines BATNA as the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — the best fallback action available to a negotiator if the parties cannot reach agreement. Preparation requires specifying an executable alternative, a bottom line, authority limits, interests, and a record.

In expansion projects, sales teams easily treat “we must win this” as a premise, and therefore arrive without an alternative. The result is that payment terms and price can only be accepted passively. Ranking concessions in advance helps: which item moves first, which item is exchanged for a condition, and which item stays fixed.

One important boundary: this public information describes the customer's capacity status and investment plan. It does not state how the investment will be procured or from whom. That part can only be verified through questioning and relationship work, not inferred from the announcement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is PSS5, and why is it useful for reading this expansion announcement?**

Under the PSS5 glossary definitions, PSS5 is a five-capability framework for professional key-account sales, composed of value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. It addresses why the customer is spending this money, who is making the decision, and where the process currently stands. The publicly reported 423 million yuan investment and 7 million-unit annual capacity plan form a set of operating numbers suitable for practicing these five dimensions.

**What does value capability specifically require here?**

It requires answering which part of the customer's operating result improves once the 423 million yuan is spent: unit output, yield, changeover efficiency, or unit cost. Under the PSS5 glossary definitions, value capability converts the sales subject from product functions into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, expressed in operating numbers. Without this, your quote reads as a cost item rather than a business case.

**The customer said only that the plant is running at full capacity. What can be read from that?**

Full utilization indicates existing lines are already at maximum load and that the near-term shortage is incremental capacity. The new project — a new line with 7 million units of annual capacity — indicates the increment is planned around a new product line. Order structure, yield levels, and per-unit cost are not in the public information; these belong to questioning territory and should not be filled in on the customer's behalf.

**How does staged funding affect quoting and delivery rhythm?**

Staged funding means budgets and approvals appear in batches. A single total price is difficult for the customer to place into their own funding schedule. A more stable approach is to align delivery, acceptance, and payment milestones with their staged commitments, and to break the overall investment into modules that can be decided in batches, so each batch can be evaluated on its own input and output.

**What do the A and D in PRTABCD stand for, and why are they blank here?**

Under the PSS5 glossary definitions, A stands for Arm — the person inside the customer organization who is embracing you — and D stands for Decision, meaning the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. The public announcement names the entity, the capacity, and the investment scale, but not who is driving the project or how the signing chain works. Both must therefore be recorded as unknown, each with its own next question.

**How do you judge where the customer stands on this expansion?**

Use sales-stage and customer-procurement-stage axes together. On the customer side, problem discovery, value judgment, and solution confirmation align with the investment plan. Responsibility assignment, build-versus-buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and contracting lack public evidence. What is certain: funds are prepared for capacity; how and from whom remain to be confirmed.

**How should payment terms and staged delivery be handled on the negotiation side?**

Lay out power, time, information, and alternatives, then design concessions and condition trades. The customer's statement that cash-flow pressure is manageable indicates cash outlays are watched. Payment terms, staged delivery, and acceptance standards can sit on one conditions table, each marked movable or exchanged — e.g., longer payment terms for a stable production schedule, not a unilateral price cut.

**What use is BATNA in an expansion project?**

Under the PSS5 glossary definitions, BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — the best fallback action available if the parties cannot agree; preparation requires specifying an executable alternative, a bottom line, authority limits, interests, and a record. In an expansion project, this means writing down what you will do if no deal is reached, then verifying whether the customer's alternatives are genuinely viable. The more concrete your alternative, the more room you have on price, payment terms, and delivery rhythm.

## Final Checklist

- Confirm which product line the incremental capacity serves.
- Map the staged funding rhythm onto a shared timeline with your delivery and acceptance milestones.
- Establish the expansion sequence and product-line priority before proposing anything.
- Identify who owns the line, and record it as a question rather than an assumption.
- Build one conditions table covering payment terms, staged delivery, and acceptance standards.
- Rank concessions: what moves first, what is traded, what holds.
- Complete the seven-category decision-information grid, marking known, unknown, source, and next question for each item.
- Write down your BATNA, bottom line, and authority limits before the meeting.
- Test where the customer stands using both stage axes, and look only for behavioral evidence.
- Separate what the public report states from what still requires verification.
