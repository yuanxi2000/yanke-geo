A customer facing cash constraints may need more than a lower price. The immediate task is to understand how spending authority, available resources and payment terms shape the decision. Before revising a proposal, establish what the customer could do if no agreement is reached.

The supplied source describes CIFI Holdings selling Shenyang CIFI Center to Yongsheng Services for RMB 299 million, with the consideration used to offset an equivalent amount of outstanding receivables in part. The transaction offers a useful starting point for sales analysis: what business problem might a customer be addressing through assets, receivables and payment arrangements?

It does not establish either party's internal motives, negotiation sequence or subsequent cooperation. Use the public action to frame questions, then investigate the customer's own circumstances.

## Start with three business questions

When a customer reports financial constraints, begin with questions that expose the structure of the decision:

- Who bears this expenditure, and which budget category would fund it?
- What resources could the customer potentially exchange or deploy?
- If no agreement is signed today, how would the customer address the same problem?

Record the answer, who provided it and who should confirm it next. An unanswered question belongs in the next meeting's agenda. It should not become an assumption in the forecast.

For example, a frozen budget could involve spending approval, cash availability or an incomplete decision process. Asking which expenditure category is restricted and what evidence approval requires helps distinguish those possibilities. The customer's explanation should determine the next action.

## Establish executable alternatives before negotiating

BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best available action a party can take if agreement cannot be reached. A possible option becomes useful preparation only when its feasibility is understood.

The source's negotiation approach considers power, time, information and alternatives when developing sustainable agreements. Prepare executable alternatives, limits, authority, interests and a written record for your own side. Investigate the customer's alternatives separately.

Questions worth verifying include:

- Are other assets available for disposal?
- Can outstanding receivables still be collected?
- Would internal policy permit installments or an exchange arrangement?
- Could payment obligations be linked to delivery or acceptance milestones?

These are investigation prompts. The CIFI transaction does not reveal either party's BATNA, and it does not establish that another customer can use the same arrangement. Confirm possibilities through customer answers and observable actions before treating them as negotiating options.

### Turn each possibility into a verification task

For every proposed alternative, record who can confirm it, what approval it needs and when an answer is expected. If a customer suggests milestone payments, identify the finance participant who can assess the terms and the delivery participant who can define acceptance. Until those checks occur, keep the option marked as unverified.

## Map the decision chain beyond the budget contact

A person discussing budget may hold only part of the information needed to reach agreement. At minimum, distinguish business users, funding approvers and delivery or acceptance owners. Each needs different evidence.

PRTABCD provides seven categories for scanning decision information. Keep confirmed facts and the next question visible in every category.

| Category | Information to establish | Practical next question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P: Past/history | Relevant customer history | What previous decisions affect this purchase? |
| R: Requirements | The customer's stated needs | Which problem must this purchase address? |
| T: Time | Deadlines and key milestones | Which business date governs the decision? |
| A: Arm | Who actively supports you | What specific support has this person provided? |
| B: Budget | Funding category and constraints | Who can confirm the available budget? |
| C: Competition and alternatives | Other ways to address the problem | What happens if this proposal is declined? |
| D: Decision | The decision chain across people and departments | Who approves, in what sequence? |

A is fixed as Arm: the person in the customer organization who actively embraces or supports you. D is fixed as Decision: the chain involving multiple people and departments. A supporter may help secure information or an introduction; that does not establish final authority. Track support and approval responsibility separately.

A practical communication sequence starts with business users to establish the problem, continues with finance to check budget and terms, and includes execution owners to confirm acceptance and handover. Adjust the sequence to the customer's process, retaining evidence from each conversation.

## Express value through investment, recovery and risk

The source's value capability translates products and services into profit, investment and operating value. For a financially constrained customer, organize the proposal around three questions:

- Investment: What resources must the customer commit, and when?
- Recovery: How would cash recovery or collection milestones work?
- Risk: What happens if delivery or acceptance conditions are not met?

Discuss reduced cash tied up, collection timing or default exposure only where the customer can verify the underlying information. Do not insert unsupported savings, return estimates or risk reductions. Record a missing number as information to obtain.

An asset offset in a public transaction does not establish a suitable commercial structure for another account. Ask about that customer's assets, receivables and payment conditions before exploring an exchange or installment arrangement.

## Use SPIN questions to uncover the constraint

SPIN organizes discovery around situation, problem, implication and need-payoff questions. Applied here, the purpose is to let customers explain their constraints and identify who can verify them.

Situation questions establish the current process: which approval step is holding up this expenditure? Problem questions locate the obstacle: what makes the present payment arrangement difficult to approve?

Implication questions explore consequences: which business milestone would be affected if the existing arrangement continued? Need-payoff questions examine a possible improvement: if payments followed acceptance milestones, what evidence would finance and business users need?

If the customer can explain budget ownership and approval evidence but is sensitive to payment timing, the structure may warrant investigation. If budget ownership remains unclear, clarify the decision chain first. Neither pattern alone proves the cause.

## Advance stages using customer behavior

The source describes two views of the buying process. The five sales stages are contact, needs, proposal, negotiation and closing. The seven customer purchasing stages are recognizing a problem, assessing value, confirming a solution exists, assigning responsibility, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing and signing.

These views have a many-to-many relationship. They should not be mechanically paired stage by stage. A salesperson preparing a quote may be dealing with a customer still deciding which department owns the problem.

Use behavior to establish progress. Who provided the budget basis? Who arranged a finance discussion? Who accepted a validation method? Visits, verbal assurances and a sent proposal do not independently establish that the purchasing decision has advanced.

Relationship quality also shows through access to verifiable information. Bring a relevant public industry information summary, a list of unresolved questions and a delivery condition that allows a defined fallback. Observe whether contacts clarify information or introduce another decision participant. Social interaction cannot substitute for decision information.

## An actionable account checklist

Before the next proposal or negotiation meeting:

- Record the customer's current alternatives and whether each is confirmed.
- List potentially exchangeable resources and the person authorized to discuss them.
- Identify business, finance and acceptance participants by name and department.
- Separate the active supporter from the wider approval chain.
- Document your alternatives, limits, authority and interests.
- Check every proposal number against an identifiable source.
- Specify the next question, its recipient and the follow-up date.
- Attach evidence of customer action before advancing the sales stage.

A compact working record can contain four fields: current alternatives, exchangeable resources, evidence from key participants and the next question. Add names, departments, dates and supporting records. Empty fields identify the next discovery task.

## FAQ

### Should a frozen budget trigger an immediate discount?

First establish who controls the budget, which spending category is restricted and whether another payment structure is permissible. Those answers help determine whether price, cash timing or decision responsibility needs attention.

### Can a public asset transaction reveal a customer's BATNA?

It reveals an action. Without further evidence, it does not reveal the alternatives available during negotiation or their relative attractiveness. Keep candidate alternatives on a verification list.

### How should an internal supporter be recorded?

Record specific support actions and their information sources under Arm. Maintain the departments, participants and approval sequence separately under Decision.

### What counts as meaningful progress?

Customer actions such as providing budget information, arranging finance involvement or accepting a validation approach support a stage assessment. Assess those actions against the customer's current purchasing task.

### How should unknown internal arrangements be discussed?

Use conditional questions: if installment payments were considered, who would approve them? Preserve the distinction between confirmed facts, unknowns and questions awaiting verification.
