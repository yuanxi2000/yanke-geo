Public news can give a key-account team a useful reason to investigate. It cannot establish a customer's budget, purchasing timetable, preferred supplier, or willingness to buy. The practical task is to turn a verified public signal into questions that help clarify the customer's decisions.

These clues support disciplined account preparation. They do not establish an opportunity's commercial scope or stage.

## Separate public facts from internal unknowns

Start with what the source actually connects: Boston Scientific, the expo coverage, the Lingang capacity upgrade, and the production challenges described above. The supplied material does not establish a specific partner, transaction amount, or event date. Leave those fields unresolved.

A capacity upgrade does not, by itself, show that a customer needs your offering or has decided to purchase externally. Treat it as a reason to investigate, with each assumption clearly marked.

Before a visit, prepare four columns:

- Known information, with its source.
- Unknown information that matters to the account.
- Questions that could resolve those unknowns.
- Customer actions or evidence to seek next.

For example, the public upgrade signal can support asking which activities require external coordination as capacity changes. It cannot support asserting that the customer lacks a particular system. Keep the source attached to the fact and the assumption attached to a question.

## Value: translate production challenges into business questions

Technical language becomes useful in a commercial conversation when it connects to an outcome the customer can evaluate. The source identifies several such connections to explore:

- Engineering transfer: how quickly can engineering changes be completed?
- Precision processes: where does quality variation create difficulty?
- Supply-chain integration: how exposed is delivery to supply interruption?
- Quality control: how does production coordination affect delivery predictability?

These are questions, not promised benefits. Do not attach percentages, savings estimates, or improvement commitments without customer evidence.

Ask which consequence matters most, how the customer evaluates it, and what information would establish its significance. The purpose is to discover whether a production issue has enough business value to justify action. A technically relevant problem does not automatically become a purchasing priority.

## Relationships: scan seven categories of decision information

PRTABCD is a decision-information scanning tool within the relationship capability. It helps expose gaps in account knowledge; it is not a set of personality profiles.

| Category | Information to establish | Practical question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P: Past or history | Relevant previous experience | What earlier work shapes the current approach? |
| R: Requirements | Needs and constraints | Which requirement needs attention first? |
| T: Time | Timing and key milestones | What customer action triggers the next milestone? |
| A: Arm | Who embraces or supports you | Who within the organization supports your involvement? |
| B: Budget | Funding ownership and approval | Where would funding sit, and who approves it? |
| C: Competition and alternatives | Options under consideration | Is the customer comparing internal development with external purchasing? |
| D: Decision | The decision chain | Which people and departments participate in the decision? |

A means Arm: the person or people embracing your involvement. D means Decision: the decision chain involving multiple people and departments. It does not mean one assumed decision-maker.

For every category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source, and the next question. Do not fill gaps with guessed names or inferred authority. The scan should tell the team which information the next conversation needs to obtain.

## Questioning: use SPIN to uncover the purchasing task

SPIN organizes questions into situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff. Use the sequence to connect operating conditions with the customer's priorities while leaving room for an unexpected answer.

### A practical SPIN sequence

1. **Situation:** How are current production capacity and quality-control activities coordinated?
2. **Problem:** Where do engineering transfer or precision-process development encounter the most difficulty?
3. **Implication:** When supply fluctuates, what happens to delivery commitments and inventory costs?
4. **Need-payoff:** Which risks would you want a supplier to address earlier, and what would that enable?

Follow the customer's answers rather than forcing every conversation through a script. If the customer identifies a different constraint, investigate it. The goal is for the customer to articulate the priority and the reason for change.

Keep public information in its proper role. Asking which activities need external coordination after a capacity upgrade invites useful detail. Declaring that the upgrade must require a particular solution supplies an answer the customer has not given.

## System: align selling activity with customer purchasing progress

The source distinguishes five sales stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. It also describes seven customer purchasing stages:

1. Recognizing a problem.
2. Assessing its value.
3. Confirming that a solution exists.
4. Establishing responsibility.
5. Deciding whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Selecting a solution and supplier.
7. Committing and signing.

These two sequences have a many-to-many relationship. There is no reliable one-to-one mapping, and a growing visit count does not establish purchasing progress.

The Boston Scientific information supports account understanding. It does not prove that a specific project has reached solution selection. Look for customer behavior: has someone accepted responsibility for the problem, confirmed its value, compared internal and external options, or provided selection criteria?

Match the next sales action to the task the customer is performing. If the customer is still assessing value, an early quotation can pull the discussion toward price before the reason to act is established. If supplier selection is underway, another general company introduction may miss the opportunity to discuss selection criteria.

## Negotiation: complete the information before setting concessions

Negotiation preparation should address power, time, information, limits, and alternatives. Missing commercial details are preparation gaps, not permission to invent bargaining positions.

BATNA means best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Make the alternative executable, and record limits, authority, interests, and the information supporting the preparation.

For this account scenario, investigate three areas:

- What the customer would do if it made no external purchase.
- What consequences, if any, the customer associates with delaying its decision.
- Which scope and payment terms the parties could exchange.

Without an established amount, do not create a quotation range. Without identified competitors or alternatives, do not assume sole-supplier status. Confirm budget ownership, comparison options, and key milestones before designing a concession sequence.

## FAQ

### What does the public information establish?

The supplied source connects Boston Scientific with eighth-expo coverage and a Lingang capacity upgrade. It describes localization challenges in advanced interventional-device production. It does not establish a specific partner, amount, or event date.

### Why examine all five capabilities?

The signal raises questions about business value, decision information, purchasing priorities, stage evidence, and negotiation readiness. Examining only value can leave decision and execution gaps unresolved.

### Can negotiation preparation begin without a budget?

Yes. Identify missing budget ownership, approval levels, alternatives, and milestones. Prepare questions and executable alternatives before discussing concessions; do not manufacture commercial terms.

### What is stronger evidence than another completed visit?

Customer actions such as accepting responsibility for a problem, confirming its value, comparing internal development with external purchasing, or supplying selection criteria provide evidence relevant to purchasing progress.

### Why leave missing details unresolved?

Filling them in would make later judgments depend on unsupported assumptions. An explicit unknown gives the team a verification task and keeps stage and negotiation decisions tied to evidence.
