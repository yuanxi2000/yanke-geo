According to Oriental Wealth Network reporting, Weibo adjusted its distribution model and introduced a creative vitality score for Gold V and Orange V accounts. The score looks at two areas, content production and proactive interaction, and evaluates original work, viewpoint, personal sharing, and genuine exchange. Accounts described as having human presence need 70 points or more to be eligible for more traffic exposure and support. The public report does not disclose partners, amounts, or specific dates, and this guide does not fill those gaps. For key-account sales, the useful lesson is the sequence: define the behavior you want, make it measurable, then let resources follow the score.

## The scorecard is the real buying language

Enterprise buyers run a quieter version of the same mechanism. Vendor tiering, bid evaluation sheets, annual performance reviews, and budget reviews all turn a judgment about whether a supplier is worth investing in into comparable numbers. A seller who cannot see the columns is left arguing price and relationship with fewer and fewer things to say.

The first job is to find the customer's table. Four questions expose most of the gap:

- Which metrics is the customer using this year: cost, delivery, yield, repeat purchase, or risk exposure?
- Who maintains the metric, how often is it updated, and who changed the last version?
- Is our current position on that metric something the customer told us, or our own estimate?
- If the scoring definition changes next quarter, how early would we know?

A common result is that our materials are thick and the customer's columns are thin. The mismatch is where points are lost.

## Value capability: find the table before building the case

Value capability moves the sales conversation from product features to an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, expressed in business numbers. Weibo turned human presence into a comparable number. Customers turn worthiness into comparable numbers through their own sheets. The work is not to admire our solution but to locate where it lands on their sheet.

### Translate human presence into profit language

The profit improvement plan, or PIP, asks for a proposal written as an investment case. It answers three questions: what profit will be increased or protected, through what mechanism and with what confidence, and within what timeframe it can be verified.

There is a boundary. Any profit increase must be calculated with the customer's data and definitions. A number reported unilaterally by sales will not survive delivery and will damage trust. A practical move is to list the operating data the customer is willing to share, mark each item as known, unknown, or awaiting a named owner, and schedule a joint calculation session.

## Relationship capability and PRTABCD: the score sits on people

Relationship capability builds customer relationships through professional capability, decision information, and trustworthy action. Its focus is understanding and covering the decision chain. PRTABCD scans seven categories of decision information: P for past, R for needs, T for timing and key milestones, A for Arm or embracer, B for budget, C for competition and alternatives, and D for decision chain.

A is fixed as Arm, the embracer, and answers who in the customer organization is embracing you. D is Decision, a multi-person, multi-department chain, not one person who signs. These two are the usual blind spots. A common error is to put the most senior person in A and one leader in D, then assume the intelligence is complete. Record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question for all seven items before deciding whom to visit next.

Once rules are published, creators who can calculate the score study how content production and proactive interaction earn points. Customer embracers behave similarly. Give them a version they can repeat and take upward, and they can carry the score for you internally and put you in their report.

| Element | Weibo's reported mechanism | Enterprise buying parallel |
|---|---|---|
| What is measured | Original work, viewpoint, personal sharing, and genuine exchange, through content production and proactive interaction | Cost, delivery, yield, repeat purchase, risk exposure, or other customer-chosen metrics |

## Questioning capability: let the customer say the standard

Do not open with a request for the scorecard, and do not start with a vague question about what the customer values most. Follow a sequence: what the current practice is, where it gets stuck, which departments and metrics the blockage affects, and what improvement would mean to the customer. These correspond to situation, problem, impact, and need-payoff questions.

The discipline is simple. Only impact the customer says aloud belongs in the proposal. Impact the seller summarizes usually stays in the seller's own report.

## System and negotiation capability: stage mismatch makes price unwinnable

System capability uses a dual-view funnel: five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages. The sales stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. The buying stages run from problem discovery to commitment and signing. Each step needs customer behavioral evidence, not visit counts.

If the customer has not confirmed ownership of the problem, price discussion is premature. The team may be negotiating terms while the buyer is still deciding whether the problem belongs to anyone. That is why stage mismatch makes price unwinnable.

Negotiation capability designs concessions and trades after clarifying power, time, information, and alternatives. BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, matters when talks stall. Write down five things before the next round: the executable alternative if no deal is reached, the bottom line, who has authority to approve exceptions, each side's interests, and a record of what was discussed and promised. An empty alternative leaves price as the only lever.

## A practical checklist for this week

1. Obtain the customer's latest review sheet or vendor scorecard. Mark each line as known, unknown, or owned by a named person.
2. Sweep your largest deal with PRTABCD. Prioritize A, the embracer, and D, the decision chain, rather than the highest title.
3. Write a one-page PIP for one live opportunity: what profit will be increased or protected, through what mechanism and confidence, and by when it can be verified.
4. Before the next negotiation, write a one-page BATNA covering alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record.
5. Ask two process questions about the last scoring round: how the purchase was evaluated and what the result changed.

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the confirmed facts about Weibo's change?**

Public reporting says Weibo introduced a creative vitality score for Gold V and Orange V accounts after adjusting distribution. It evaluates content production and proactive interaction across original work, viewpoint, personal sharing, and genuine exchange. Accounts with human presence at 70 points or more may receive more exposure and support. Partners, amounts, and dates are not disclosed. For sales, the confirmed fact is that a content quality was converted into a measurable score.

**Q2: Why does a platform score matter to key-account sales?**

Scoring turns an abstract requirement into a resource-allocation rule. Higher-scoring accounts get exposure and support, so resources follow scores. Enterprise customers do the same through vendor tiers, bid scoring, and annual reviews. A seller who cannot read the table competes on price and relationship with fewer arguments.

**Q3: How does value capability work with an existing scorecard?**

Get the review sheet or vendor scorecard and mark each item as known, unknown, or awaiting a named owner. Then arrange a joint session to complete it. The customer calculates the score using their definitions. Sales can make the calculation fair and the data complete; it cannot substitute its own number for the customer's.

**Q4: What if the customer will not share the criteria?**

Ask about process and consequences instead of demanding the sheet. How were similar purchases evaluated? What did the last score change? Who consolidates the result this time? Use situation, problem, impact, and need-payoff questions. When customers discuss impact, they often reveal the dimensions. That is closer to the real standard than a blank form.

**Q5: What is the usual A and D mistake in PRTABCD?**

Putting the most senior person in A and a single leader in D. A is Arm, the embracer: who in the organization is embracing you. D is Decision: a multi-person, multi-department chain, not one signer. For all seven items, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. Then decide the next visit.

**Q6: What goes into BATNA preparation?**

Write the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. What can you do if no deal is reached? Below which line will you not go? Who can approve an exception? What are each side's interests? What was discussed and promised? Without an alternative, negotiation collapses into discounting.
