A V2G headline can look like a ready-made business case. A report says one vehicle may earn 3,000–5,000 yuan a year and that several automakers are entering V2G. For a key-account seller, neither claim is customer-specific proof. The task is to turn the headline into an investment case the customer can calculate, defend and own internally.

## Separate what the headline knows from what it does not

The public source identifies Chery Green Energy as the company subject and comes from an East Money report. Its two judgments are that a single vehicle may earn 3,000–5,000 yuan a year and that several OEMs are entering V2G. The counterparty, the exact amount and the date are not disclosed. Put those three items in an unknown column, not in an assumption column.

Two misreadings are common. The first turns several OEMs are entering into this customer is already buying. The second turns 3,000–5,000 yuan into this customer already accepts the return. The first pushes you to quote before the customer has confirmed the problem. The second pushes you to tell peer stories before the customer has run the numbers. Both speed up the process and reduce credibility.

V2G means a vehicle can send power back to the grid while parked, converting idle battery time into revenue. But can generate revenue is not the customer will pay. Three questions sit in between: who owns the revenue, who calculates it, and who carries the risk. Until those are answered, the 3,000–5,000 yuan figure is an industry number, not a line in the customer budget.

## Break the number into four pockets

A single annual figure is too blunt for a key account. Split it into four pockets, each with its own buyer, calculation basis and accountable person.

| Pocket | Buyer | Calculation question | What must be settled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle running cost | Owner or driver | How much less will one vehicle spend in a year? | Baseline cost, participation frequency, electricity price spread |
| Fleet asset turnover | Fleet or operations | What net gain can the same battery earn while parked? | Vehicle availability, dispatch schedule, settlement cycle |
| Site and grid resource reuse | Site owner or power manager | Are chargers, transformers and parking slots being used more fully? | Capacity limits, metering, site control |
| Risk cost | Finance, safety or operations | Who absorbs battery degradation, settlement errors and failed response? | Responsibility, guarantees, penalties and records |

Four pockets correspond to four buying arguments. An owner cares about payback. A fleet cares about net gain per vehicle per year. A site owner cares about resource utilization. A risk owner cares about who steps in when performance deviates. The same 3,000–5,000 yuan sentence means different things to each, and one pitch will move only one of them.

### What the same number sounds like to different buyers

To the owner, it is annual savings. To the fleet, it is net gain per vehicle. To the site owner, it is higher utilization of chargers, transformers and parking. To the risk owner, it is a question about who absorbs degradation, settlement error and failed dispatch. Prepare a separate calculation for each pocket; do not use one slide for all four.

## Ask questions that make the customer state the assumptions

The 3,000–5,000 yuan figure rests on assumptions: participation frequency, event duration, electricity price spread, usable battery capacity and settlement period. Do not set these for the customer. Use questions to bring them out. SPIN offers a practical sequence.

- Situation questions: How many vehicles, chargers and how much distribution capacity are in scope?
- Problem questions: Which link worries you most when dispatch is called?
- Implication questions: If response fails or penalties apply, what does that cost over a year?
- Need-payoff questions: If a proposal could contain that uncertainty, how would you want to settle it?

The goal is a number the customer has calculated, not a number you have repeated. A customer saying we think we can recover about this much a year is stronger than ten seller statements. Also align the calculation basis. The customer may count vehicles; you may count charged and discharged kilowatt-hours. Agreeing on the basis matters earlier than agreeing on price.

## Read the decision chain across vehicles, power and money

V2G is not a purchasing-department decision. It usually involves the people who manage vehicles, the people who manage power and the people who manage money. Use the seven PRTABCD decision items to expose blind spots: prior pilots, vehicle and site needs, timing and assessment milestones, who is embracing the initiative, the budget line, competitors or alternatives, and how many signatures the decision requires. A means Arm, the person in the customer organization who is actively embracing the work. D means Decision, a multi-person, multi-department chain rather than one authority figure.

The discipline is plain. For each item, write what is known, what is unknown, the source of information and the next question. Many projects stall because a seller treats one executive nod as a completed decision chain, while the people who control settlement and safety have never been asked.

## Treat a pilot as evidence, not a closed sale

Look at key-account sales on two axes. The seller moves through contact, need, solution, negotiation and close. The buyer moves through seven purchasing stages, from discovering a problem to committing to a contract. The two axes relate many-to-many; they do not need to line up one-to-one.

The common V2G mismatch is a seller in solution mode while the customer is still confirming that a solution exists. The customer may agree to install a few units, but no one owns the result and no budget line exists. A pilot is one of the most overestimated forms of customer evidence. Installing equipment and holding meetings do not prove that the customer has assigned responsibility or decided whether to build or buy.

Ask three questions: Who will issue the pilot conclusion? By what standard will it be issued? If the conclusion is positive, which process follows? If these cannot be answered, the project is still between contact and need.

## Trade the return expectation for conditions

Negotiation capability is about forming a sustainable agreement around power, time, information and alternatives. In V2G, the customer is buying equipment and a multi-year return expectation. Concessions therefore cannot be price-only. Link unit price to response commitments, settlement periods and expansion pace.

- If the customer wants a lower unit price, specify minimum participation frequency and settlement arrangements.
- If the customer wants a longer payment period, reduce the first-phase scale and expand later against verified results.
- Before the meeting, write both BATNAs. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, meaning the best fallback if no deal is reached. For the seller, ask what other project can proceed if this one does not. For the customer, ask how else the problem can be solved. Clear alternatives on both sides make price a real discussion rather than a one-way concession.

## One-page operating checklist

Use this checklist before the next customer meeting or proposal.

- [ ] Write one sentence naming Chery Green Energy's role in this deal and why the customer must act now. If you cannot, do not quote yet.
- [ ] Split the 3,000–5,000 yuan figure into four pockets. For each, name the buyer, calculation basis and person accountable for the number.
- [ ] Prepare two questions for each SPIN category so the customer states the assumptions instead of repeating your figure.
- [ ] Fill all seven PRTABCD items. Put unknowns in the next-question column, not in an assumption column.
- [ ] Mark one position on the seller five stages and one on the buyer seven purchasing stages. Support both with customer behavior evidence.
- [ ] Write both BATNAs before negotiating and list three tradable conditions, with price as only one.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a seller do first when Chery Green Energy appears in a V2G headline?** Separate known from unknown. The company subject and the two headline judgments are known; counterparty, amount and date are not. Record the unknowns and ask before treating industry heat as customer demand.

**Can the 3,000–5,000 yuan figure go on the proposal cover?** Not directly. It carries assumptions about participation, price spread, battery capacity and settlement. Have the customer calculate a range, then put that range in the proposal and name who will verify it.

**Is a several-unit pilot real progress?** Only if someone owns the conclusion, a standard exists for judging it, and a positive result has a next process. Otherwise the customer may only be confirming that a solution exists.

**Why is the V2G decision chain often misjudged?** It spans vehicles, power and money, and purchasing is only one part. In PRTABCD, D is a multi-person chain, not one approver; A is the active embracer. Mixing them up turns one nod into false clearance.

**What can be traded besides price?** Response commitments, settlement periods, first-phase scale and expansion pace. Tie a lower price to minimum participation and detailed settlement, or a longer payment period to a smaller first phase with later expansion.

**Which SPIN questions are often missed?** Implication and need-payoff. Sellers often ask situation and problem questions, then rush to a solution. Without the cost of the problem and the desired return, the customer has no reason to conclude that change is worth it.
