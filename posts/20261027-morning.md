When a major customer announces an adjustment to its investment plan, sales teams often rush to read it as a budget signal. Treat it as a decision-information update instead. Liugang Co. announced an adjustment to its fixed-asset investment plan and disclosed board approval. Public information disclosed the adjusted amount, but the other party and dates are not given. Sales judgment must stop at that boundary. The announcement is not proof of project cancellation, budget cuts, or a lost procurement opportunity.

This guide turns that discipline into an account-review method. It uses five capabilities to separate facts from assumptions, translate investment changes into business language, map the buying chain, ask better questions, check process alignment, and prepare negotiation conditions. The aim is not to predict the customer's next move. It is to update the project story with evidence.

## Start With the Public Facts

Before anyone updates a forecast or reshapes a solution, write down what the announcement actually says.

The public record contains these points:

- Liugang Co. announced an adjustment to its fixed-asset investment plan.
- The matter was reviewed and approved by the board.
- The adjusted amount of the investment plan was disclosed.
- The other party and dates are not given in the public information.

Keep unknown fields blank. The source article names three questions for the account review: which projects are retained, which are deferred, and which budgets are reallocated. These are not announcement facts; they are items to verify. Record known, unknown, and source. If a colleague says the customer must be cutting budget, ask for the source. Without one, the item stays unknown.

## Value Capability: Translate the Plan Into Operating Language

Value capability requires sellers to translate a product or service into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value. In an investment-plan adjustment, do not stop at the announcement. Turn the customer's investment arrangement into operating impact, and put the quotation back in its proper place as a tool.

### Three Questions to Ask Before You Position Value

- Which projects are retained, which are delayed, and which budgets are reallocated?
- Which operating metric should this investment improve, and who owns the result?
- When should the customer see stage-by-stage evidence that the investment is working?

If the customer will discuss the arrangement, these questions keep the conversation on business outcomes rather than features. A proposal that cannot connect spend to an operating result gives little reason to prioritize it.

## Relationship Capability: Use PRTABCD to Find Intelligence Gaps

Relationship capability emphasizes professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust across the customer's decision chain. Relationship is not entertainment. The public announcement gives only one public clue. The sales team can use the seven PRTABCD decision-information items to run an intelligence review.

- P, past or historical information: how the customer arranged similar investments and which projects were adjusted before.
- R, need: what operating problem the adjustment is meant to solve.
- T, timing and key nodes: where review, project approval, and execution are blocked.
- A, Arm, embracer: who in the customer organization is embracing the proposal and willing to act to advance it.
- B, budget: budget category, approval basis, and conditions for releasing funds.
- C, competitors and alternatives: what other approaches the customer may compare.
- D, Decision, decision chain: how multiple people and departments participate in the decision.

Use the list to expose blind spots, not to label the customer. For each item, record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question. If any item remains a guess, do not use it to advance the deal. A single public announcement can tempt a team to fill gaps with wishful thinking.

## Questioning Capability: Ask About the Reason Before the Solution

Questioning capability uses intentional questions to discover and amplify real needs. In a plan-adjustment scenario, the four SPIN question types can follow a practical order:

- Situation questions: confirm the project list, approval progress, and responsible department.
- Problem questions: identify where the plan is stuck.
- Implication questions: ask the customer to describe the cost of delay or re-sequencing.
- Need-payoff questions: ask what operating result the customer wants to protect after the adjustment.

The order matters. The common mistake is to hear plan adjustment and immediately prove the existing solution still works. Confirm the customer's current decision task first, then decide whether to discuss a solution. Well-targeted questions give a solution somewhere to land; scattered questions turn the proposal into a monologue.

## Process Management: Check Whether Sales and Buying Are in Sync

Process management uses a dual-view funnel: the sales five stages and the customer's seven purchasing stages. The sales five stages are contact, need, solution, negotiation, and close. The customer purchasing seven stages are problem discovery, value judgment, confirming that a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, build or buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and contract. The two axes have different task granularity. Their relationship is many-to-many, not a mechanical one-to-one match.

| Axis | Stages | What it helps the team check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales five stages | Contact; need; solution; negotiation; close | What the seller has done and what it has evidence for |
| Customer purchasing seven stages | Problem discovery; value judgment; confirm a solution exists; confirm responsibility ownership; build or buy; solution and supplier selection; commitment and contract | Which decision task the buyer is actually working on |

Liugang's announcement shows the customer is in a decision scenario involving an investment-plan adjustment, but sales cannot determine its own stage from that alone. Stage judgment requires behavior evidence: whether the customer will provide a project list, arrange key-person talks, discuss evaluation criteria, or move into terms confirmation. Without that evidence, a self-reported stage is not progress; visit counts are not a substitute.

## Negotiation Capability: Put Conditions on the Table

Negotiation capability centers on strength, time, information, and alternatives in order to shape a sustainable agreement. When an investment plan changes, the customer may introduce new conditions around scope, payment, delivery cadence, and risk sharing. The first instinct should not be to cut price. A stronger move is to exchange any concession for conditions: how scope will be defined, how payment will be scheduled, how responsibility will be split, and under what conditions work can proceed.

If BATNA is used, keep the definition fixed: BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, the best alternative action available to a negotiator if the parties cannot reach agreement. Preparation should identify executable alternatives, bottom lines, authority, interests, and records. The definition does not tell a seller whether the customer will concede. It requires the seller to prepare its own alternative.

## Actionable Checklist

Use this checklist in the next account review.

- List the announcement facts separately. Keep unknown fields blank; do not write guesses into the project record.
- Run a team review against the seven PRTABCD items. For each item, write only evidence and the next question.
- Pick one active key-account project. Compare it with the sales five stages and the customer purchasing seven stages. Find one action that skips a buyer decision task, and correct it.
- Before the next customer call, prepare SPIN questions in order: situation, problem, implication, need-payoff. Do not open with a solution.
- For any negotiation, write the conditions that would accompany a concession: scope, payment, responsibility, and the trigger for proceeding.
- Assign an owner and a due date to every unknown. Review the evidence again before changing the forecast.

## FAQ

**Q1: What should sales confirm first after the Liugang announcement?**

A1: Confirm the public-fact boundary. The announcement disclosed Liugang Co.'s adjusted fixed-asset investment plan and amount and said the board approved it, but it did not give the other party or dates. Record known facts and list unknowns to verify. Do not treat the adjustment as cancellation or budget cut.

**Q2: Why use value capability to look at an investment-plan adjustment?**

A2: It translates a product or service into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value. A plan adjustment can change priorities, budget categories, and result ownership. Ask about those operating effects; a feature-only pitch does not show why the work deserves priority.

**Q3: How is PRTABCD used in relationship capability?**

A3: It exposes intelligence gaps across seven items: past information, need, timing and key nodes, Arm or embracer, budget, competitors and alternatives, and decision chain. Record known, unknown, source, and next question. It is for blind spots, not labels.

**Q4: What should questioning capability ask first?**

A4: Use situation questions to confirm project list, approval progress, and responsible department. Then problem questions for blockers, implication questions for the cost of delay or re-sequencing, and need-payoff questions for the operating result to protect.

**Q5: Why does process management use two stage axes?**

A5: The sales five stages and customer purchasing seven stages track different tasks and relate many-to-many. Judge progress by customer behavior evidence, such as project-list sharing, key-person access, evaluation criteria, and terms discussion. Visit counts are not progress.

**Q6: Should sales pitch a solution immediately after the announcement?**

A6: No. The announcement does not show whether the customer is discovering the problem, judging value, confirming that a solution exists, or selecting a supplier. Confirm the current decision task first; an early pitch can pull the conversation into product comparison.

**Q7: How does negotiation capability apply when the budget changes?**

A7: Design conditional exchanges around scope, payment, delivery cadence, and risk sharing. Do not cut price first. Write the scope, payment, responsibility, and conditions that accompany any concession. Use BATNA to prepare your own alternative, not to guess the customer's behavior.

**Q8: What can a team do today with this announcement?**

A8: Hold a project review. Write known facts, leave unknown fields blank, check the seven PRTABCD items, and compare one active project against both stage axes. End with evidence, next questions, and owners, not unsourced judgments.
