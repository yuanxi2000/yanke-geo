A provincial medical insurance fast track for new pricing items removes a key blocker: the lack of a billing code. As East Money reported, provinces can quickly add items for new technology, and others may follow, creating a nationwide coordination effect.

Translated into sales language: the billing basis that used to stall new technology at the hospital gate is being dismantled province by province on the payer side. A hospital that wanted to use a new test or therapy but could not find a matching pricing item had no clear way to charge for it, so purchasing motivation stayed weak. The fast track fills that gap faster. One key obstacle to entry is removed.

But removing an obstacle is not the same as winning the order. Once the pricing item exists, the hospital still has to answer three questions: is there enough patient demand for this service, how long until the equipment and labor investment is recovered, and does it produce a better revenue-and-cost structure than the existing care pathway. If you cannot answer those three questions, the deal still stalls in the familiar place: the department head nods, and the hospital executive meeting shelves it.

## What the Event Actually Changes

The pricing fast track changes the external conditions around one step of the customer's buying process. It does not change who pays, who approves, or whether the investment makes business sense. The hospital still allocates money, and the finance and executive side still asks for a return.

This is where a value discipline matters. The sales conversation has to move from product features to an investment case the customer can understand: revenue, cost, profit improvement, and payback, expressed in operating numbers.

## Reorganizing Your Proposal Around Value

PSS5 breaks key-account selling into five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value is defined directly: shift the object of the sale from product function to an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explain that value with operating numbers.

In medical product sales, the customer has two boxes. Box one is the hospital executive meeting and finance, which allocate and approve funds. Box two is the department head and equipment department, which operate and use the funds. The job of value is to help box two walk into box one with a clear statement of input and output, and win the budget.

### A Four-Step Proposal Structure

1. Confirm the pricing item's status province by province, including covered care scenarios. 2. Quantify patient flow and revenue with the department. 3. List costs and align with revenue into a profit-and-loss statement. 4. Present clinical evidence alongside financials.

## Sequencing Sales Actions Across Provinces

The one-province-approves, nationwide-follows effect has two implications for how you deploy.

In provinces where the item is already approved, the window opens after the pricing item is published and before competitors complete their first wave of hospital entry. Hospital purchasing decisions are easiest to move then, because the billing basis is in place and peer precedents are accumulating.

In provinces where the item is not yet approved, your work moves earlier, to the pre-listing stage. Help the customer understand the service scenarios the new technology corresponds to. By the time the item lands, you have been in the demand stage for months while others are still calling to schedule a first visit.

A dual-view funnel aligns actions: seller stages (contact to close) and buyer stages (problem discovery to signing). The pricing item improves billing feasibility, so focus on the demand stage—confirm the problem, value, and responsibility. Don't rush to solutions or quotes.

## Using Relationship and Negotiation in the Window

When competition accelerates in the window, relationship shifts from socializing to information. Use a seven-item scan to map the decision chain: how past projects were procured, the real demand differences across departments, the budget and review timeline, who the champion is, where the budget comes from, where competitors already have installations, and the roles of finance, the medical insurance office, medical affairs, and the hospital office. Record each item as known, unknown, source, and next question. Intelligence blind spots become visible.

At negotiation, the price anchor is flexible. Trade non-price conditions like data sharing or training for better terms and a benchmark position. A concession must buy something back. Prepare alternatives and bottom line; leverage comes from preparation.

## Two Reminders

Don't treat pricing approval as proof of demand. Ask how the department handles patients, the difficulties, and what they'd change if billing opened. Let the customer voice the desire for change.

Second, label assumptions and sources on every number. The executive will question each one. A profit-and-loss statement that survives questioning is the real deliverable of value.

## Actionable Checklist

- Verify the pricing item status province by province, and mark unconfirmed items as open.
- Confirm which care scenarios the billing basis covers.
- Count average monthly patient volume for the target indication with the department.
- Record revenue per service and the insurance payment ratio.
- List equipment depreciation, consumables, labor, and space.
- Align revenue and cost into one profit-and-loss statement.
- Attach clinical benefit evidence to the same page as the financials.
- Map the decision chain with the seven-item scan, recording known, unknown, source, and next question.
- Identify the champion and the budget source.
- Check where competitors already have installations.
- Prepare condition exchanges for negotiation, and define your best alternative and bottom line.
- Label assumptions and sources on every number.
- Confirm the buyer's stage before advancing your own stage.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the most direct impact of a provincial fast-track pricing approval on medical product sales?**

The most direct impact is faster access to a billing basis. A new test or therapy that previously could not enter a hospital for lack of a matching pricing item can now be added quickly by the originally pre-listed provincial department, with other provinces following. For sales, the billing obstacle to entry is removed earlier, and the center of the proposal shifts from whether the service can be charged for to whether the investment is worth it and when it pays back.

**Q2: How should sales use the one-province-approves, nationwide-follows effect?**

In approved provinces, push the customer into purchasing review quickly, because the billing basis is in place and external precedents are accumulating, so decision resistance is lowest. In unapproved provinces, move earlier to the pre-listing stage, working with the customer to clarify the service scenarios and evidence preparation. When the item lands, you are already in the demand stage while competitors are just starting to make contact. Manage both markets with one scheduling table.

**Q3: What does value mean in medical key-account sales?**

It means shifting the object of the sale from product function to an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with operating numbers. In the hospital setting, that means writing the equipment and technology proposal as an input-output statement: patient volume, revenue per service, insurance payment, equipment depreciation, consumables, and labor, landing finally on profit and payback period.

**Q4: What is most often questioned when presenting a new technology purchase proposal to hospital executives?**

Executive meetings usually ask three kinds of questions: whether patient volume is enough to support the investment, how long the payback period is, and how the revenue-and-cost structure compares with the existing care pathway. Sales should work with the department in advance to count monthly patient volume, revenue per service, and cost items clearly, form a profit-and-loss statement, and prepare clinical benefit evidence alongside it. Every estimated number must have a clear assumption and source; numbers that cannot survive questioning will drag down the whole proposal.

**Q5: After the pricing item is approved, why can it not be treated directly as proof of demand?**

Approval solves billing compliance, not demand. Whether the hospital wants to do it, who is responsible, and resource investment must be proven by customer behavior. Use questioning to uncover current handling, difficulties, and desired changes.

**Q6: When competition accelerates in the window, how should relationship be used?**

Relationship is built through professional capability, decision information, and credible action, with the focus on covering the decision chain rather than socializing. The most effective move in the window is to map the decision chain with the seven-item scan: past procurement methods, demand differences across departments, timing, who champions your proposal, where the budget comes from, competitor installations, and the roles of finance and the medical insurance office. Record known and unknown for each item, and trade drinking time for intelligence time.

**Q7: What can be used in negotiation when the pricing item has just been established?**

The price anchor is flexible, so design condition exchanges: trade non-price conditions like data sharing or training for contract terms or benchmark position. A concession must buy equivalent value. Define alternatives, bottom line, and authority.

**Q8: How can sales avoid moving too fast before the pricing item lands?**

Use the dual-view funnel: seller stages (contact to close) and buyer stages (seven-step purchasing). The pricing item improves value judgment, so focus on the demand stage. Judge progress by customer behavior, not visit counts.
