# Judging First-Deal Quality with PSS5: A Guide for Key-Account Teams

A signed first project can look like proof that a new offer works. In key-account sales, it is often only the start of evidence gathering. DeepBlue Technology's DFAgent has entered the film-AI field, and its first signed project is still at an early stage. The final film result of *Climate War* is the first public sample for testing the long-form consistency challenge. This guide shows how a sales team can use PSS5 to judge the quality of such a first deal without filling missing facts with optimism.

## Draw the fact boundary first

Start with what the source can support. The verified facts are narrow: the subject is DeepBlue Technology; DFAgent has entered a film-AI scenario; the first signed project is at an early stage; and the final effect of *Climate War* is the first public sample for testing long-form consistency. The report does not give the partner, contract value, or event date. Those gaps should be labeled as intelligence gaps. They are not blanks to be filled with guessed names, numbers, or timelines.

This boundary matters because a first-deal assessment is only as good as its evidence. A fact table should separate what is known, what is unknown, who said it, and what question comes next. If a field has no public source, write unknown. Do not convert a signed project into proof of client value, completed procurement, or repeatable demand.

## Map the five capabilities

PSS5 works as one integrated view, not five separate opinions. The table below turns each capability into a core question and the evidence a team should collect.

| Capability | Core question | Evidence to collect | Useful output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value | Can the client make the economics work? | Cost, rework, consistency, budget, and responsibility effects | Testable benefit hypothesis |
| Relationship | Who holds decision information? | PRTABCD fields, sources, gaps, and next questions | Decision-chain map |
| Questioning | What would the client accept as proof? | SPIN answers in the client's own words | Acceptance criteria |
| System | Where is the client in its buying process? | Customer behavior at sales and procurement stages | Stage evidence |
| Negotiation | What conditions make validation executable? | Scope, acceptance, payment, remedy, expansion, BATNA | Conditional first-deal terms |

## Value capability: Turn capability into client economics

Value capability asks the seller to translate product features into investment, return, and profit improvement. In this film-AI case, DFAgent must answer whether the film party can reduce rework, stabilize final-film consistency, and make budget and responsibility more controllable. The specific improvement must come from the client during validation. A salesperson should not write the client's benefit number for them.

Three questions come first:

- What rework and coordination costs does the film party currently carry for long-form consistency?
- Which part of the production process can DFAgent affect, and who validates the size of that effect?
- If the final film misses the standard, how does value get returned, remedied, or redone?

## Relationship capability: Use PRTABCD to expose unknowns

Relationship capability requires professional insight and decision information to penetrate the decision chain. Public information covers only DeepBlue's side. The customer side must be recorded field by field, with known facts, unknowns, sources, and next questions. A is fixed as Arm, meaning who is embracing you. D is Decision, meaning a multi-person, multi-department decision chain.

### A worked PRTABCD scan for the film-AI case

- P, past: Does the film party have prior film-project cooperation or similar validation?
- R, need: Is the core problem long-form consistency, coordination, or budget?
- T, timing: Which decision node is next, and who confirms it?
- A, Arm: Who is the champion willing to invest resources in validation?
- B, budget: Does budget exist, who controls it, and when is it released?
- C, competition: Which alternative solutions is the client comparing?
- D, Decision: Which departments sit on the decision chain?

For each line, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. That discipline keeps the team from treating a friendly contact as the whole decision chain.

## Questioning capability: Turn final-film effect into acceptance criteria

Questioning capability uses intentional questions to discover and amplify real demand. Around the *Climate War* validation, SPIN questions can move from situation to problem, implication, and need payoff. The aim is to learn what evidence the client would accept as proof that the agent closes a long-form production gap.

- Situation: Which parts of long-form production still depend most on repeated manual confirmation?
- Problem: Which consistency issues would push the final film away from expectations?
- Implication: If those issues reach post-production, how do budget, schedule, and team responsibility change?
- Need payoff: If DFAgent passes validation, which business result does the film party most want to protect or improve?

The questions should follow the client's decision task. They should not become a salesperson's self-answered script.

## System capability: Judge stage by customer behavior

System capability manages the process through two views: a five-stage sales funnel and a seven-stage customer procurement process. The sales stages are contact, need, solution, negotiation, and close. The procurement stages run from problem discovery and value judgment through confirming that a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and reaching commitment and contract.

A signed first project does not automatically mean the customer has completed every procurement decision. Visit counts are not a substitute for customer behavior evidence. For this case, the early stage means the team should still confirm value judgment, responsibility ownership, solution selection, and acceptance conditions.

## Negotiation capability: Negotiate validation before volume

Negotiation capability designs condition exchanges around power, time, information, and alternatives. At the first-deal stage, the focus should be validation scope, acceptance criteria, payment milestones, failure remedy, and conditions for later expansion. A seller without a clear BATNA is likely to make price the only lever. BATNA should be prepared with the term's discipline: executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records.

## Put the five capabilities on one battle map

The five dimensions should check one another against the same customer decision task. Value provides a testable benefit hypothesis. Relationship identifies who holds decision information. Questioning turns the hypothesis into a need the client states. System confirms the stage through customer behavior. Negotiation converts validation into executable conditions. For the DeepBlue case, the source supports analysis of direction and validation conditions only. The final film effect and customer procurement evidence remain public unknowns.

## FAQ

**Q1: Which facts from the source can be used directly?**  
Only the public facts: DeepBlue Technology is the subject, DFAgent has entered film AI, the first signed project is at an early stage, and the final effect of *Climate War* is the first public sample for testing long-form consistency. Partner, contract value, and date are not public and should be marked unknown.

**Q2: Why use all of PSS5 instead of only value capability?**  
The case exposes several judgment gaps at once: value needs film validation, the decision chain is not public, demand must be asked out, the project is only at an early stage, and negotiation conditions are not visible. A five-capability view keeps those gaps on one map.

**Q3: Where should value questions start?**  
Start with the business result the film party wants to improve, not with model features. Ask who bears rework, coordination, and budget pressure; which process DFAgent can affect; and what evidence validates the improvement. The client supplies the numbers.

**Q4: How does relationship work when the decision chain is unknown?**  
Use PRTABCD to list past, need, timing, Arm champion, budget, competition, and Decision chain. For each field, record known facts, unknowns, sources, and the next question. A is Arm, who is embracing you. D is Decision, the multi-person, multi-department chain.

**Q5: What does system capability review after a first signature?**  
Review customer behavior, not sales feeling. A signed project may be the start of formal commitment, but the team still needs evidence on value judgment, responsibility ownership, solution selection, and acceptance conditions.

**Q6: What should negotiation protect in the first deal?**  
Protect validation scope, acceptance criteria, payment milestones, failure remedy, and later expansion. Prepare BATNA properly. Without alternatives, price becomes the only lever; with alternatives, the seller can exchange conditions for conditions.
