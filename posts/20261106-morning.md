A complex partnership can offer a useful lesson in key-account selling: make evidence the bridge between capability and commitment. In the oncology collaboration described in the supplied source, early research comes first, agreed clinical milestones come next, and an option to obtain exclusive rights follows.

For sales teams, the practical lesson is to explain what will be demonstrated, how it will be assessed, and what commitment that evidence could justify. This guide translates that structure into account planning, discovery, value assessment, and negotiation. The recommended practices below are applications of the source, not claims about either party's internal processes.

## Establish the factual boundary

The supplied source attributes the collaboration details to public reporting by Eastmoney. It describes six oncology projects: five from Sino Biopharmaceutical's wholly owned subsidiary LaNova Medicines and one from its group company Beijing Tide Pharmaceutical.

According to that account, the group handles early research and development. After agreed clinical development milestones are achieved, the counterparty has an option to obtain exclusive development, manufacturing, and commercialization rights. An option should not be described as an automatic transfer or an obligation to proceed.

The counterparty's identity, payment amounts, and signing date are not provided as confirmed fields in the supplied material. Leave them unfilled. The reported structure also does not establish commercial success, customer outcomes, or how the organizations reached their decisions.

Keep reported facts, unanswered questions, and proposed actions visibly separate throughout an account review.

## Turn technical capability into an investment case

A project count describes scope. It does not, by itself, explain buyer value. The value discussion needs to connect an operational change to an economic consequence and then identify the conditions under which that consequence could occur.

Use three steps to build a profit growth proposal, described in the source as a PIP:

1. Identify the affected activity. Explain whether the proposal changes research, production, or commercialization.
2. Define a measurable improvement. Use indicators that can be checked instead of adjectives such as faster or better.
3. Translate the proposed change into revenue, cost, profit, payback time, or opportunity cost.

For an early research partnership, relevant questions include the buyer's time commitment and exposure to failed development. Milestones provide checkpoints for evaluating evidence. Exclusive rights concern potential future cash flows; they do not establish that those cash flows will materialize.

Ask what evidence supports each assumption. Depending on the milestone, that discussion may include sample size, evaluation endpoints, and timing. These are items to specify and verify, not details confirmed for this collaboration.

| Structural element | Buyer-value question | Planning task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early research | What time and failure exposure are being addressed? | Identify the affected activity and assumptions |
| Clinical milestone | What evidence would justify proceeding? | Specify criteria, timing, and verification |
| Exclusive-rights option | What future opportunity could justify exercising it? | Connect rights to a conditional financial case |

## Map relationships and decision authority

The source identifies group-level decision makers and two project-originating businesses as relevant organizational perspectives. It does not provide their internal approval arrangements. For a comparable account, investigate those arrangements before treating access to one contact as organizational support.

The PRTABCD scan provides seven prompts:

- P: Past history and relevant prior dealings.
- R: Requirements and underlying needs.
- T: Timing and critical milestones.
- A: Arm, meaning the internal supporter willing to help advance the work.
- B: Budget.
- C: Competitors and alternative approaches.
- D: Decision, meaning the decision chain across people and departments.

For every item, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source, and the next question. Assign someone to close each material gap.

Distinguish operating managers who use the funds from people responsible for allocating and approving them. The former need an operating case; the latter need conditions for continuing investment.

Timing and alternatives deserve particular attention. Who determines that a milestone has been met? Where are the criteria documented? What could the buyer pursue instead? Unanswered questions here make an opportunity assessment provisional.

## Assess progress from both sides

The source separates five seller stages from seven buyer stages.

The seller stages are contact, needs discovery, proposal, negotiation, and closing. The buyer stages are recognizing a problem, assessing value, confirming that a solution exists, assigning responsibility, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing and signing.

These sequences have different levels of detail and a many-to-many relationship. A proposal does not establish that the buyer has assigned an owner. Negotiation activity does not prove that its evaluation criteria are settled.

In an account review, mark the buyer's position and the seller's position separately. Attach an observable customer action to each assessment: an accepted responsibility, documented evaluation criteria, or written commitment. If supporting evidence is absent, lower the assessed stage. Then identify who must resolve the gap between the two positions.

## Use discovery to establish the conditions

### Follow the four SPIN question types

Use situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions to move from context to a buyer-defined reason to proceed.

- Situation: What does the current pipeline and development schedule look like?
- Problem: Which early research activities are most exposed to failure?
- Implication: What would failure mean for time and committed funds?
- Need-payoff: What milestone evidence would make additional investment worthwhile?

The purpose is to establish where proposed conditions come from. When a buyer explains the consequence of a problem and the evidence needed to justify investment, the proposal has a clearer basis. Do not treat these suggested questions as evidence that the reported collaboration used this discovery process.

## Negotiate conditions before price

A milestone structure breaks a large commitment into verifiable conditions. Before negotiating price and scope, clarify what must happen, who will verify it, and which rights or commitments follow.

For each proposed milestone, document:

- The achievement and acceptance criteria.
- The person or body authorized to confirm achievement.
- The evidence and timing required for assessment.
- The process for resolving a disagreement.
- The rights or commitments available after acceptance.

Include those conditions in the financial assessment. A projected benefit is difficult to evaluate if the event that enables it remains ambiguous.

Prepare a one-page BATNA record: the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, or the best available action if agreement cannot be reached. Identify alternative partners or approaches, their timing and required investment, minimum acceptable conditions, signing authority, negotiable interests, and communications that must be retained.

Review concessions against that record. Specify the reciprocal condition sought for each concession. Also assess delivery capability before accepting milestone obligations: commitments can concentrate risk on the party responsible for meeting them.

## An actionable account checklist

Use this sequence before committing substantial proposal resources:

- [ ] Create a fact register and leave unconfirmed identities, amounts, and dates blank.
- [ ] Complete all seven PRTABCD rows with knowns, unknowns, sources, next questions, and owners.
- [ ] Connect the proposed operational change to a conditional financial benefit.
- [ ] Define milestone criteria, verification authority, evidence, and dispute handling.
- [ ] Mark buyer and seller stages separately, with customer behavior supporting each placement.
- [ ] Prepare the one-page BATNA and identify authority to make commitments.

For a first week on a comparable account, prioritize the fact register, relationship scan, and buyer-stage assessment. Use those findings to decide whether proposal development is justified.

## FAQ

### Why start with value when the structure involves negotiation?

The sequence begins with demonstrating something the buyer can assess. Value work explains why that evidence matters economically. Negotiation then determines the conditions and exchanges through which the parties commit. Discussing price before establishing the evidence leaves the investment case unresolved.

### Does reaching a milestone mean exclusive rights transfer automatically?

The supplied account describes an option to obtain exclusive rights after agreed clinical milestones. It does not establish an automatic transfer, an obligation to exercise the option, or undisclosed payment terms.

### Is an internal supporter the decision maker?

The PRTABCD framework treats these as separate questions. Arm identifies someone willing to support progress. Decision maps the broader chain of people and departments involved in approval. Support from one person does not establish the entire chain.

### What distinguishes progress from polite interest?

Look for customer actions: someone accepts responsibility for objectives and resources, evaluation criteria and verification methods are documented, or budget and terms enter formal confirmation. Verbal interest without an owner or timetable warrants further discovery.

### What makes a BATNA usable?

It names an executable alternative, its timing and investment, minimum acceptable conditions, and the relevant authority. Keep it brief enough to consult during negotiation and use it to assess whether proposed concessions secure worthwhile conditions.
