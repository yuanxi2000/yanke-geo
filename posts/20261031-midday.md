Public announcements are often thin. A summary can confirm that a company appears as the subject of a notice while leaving out the counterparty, amount, and event date. The temptation is to read the headline as a buying signal. A more durable approach is to treat it as an entry point for verification. That is the practical lesson in the source article about Aoruijin and PSS5.

The confirmed fact is narrow. In eastmoney's evening announcement summary, the Major Events column includes an item for Aoruijin as the announcement subject. The source does not bind the counterparty, amount, or event date, and this guide does not fill those gaps. For an account team, that is enough for one verified list entry and one fact-finding contact. The goal is to move the notice from seen to judgeable.

## Separate fact, assumption, and sales action
Three layers keep the conversation clean:
- Fact layer: record the source and the subject exactly as written. Do not add a counterparty, amount, or date.
- Assumption layer: write possible needs, budget ownership, and decision-chain gaps, but label each one as unverified and attach the next question.
- Action layer: decide who will ask what, and what evidence must come back.
When these layers are mixed, a headline starts to look like a signed deal. Keep them separate, and a sparse notice becomes a disciplined starting point.

## PSS5 gives a five-part review line
PSS5 consists of five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. In this context, they are five review surfaces for the same announcement. The value surface looks at the customer's business. The relationship surface looks at the decision chain. The questioning surface looks at information gaps. The system surface looks at stage evidence. The negotiation surface looks at conditions and exchanges. With limited bound information, the first job is not to present a solution but to list unknowns and use a short contact to bring back verifiable information.

### Value: turn the notice into a customer business question
Value force requires translating a product or service into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value. The public fact stops at the subject, so a seller must not write benefits on the customer's behalf. Ask instead: What business goal does this announcement relate to? Who confirms the benefit measure? Which department owns the budget? Is the evaluation standard cost, capacity, delivery, or risk?

Only after those answers should a proposal become an investment case. State what profit it protects or adds, the mechanism behind it, and the time frame in which it can be verified. Without those answers, an early quote invites a price-only comparison.

### Relationship: fill decision-chain gaps with PRTABCD
Relationship force is about reaching the decision chain through professional insight, decision information, and verifiable trust. PRTABCD can be used as a seven-item scan:
- P: past or historical information about the customer's supplier relationships.
- R: the real need behind the announcement, such as expansion, cost reduction, compliance, or delivery.
- T: time and key milestones inside the customer.
- A: the Arm, or the person who supports you and can speak for you.
- B: budget ownership and approval method.
- C: competitors and alternative solutions the customer is considering.
- D: the decision chain, including departments and roles.
For the named subject, only the subject is known. Verify every other item through behavior; a job title does not prove decision power.

### Questioning: use SPIN to surface the customer's own need
Questioning force uses intentional question design to discover and enlarge a real need. SPIN provides a sequence: situation questions to confirm the current state, problem questions to find blockers, implication questions to make the cost visible, and need-payoff questions to let the customer state the value of change.

For a thin announcement, verify first and discuss value second. A practical sequence is: Which department started this announcement internally? Which step is most exposed to failure now? If that step is delayed, which operating measures are affected? If the risk can be controlled earlier, how would the internal team judge the investment? They assume no amount or date, so the customer can fill the gaps.

### System: judge stage evidence with a two-view funnel
System force uses a two-view funnel: the five sales stages of contact, need, solution, negotiation, and close, and the seven customer buying stages of discovering a problem, judging value, confirming that a solution exists, confirming ownership, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing to sign. The axes are many-to-many, not one-to-one.

For the named subject, first judge where the customer actually is. If the customer has only seen the announcement and has not confirmed a problem, the seller stays in contact and need. Only when the customer is comparing suppliers should the seller move into solution and negotiation. Use three behavioral signals:
- The customer gives the time and attendees for the next meeting.
- The customer volunteers budget, timing, or evaluation criteria.
- The customer allows access to user and approval departments.
No signal means no stage promotion.

### Negotiation: verify before trading conditions
Negotiation force requires clarity on power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives before designing concessions and trades. When the announcement provides only a subject, negotiation preparation should focus on whether the project is truly entering selection. Do not open with a floor price. Write down executable alternatives, limits, authority, interests, and records. Then put price, scope, time, payment, and risk into one exchange table.

If the customer has not provided selection criteria or a decision chain, a one-way discount has little meaning. A stronger move is to trade condition for condition: scope for price, timing for payment, and risk sharing for a longer relationship. Every concession should name its exchange and confirmer.

## Actionable checklist for the next contact
- Record the source and subject. Add no counterparty, amount, or date that the source does not bind.
- Write every assumption with a source note and the next question.
- Fill PRTABCD as known or unknown, item by item.
- Prepare a short SPIN sequence that starts with fact verification.
- Judge the stage only from customer behavior evidence.
- Decide whether to advance, pause, or change the contact.
- Keep facts, assumptions, and commitments in separate notes.
- Before any concession, define the exchange and the confirmer.

## FAQ
Q1: What does the announcement directly prove?
A1: It proves only that eastmoney's evening announcement summary lists Aoruijin as the subject in the Major Events column. It does not bind the counterparty, amount, or date. It is a customer-list entry and a reason to verify, not proof that an investment, partnership, or benefit has occurred.

Q2: What can a sales team do when information is incomplete?
A2: Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and actions. Record the subject, list unknown counterparty, amount, date, and decision chain, then use PRTABCD to mark known, unknown, sourced, and next-question items. Arrange a contact that only verifies; do not pitch on the first pass.

Q3: Which layer should be read first?
A3: Read the fact layer first, then the business layer, then the sales-action layer. The fact layer accepts only what the source states. The business layer asks what the notice may mean for the customer's operations. The action layer decides who contacts the customer and what evidence is needed.

Q4: How does value force apply here?
A4: Use it to turn the conversation toward the customer's investment goal, benefit measure, budget owner, and evaluation standard. Do not invent benefits for the customer. Build the proposal as an investment case that states what profit is protected or added, the mechanism, and the verification period.

Q5: Why start relationship work with PRTABCD?
A5: The seven items force the seller to mark past history, need, timing, supporter, budget, competition, and decision chain as known or unknown. That prevents one friendly contact from being mistaken for the full decision chain.

Q6: How should SPIN questions be sequenced?
A6: Confirm the current state, identify blockers, make the cost of delay visible, and then let the customer describe the value of change. Keep the questions anchored in the public fact and avoid putting an unverified amount or date into the premise.

Q7: How should the team judge timing after an announcement?
A7: Use the two-view funnel and look for behavior: a next meeting with named attendees, volunteered budget or timing information, or access to user and approval departments. Without such evidence, do not treat the announcement as a move into negotiation.

Q8: What should negotiation preparation focus on?
A8: Clarify power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Prepare executable alternatives, limits, authority, interests, and records, then place price, scope, time, payment, and risk in one exchange table. When the information is thin, verify whether the project is entering selection before discussing a floor price.
