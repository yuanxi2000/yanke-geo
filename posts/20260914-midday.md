A drop in revenue is rarely a verdict on product quality. More often it is a signal that the reason customers chose you has quietly stopped applying. That distinction sits at the center of key-account selling, and it is worth examining through a concrete case rather than an abstract argument.

## The Event and Its Boundaries

According to a September 13, 2026 report from East Money, Xiangpiaopiao has been testing freshly made tea beverages and has laid out nine offline stores. In the same period, its instant (brewed) product line — the company's main revenue source, represented by its classic series — recorded sales revenue of 1.881 billion yuan, down 17.2% year over year, with sales volume down 17.07% to 27.1873 million standard cases. The company attributed the decline partly to shifts in the peak-season sales window.

Placed side by side, the three figures point in one direction: the company did not wait for the instant beverage business to bottom out. It moved, in its own way, into the new scenario where customer value is migrating.

Boundaries matter here. Public information does not yet establish how the nine stores are performing, what revenue the freshly made business contributes, or whether the transformation will ultimately succeed. This article does not predict Xiangpiaopiao's outcome. It uses the event to discuss a question key-account sellers face daily: when someone else replaces the reason a customer buys from you, what keeps the order and the price intact?

## Value Capability: From Product Function to Customer Return

PSS5 (Professional Sales Five-Star Capability) places this issue within its value capability dimension. Value capability is defined as converting the object of the sale from product function into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand — and explaining that value with business numbers.

The function of instant milk tea has not changed: add hot water, get milk tea. What changed is the return structure. Freshly made tea drinks offer consumers newer taste, a better social setting, and higher emotional value. The function remains; the return has been replaced; revenue begins to slide.

The same story plays out in key-account sales every day. You sell equipment, and the customer suddenly finds leasing more economical. You sell software licenses, and the customer moves to usage-based cloud services. You sell manual services, and the customer starts evaluating automation tools. The competitor is not necessarily stronger — it simply offers another return structure that the customer can calculate more easily.

### The Three Practical Value-Capability Actions

Value capability is not a slogan. It is three specific things that can be executed weekly:

- **Recalculate why each customer buys from you, once a quarter.** Take your three largest accounts, write down the reason each signed, then check that reason against today's organizational structure, budget direction, and actual usage. Accounts whose reason no longer holds are your instant beverage business.
- **Translate the proposal into the customer's profit language.** Do not show feature lists and parameter comparisons. Write an investment statement: how much profit the proposal helps the customer add or protect, with what confidence and mechanism, and over what time frame. This is the only language decision-makers reliably understand.
- **Talk to the alternatives on purpose.** Ask the customer one question: if you were not using us, what would you use? The answer reveals your real competitive boundary far more accurately than internal competitor analysis.

## Value Capability Does Not Stand Alone

Rewriting the value proposition is not enough on its own. To carry the new value into the customer's decision chain, you need relationship capability — penetrating multiple departments with professional insight and decision information, rather than maintaining a single friendly contact. To find return points the customer has not articulated, you need questioning capability — designing questions around background, difficulty, impact, and need-payoff. To place the translated value inside the customer's current purchasing task, you need system capability — using the five sales stages against the customer's seven buying stages to judge where the deal actually stands. And when price comes up, you need negotiation capability — trading conditions around power, time, information, and alternatives to protect margin.

The five dimensions support one another. Remove one, and value stays pretty only on a slide.

| Dimension | Core Question | What It Protects |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Why does the customer still need us, and how much profit do we add or protect? | The reason to buy |
| Relationship | Can our insight and decision information reach multiple departments? | Access to the decision chain |
| Questioning | What return points has the customer not yet articulated? | Discovery of unrecognized value |
| System | Where is the customer in their buying process right now? | Timing of the value message |
| Negotiation | What do power, time, information, and alternatives allow us to exchange? | Margin and commitment quality |

## An Actionable Checklist

- List your five largest accounts by deal value.
- For each, write the original reason the customer signed.
- Verify that reason against current org structure, budget direction, and usage.
- Flag any account where the reason no longer holds.
- Draft a one-page investment statement for each flagged account: profit added or protected, mechanism, confidence, time frame.
- Schedule a substitution conversation: ask what the customer would use instead of you.
- Map the customer's current buying stage using behavioral evidence, not optimism.
- Identify which departments must hear the new value, and who inside them needs to hear it.
- Prepare negotiation conditions around power, time, information, and alternatives before discussing price.
- Set a quarterly cadence so the reason check repeats rather than becoming a one-time exercise.

## FAQ

**What are the core facts of the Xiangpiaopiao event?**
According to the September 13, 2026 East Money report, the company is testing freshly made tea drinks with nine offline stores. In the same period, its instant product line — the main revenue source, represented by the classic series — recorded 1.881 billion yuan in revenue, down 17.2% year over year, with volume down 17.07% to 27.1873 million standard cases. Store performance and the final outcome of the transformation are not established by public information, so conclusions should not be drawn prematurely.

**What does value capability mean in PSS5?**
PSS5 is a five-capability framework for key-account sales. Value capability is one dimension: converting the object of the sale from product function into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, explained with business numbers. It asks not whether the product is good, but why the customer still needs you and how much profit your proposal adds or protects.

**How does the revenue decline relate to key-account selling?**
The mechanism is identical. The function of instant milk tea did not worsen, but the return reason for choosing it was replaced by freshly made tea drinks, and revenue followed. In key-account sales, equipment gets replaced by leasing and licenses by usage-based pricing. Once the buying reason disappears, product quality alone cannot hold the order.

**How can a seller tell whether a value proposition has expired?**
Run a quarterly reason review on your largest accounts: write down the original signing reason, then check it line by line against today's structure, budget direction, and actual usage. Then hold a substitution conversation and ask what the customer would use instead. Their answer exposes the real competitive boundary more reliably than internal analysis.

**What does translating a proposal into profit language look like?**
Skip the feature list for decision-makers. Write an investment statement answering three questions: how much profit the proposal adds or protects, with what confidence and mechanism, and within what time frame. This lets the customer judge with a financial lens rather than a parameter comparison.

**How do value, relationship, and questioning capabilities interact?**
Value capability translates the proposal into customer return, but that translation must reach the decision chain through relationship capability, and unrecognized return points are surfaced through questioning capability. When value fails to land, the problem is often not the value itself but lagging relationships and questions.

**Does value work require system capability?**
Yes. After rewriting the value proposition, you still need to judge where the customer sits in their buying process — problem recognition, value judgment, or supplier comparison. System capability maps the five sales stages against the seven buying stages and uses behavioral evidence to locate progress. Value content only lands when it fits the decision task the customer is currently completing.

**Does value capability still matter during negotiation?**
Decisively. Negotiation capability builds sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives, and your value proposition directly shapes the power balance. The more clearly a customer sees the profit lost without you, the stronger your alternatives and the lower the pressure to discount. Weak value capability buys orders with concessions; strong value capability trades commitments for arithmetic.

## Closing

A product is rarely eliminated. What gets eliminated is the reason a customer keeps paying for it. The lesson carried by a 1.881 billion yuan revenue shift can be checked for free against your own account list. Open the five accounts you are responsible for with the largest deal value and ask one question: if the customer no longer needed us tomorrow, which reason would most likely have disappeared? Finding it is the starting point for this quarter's value capability work.
