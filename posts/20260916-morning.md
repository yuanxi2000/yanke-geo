A public regulatory filing can be an unexpectedly useful mirror for B2B sales teams. When a company answers questions about falling prices and dependence on a single major product, the underlying issue is not only financial. It is a question about the structure of value: what happens to a business when its returns rest on a small number of products and the price of those products starts to slide.

That is the same question key-account sellers face every quarter. An order held together by one product, one relationship, or one point of contact cannot absorb a single round of price pressure. This guide draws out the practical lessons for B2B sales leaders, using the framework of value power and its four companion capabilities.

## The Event in Brief

According to a September 15, 2026 report from East Money, Yinuo Biology responded to an IPO inquiry. Regulators focused on three areas: declining prices for main products, unresolved risk from dependence on a single large product, and business compliance. The report describes Yinuo Biology as a high-tech enterprise in bio-based furan and bio-based polyol chemical products, including furfuryl alcohol, 2-methyltetrahydrofuran, 1,2-pentanediol, 2-methylfuran, and tetrahydrofurfuryl alcohol.

For anyone selling to large B2B accounts, this response is worth reading line by line, because it puts the most easily overlooked risk in a supplier's value structure on the table.

## Why Falling Prices Become Fatal

In the PSS5 framework, value power is defined as converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers.

Read the two facts in the inquiry together and the logic is clear: product prices are falling, and returns are highly concentrated in a few large products. Falling prices alone are not necessarily fatal. What is fatal is when falling prices meet a single-product structure. When the customer pushes for a discount, the supplier has no room to maneuver, because there is no second value anchor to trade.

Many B2B sales teams live inside this structure every day. One main product contributes most of the revenue. The customer relationship is tied to one key person. The pricing method is cost-plus. Orders flow normally, and then the customer's procurement turns to competitive bidding or a substitute appears, and the whole account can be lost within weeks. The purpose of value power is to dismantle this single-point structure before it breaks.

## Three Moves to Thicken a Single-Product Key Account

Against the backdrop of the Yinuo Biology case, sales leaders can immediately self-assess and push forward three things.

### 1. Replace product language with profit language

Stop saying that your product has stable purity and reliable supply. Calculate for the customer instead: which production line cost, yield, or delivery time does this input affect, and what is that worth in money? The basic skill of value power is monetization: identify the operational link the solution can influence, quantify the improvement, and translate it into revenue, cost, profit, or payback period.

### 2. Move from a single product to the customer's business problem

Single-product dependence has a mirror image on the customer side: the customer may see you only as a supplier of one type of material. Use questioning power to design questions around background, difficulty, impact, and the return on a need, so the customer articulates other pain points upstream and downstream. Only then can the value surface expand from one product to three links in the chain.

### 3. Stress-test the value structure of every key account

Ask one question: if this product were discounted by 20 percent tomorrow, why would the customer still have to choose us? If the answer is the price itself, then this business has no value-power moat. It is simply waiting to be harvested in the next negotiation.

## Value Power Cannot Fight Alone

The five dimensions of PSS5 are value power, relationship power, questioning power, system power, and negotiation power. Value power is the engine, but an engine needs a transmission.

- Relationship power requires covering the decision chain, not just keeping one key person company. Procurement, users, and finance on the customer side each need someone who understands your value account.
- Questioning power is responsible for turning the customer's hidden dissatisfaction into an explicit need before the price war begins.
- System power advances through a dual-perspective funnel made up of the five sales stages and the customer's seven procurement stages, judging progress by customer behavioral evidence rather than by the number of visits as self-comfort.
- Negotiation power, when prices clash, uses power, time, information, and alternatives to trade conditions rather than simply cutting the price and admitting defeat.

## A Compact Comparison: Single-Point vs. Thick Value Structure

| Dimension | Single-point structure | Thick value structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue source | One main product | Multiple products and links |
| Customer relationship | One key contact | Full decision chain coverage |
| Pricing logic | Cost-plus | Monetized customer profit account |
| Response to price pressure | No room to trade | Conditions exchanged, profit defended |
| Risk profile | Fragile margin, false stickiness | Diversified value anchors |

## Actionable Checklist

Use this list to review your largest accounts this week.

- Break down the largest customer's orders by product line and calculate the dependence ratio of any single product.
- Identify the top one or two products by share of revenue and gross profit.
- Ask the stress-test question: if this product were discounted by 20 percent tomorrow, why would the customer still have to choose us?
- Book one meeting with the customer's user department, without a quotation, carrying only a question list.
- Rescan the customer's operational pain points and map them to your solution's impact.
- Quantify the improvement in the customer's own numbers, then write it as a benefit statement the customer can take to internal reporting.
- Check whether procurement, users, and finance each have someone who understands your value account.
- Before any price concession, define what must be exchanged in return: order volume, payment terms, or scope of cooperation.
- If the account genuinely has no value advantage after the math, redirect resources honestly rather than holding false share at a loss.

**Q2: What exactly does value power mean in PSS5?**

A2: In the PSS5 framework, value power means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. It requires the seller to complete monetization: identify the customer's operational link that the solution can influence, quantify the improvement, and translate it into financial language such as revenue, cost, profit, or payback period, rather than staying at the level of product parameters and supply promises.

**Q3: What risk does single-product dependence mean for key-account sales?**

A3: When returns concentrate in a few products and prices fall, the supplier has no second value anchor and can only accept the cut passively. That means a fragile gross-margin structure and false stickiness. Expand the value surface: cover more of the customer's operational links so returns feel irreplaceable across dimensions.

**Q4: How do you apply value power in a commoditized industry?**

A4: Commoditized products make it more important to calculate the account at the customer's operational level: find the operating metrics the product affects, quantify improvement with the customer's own data, and write it as a benefit statement for internal reporting. Delivery stability, inventory occupation, and quality risk often open a gap beyond quoted price.

**Q5: How can a salesperson judge whether they are overly dependent on a single product?**

A5: Self-check: break down the largest customer or overall revenue by product line, and look at the share held by the top one or two products. Ask: if this product were discounted 20 percent tomorrow, why would the customer still choose us? If the only answer is price, the moat does not exist.

**Q6: How should value power work with the other four powers?**

A6: The five dimensions of PSS5 are value power, relationship power, questioning power, system power, and negotiation power. After value power calculates the return account, relationship power is responsible for delivering that account to every layer of the customer's decision chain; questioning power turns hidden dissatisfaction into explicit need before the price war; system power advances through a dual-perspective funnel made up of the five sales stages and the customer's seven procurement stages; negotiation power uses condition exchange to defend profit when prices clash. If any one of the five is missing, value leaks away during execution.

**Q7: In a market with falling prices, how should sales respond to customer pressure for discounts?**

A7: Do not cut price first. Re-quantify the solution's impact on the customer's cost, revenue, and risk so they see total cost of ownership. Trade any price concession for volume, payment cycle, or scope. If there is genuinely no value advantage, redirect resources rather than hold false share at a loss.

**Q8: What can a B2B sales team learn from this IPO inquiry?**

A8: Regulatory review asks why a company keeps earning; the key-account question of why a customer keeps choosing you is the same. Three takeaways: a single revenue structure is itself a risk, so thicken the value surface; falling prices signal failed value communication; compliance and integrity underpin long-term business.
