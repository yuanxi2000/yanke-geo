A bid announcement is more than a notification; it is a strategic map. When Nanjing University published its 5,583,400.00 yuan high-performance computing platform procurement, the details—buyer, winner, amount, brand, and score—became raw intelligence for any major account sales professional. This guide applies the PSS⁵ framework to dissect that announcement and extract actionable next steps, whether you won, lost, or are simply tracking the account.

## The Five Forces of PSS⁵: Turning Information into Action

PSS⁵ is a professional selling framework for major accounts, built on five forces: Value, Relationship, Questioning, System, and Negotiation. Each force offers a lens to convert a public record into a private advantage. Below, we walk through each force using the Nanjing University case as a concrete example.

### System Force: Place the Announcement in a Dual-Perspective Funnel

System force uses a dual-perspective funnel: the customer's seven-stage buying process and the seller's five-stage selling process. This announcement sits at Stage 7 of the customer's buying process—Commitment and Signing. The customer has completed budget, terms, responsibilities, and risk confirmation, and has made a formal commitment. For the winning seller, this corresponds to Stage 5 of the selling process: Closed. For losing sellers, this project cycle is closed.

However, university procurement is rarely isolated. The Visual Computing and Intelligent Perception Research Center that bought high-performance servers will likely need storage expansion, network upgrades, software licenses, maintenance services, and second-phase capacity. The current win is a starting point for a migration strategy, not an end.

If you are tracking this university but did not win, do two things immediately:

- Log the announcement in your sales funnel. Mark the customer's buying stage as 7, and note the migration opportunity window.
- Re-enter at the contact stage. Design a reason for your next visit around the likely ancillary needs of the installed equipment—maintenance, expansion, or software.

### Relationship Force: Scan Intelligence Blind Spots with PRTABCD

Relationship force emphasizes building trust through professional competence, decision information, and credible actions. PRTABCD is a tool to scan seven types of decision information. This announcement fills several gaps:

- **P (Past/History):** Did this research center make similar purchases before? Who supplied them? Check historical announcements.
- **R (Requirement):** The winning bid includes management servers and AMAX-brand high-performance computing servers, indicating a core need for computational power.
- **T (Time and Milestones):** The public notice period is one working day, meaning the signing window is imminent. Also track the implementation timeline.
- **B (Budget):** The 5,583,400.00 yuan figure reveals the scale of this center's equipment spending.
- **C (Competitors and Alternatives):** Jiangsu Zhian Information Technology Co., Ltd. won with AMAX as the brand; other brands and resellers lost.
- **D (Decision Chain):** The buyer (Nanjing University), the procurement agency (Jiangsu Bidding Center Co., Ltd.), and the research center as end-user form the basic decision framework.

Note that **A (Adopter)** is not visible in the announcement. You must discover who inside the customer supports your solution through direct contact. PRTABCD is not about filling a table; it is about identifying what you do not know and designing questions to fill those gaps.

### Value Force: Understand the Customer's Investment Logic

Value force requires translating product features into customer-understandable investment, returns, and profit improvement. Universities do not pursue profit, but they have an investment logic: What research output will this 5,583,400.00 yuan support? What computational bottleneck will it solve? Within what timeframe will it enable specific projects or publications?

The winning content—management servers and AMAX high-performance computing servers—shows the customer wants a platform to support visual computing and intelligent perception research, not just a machine. If you are a salesperson following this account, your value proposition cannot be "our servers are faster." Instead, answer: How will your solution improve compute utilization, job scheduling efficiency, data throughput, or model training time? How do those improvements translate into research output?

This is the PSS⁵ PIP (Profit Improvement Plan) concept adapted to academia: write a plan that explains how your solution increases or preserves value, the mechanism, and the timeframe. For universities, replace profit with research efficiency or resource utilization.

### Negotiation Force: Read the Competitive Signal in a 97.00 Score

The comprehensive score of 97.00 is a data point worth analyzing. In government procurement's comprehensive scoring method, 97 is high, indicating the winner scored well across price, technology, and service. What does this mean for losers?

Negotiation force emphasizes designing concessions and trades based on clear power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives. Although this deal is closed, the 97.00 score helps you gauge the power balance for the next bid. If the gap is small, your proposal and price are competitive; adjust slightly next round. If the gap is large, you have a clear weakness in technical solution or service commitment—re-examine your offering.

BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement) applies here too. If you cannot reach an agreement, what is your best alternative action? For sales, if you lose this project, your BATNA might be similar needs at other universities in the region or the next procurement window at the same research center. Define your executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records so you are prepared for the next opportunity.

### Questioning Force: Reverse-Engineer Customer Needs from the Announcement

Questioning force uses SPIN (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-Payoff) to progress needs discovery. The announcement is an outcome, but you can reverse-engineer the customer's need chain:

- **Situation Questions:** What are the research directions of the Visual Computing and Intelligent Perception Research Center? What is their current compute capacity? How large is the team?
- **Problem Questions:** Is current compute insufficient for model training? Are job queues too long? Is GPU utilization low?
- **Implication Questions:** How does compute shortage affect project progress? Publication cycles? Graduate training efficiency?
- **Need-Payoff Questions:** If the bottleneck is resolved, how much more research output can the center achieve, and in what timeframe? What new research directions become possible?

These questions are not for asking the customer (this deal is done). They help you form hypotheses before your next contact. Then use MBVS (Motivation, Benchmark, Vision, Solution) to validate those hypotheses with key people, rather than showing up with a product brochure.

## Three Actionable Judgments for Major Account Sales

First, treat this announcement as a lead in your system force funnel. The customer's buying stage is at 7, but the migration strategy window is opening. High-performance computing platforms naturally lead to maintenance, expansion, and software needs.

Second, use PRTABCD to fill intelligence gaps. The announcement gives you partial R, T, B, C, and D. You must dig for P and especially A (the adopter). Find someone inside the research center who sees value in your approach, and build trust through professional insight.

Third, redesign your next visit reason using value force. Do not say, "We also sell servers." Instead, say, "I studied the configuration of the high-performance computing platform you just purchased, and I would like to discuss directions for future compute expansion and job scheduling optimization." That is how you translate product features into customer value.
