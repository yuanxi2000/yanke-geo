Public project milestones are easy to treat as news: acknowledge, archive, move on. For key-account sales, a milestone such as the first unit at the Weijiachong pumped-storage project entering commercial operation is more useful as customer behavior evidence. The buyer could not let the unit enter commercial operation until budget, terms, responsibility, and risk had been confirmed internally. One procurement cycle has closed, and the next decision cycle has begun.

## Read the milestone on two levels

Commercial operation has an engineering layer and a customer-behavior layer. The engineering layer says one unit moved from construction and commissioning into commercial operation. The customer-behavior layer says the buyer completed internal confirmations. Sales should read the second layer because it shapes what the person across the table is thinking about next.

| Lens | Core question | What the milestone indicates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering milestone | Did the unit move from construction and commissioning into commercial operation? | A project event has occurred. |
| Customer behavior | Did the buyer finish internal confirmation of budget, terms, responsibility, and risk? | Evidence of commitment and signing; the next cycle starts here. |

Public reporting also adds useful context: the project is on the southern foot of the Dabie Mountains; large-section vertical-shaft rock joints are pronounced; plant space is limited and construction is difficult; the layout uses a runway-type semi-underground powerhouse, with curved structures to strengthen the main structure and accommodate equipment arrangement. Those details do not prove a sales opportunity. They do describe long-running customer problems—engineering conditions, space constraints, and equipment fit—that can open a substantive conversation.

## Use a dual-axis funnel to locate progress

PSS5's system capability reads progress on two axes. The seller axis has five stages: contact, need, solution, negotiation, and close. The buyer axis has seven stages: problem discovery, value judgment, solution confirmation, responsibility ownership, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The tasks are at different granularity, so the relationship is many-to-many. Forcing a one-to-one match is the fastest way to misread the account.

First-unit commercial operation means the buyer has completed the seventh item in this procurement round. The next need often starts again at problem discovery or value judgment. Treating the milestone only as a reason to send congratulations wastes real evidence. Treating it as the start of a new set of customer decision tasks creates the next useful contact.

### Locate the buyer before planning the next seller move

In an account review, ask first where the buyer is now and which customer behavior proves it. Then ask which seller action fits that position. Do not use visit count, optimism, or internal activity as a substitute for buyer evidence.

## Value force: turn operating topics into a business case

After commercial operation, the customer's attention tends to move from the engineering milestone to operating performance. Useful topics include availability, maintenance windows, spare-parts turnover, and the opportunity cost of unplanned outages. These are open business questions to verify with the customer, not conclusions to present as facts. Ask who owns the post-operation metrics and which metrics enter the annual evaluation. Then choose the version of the material for the layer you are meeting. Translate product features into investment, return, and profit improvement. The funding-and-approval owner and the operating-and-use owner have different concerns; help the operating owner make the business case for resources.

## Relationship force: close the decision-information gaps

PRTABCD is a practical way to expose blind spots across seven categories of decision information: past and historical information, needs, time and key milestones, champion, budget, competitors and alternatives, and decision chain. For each item, record four things: known, unknown, source, and next question. Public information may supply the entity, the milestone, location, geology, plant space, and layout. Decision chain, budget, and champion usually still need on-site verification.

- Champion: who is actively pushing the first post-operation action, and why?
- Decision chain: how many people and departments must approve, and who can veto?
- Time and key milestones: what internal calendar shapes the next round?
- Competitors and alternatives: will the customer keep the incumbent team or re-bid?

Relationship strength comes from professional insight, decision information, and verifiable action. Reducing unknowns is more useful than making the contact list longer. Each person on the decision chain is judging whether you can provide information they cannot easily get elsewhere.

## Questioning force: make hidden dissatisfaction speak

SPIN moves through situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions. After commercial operation, concerns often sit inside operating details. Ask; do not conclude for the customer.

- Situation: after commercial operation, how are operating arrangements, the maintenance plan, and duty shifts set for this unit?
- Problem: which operating data cannot be obtained, or arrives too slowly?
- Implication: if the data is late, which team's judgment or which report is affected?
- Need-payoff: if the data arrived earlier, which manual reconciliation step could be removed?

Quantity is not the test. The test is whether the customer begins to state the need in their own words. If answers remain yes or no, the conversation is probably still at the situation layer.

## Negotiation force: write both sides' alternatives first

Negotiation capability requires clarity on strengths, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives before designing concessions and condition exchanges. BATNA—the best alternative to a negotiated agreement—is the best fallback action available if no agreement is reached. Put it in writing: executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. After commercial operation, spares, technical upgrades, and O&M topics can all require negotiation. If the seller has not written its own alternative, price becomes the only exchange. Estimate the customer's alternatives too: staying with the current supplier, building in-house, or deferring to the next maintenance window. With alternatives on the table, the conversation can be about conditions rather than positions.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does first-unit commercial operation mean for a key-account seller?**

It is verifiable customer behavior evidence: the buyer completed budget, terms, responsibility, and risk confirmation for this procurement round. It is not an automatic order. Use it to close this round in your account map and to locate the next round's starting point.

**Q2: Do the seller's five stages and the buyer's seven stages line up one to one?**

No. They describe different task granularity and have a many-to-many relationship. A seller may believe the account is in negotiation while the buyer is still confirming responsibility ownership. Use customer behavior evidence to judge position.

**Q3: How should PRTABCD be used when public information is thin?**

Use it more deliberately. Its value is making blind spots visible. Write known, unknown, source, and next question for every category. The table then becomes a visit outline, and each visit updates it.

**Q4: What should the first post-operation visit focus on?**

Operating performance, not a product pitch. Ask about availability, maintenance windows, spare-parts turnover, unplanned outage opportunity cost, who owns those metrics, and how they enter annual evaluation. Verify each topic with the customer.

**Q5: How many SPIN questions are enough?**

Quantity is not the standard. Move from situation to problem, implication, and need-payoff until the customer adds detail and states the need in their own words. If answers stay yes or no, ask deeper impact and return questions.

**Q6: Must all five PSS5 capabilities be used at once?**

No. A common order is questioning to surface need, value to translate it into business language, relationship to fill decision information, system to locate the stage, and negotiation to exchange conditions. Strengthen the weakest capability first: if information gaps dominate, work on relationship; if position is unclear, return to stage alignment.

**Q7: How should BATNA be prepared?**

Write the executable fallback, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Also estimate the customer's alternatives. Without a seller BATNA, price tends to become the only lever.
