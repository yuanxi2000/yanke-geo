When a major customer launches an ESG investigation, it sends a clear signal: trust in a supplier is never permanent. This article examines the case of Xingyu Co., a Chinese auto parts maker whose Hong Kong listing hit a snag in 2025, and draws practical lessons for anyone in key-account sales. The source material is a Chinese business analysis that connects the event to a specific sales framework. We'll translate those insights into actionable advice for building resilient customer relationships.

## What an Investigation Really Means

Many salespeople assume that a strong customer relationship is measured by order volume or the frequency of business dinners. The Xingyu case shows a different reality: a customer keeps you as a supplier only if you remain safe on their risk list. Overseas automakers like Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz have strict compliance and reputational requirements. When negative news about a supplier surfaces, multiple departments—procurement, compliance, public relations—get involved. At that point, the customer is not evaluating your product quality; they are evaluating your overall credibility as a business partner.

In the PSS5 framework, "relationship power" is defined as building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and trustworthy actions. The focus is on understanding and covering the customer's decision-making chain, not on equating relationships with entertainment. Xingyu's cooperation with Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz is substantial—around 9.874 billion yuan in sales from its top five customers in 2025, accounting for 64.7% of revenue. Achieving that scale suggests product and delivery quality are up to par. But the customer-initiated investigation reminds us that the second half of relationship power is risk credibility—an area most salespeople rarely see or actively manage.

## Three Proactive Moves for Key-Account Sellers

### 1. Map the Decision Chain with PRTABCD

PRTABCD is a tool for scanning decision information. It stands for: P (Past information), R (Requirements), T (Time nodes), A (Arm/Advocate), B (Budget), C (Competitors and alternatives), and D (Decision chain). For each item, you record what you know, what you don't know, your information source, and your next question. The 'A' is the advocate—someone inside the customer organization who embraces you. The 'D' reminds you that large-account procurement involves multiple people and departments, not just one decision-maker.

In the Xingyu case, the investigation likely involves both compliance and procurement. If you only maintain the procurement contact, you might not even hear about a compliance issue until it's too late.

### 2. Build Trustworthy Actions in Peacetime

Trustworthy actions are not holiday gifts. They are verifiable behaviors: proactively sharing quality data, documenting problem responses, and giving advance notice of key changes. Customer trust accumulates through small, checkable actions, not through one big banquet.

### 3. Recognize the Fragility of Dependency

Xingyu's top five customers account for 64.7% of revenue. High concentration means any change in one customer's attitude has an outsized impact. If your sales performance also depends on two or three customers, ask yourself: Who are the alternative suppliers in the customer's eyes? Are you regularly testing your MBVS assumptions about key people's motivations, benchmarks, visions, and solutions?

## A Supplier's Perspective: What Would You Do?

Suppose your key customer launches a similar investigation tomorrow. What can you do? Ask yourself these questions:
- Do you know what each key person on the decision chain is most worried about right now?
- Do you have an advocate inside the customer who would speak for you and give you early risk signals?
- For projects in the solution stage, do you have behavioral evidence that the customer is truly moving forward, or is it just your gut feeling?

The five sales stages—contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing—each require evidence of customer behavior to judge progress. The Xingyu case reminds us that closing is not the end. Even after the contract is signed, you must continue to maintain relationship power because a customer can restart its review at any time.

## Actionable Checklist for Today

- List your top three customers and map their decision chains. Note how many contacts you have in each department. The blanks are your blind spots.
- For each key customer, write down one advocate hypothesis. Within two weeks, verify it through a formal or informal conversation: Is that person really speaking for you?
- For your three largest active projects, write down the most recent behavioral evidence that the customer is genuinely advancing. If you can't write anything, treat the project as a false continuation.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the most direct lesson for key-account sellers from the Xingyu case?**

A1: Customer trust in a supplier is dynamic, not permanent. Xingyu's top five customers contributed about 9.874 billion yuan in sales, 64.7% of revenue, yet negative news triggered ESG investigations from Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz. The lesson: relationship power must extend to compliance and risk departments, not just the procurement contact.

**Q2: What does 'relationship power' mean in PSS5?**

A2: In PSS5, relationship power is building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and trustworthy actions. It focuses on understanding and covering the decision chain, not on entertainment. It answers three questions: Who do you know on the decision chain? How much decision information do you have? What verifiable trust records do you have with the customer?

**Q3: What is PRTABCD and how do I use it?**

A3: PRTABCD is a decision-information scanning tool. It covers P (past information), R (requirements), T (time nodes), A (advocate), B (budget), C (competitors and alternatives), and D (decision chain). Use it to record what you know, what you don't know, your information source, and your next question for each item. This exposes your intelligence blind spots instead of relying on impressions.

**Q4: Why is 'A' in PRTABCD an advocate, not an approver?**

A4: In PRTABCD, 'A' stands for Arm, meaning advocate—someone who embraces you inside the customer organization. Approvers are part of the decision chain (D). The advocate's value is that they are willing to relay information and warn you of risks. In the Xingyu case, if the supplier had an internal advocate, they might have received signals before the investigation began.

**Q5: What does high customer concentration mean for a supplier?**

A5: Xingyu's top five customers account for 64.7% of revenue. Such a structure amplifies any change in a major customer's attitude. For salespeople, if your personal performance is also concentrated in two or three customers, acknowledge the fragility: deepen value share with existing customers, avoid putting all your time into a single relationship, and regularly test your assumptions about key people's motivations.

**Q6: What are 'trustworthy actions' and how do they differ from regular relationship maintenance?**

A6: Trustworthy actions are behaviors the customer can independently verify, such as proactively sharing quality data, documenting problem responses, and giving advance notice of key changes. Relationship maintenance leans toward interpersonal interaction; trustworthy actions lean toward facts and evidence. When a customer launches a supplier investigation, what saves you is not who you've dined with, but the verifiable record you've built.

**Q7: How do the sales funnel and relationship power relate?**

A7: PSS5's sales funnel has five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. Each stage requires behavioral evidence to judge progress. Relationship power determines whether you can get real information at each stage: the more complete your decision-chain coverage, the better you can identify false continuations and obtain real progress evidence. Salespeople with weak relationships often fill their funnel with self-perception rather than customer behavior.

**Q8: How do I use MBVS assumptions in maintaining key accounts?**

A8: MBVS means proposing and testing hypotheses about a key person's Motivation, Benchmark, Vision, and Solution. These are assumptions, not facts. After each interaction, update the verification status of these four items. If you notice a change in motivation, adjust your relationship investment accordingly, avoiding the trap of using outdated knowledge to handle new situations.
