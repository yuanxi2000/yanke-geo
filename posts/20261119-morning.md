A manufacturing policy announcement can help a key-account sales team decide where to investigate. It cannot establish that a customer has a funded project, an agreed buying process, or an intention to purchase externally. The useful work begins when a broad policy signal becomes a set of account questions supported by customer evidence.

Anhui's plan to develop nine additional development zones at the RMB 100 billion scale provides a starting point. This guide explains how to turn that signal into an account list, investigate decision responsibilities, and choose sales actions that match the customer's purchasing progress. The approach remains useful whenever public plans suggest potential demand without revealing an actual procurement.

## Separate reported facts from sales hypotheses

The supplied source attributes the public facts to an Eastmoney report: Anhui plans nine additional development zones at the RMB 100 billion scale, and the planning document discusses AI in manufacturing, industrial large models, smart factories, digital workshops, and trusted industry data spaces.

Those facts establish a direction for investigation. They do not identify which zones will move first, who will own particular projects, where budgets will come from, or how suppliers will be selected. The source provides no project counterparties, transaction amounts, or transaction dates.

Keep two categories distinct in account records:

- **Known facts:** information supported by the supplied reporting or attributable customer evidence.
- **Working hypotheses:** possible needs, responsibilities, purchasing windows, or value propositions that still require verification.

For example, a zone may care about operating efficiency, but that remains a hypothesis until customer conversations establish its relevance.

## Build a manageable account list

Treat the nine-zone policy target as an account-research scope. Build individual records as identities and evidence become available; do not fill missing entries with assumptions.

Apply three filters before committing substantial sales effort:

1. **Industry fit.** Does the zone's industrial focus match your offering and delivery capability? Look for public activity involving industrial large models, smart factories, or digital workshops.
2. **Responsibility.** Who owns enterprise transformation objectives, manages data spaces, organizes supplier selection, and accepts delivery? Record unresolved responsibilities explicitly.
3. **Purchasing evidence.** Is there evidence of a budget source, a time window, evaluation criteria, or alternatives under consideration?

Keep leads without purchasing evidence in an observation pool. Each priority account should have at least one recorded customer action, such as requesting a site investigation, sharing a transformation list, convening several departments, or clarifying acceptance criteria.

Keep the account table small: leading industry, key stakeholder roles, known problems, budget evidence, competitive alternatives, and the next question. Record evidence alongside the relevant field.

## Apply the five capabilities together

The five PSS5 capabilities provide complementary ways to examine an account. Their value comes from using them together throughout the opportunity.

### Translate technology into operating value

Value capability starts with the customer's business language. For a development zone, possible concerns include industrial clustering, the reach of enterprise digital transformation, and park operating efficiency. For resident enterprises, possible concerns include investment payback, production stability, and order opportunities.

Use interviews to establish which concerns matter. Ask what the customer would need to increase, protect, or improve, and how it would judge that change. Describing model or agent features alone leaves the business case incomplete.

For each priority account, prepare a one-page PIP, or profit growth proposal. Explain what profit the customer could increase or protect, what supports that proposition, and the period over which it could be tested. A product quotation does not answer these questions.

### Map relationships through PRTABCD

Relationship capability requires understanding the decision chain. A zone management committee, platform company, park operator, resident enterprise, IT department, and procurement department may influence different tasks. Establish their actual roles through evidence.

Use the seven PRTABCD fields:

- **P — Past:** relevant history and previous information.
- **R — Requirements:** needs and problems to address.
- **T — Time:** timing and critical milestones.
- **A — Arm:** the supporter embracing your involvement within the customer organization.
- **B — Budget:** funding evidence and unresolved budget questions.
- **C — Competition:** competing suppliers and alternative approaches.
- **D — Decision:** the decision chain across people and departments.

For every field, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source, and the next question. A supportive contact does not establish the entire decision chain.

### Ask questions that develop the business case

Questioning capability follows the SPIN sequence: situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff. Begin with how work happens today, explore difficulties, understand their consequences, and then examine the value of resolving them.

Relevant questions might concern enterprise transformation obstacles, disconnected data, responsibility for putting models into use, or acceptance standards. Establish the problem and its consequences before making budget the opening topic.

### Use a system to judge progress

System capability connects your sales process with the customer's purchasing tasks. The seller's five stages are contact, needs discovery, solution, negotiation, and closing.

The customer's seven stages are discovering a problem, assessing value, confirming that a solution exists, assigning responsibility, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting solutions and suppliers, and committing and signing.

These are different views of the opportunity. Their relationship is many-to-many, and different participants may be working on different purchasing tasks simultaneously.

### Prepare negotiation conditions early

Negotiation capability examines power, time, information, and alternatives. If an opportunity involves competitive comparisons, phased implementation, or acceptance conditions, prepare possible exchanges before making commitments.

Define scope, delivery, payment, and risk clearly. Decide which conditions could be exchanged and what each concession would require in return. This preparation helps keep commitments connected to the agreement's actual boundaries.

## Match the next action to customer evidence

The comparison below suggests useful responses. It is a working guide, not a fixed correspondence between the two processes.

| Customer purchasing task | Possible sales emphasis | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovering a problem | Contact and background interviews | Customer describes a specific difficulty |
| Assessing value | Needs discovery using SPIN | Customer explains consequences and desired returns |
| Confirming a solution exists | Joint solution development | Customer helps define success and validation |
| Selecting solutions and suppliers | Clarifying competition and the decision chain | Customer explains alternatives and selection roles |
| Committing and signing | Negotiation and closing | Customer addresses scope, payment, and acceptance |

Responsibility assignment and the build-or-buy decision also need explicit attention. A customer may recognize value while still lacking an agreed owner or a decision to purchase externally.

Before advancing an opportunity, ask: what did the customer do that shows progress on the next decision task? Repeated visits and positive conversations cannot answer that question by themselves.

## Use an actionable account checklist

At each account review, work through the following checklist:

- [ ] Separate reported facts, customer-confirmed information, and hypotheses.
- [ ] Check industry fit against your offering and delivery capability.
- [ ] Identify responsibility for transformation, data, supplier selection, and acceptance.
- [ ] Update all seven PRTABCD fields, including sources and unknowns.
- [ ] Use SPIN questions to clarify problems, consequences, and potential returns.
- [ ] Prepare or revise the one-page PIP with its basis and validation period.
- [ ] Record the customer's current purchasing tasks and supporting behavior.
- [ ] Identify budget evidence, timing, evaluation criteria, and alternatives.
- [ ] Choose the next question or action that resolves a specific uncertainty.
- [ ] Prepare conditional exchanges before negotiating commercial commitments.

Review the dual-view funnel on a consistent schedule. A useful update states what the customer did, what that establishes, and what should happen next. An entry such as 'the meeting went well' provides too little evidence to justify advancement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the policy announcement establish nine active opportunities?

No. It provides an account-screening starting point. A policy target does not establish that a particular zone has begun procurement or that a resident enterprise has chosen to buy externally.

### What distinguishes Arm from Decision in PRTABCD?

Arm identifies the supporter embracing your involvement. Decision describes the wider chain of people and departments involved in decisions. Knowing a supporter leaves other responsibilities and influences to investigate.

### What counts as meaningful progress?

Record customer actions: sharing transformation requirements, arranging a cross-department meeting, clarifying acceptance standards, identifying funding, or organizing supplier comparisons. Interpret each action in context; evidence of one task does not prove every purchasing task is complete.

### What should happen when important information is missing?

Keep it marked as unknown and formulate the next verification question. If purchasing evidence remains absent, retain the lead in the observation pool. Do not supply missing counterparties, amounts, dates, or commitments through inference.
