## A ground-breaking is not a purchase order

A large manufacturer starting construction looks like an opportunity. It is better read as an unresolved procurement window: nothing has hardened, so positioning matters more than pitching. This article uses a public report on a Fuyao Group project in Hefei, Anhui, plus a five-capability framework for key-account sales. It does not predict the outcome. It separates knowns from assumptions and converts a thin public signal into disciplined questions.

### What the public report actually confirms

- Subject: Fuyao Group.
- Event: a ground-breaking ceremony for an Anhui trim-parts and mold production base in Zipeng Town, Feixi County, Hefei.
- Investment and scale: 2 billion yuan total investment, about 310 mu planned land, roughly 260,000 sq m floor area.
- Scope: research center, modern manufacturing plants, specialized laboratories, with products covering plastic parts, intelligent assemblies, electronic modules, and precision molds.

Those four categories are all. The report does not name who runs the build-out, sets technical standards, funds purchases, or when selection begins. Record gaps as unknowns, not guesses; open questions are cheaper than defending a fabricated org chart.

## Value capability: translating technical talk into operating numbers

Value capability moves a sales conversation from product features toward the investment, returns, and profit improvement the customer understands, expressed in operating numbers. A 2 billion yuan base will be judged by on-schedule production, unit manufacturing cost, yield, and room for expansion. If your equipment, molds, tooling, or inspection solution touches those segments, translate them into the customer's operating account and monetize into revenue, cost, profit, or payback. Staying at parameter comparison keeps you in the pool of interchangeable options, a comfortable place to stand and a poor place to negotiate from.

## Relationship capability: filling in intelligence with PRTABCD

Relationship capability builds customer relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, with emphasis on understanding and covering the decision chain. A new base whose organizational shape is not visible publicly is exactly where a structured intelligence checklist earns its keep. The PRTABCD checklist lays out seven pieces of decision information; for each, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question.

- P (Past): what this customer has built before and which practices it has used.
- R (Requirement): the specific problem the build-out team needs to solve now.
- T (Time): which milestones are fixed and which are still open.
- A (Arm): who inside the customer organization is embracing you.
- B (Budget): which budget line the money comes from and who approves it.
- C (Competitor): who else the customer is comparing, and whether internal self-build is an option.
- D (Decision): which people and departments jointly participate in the decision.

The two entries most often filled in wrongly are A and D. A asks who inside the organization is embracing you; D refers to a multi-person, multi-department decision chain and the roles and influence along it. Confuse the two and every subsequent action aims at the wrong person, with effort increasing the further off course you go.

## Questioning capability: asking the requirement into existence

Questioning capability designs questions around background, difficulty, impact, and payoff so the customer sees the problem and the value of changing, mapping onto SPIN's four question types.

- S (Situation): pin down facts such as product direction and validation requirements.
- P (Problem): surface sticking points likely during build-out.
- I (Implication): lay out the cost those sticking points impose.
- N (Need-Payoff): let the customer state the benefit of resolving the situation.

What you walk away with is information; what the customer feels is being understood. Get the background first, explore impact in the middle, and hand the payoff statement to the customer at the end. Reverse the order and questioning turns into interrogation.

## System and negotiation capability

System capability uses a two-perspective funnel to manage stage matching, behavioral evidence, and continuous action. Sales stages: contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, closing. Procurement tasks: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution, confirming responsibility, deciding self-build versus purchase, selecting supplier, and committing. The two axes relate many-to-many; no one-to-one alignment is required. Only customer behavioral evidence shows whether buyer and seller are in sync; visit counts are not a substitute.

Negotiation capability designs concessions and conditional exchanges based on clarity about power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives, in pursuit of a sustainable agreement. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best course available if the two sides cannot reach terms. Preparing it means being explicit about the executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and the record you keep. With a base that has just broken ground, putting your alternative and time window on the table often changes the other side's pricing rhythm more than repeating product advantages does.

## Practical checklist

- List the facts the public report states, and stop at their edge.
- List unknowns as questions, not assumptions.
- For each of P, R, T, A, B, C, D, record known, unknown, source, and next question.
- Identify which segment of the customer's operating account your solution touches, and express it in money terms.
- Draft SPIN questions for all four types before the meeting.
- Identify which of the customer's seven procurement tasks the account currently sits at.
- Look for behavioral evidence, not visit counts, when judging sync.
- Prepare BATNA, bottom line, authority, interests, and record before any pricing discussion.
- Re-check A and D after every meeting, since they are the easiest to confuse.

## FAQ

### What does the ground-breaking have to do with key-account sales?

Public reporting shows a trim-parts and mold base with 2 billion yuan investment, about 310 mu planned land, roughly 260,000 sq m floor area, and products including plastic parts, intelligent assemblies, electronic modules, and precision molds. If your product serves such a line, mold, or lab, scan for the decision chain early. Procurement timelines remain unspecified.

### Why separate facts from unknowns?

Writing an inference as fact distorts later actions. The report confirms subject, place, investment, and construction scope but omits build-out lead, budget source, selection standards, and competitors. An imagined org chart leads to wrong questions and contacts; keeping unknowns explicit is faster than assuming.

### How does value capability apply here?

It shifts the sales object from features to the investment, return, and profit improvement the customer understands. The build-out team faces on-time production, unit cost, yield, and expansion room. Convert your solution's affected segments into the customer's operating account, monetized as revenue, cost, profit, or payback. Speak only in parameters and you become a comparable-price supplier.

### What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?

A is Arm: who inside the customer organization is embracing you. D is Decision: the multi-person, multi-department decision chain and the roles and influence along it. The others are Past, Requirement, Time, Budget, and Competitor. Every cell should record known, unknown, source, and next question.

### Why ask before proposing?

On a new-build project you don't know the customer's organizational shape or evaluation standards, so presenting a solution first trades guesses for trust. Ask facts, sticking points, and costs first; then solutions land.

### How is BATNA useful at the quotation stage?

If asked to quote without evidence of supplier selection, gather intelligence rather than concede on price.
