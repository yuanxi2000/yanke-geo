When two industry giants sign a strategic cooperation agreement, it's easy to dismiss it as a headline event with little relevance to your daily work. But look closer: the fact that both parties are willing to commit to a formal, conditionally effective agreement signals that their top-level relationship has matured. What's worth studying is every step that led to that signature.

This article unpacks a recent public example—the strategic cooperation between the Agricultural Bank of China and China Tobacco—through the lens of PSS⁵, a framework for large account sales. We'll examine the relationship dynamics, the sales process, and the negotiation tactics embedded in the deal, then translate them into actionable insights for anyone selling to banks or similar complex organizations.

### The Deal: A Public Confirmation of Top-Level Cooperation

Two signals stand out from this cooperation. First, the structure is multi-layered: it involves headquarters-to-headquarters agreements plus multiple provincial-level companies. Second, the agreement is conditionally effective—signed but with legal force pending certain conditions. For those selling to banks, this demonstrates how a major client relationship evolves from routine interactions to a written, conditional strategic partnership.

We'll dissect this event using the five dimensions of PSS⁵: Value Force, Relationship Force, Questioning Force, System Force, and Negotiation Force. Our approach: first, assess the relationship level; second, determine where the transaction sits in the procurement cycle; and third, infer the value and terms negotiations between the parties.

## Relationship Force: The Agreement Is a Confirmation, Not a Starting Point

Relationship Force builds client relationships through professional competence, decision-making information, and credible actions, focusing on understanding and covering the client's decision chain. A strategic cooperation agreement doesn't appear out of thin air. It typically follows years of business, settlement, and service interactions, where key contacts have built mutual trust, making them willing to elevate the relationship to a legally binding document.

For bank client sellers, three implications emerge:

- **Relationships have levels**: Day-to-day business cooperation is the base; solution-level cooperation is the middle; headquarters-to-headquarters strategic agreements are the top. Know where you stand and who must approve an upgrade.
- **Multi-layer structures mean multiple touchpoints**: Even if headquarters signs, provincial companies must implement. This opens windows for suppliers at the provincial level.
- **Evidence of relationship strength is action, not words**: The client's willingness to invest legal and approval resources to push the agreement forward says more than any verbal commitment.

### Using PRTABCD: A Salesperson's Intelligence Scan

Within Relationship Force, the intelligence tool PRTABCD scans seven types of decision information to identify blind spots. For each item, record what you know, what you don't, the source, and your next question. Apply this to the cooperation:

- **P – Past history**: What precedents exist from prior interactions? These set the default evaluation criteria for new suppliers.
- **R – Requirements**: After the strategic cooperation, what specific project needs will emerge in the regions? Which align with your products?
- **T – Timing and key milestones**: The agreement is conditionally effective. When will conditions be met? This directly affects your sales pipeline scheduling.
- **A – Advocates**: Who in the client organization embraces you? Are there key people in provincial companies who already support your solution?
- **B – Budget**: Strategic cooperation often comes with supporting budgets. Which line items? Who approves? You need to map this out.
- **C – Competitors and alternatives**: Agreements between giants raise the client's benchmark. Your solution must match this new standard.
- **D – Decision chain**: Banks and tobacco companies have multi-person, multi-department decision chains. Headquarters signing doesn't mean provincial procurement is decided by the same people. Verify at each level.

## System Force: Plotting the Agreement on a 7x5 Grid

System Force uses a dual-funnel map: the client's procurement seven stages (from problem discovery to commitment and signing) and the seller's five stages (contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing). Placing these on a 7x5 grid lets you use client behavior evidence to determine if the two sides are in sync—this is called "buyer-seller alignment."

For this cooperation, the parties have completed solution and supplier selection and are now in the commitment stage. The agreement is signed, but the conditional effectiveness means they haven't fully completed stage seven; formal commitment still hinges on conditions.

The lesson for sellers: nearing signing doesn't mean you can relax. Until all conditions are met, the project can stall or regress. Manage your pipeline using client-side exit evidence—like completed internal approvals or a clear effective date—rather than your own visit counts or self-reported percentages.

## Value Force and Negotiation Force: The Offense and Defense Behind the Agreement

Value Force requires translating cooperation into the client's profit and operational improvement. A signed strategic agreement means the negotiation table saw value validation: the bank provides financial services to the tobacco system; the tobacco system brings business scale to the bank. Both sides did their math. When writing large account proposals, avoid feature lists. Instead, answer three questions: How much profit will you add or protect for the client? What is the mechanism for ensuring that value? Over what timeframe?

Negotiation Force reminds you to pay attention to the conditions in the agreement. The conditional effectiveness is itself a negotiation tactic: use conditions to lock in risk, secure the intent, and activate when conditions are met. In your own contract negotiations, prepare similar chips—like phased effectiveness, milestone-based payments, or verification criteria—and know your bottom line and BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement) before you start, to avoid impulsive concessions.

## Actionable Checklist: Four Moves You Can Make This Week

1. **Push every high-level meeting toward written form**: Verbal intentions that don't evolve into conditional, milestone-based written arrangements will leave the relationship stuck at its current level.
2. **Use PRTABCD to inventory your intelligence blind spots**: For each item, write down your next question and bring it into your next client visit.
3. **Plot each of your major accounts on the 7x5 grid**: Identify the biggest buyer-seller misalignment and adjust your actions this week.
4. **Rewrite a proposal in progress**: Replace feature descriptions with profit language, and prepare at least one executable alternative as a negotiation fallback.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS⁵?**

A1: PSS⁵ (Professional Sales Star 5) is a methodology developed by Xu Hanbo, a partner at Yanke, based on over 20 years of large account sales experience. It is registered with the National Copyright Administration. It comprises five dimensions: Value Force, Relationship Force, Questioning Force, System Force, and Negotiation Force, helping sales teams advance large deals from contact to signing.

**Q2: What can a conditionally effective strategic cooperation agreement teach salespeople?**

A2: It shows two things: the top-level relationship is mature enough to be written down, but the deal isn't fully closed—its force depends on conditions. The lesson: nearing signing doesn't mean the project is safe. Continuously verify client behavior evidence like internal approvals and effective timelines. Keep pushing until all conditions are met to prevent last-minute stalls.

**Q3: What is PRTABCD and how do I use it?**

A3: PRTABCD is an intelligence scanning tool within Relationship Force. It uses seven types of information to identify blind spots: P (past history), R (requirements), T (timing and milestones), A (advocates), B (budget), C (competitors/alternatives), and D (decision chain). For each, record what you know, what you don't, the source, and your next question. This systematic approach replaces gut feelings with structured intelligence.

**Q4: How do I determine which procurement stage a major account is in?**

A4: Use the client's procurement seven stages and compare against client behaviors: problem discovery, value assessment, solution confirmation, responsibility assignment, build-vs-buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment/signing. Base your judgment on client-side evidence—such as completed internal approvals or allocated budgets—not on your own visit counts or self-reported progress percentages.

**Q5: Is Relationship Force just about wining and dining?**

A5: No. PSS⁵ defines Relationship Force as building client relationships through professional competence, decision-making information, and credible actions, with a focus on understanding and covering the decision chain. Socializing can maintain warmth, but what elevates the relationship is your consistent delivery of professional insights and a verifiable track record. The hallmark of a top-level relationship is the client's willingness to invest legal and approval resources to advance a written agreement.

**Q6: How do I apply Value Force in a proposal?**

A6: Translate your product's features into the client's language of investment, return, and profit improvement. Answer three questions: How much profit will you add or protect? What is the mechanism to ensure that value? Over what timeframe? A mature proposal reads like an investment memo, enabling the client to justify the budget internally.

**Q7: What is buyer-seller alignment and how do I avoid jumping ahead?**

A7: Buyer-seller alignment is a method in System Force: place the client's procurement seven stages and your sales five stages on a 7x5 grid, then confirm the buyer's current decision task and the seller's corresponding task. If the client is still evaluating value, pushing for a signature is jumping ahead. Adjust your actions to match the client's stage to reduce resistance.

**Q8: What should I prepare before negotiation to avoid unnecessary concessions?**

A8: According to Negotiation Force, first clarify four things: power balance, time pressure, information asymmetry, and alternatives. Know your bottom line and your BATNA—the best alternative if no agreement is reached. Also design conditional exchanges, such as phased effectiveness or milestone-based payments, to use structural arrangements instead of unilateral price cuts.
