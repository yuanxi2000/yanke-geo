A public report that the Songyuan Phase II project has started construction is not a buying signal by itself. It is customer-side behavioral evidence: the customer has moved a project into large-scale construction. The useful seller response is not to celebrate the headline or push a standard quote. It is to locate the account inside a dual-view funnel, identify which purchasing decision task the customer is completing, and choose the next action that matches that task.

## What Phase II Proves and What It Does Not

The public report confirms two things: Phase II began construction in Songyuan, Jilin, and after completion the green ammonia annual capacity will reach 600,000 metric tons. The report does not give transaction value, owner identity, or procurement arrangements. Keep those three blank in the analysis. It is reasonable to infer that someone at the project level has claimed the goal and committed resources. It is not reasonable to infer who signs, how the budget is routed, or how the supplier list will be set. Treating an inference as fact will make later actions miss the real decision path.

## Place the Start in the Customer's Seven-Stage Buying Process

The customer buying process has seven stages: problem discovery, value judgment, solution feasibility, responsibility ownership, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. When a project reaches Phase II construction, the first five are usually already answered at the project level by someone. The seller's real contest sits between solution and supplier selection and commitment and signing. Those two steps do not finish in one meeting. The customer will move back and forth among evaluation criteria, validation methods, and contractual risk.

Three common misalignments follow:
- The seller is still explaining industry prospects while the customer is comparing delivery and operations capability.
- The seller brings a standard price sheet to a phased production plan while the customer is asking how risk will be shared.
- The seller maintains one contact but has not confirmed who is accountable for Phase II production outcomes.

All three share one pattern: the seller explains progress through the seller's own actions. The PSS5 system capability asks for a different test: look for observable customer behavior, such as a stated budget basis, an arranged solution review, or responsibility assigned to a specific role.

## Dual-View Funnel: Seven Customer Tasks Against Five Sales Stages

The sales five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. The dual-view funnel puts them on the same map as the customer's seven stages. Seven against five is a many-to-many relationship. The numbers do not need to match, and mechanical one-to-one mapping is wrong. Same-frequency means the sales action serves the customer task currently being done. If the customer is still judging value, leading with payment terms is skipping ahead.

| Dimension | Customer buying seven stages | Sales five stages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content | Problem discovery; value judgment; solution feasibility; responsibility ownership; build versus buy; solution and supplier selection; commitment and signing | Contact; needs; solution; negotiation; close |
| Unit of progress | Customer decision tasks | Seller actions |
| Relationship | Many-to-many | Many-to-many |
| Main risk | Treating seller activity as customer progress | Skipping ahead when the customer is still judging value |

Use the map to diagnose whether the account is synchronized, leading, lagging, or misaligned before choosing the week's action.

## PRTABCD: Fill the Two Empty Cells First

PRTABCD uses seven categories of decision information to expose intelligence gaps. For every item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. The seven are P past and historical information, R requirement, T time and key milestones, A Arm, B budget, C competitors and alternatives, and D Decision. A is who in the customer organization embraces you; it is not Authority or Approver. D is the multi-person, multi-department decision chain; it is not a single decision maker.

In a project like this, A and D are often empty. If those two cells are empty, a detailed proposal still has no routing. You do not know who should see it first.

### The four-question PIP

Value capability translates product features into investment and return language the customer understands. The public scale figure is 600,000 metric tons of green ammonia per year. A larger scale can make continuous operation, unit cost, and delivery cadence more sensitive issues. How sensitive, the public information does not say; do not calculate it on the customer's behalf. Draft a one-page PIP that answers four questions: What profit can be increased or protected? What is the mechanism and confidence behind it? Over what time range? Who will validate it? The page should let the operating manager who uses the funds in box 2 speak to the capital allocation and approval layer in box 1.

## Negotiation Capability: Write Both BATNAs Before You Meet

Negotiation capability is about designing concessions and conditions around power, time, information, and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action each side can take if no deal is reached. Preparation should state executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. In a project like this, variables more valuable than price are often delivery batches, acceptance criteria, payment cadence, and responsibility boundaries. These can become condition trades. If you cannot write one BATNA for each side, postpone the negotiation conversation; otherwise price is likely to become the only lever.

## Field Checklist for a Key-Account Review

- [ ] Can you name the customer decision task now in play?
- [ ] What observable evidence did you obtain on the last interaction?
- [ ] Which PRTABCD cell is still blank, and what is the next question?
- [ ] Is your proposal routed to the operating and approval layers?
- [ ] Have you separated public facts from internal inferences?
- [ ] Which non-price conditions could be exchanged?
- [ ] What specific customer action will you ask for next?

## FAQ

**Q1: What does the Phase II start mean for equipment or engineering-services sellers?**
It is customer-side evidence that the project has entered large-scale construction. Return to the dual-view funnel and answer three questions: Which purchasing decision task is the customer completing now? When did we last obtain observable customer evidence? What specific action should we ask the customer to take next? If those questions cannot be answered, progress is only a feeling.

**Q2: Why put the customer's seven stages and the seller's five stages on one map?**
The units differ. The seven stages are customer decision tasks from problem discovery to commitment. The five stages are seller actions: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close. Separately, sellers can mistake their own activity for customer progress. On one map, the account can be diagnosed as synchronized, leading, lagging, or misaligned before deciding the week's action.

**Q3: What are A and D in PRTABCD?**
A is Arm, the embracer: who in the customer organization is embracing you. It is not Authority or Approver. D is Decision: the multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single decision maker. For every PRTABCD item, record known, unknown, source, and next question. A blank is intelligence: it shows who you have not yet met or what you have not yet confirmed.

**Q4: What are the five PSS5 capabilities?**
Value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value translates product into customer profit and investment value. Relationship uses professional insight and decision information to penetrate the decision chain. Questioning uses intentional questions to expand real demand. System uses the dual-view funnel made of the sales five stages and customer seven stages to manage the process. Negotiation builds sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

**Q5: What is the first value step for a large project like this?**
Translate the scale figure into the customer's operating language. The reported 600,000 metric tons per year signals a context in which continuous operation, unit cost, and delivery cadence matter, but public information is not enough to quantify the customer's sensitivity. Start with the four PIP questions: profit increased or protected, mechanism and confidence, time range, and validator.

**Q6: How should BATNA be used in negotiation preparation?**
Write down what each side can do if no deal is reached. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Preparation should define executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. If you cannot write it, your leverage is not yet formed, and entering talks can turn price into the only variable. Then list tradeable conditions such as delivery batches, acceptance criteria, and payment cadence.

**Q7: How do I know a customer visit actually moved the project?**
Look for new customer behavior. Common observable evidence includes a budget basis or source, agreement to a solution review or technical validation, responsibility assigned to a specific role, or a proactive next meeting with an agenda. If none of these appear, the visit mainly collected information; the stage should not move forward. Apply this test to the pipeline to filter out opportunities that feel good but have no customer-side evidence.

**Q8: What is the most common judgment error after Phase II starts?**
Substituting seller activity for customer behavior. Three visits and two revised proposals are not progress unless the customer has acted observably, for example by giving a budget basis, arranging a review, or assigning responsibility to a specific role. Using customer-side evidence to judge stage prevents self-comfort and avoids putting resources into stalled cells.
