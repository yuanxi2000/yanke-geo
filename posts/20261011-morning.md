A public announcement is public, but your judgment is private. It reveals the owner, budget, and financing structure early. The first move is not to celebrate the headline number. Split what you have into confirmed facts and open blanks. Confirmed facts verify the project is real and estimate scale. The speed at which you fill the blanks determines whether you can enter before selection criteria are locked. This guide uses one public announcement: a hot-spring photovoltaic project with 350 million yuan total investment, 80,000 kW capacity, and 20% capital, the rest debt-financed. It shows how to turn disclosure into a practical reading.

## Split the Announcement into Two Columns

What matters is not the news itself but the three things it puts on the table early. The owner entity, budget size, and financing structure are visible. Everything else—tender milestones, procurement package division, technical evaluation standards, whether competitors are already inside—is blank.

Confirmed: the owner is a wholly owned subsidiary of a provincial energy investment group. The site is specific and on sloped ground in a Yunnan county. The entity is real and the site concrete. Total investment is 350 million yuan. Capital is 20 percent, with the remainder financed through bank loans and other debt. This signals the owner’s price sensitivity and that payment terms will be key at the negotiating table. Rated capacity is 80,000 kilowatts, determining equipment and engineering scale, and therefore what level of person and materials to bring.

Blank: tender milestones, procurement package division, technical evaluation standards, and whether competitors have already entered. None appear in the announcement. These are precisely what sales intelligence must fill.

## Locate the Window with the Customer’s Seven-Stage Procurement Process

A dual-perspective funnel maps the customer’s seven decision tasks and the seller’s five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing. The two axes correspond many-to-many. Judge your position by customer behavior evidence: what problem the customer has acknowledged, what action the customer has committed to. That is more reliable than visit counts or your own sense of progress.

Reading the announcement against this map: the owner has publicly disclosed an investment plan, so investment intent and the project entity are clear. Earlier customer-side decision tasks have likely been completed. The critical task now falls on solution and supplier selection, followed later by commitment and signing. Entering now is in time to arrive before evaluation criteria are written. Alignment requires serving the customer’s current decision task; help them think through technical standards and success criteria. Most suppliers are stuck at contact, while the customer is near the sixth decision task. You need an entry point and a commitment to continue the conversation.

## Scan Seven Information Gaps with PRTABCD

Relationship capability starts with decision information. PRTABCD uses seven categories to scan a customer intelligence blind spot. For each category, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question to ask.

- P (Past): The subsidiary’s background and the parent’s previous moves in new energy are not in the announcement. Start with public business registration and historical announcements.
- R (Requirement): 80,000-kilowatt capacity is known, but the owner’s technical preferences and evaluation standards are not. Next question: when selecting, how much weight goes to initial investment versus long-term returns?
- T (Time): Construction pace and procurement milestones are unknown. Next question: from filing and tender to construction and commissioning, when are key milestones set?
- A (Arm): Who is willing to take your call and pass your materials internally? The announcement does not show this. Look for a first contact through local industrial supporting services and professional channels.
- B (Budget): The 350 million yuan total investment and 20 percent capital are known, but how procurement packages will be divided is not. Next question: which unit manages equipment, construction, and O&M respectively?
- C (Competitor): Whether competitors are already inside and what alternatives the owner holds will not appear in the announcement. Cross-verify through both customer and channel sides.
- D (Decision): A subsidiary builds while the parent announces. This structure usually means approval and execution are distributed across two organizational layers. This is a hypothesis to verify. Next question: who signs off on technical standards, and who signs off on commercial terms?

## Write Your Product into the Project’s Profit Account

Value capability requires translating your product or service into investment, revenue, and profit improvement the customer can understand, supported by business numbers. In a project like this, the owner’s account is concrete: for the same 80,000-kilowatt capacity, how much can your solution save on total investment, how much more can it generate, and how much less will O&M cost later?

Convert those impacts into money and write a Profit Improvement Proposal. It answers three questions: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame it will be realized. One common pitfall: forcing a generic brochure value proposition onto the project. The same equipment used on flat ground and on sloped terrain has different construction difficulty and cost structure. The value argument must stay close to the site conditions given in the announcement, and the numbers must reconcile with the owner’s investment calculations.

## Ask Better Questions and Negotiate on Structure

At first contact, use SPIN: Situation (progress), Problem (worries about terrain and interfaces), Implication (impact on schedule and revenue), Need-payoff (value of solving). Let the customer articulate the problem and value.

At the negotiation stage, read the signal from the financing structure first. When capital is only 20 percent and the majority depends on loans, the owner is usually highly sensitive to total cost and payment pace. Prepare two things in advance.

First, use BATNA—the best alternative to a negotiated agreement—to clarify your executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Know under what conditions you can walk away.

Second, design concessions and conditional exchanges. Every time you concede on price, trade it back for equivalent conditions on payment milestones, order scope, or acceptance standards. Set the goal as a sustainable agreement, not a one-time price cut.

## A Checklist You Can Use This Week

- Verify public information on the project company and its parent, and build a customer file with all seven PRTABCD categories filled in as known, unknown, and next question.
- Calculate one account for your product line: in this 350 million yuan, 80,000-kilowatt project, can you influence total investment, generation, or O&M cost? Build the skeleton of a PIP first.
- Find the first person in the project location’s industrial supporting network or professional channels who is willing to take your call. Set one goal for the meeting: obtain a commitment to continue the conversation.
- Before the meeting, prepare SPIN questions that match the customer’s current decision task.
- Prepare a BATNA summary for your own side, including walk-away conditions and concession trades.
- Track public updates for tender milestones and procurement package division, and update the blank column as facts emerge.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS5 and what dimensions does it include?** PSS5 is a five-capability framework for professional key-account sales: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. These answer how to translate product into customer profit, penetrate the decision chain, use questions to discover real needs, manage the process with a dual-perspective funnel, and negotiate a sustainable agreement.

**Q2: When a company publishes an investment announcement, what is the first step?** Intelligence decomposition. Split confirmed facts—owner entity, investment size, financing structure, site—from unstated items—tender milestones, evaluation standards, decision chain, competitor moves. Then use PRTABCD to write one question for each unknown. The announcement only solves whether the project truly exists. Whether you can enter depends on how fast you fill the blanks.

**Q3: Where does this fall in the customer’s seven-stage procurement process?** The seven stages: discovering a problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility, deciding build or buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment. The window is solution and supplier selection: enter before evaluation criteria are written.

**Q4: What should suppliers watch for in negotiation when capital is only 20 percent and the rest are bank loans?** This structure signals sensitivity to total cost and payment pace. Use BATNA to clarify alternatives and bottom line, and trade concessions for payment terms or scope.

**Q5: What is a PIP?** A Profit Improvement Proposal states how much profit your solution increases or protects, with confidence, mechanism, and timeframe, making your economic logic the customer’s standard.

**Q6: In PRTABCD, what do A (Arm) and D (Decision) mean?** A stands for Arm, the person who embraces you; D for Decision, the buying chain. Sales must understand key people in both subsidiary and parent layers, not bet on a single contact.

**Q7: What does buyer-seller alignment mean in PSS5?** Align the customer’s seven decision tasks with the seller’s five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing. Every conversation should serve the customer’s current decision task—not rush to quote.

**Q8: Without personal connections, how do you enter a newly announced project?** Use verifiable leads—project company, parent channels, local suppliers. In the first meeting, ask SPIN questions about milestones, worries, and impact; aim for a commitment to continue.
