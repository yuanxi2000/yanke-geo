When a resource-based industry leader announces a joint research center with a top university, it is more than a press release. It is a public declaration of where the customer intends to invest its most critical resources for years to come. For key-account sellers, this kind of event is a signal to act before formal procurement begins. This guide explains how to read such signals and position yourself early in the customer's decision process, using a practical framework called PSS⁵.

## The Event as a Public Signal

A joint research center between a company like Northern Rare Earth and Tsinghua University is a strategic move to pool resources for tackling key technology challenges. For outsiders, it reveals the customer's long-term R&D agenda. Different stakeholders—competitors, upstream suppliers, downstream users—will interpret it differently, but all can see that the customer is committing to a specific technical direction.

The most common mistake sellers make is waiting for a procurement announcement. By then, the customer has typically advanced to step six of the seven-step purchasing process: solution and supplier selection. Your bid becomes a price comparison. Instead, recognize that such collaborative events usually occur in the first two steps: problem identification and value assessment. The customer is still defining the problem and judging whether it is worth solving. Your job now is not to pitch products but to build relationships and ask questions that help shape the customer's thinking. If you do that well, when the customer formally defines the problem, you are already in the room.

## Relationship Power: The Expanded Decision Chain

PSS⁵ defines relationship power as building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible actions. It emphasizes understanding and covering the customer's decision chain, not equating relationships with entertainment. When a resource giant partners with a top university, it extends its internal technical decision chain into the academic world. For suppliers, this network includes three types of roles worth scanning:

- Internal technical and procurement lines within the customer organization.
- Academic leaders and their teams at the research center.
- Industrialization partners surrounding the center.

A practical tool for this scan is PRTABCD, which captures seven types of decision information. Two elements are especially critical here: D (Decision chain) and A (Arm, or champion). Who in the customer organization embraces the new R&D agenda? Through which budget and project lines will funding flow? Answers to these questions determine your competitive position years down the road.

### Actionable Steps to Penetrate Decision Information

Here are four concrete actions you can take immediately after spotting such an event:

1. **List the three types of organizational roles** involved in the research center. For each, note what you know, what you don't know, the source of information, and your next question. This forms your PRTABCD evidence table.
2. **Identify at least one champion**—someone inside the customer who is pushing the R&D agenda. Understand their attitude toward current suppliers and whether they can introduce you to technical evaluation circles.
3. **Design questions around the announced research directions.** For each direction (e.g., new rare earth materials, high-purity rare earth bioseparation, efficient comprehensive utilization of rare earth resources), prepare background questions, difficulty questions, impact questions, and need-payoff questions. Use these to verify which direction is most likely to become a procurement need.
4. **Do not rush to propose solutions.** Instead, use customer behavior evidence to judge where they are in the purchasing process. Are they taking ownership of the problem? Are they comparing build-versus-buy? Only then decide how much effort to invest.

## Five Forces in Concert: Avoid Single-Point Betting

Relying solely on relationship power is insufficient. PSS⁵ integrates five dimensions that must work together:

- **Value Power:** Translate your products and services into improvements in the customer's profit and investment. Write a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP), not a feature list.
- **Relationship Power:** As discussed, cover the decision chain with professional competence and credible actions.
- **Questioning Power:** Use questions to discover needs and help the customer see their problems clearly.
- **System Power:** Manage your sales process with a five-stage approach, using customer-side exit evidence to gauge progress, not your own visit count.
- **Negotiation Power:** Clarify your power, time, information, and alternatives in advance so you are not passive when commercial discussions begin.

Let's walk through a scenario. Suppose you are a supplier of rare earth equipment or testing services. Upon seeing the joint research center news, you first complete a decision information scan to confirm the customer's internal project approval path. Next, through industry conferences or champion referrals, you establish professional dialogue with the research center team. Use questioning power to identify which parts of the research directions require external capabilities. Once the customer reaches the stage of confirming that a solution exists and deciding whether to build or buy, you submit a value proposition based on operational numbers, showing how your solution shortens R&D verification cycles and reduces trial-and-error costs. This sequence ensures each contact serves the customer's current decision task—what we call "buyer-seller synchronization."

Conversely, if you skip the first two steps and arrive with product brochures, you face a review committee with already-formed technical standards, and your chance of winning is extremely low. The outcome of key-account competition is often decided before requirements are written into a bid document. The joint research center is both an invitation and a termination notice: those who understand it will position relationships and information early; those who do not will only be price-takers in tender documents.

## FAQ

### Q1: What is the purpose of the joint research center between Northern Rare Earth and Tsinghua University?

A1: Such centers are typically co-founded by a company and a university to tackle key core technologies. They are a way for the company to make its long-term R&D agenda public. For suppliers, this is a signal of future supporting needs and a chance to align early.

### Q2: What is PSS⁵?

A2: PSS⁵ (Professional Sales Star 5) is a systematic methodology for key-account sales, developed by Yanke partner Xu Hanbo based on over two decades of practical experience. It comprises five capabilities: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Each addresses a different aspect of selling: articulating business value, penetrating the decision chain, discovering needs through questions, managing the sales process, and reaching sustainable agreements.

### Q3: When I see a customer forming a research center with a university, what should I do first?

A3: The first step is not to contact the customer to pitch products. Instead, conduct a decision information scan using PRTABCD. List what you know and don't know about the customer's past investments, needs, timing, champions, budget lines, competitor moves, and decision chain. Create an evidence table and then decide how to initiate a professional dialogue, avoiding blind visits that waste trust.

### Q4: What do A and D stand for in PRTABCD?

A4: A stands for Arm, meaning the champion—the person in the customer organization who embraces you or your solution. D stands for Decision, referring to the decision chain, emphasizing that key-account purchases involve multiple people and departments. In a university-industry collaboration, these are crucial because technical review and procurement decisions are spread across both the company and the academic team. Missing either side can nullify your early investment.

### Q5: Why can't I wait for the procurement announcement?

A5: By the time a procurement announcement is made, the customer is usually at step six of the seven-step purchasing process—solution and supplier selection. Evaluation criteria, technical specs, and even preferred suppliers are already set internally. At that point, you can only compete on price, with little profit or influence. Events like research center launches occur earlier, when the customer is still identifying problems and assessing value. Entering then allows you to shape the problem definition and get your capabilities written into the customer's standards.

### Q6: Is relationship power just about dining and entertainment?

A6: No. PSS⁵ defines relationship power as building relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible actions. Entertainment may create goodwill, but it does not provide decision information or position. Effective relationship actions include providing industry insights to help champions get internal approval, contributing verifiable professional judgment in key technical discussions, and maintaining a contact network across technical, procurement, and finance lines so the customer thinks of you first when needs arise.

### Q7: How do I know whether to propose a solution now?

A7: Look at customer behavior evidence, not your own enthusiasm. In the seven-step purchasing process, a proposal is taken seriously only after the customer confirms the problem is worth solving, sees that solutions exist, and someone takes ownership. If your contact is still exploring, a premature proposal will be filed away as reference material. Use questioning power to confirm the customer's stage, ask background, difficulty, impact, and need-payoff questions to help them see the problem, and only present a solution when the customer asks how to solve it.

### Q8: How can suppliers build long-term advantage from such university-industry events?

A8: Treat the event as an entry point into the customer's long-term network. First, scan the organizational roles and decision chain. Second, establish professional dialogue with the research center team through industry conferences or champion referrals, showing how you can solve specific problems. Third, use value power to translate your service into improvements in R&D efficiency, cost, or profit, forming a profit improvement proposal. Fourth, manage your investment pace with the five-stage sales process, using customer behavior evidence to guide each step. Once you establish a position, you are no longer an outsider in future supporting needs.
