A customer's senior leadership choosing to spend time at your site is one of the hardest pieces of evidence that a relationship is real. A recent event offers a useful mirror for anyone selling to large accounts: are you waiting passively for customers to come to you, or are you actively building reasons worth showing up for?

## What the visit signals

Start with the facts. According to a September 25, 2026 report by East Money, a Zimbabwean government delegation visited Yanke Group for an inspection and exchange. The same report disclosed that a lithium spodumene ore processing base with an annual capacity of 2.3 million tons, invested and built by Yanke Group in Zimbabwe, was completed and put into operation in 2024. The company is currently pushing forward construction of a lithium sulfate production line as planned, while simultaneously implementing a tin-tantalum-niobium polymetallic comprehensive recovery project.

In sales terms, this was a top-level field verification: the customer bypassed slides to see the plant, production, and progress, indicating they are now verifying and confirming trust.

## Relationship capability is not about entertaining; it is about letting the customer vouch for you

PSS5 defines relationship capability as building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action. The emphasis is on understanding and covering the customer's decision chain, not equating relationships with socializing. A government delegation traveling across borders for an inspection is a live version of that definition: the customer replaces verbal promises with verifiable action.

Review your own key-account list and ask three questions:

- Is there anyone on the customer side willing to take responsibility internally for your proposal?
- Do you know who is responsible, who pays, and who uses it?
- When was the last time the customer proactively visited your site or initiated communication?

If you cannot answer all three, you have visit records, not a relationship.

Question one maps to the A in a PRTABCD scan: the embracer, the person in the customer organization who embraces you, speaks for you, and takes on risk for you.

Question two maps to D: the decision chain. Multiple people and departments participate in decisions. Who holds the budget, who uses the solution, who approves it, and where are the information gaps?

Question three maps to T: timing and key milestones. Are the customer's critical time windows synchronized with your pace of advancement?

The way to use PRTABCD is to record, for each of seven categories of decision information, what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question to ask. This inspection shows that a continuous communication channel already exists between Yanke Group and the Zimbabwean government. As a salesperson, compare that with your largest deal: of the seven items, how many can you fill in solidly? Most people cannot fill three, and the rest is guesswork.

### Why customers show up: the compounding of credible action

Senior customer time is expensive; flying across borders presupposes trust worth verifying in person. The 2.3-million-ton processing base completed in 2024 is visible, and the lithium sulfate line and polymetallic recovery project are trackable progress. The customer sees delivery, not promises.

The implication for sales action is direct: rather than repeatedly inviting a customer to dinner, deliver every commitment on time so the customer accumulates enough verifiable evidence. Trust is built up, not talked up.

## Calibrate your distance from the customer with a dual-perspective funnel

PSS5 system capability uses a dual-perspective funnel composed of the customer's seven procurement stages and the seller's five stages. On the customer side, the seven decision tasks are: discovering a problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, deciding whether to build or buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. On the seller side, the five stages are: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. Progress is judged by customer behavioral evidence, not by how many visits you made.

A government inspection mirrors high-level customer verification, typically during solution and supplier selection or commitment and signing. If this occurs, avoid rushing to quote; instead, use buy-sell synchronization to check which decision task the customer is completing and whether your action serves it. Selling out of sequence burns trust.

## Four actions you can take this week

1. Scan the largest customer with PRTABCD, noting unknowns.
2. Find an embracer and ask how far they will push internally.
3. Review and settle all commitments from the past three months.
4. Map the customer on the 7x5 funnel using behavioral evidence.

The value of the Zimbabwean delegation's visit to Yanke Group for salespeople is not in the news itself but in the completed state of relationship capability it demonstrates: the customer shows up proactively, verifies with their own eyes, and builds trust at the senior level of the decision chain. You do not need a cross-border project to do this. You only need to start with delivering your next commitment.

| Evidence | Promises and slides | Verifiable delivery records |

## Actionable checklist

- Run a PRTABCD scan on your largest account and mark every unknown item.
- Identify at least one embracer and confirm their willingness to advocate internally.
- Verify that you know who pays, who uses, and who approves.
- Audit the last three months of commitments and close any gaps.
- Plot the account on the 7x5 dual-perspective funnel using behavioral evidence.
- Confirm your next action matches the customer's current decision task.
- Create one opportunity for the customer to verify your delivery on site or through data.
- Build at least one willing contact at each level of the decision chain.

## FAQ

**Q1: What exactly is relationship capability in PSS5?**

A1: It means building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action, with emphasis on understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. It is not socializing or dining. The test is customer behavior: whether the customer initiates communication, allows you access to higher levels, and shares information ahead of key milestones.

**Q2: What does it mean when a senior customer visits a supplier site?**

A2: It indicates a basic foundation of trust already exists and the customer is verifying real capability and delivery record in person. In the seven procurement stages, this typically occurs near solution and supplier selection or commitment and signing. The right response is to provide verifiable facts and smooth arrangements, not to push a quote.

**Q3: What is PRTABCD and how is it used daily?**

A3: It is a tool for scanning blind spots in customer intelligence across seven categories: P for past history, R for requirements, T for timing, A for embracer, B for budget, C for competition and alternatives, and D for decision chain. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. Update weekly. Items you cannot fill reveal trust and information gaps.

**Q4: What role does an embracer play?**

A4: An embracer is someone in the customer organization who speaks for you, takes on risk for you, and is willing to push the cooperation forward. An embracer is not necessarily the contract signer or the most senior person. Three tasks for the salesperson: confirm they exist, judge their sphere of influence, and help them persuade their superiors using business value.

**Q5: How do I know whether buying and selling are synchronized?**

A5: Map the customer's seven procurement stages and the seller's five stages on one chart, judging each side by customer behavioral evidence. For example, discussing price while the customer confirms a solution exists is out of sequence; doing needs research after supplier selection is behind. Base judgments on what the customer did, not visit counts.

**Q6: Without a cross-border project, what can a small team learn?**

A6: The transferable part is how credible action accumulates: deliver every commitment on time so the customer holds verifiable evidence; proactively create opportunities for the customer to visit or verify, such as inviting them to a delivery site or sharing operating data; and build at least one willing contact at each level of the decision chain. Scale differs, but the way trust is built is the same.

**Q7: How does relationship capability relate to the other four capabilities?**

A7: PSS5 consists of five dimensions: value capability translates the product into customer profit and business value; relationship capability covers the decision chain; questioning capability uncovers real needs; system capability manages the process with a sales funnel and stage judgment; and negotiation capability forms sustainable agreements. Relationship capability answers who will support you; the other four answer what you support with, how you advance, and how you close.

**Q8: Of the seven PRTABCD items, which should be filled first?**

A8: Prioritize D, the decision chain, because it determines information flow, budget approval, and risk bearing. Without it, the other six items cannot be directed. Then look at the embracer; if no one embraces you, downgrade the opportunity and shift resources.
