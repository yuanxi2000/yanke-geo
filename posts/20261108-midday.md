A capacity announcement gives a sales team something concrete to discuss. It does not, by itself, establish customer value. The useful conversation begins when that capacity is connected to a customer's delivery constraints, inventory commitments and supply risks.

The supplied source reports that Chongda Technology's Thailand base has planned monthly capacity of 200,000 square meters on approximately 112,000 square meters of land. It targets overseas key accounts in communications, servers and related sectors, with products including high-layer-count printed circuit boards and advanced high-density interconnect, or HDI, boards. The source describes construction as progressing according to plan, with stronger overseas supply flexibility and shorter customer delivery cycles as objectives.

Those are starting facts and stated objectives. Turning them into a credible account strategy requires customer evidence, clear boundaries around uncertainty and agreements that connect commitments to conditions.

## Establish what the announcement actually supports

Before a customer meeting, prepare an evidence card containing the planned capacity, approximate site area, target sectors and product scope. Keep the word *planned* attached to the capacity figure wherever it appears.

Separate disclosed facts from account-specific questions:

| Disclosed information | What still needs confirmation |
| --- | --- |
| Planned monthly capacity: 200,000 square meters | Capacity available for a particular customer's orders |
| Land area: approximately 112,000 square meters | How the site supports that customer's supply requirements |
| Target sectors include communications and servers | Specific customers and their purchasing requirements |
| Product scope includes high-layer-count and advanced HDI boards | Qualification results, delivery commitments and actual scheduling |

The source does not establish customer names, order prices or committed delivery performance. Site area and aggregate capacity cannot answer those questions. Carry the gaps into discovery as questions, rather than filling them with assumptions.

## Translate capacity into the customer's operating case

Value capability means expressing a product or service in terms of the customer's profit, investment and operating needs. For this case, start with three effects to investigate:

- Whether a shorter delivery cycle would change inventory commitments and cash tied up in stock.
- Whether greater supply flexibility would reduce exposure to shortages or interrupted supply.
- Whether overseas local supply would reduce transport and cross-border delivery uncertainty.

Each is a hypothesis for customer confirmation. The announcement alone proves none of these benefits for a specific account. Use customer data wherever available, and label verbal confirmation separately from measured evidence. Do not substitute a seller's estimate for the customer's operating figures.

### Build a short investment case

Structure the discussion around the customer's purchasing task: What overseas orders are increasing? What delivery dates do they require? Where does the existing supply arrangement encounter constraints? Which portion of those orders could the Thailand base potentially support?

For communications or server accounts, connect those questions to product mix, qualification requirements and order timing. Planned aggregate capacity does not establish that the required board type will be qualified or scheduled when needed.

The resulting account note should connect an identified constraint to a possible supply response and a customer-confirmed operating effect. Where evidence is missing, write down the next question.

## Ask about the problem before presenting capacity

Questioning capability depends on deliberate discovery. Leading with a large capacity figure can leave the conversation at the level of a supplier introduction, especially when the customer has not acknowledged a delivery problem.

Use a sequence that makes the customer's reason for considering change explicit:

1. Ask where overseas supply currently involves waiting.
2. Ask which orders and downstream customers those delays affect.
3. Ask what those effects mean for inventory, delivery obligations or supply continuity.
4. Discuss whether the planned Thailand capacity could address the identified constraint.

The purpose is to let the customer articulate the need. A credible value discussion follows an acknowledged purchasing problem; it does not depend on the seller declaring that more capacity must be beneficial.

## Map support across the buying organization

Relationship capability combines professional insight, decision information and verifiable trust. An interested contact can help open a conversation, but their interest does not establish organizational agreement.

Use PRTABCD to record seven categories of account information:

- **P — Past:** Relevant history and previous dealings.
- **R — Requirements:** The customer's needs.
- **T — Time:** Timing and critical milestones.
- **A — Arm:** The internal supporter who embraces the proposed direction.
- **B — Budget:** Available funding and budget information.
- **C — Competition:** Competitors and alternative approaches.
- **D — Decision:** The decision chain across people and departments.

For each category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source and the next question. Keep the supporter and decision chain distinct: one identifies an advocate; the other explains how the organization reaches a commitment.

The capacity announcement can earn attention. Understanding the customer's purchasing responsibilities is what makes that attention useful.

## Judge progress through customer behavior

Process capability uses two views of the opportunity. The five sales stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation and closing. The seven customer purchasing stages are recognizing a problem, assessing value, confirming that a solution exists, assigning responsibility, deciding whether to build internally or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing and signing.

These views have a many-to-many relationship. A seller's stage label should therefore be checked against what the customer actually does. Meeting frequency is insufficient evidence of purchasing progress.

For example, sharing seasonal overseas order patterns can support an assessment that the customer is investigating a problem. Discussing who owns delivery responsibilities can indicate work on responsibility assignment. Including the Thailand base in a supplier comparison provides evidence of movement into selection.

Keep those signals distinct. A useful conversation about a supply problem does not establish that supplier selection has begun. Record the observed behavior and the next action needed to advance the purchasing task.

## Negotiate commitments through conditional exchanges

Negotiation capability requires preparation around bargaining strength, time, information, limits and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best executable action available if agreement cannot be reached.

Consider both sides. If the customer cannot obtain scheduling at the Thailand base, what alternative supply action is available? If Chongda Technology does not secure the account, what business impact and alternative action would follow? Where the answers are unknown, treat them as information gaps.

Preparation should identify executable alternatives, minimum acceptable terms, approval authority, interests and a written record. BATNA helps ground the discussion; it is not a device for threatening the customer.

Discuss delivery timing, batch size, payment, forecast sharing and annual framework terms together. A request for shorter lead times should prompt discussion of earlier forecasts, consolidated orders and clear acceptance responsibilities. A request for a lower price should prompt clarification of service scope, response levels and risk allocation.

Make concessions conditional, and record the conditions in terms both parties confirm. Do not turn planned capacity into an unconditional promise of delivery.

## Action checklist for an account review

- [ ] Prepare an evidence card with planned capacity, approximate land area, target sectors and product scope.
- [ ] List unknown customer names, prices, qualification results, scheduling and delivery commitments as unresolved questions.
- [ ] Obtain customer confirmation of delivery-cycle, inventory and supply-interruption concerns.
- [ ] Connect the possible supply response to a specific portion of the customer's orders.
- [ ] Complete PRTABCD with sources, gaps and next questions.
- [ ] Identify the internal supporter and map the wider decision chain.
- [ ] Support stage assessments with observable customer behavior.
- [ ] Prepare BATNAs, limits and approval authority before negotiation.
- [ ] Record conditional exchanges covering timing, volume, payment and forecasts.

Managers can use the same checklist in account reviews. Look for customer-confirmed operating effects, willingness to include the base in supplier selection and concrete exchanges of conditions. These provide stronger evidence of progress than repeated presentations of capacity figures.

## FAQ

### Does 200,000 square meters mean capacity is available now?

No. The source describes planned monthly capacity. Actual allocation and delivery depend on matters including product mix, qualification progress and order timing. It does not establish a particular customer's available scheduling.

### How can capacity become a financial discussion?

Connect a confirmed supply constraint to delivery cycles, inventory commitments or interruption risk. Ask the customer to validate the operating effect using its own information. Keep unverified effects explicitly provisional.

### Why is an internal supporter insufficient?

An advocate's support and an organization's purchasing decision are different pieces of information. PRTABCD keeps the supporter visible while requiring a separate map of the people and departments involved in commitment.

### What evidence shows meaningful sales progress?

Customer actions such as sharing seasonal order information, discussing delivery responsibilities or including the base in a supplier comparison can support different stage assessments. Interpret each action in context rather than treating all engagement as equivalent.

### What belongs in a negotiated capacity agreement?

The discussion should connect requested delivery, volume, payment and service conditions with reciprocal commitments, including forecast sharing where relevant. Confirm concessions and their conditions in a shared record.
