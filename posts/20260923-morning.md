A large telecom operator moving compute resources onto a single scheduling platform is not just a technology headline. For B2B key-account sellers, it is a signal that the way supply is delivered is changing. When supply becomes platform-based, customers stop buying a single machine or a single leased line. They start buying schedulable operating capability. The seller's vocabulary has to change with it.

## What the Source Actually Says

According to reporting cited in the source material, China Telecom has used its Xirang platform to schedule compute across regions, architectures, and providers. It is building four localized ten-thousand-card intelligent compute clusters in regions such as Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei, the Yangtze River Delta, and the Greater Bay Area, deploying industry compute nodes in more than 300 key cities, and working with partners to accelerate deep adaptation between domestic compute and models.

Those are the public facts the source explicitly supports. The business analysis below stays inside that boundary.

Translated into sales language: a very large supplier is consolidating scattered compute resources into a unified entry point. Customers will be able to call on resources across regions and architectures according to business need. For key-account managers whose business revolves around this kind of supply, the opportunity window lies in a redefinition of the customer's buying logic. The comparison shifts away from hardware specifications and unit price, and toward scheduling efficiency, business fit, and long-term operating return.

PSS5 offers a way to judge that shift. It is a five-capability framework covering value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation, and each dimension has its own set of actions.

## Value Capability: Turning Compute From a Cost Line Into a Profit Line

Value capability converts product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement expressed in operating numbers. In integrated compute scheduling, the customer buys capacity elasticity: faster training, closer inference, and absorbed idle resources. A key-account manager should translate these into earlier revenue, lower cost, and reduced capital tied up, not just bandwidth and compute specifications.

Write a Profit Improvement Proposal answering how much profit the solution adds or protects, the mechanism and levers, and the time frame. It is not a product quotation. Even if finance accepts only one item, such as converting cross-region queue wait times into the opportunity cost of a delayed launch, it carries more negotiating leverage than a competitor's price sheet.

## Relationship and Questioning Capability: Finding Your Place in an Ecosystem Alliance

The source says China Telecom is working with partners on adaptation, lengthening the decision chain: operators, integrators, model vendors, and industry customers. Relationship capability uses professional insight, decision information, and credible action to penetrate that chain. PRTABCD scans past history, requirements, timing, the adopter, budget, competitors, and the decision chain. The adopter answers who embraces you; the decision chain reminds you it is multi-person and multi-department. Record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question.

Questioning capability uncovers the customer's real problem. Design questions around background, difficulty, impact, and return, letting the customer see scheduling value themselves. Which tasks were delayed by scheduling? Which revenue milestone was affected? If scheduling improved, which business line benefits first? These lead the customer to state the value of change in their own words, more persuasive than calculating it for them.

## System and Negotiation Capability: Managing Momentum With a Dual-View Funnel

System capability manages the process with a dual-view funnel: the sales funnel and the customer's seven-stage buying process. The seller moves through contact, requirements, proposal, negotiation, and close. The buyer discovers the problem, judges value, confirms a solution, confirms ownership, decides build-or-buy, selects a vendor, and commits. Progress is judged from customer behavior, not visits. During platformization, customers stall at build-or-buy; present total cost, time, and risk comparisons instead of more demos.

Negotiation capability builds a sustainable agreement around power, time, information, and alternatives. When the supply side becomes a platform, the customer's alternatives multiply, and preparing your BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, becomes more important. Clarify your executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Every concession must be exchanged for a condition: trade a longer contract term for price room, or trade a joint validation for implementation resources. A concession without an exchange only trains the customer to push on price.

### A Compact Comparison: Old Buying Logic vs. Platform Buying Logic

| Dimension | Pre-platform buying logic | Platform-era buying logic |
|---|---|---|
| Primary comparison | Hardware specs, unit price | Scheduling efficiency, business fit |
| What is purchased | A machine, a leased line | Schedulable operating capability |
| Value framing | Cost item | Capacity elasticity and profit improvement |
| Decision chain | Narrower, fewer parties | Longer, spanning operators, integrators, model vendors |
| Seller's key tool | Quotation and demo | Profit Improvement Proposal and dual-view funnel |

## Five Action Items for Key-Account Sellers

1. Reclassify compute spending as capacity investment or cost outlay, and write the improvement in profit language using a Profit Improvement Proposal.
2. Complete decision-chain intelligence with the PRTABCD scan, focusing on the adopter and drawing the chain as an org chart.
3. Design next visit's questions around background, difficulty, impact, and return, drawing out the customer's own words on scheduling efficiency and business impact.
4. Check positions with the dual-view funnel; at build-or-buy, stop proposals and discuss total cost and risk.
5. Before negotiation, write down your BATNA, bottom line, tradeable conditions, and alternatives. Every concession must earn an equivalent return.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS5, and how does it relate to this compute buildout?**

PSS5 is a key-account sales framework built around five capabilities: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. The Xirang platform's push toward integrated compute scheduling changes the criteria customers use to judge compute purchases. This article uses the framework to break down what the seller should do in each dimension.

**Q2: After compute becomes platform-based, how should value capability show up?**

Value capability requires translating product features into the customer's investment, return, and profit improvement. The customer is not buying compute cards but capacity elasticity: faster training completion, closer inference response, and absorption of idle resources through scheduling. A Profit Improvement Proposal that states how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and by when, moves the customer's investment judgment more than a quotation does.

**Q3: How is PRTABCD used in compute-related key-account projects?**

PRTABCD is a decision-information scan under relationship capability, with seven items: past history, requirements, timing and key milestones, the adopter, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain. Compute projects have long decision chains involving operators, integrators, and model vendors. Record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question for each item, especially who is embracing you and which departments sit on the decision chain, to avoid intelligence blind spots.

**Q4: What should a seller do when the customer says they want to build compute in-house?**

Under the customer's seven-stage buying process, this corresponds to the build-or-buy step, where the customer compares internal build against external purchase on capability, time, risk, and total cost. Do not keep piling on product demos. Instead, offer a checkable comparison: the build cycle, team cost, and technical risk of self-building versus the total cost and time-to-launch of external scheduling. Helping the customer complete this judgment task earns more trust than rushing a proposal.

**Q5: What is the relationship between the five sales stages and the seven buying stages?**

In the system capability of PSS5, the seller's funnel is fixed at contact, requirements, proposal, negotiation, and close. The buyer's side is the seven-stage purchasing process, from discovering the problem to committing and signing. The two axes form a many-to-many relationship. They are not required to correspond one-to-one, and no third set of stages should be invented. Put both sides on the same chart, judge whether they are synchronized, leading, lagging, or misaligned based on customer behavior evidence, and then decide the next move.

**Q6: What should be watched in negotiation when facing a platform-scale supplier?**

Platformization means the customer has more alternatives, and the seller's leverage may decline. Negotiation capability requires designing concessions and condition exchanges on a clear understanding of power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Before entering negotiation, prepare your BATNA, clarifying executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Every concession should earn an equivalent condition, such as a longer contract term or a joint validation opportunity.

**Q7: How can questioning capability be practiced in compute scheduling projects?**

Questioning capability designs questions around background, difficulty, impact, and return on need, so the customer sees the problem and the value of change for themselves. You can ask: which tasks have been delayed because resources could not be scheduled? Which business line's revenue milestone did the delay affect? If scheduling efficiency improved, which business would benefit first? The customer stating the impact aloud is more persuasive than the seller calculating it, and it gives the later profit proposal the customer's own testimony.

**Q8: Is an ecosystem alliance like the Xirang platform an opportunity or a threat for smaller suppliers?**

The source says China Telecom is working with partners to accelerate deep adaptation between domestic compute and models, suggesting the platform builds an adaptation layer rather than doing everything itself. Suppliers can embed capability into the scheduling entry point and reach industry customers through the platform, but face commoditized competition. The response: use value capability for quantifiable operating difference and relationship capability for a professional position in the decision chain, not price alone.
