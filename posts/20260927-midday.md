When a customer is willing to stake 15% of its total assets on a new business direction, the sales question stops being "is the product good?" and becomes "what makes this investment recoverable?" That shift is the whole game in key-account sales, and a recent public procurement case gives us a rare, concrete sample to work from.

## Why This Case Matters to Key-Account Sales

According to East Money, Digital China Information Technology committed 15% of total assets to server procurement for transformation into an intelligent-computing operator. Its share price fell 34% that year. Payment: 30% advance after signing via joint account, conditioned on performance and advance-payment bonds; equipment delivered in batches, with 70% paid per batch after acceptance.

For anyone selling large B2B deals, this is not a distant financial news item. It is a ready-made procurement sample. Why a company would commit so heavily, how the buyer manages risk through contract terms, and what a supplier must bring to close are all written into public information. Using the five dimensions of PSS5 to break down this sample makes it directly applicable to the opportunities in your own pipeline.

## Value: Translating a 15% Asset Bet into Profit Language

In PSS5, value capability means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, expressed in operating numbers. The Digital China Information Technology investment is worth attention precisely because it is itself a value proposition: the company judged that future returns from intelligent computing would justify moving 15% of total assets away from existing business.

At the sales table, the decision-maker runs the same calculation. You are not selling servers, software, or services, but "when does this money turn into profit." A PIP answers three questions: how much profit can be added or protected, the confidence and mechanism, and the time frame. Board-level approval shows the organization has translated features into investment language. Your large deal must do the same.

## System: Reading Contract Terms to Locate the Buying Stage

PSS5's system capability uses a dual-perspective funnel built from the customer's seven buying stages and the seller's five selling stages. The customer's seven buying stages are: recognizing the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, deciding build-or-buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and signing. From the public terms, this transaction has already reached stage seven. The contract, payment cadence, and acceptance standards are all on paper.

What is more instructive is how the payment design manages schedule risk for both sides. Breaking the terms down one by one:

- The advance is 30% of total contract value, paid through a jointly managed account, so the buyer retains control over where the funds flow.
- Payment is conditioned on obtaining a performance bond and an advance-payment bond, shifting supplier default risk to a guarantor.
- Equipment is delivered in batches, and the remaining 70% for each batch is paid only after that batch passes acceptance, tying acceptance and payment together one-to-one.

These three points show the customer still performing risk control at commitment-and-signing. If the customer has not defined acceptance standards or discussed payment cadence, the deal is likely still at solution and vendor selection. Pushing for signature skips stages. Judge progress by behavioral evidence, such as acceptance discussions or confirmed budget lines, not visit counts or feelings.

### A Compact Comparison: What the Terms Reveal

| Contract term | Risk the buyer manages | What it signals about deal stage |
|---|---|---|
| 30% advance via jointly managed account | Control over fund flow | Commitment reached, but execution risk still guarded |
| Performance bond and advance-payment bond | Supplier default risk transferred to guarantor | Buyer has clear bottom line and leverage |
| Batch delivery, 70% paid after acceptance | Acceptance tied to payment | Detailed execution terms already negotiated |

## Negotiation: Power and Alternatives Behind the Terms

PSS5's negotiation capability emphasizes designing concessions and condition exchanges around power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. In this transaction, the buyer's demand for bonds and a jointly managed account shows the buyer holds stronger power and a clearer bottom line at the table. The supplier's acceptance of batch-based payment amounts to trading cash flow for the order.

Write down your BATNA first: your best alternative if no agreement, and your bottom line and authority. The side without an alternative accepts the other's payment terms. The side with an executable alternative can exchange conditions, such as longer payment for higher total price, or advance for faster scheduling.

## Relationship: A 34% Share-Price Drop Is a Signal to Watch the Decision Chain

The report also mentions the company's share price fell 34% during the year. Price volatility means internal debate over the pace of transformation will intensify, and both supporters and skeptics along the decision chain will become more active. PSS5's relationship capability requires covering the decision chain with professional ability, decision information, and credible action, using the PRTABCD seven-item scan (P for past, R for requirements, T for timing, A for arm, B for budget, C for competitors, D for decision chain) to record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question for each item. Pay special attention to A=Arm, meaning who in the customer organization is embracing you, and D=Decision, meaning the multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single individual. When market sentiment shifts sharply, whose concerns are amplifying and whose funding responsibility is increasing will determine which way your deal goes.

## Four Actionable Moves

1. Mark each large deal against the customer's seven buying stages, noting behavioral evidence.
2. Rewrite proposals as PIPs answering the three investment questions.
3. Mirror the payment structure: propose batch delivery with acceptance-linked payment.
4. Complete one PRTABCD scan per key account, identifying the arm and decision-chain roster.

Large deals are never closed because the product is good. They close because the customer has calculated the investment, managed the risk, and found a counterparty they can trust. This transformation procurement lays all three out in the open, and it is worth checking against your own opportunities item by item.

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the core facts of this server procurement?**

A1: According to East Money, Digital China Information Technology committed an amount equivalent to 15% of total assets to server procurement as part of a transformation toward becoming an intelligent-computing operator, and its share price fell 34% during the year. The disclosed payment terms include a 30% advance after signing, paid through a jointly managed account, conditioned on performance and advance-payment bonds, with equipment delivered in batches and the remaining 70% paid after acceptance of each batch.

**Q2: What is PSS5, and what dimensions does it include?**

A2: PSS5 is a key-account sales framework developed by Xu Hanbo () of Yanke, composed of five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value translates products into the customer's profit and investment language, relationship penetrates the decision chain, questioning uncovers real needs, system manages the sales process, and negotiation secures sustainable agreements.

**Q3: What is value capability, and how is it used in transformation deals?**

A3: Value capability converts the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement expressed in operating numbers. For a transformation deal, the customer must justify heavy investment internally, so the seller should prepare a profit improvement proposal answering how much profit can be added or protected, the confidence and mechanism, and the time frame, so the decision-maker can present it to the funding approval layer.

**Q4: How do the customer's seven buying stages relate to the seller's five selling stages?**

A4: The customer's seven buying stages are sequential decision tasks: recognizing the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership, deciding build-or-buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and signing. The seller's five stages are contact, requirements, solution, negotiation, and closing. The axes form a many-to-many dual-perspective funnel, with behavioral evidence judging synchronization.

**Q5: Why is batch acceptance linked to payment considered a good large-deal structure?**

A5: This structure manages risk in segments: the buyer starts the project with a 30% advance but controls fund flow through a jointly managed account and bonds; the supplier delivers and passes acceptance batch by batch before receiving the corresponding 70% balance. For the seller, proactively proposing such a structure can actually reduce the customer's decision resistance, because it turns the customer's concerns into executable contract terms rather than verbal promises.

**Q6: What is PRTABCD, and what do A and D refer to?**

A6: PRTABCD is a scanning tool using seven decision information types to identify blind spots: P past history, R requirements, T timing, A arm, B budget, C competitors, D decision chain. A=Arm is who embraces you; D=Decision is the multi-person chain, not a single decision-maker. It belongs to relationship capability's information scanning.

**Q7: What should negotiation capability prepare for in key-account sales?**

A7: Negotiation capability requires designing concessions and condition exchanges on the basis of clear power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Practical preparation includes at least: writing down your BATNA, clarifying your bottom line and authority, and converting the customer's payment, delivery, and acceptance demands into exchangeable chips, such as trading payment period for total price or an advance for production priority, rather than making unilateral price concessions.

**Q8: How do you judge that an opportunity has truly reached the signing stage rather than relying on a feeling?**

A8: Use customer behavioral evidence, not visit counts or funnel percentages. At commitment-and-signing, the customer confirms budget lines, discusses payment cadence, defines acceptance standards, and pushes contract review. If none appear, the opportunity is likely still at solution or selection even if the customer seems enthusiastic. Step back to help the customer complete the current decision task rather than pushing for signature.
