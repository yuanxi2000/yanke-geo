When a company announces a capacity expansion, different readers see different things. A seller may see orders; a buyer may see new bargaining room. A key-account seller has to read both signals in the same announcement and then write them into a value proposition. This guide uses one reported event to show how that reading can be structured, and it stays within what the source actually states.

## The Event Itself: A Capacity and R&D Build-Out

East Money Information reported that Junheng Technology, a subsidiary of Huilv Ecology, plans an R&D and production base in Wuhan East Lake High-Tech Development Zone to consolidate its optical module market position and technical capability, improve R&D and production conditions, and raise operational efficiency. This is supply-side expansion: building a base and expanding high-speed optical module capacity in optical communications.

For most salespeople, this kind of news gets scrolled past. But if you happen to sit in optical communications, data centers, or an adjacent equipment supply chain, the announcement is worth five minutes of attention. It answers at least one question: over the coming period, supply capability in this link of the chain will become stronger, and the bargaining dynamics around price and delivery will shift accordingly.

## Value Power: Translating Expansion into the Customer's Books

PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, built from five dimensions: value power, relationship power, questioning power, system power, and negotiation power. Start with value power: translating a product or service into investment, returns, and profit improvement that the customer can understand, and using business numbers to explain the value rather than stopping at features.

The Junheng expansion means different things depending on your position. As an upstream supplier, it may mean rising demand for equipment, materials, engineering, and testing; convert your solution into unit-output returns and ramp-up gains for the new base. As a downstream customer, stronger supply changes your bargaining chips, so re-evaluate alternatives.

There are only three concrete moves:

- Confirm which type of customer and which type of order the expansion is meant to serve.
- Rewrite your quote in the customer's financial language, for example by helping shorten capacity ramp-up time or reduce unit defect cost.
- Use a profit improvement proposal to write that account as an investment case, answering how much profit can be added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame it will be realized.

Only after these three moves are you negotiating with value power rather than knocking on doors with a product brochure.

## System Power: Where the Expanding Customer Sits in the Buying Process

PSS5's system power uses a dual-perspective funnel to track progress. The customer buying seven stages cover problem discovery, value judgment, confirmation that a solution exists, confirmation of responsibility ownership, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The sales five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. Junheng Technology's announcement of an R&D and production base shows that it has already made a choice on the build-or-buy question, and the corresponding project procurement will progressively move into selection and signing stages.

The reminder for sellers is direct: do not knock on the door of a customer that has already reached the selection stage with a generic introduction. Being in sync with the buyer means making your actions serve the customer's current decision task, and judging position by customer behavioral evidence rather than by how many times you have visited.

| Perspective | Stages | What it tells you |

| Customer buying | Problem discovery, value judgment, solution exists, responsibility ownership, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, commitment and signing | Which decision task the customer is currently completing |

The two axes are many-to-many. Use customer behavioral evidence to judge whether you are synchronized, ahead, behind, or misaligned, then let your next action serve the decision task the customer is actually working on. Avoid jumping stages.

## Relationship Power and Questioning Power: Seven Categories of Gaps Behind the Announcement

The announcement gives direction but not detail. Relationship power covers the customer's decision chain. The PRTABCD scan covers past history, requirements, timing, Arm (who embraces you), budget, competitors, and Decision (the multi-person, multi-department chain). Record knowns, unknowns, sources, and next questions. Questioning power uses SPIN to let the customer articulate expansion pace, quality thresholds, and delivery pressure.

## Three Judgments You Can Take Away Today

- An expansion announcement in the supply chain is a demand signal. First judge where you sit in the customer's buying seven stages, then decide your action. Do not visit blindly.
- For a customer in expansion, rewrite your quote into the business language of capacity ramp-up, unit cost, and delivery assurance. Replace the product introduction with a profit improvement proposal.
- Use PRTABCD to fill in the seven categories of decision information, especially timing milestones and the decision chain, so you do not hand a proposal to someone without influence in the wrong window.

The announcement is public; the opportunity is not. The same information sits in front of every competitor. The difference is who first translates capacity expansion into value the customer is willing to sign off on. That is the basic skill PSS5's five capabilities are meant to practice daily.

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify the subject, the action, and the direction stated in the announcement, and separate reported facts from your own inferences.
- Determine whether you are upstream, downstream, or adjacent to the expanding company.
- Locate the customer's position in the buying seven stages using behavioral evidence, not visit counts.
- Convert your offer into the customer's financial language: ramp-up time, unit defect cost, delivery assurance.
- Draft a profit improvement proposal that answers how much profit is added or protected, the confidence and mechanism, and the time frame.
- Run a PRTABCD scan and log known, unknown, source, and next question for each of the seven items.
- Prepare your BATNA before entering negotiation, specifying executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records.
- Match your next action to the customer's current decision task and avoid jumping stages.

**Q3: When a customer or a supply-chain company publishes an expansion announcement, what should a seller do first?**

A3: The first step is not to call, but to judge where the company sits in the customer buying seven stages. The announcement itself shows the company has already acted on the build-or-buy decision, and subsequent supporting procurement will move toward solution and supplier selection. Your action should match the selection task: prepare evaluation criteria, verification methods, and case evidence rather than handing over a generic introduction.

**Q4: What is a profit improvement proposal (PIP)?**

A4: A profit improvement proposal frames your solution's operational improvement as an investment case, answering how much profit is added or protected, the confidence and mechanism, and the time frame. It is not a product quote sheet. For an expanding customer, convert your equipment, materials, or service proposal into business numbers such as capacity ramp-up speed and unit defect cost, helping the buyer argue for budget internally.

**Q5: How is the dual-perspective funnel used?**

A5: The dual-perspective funnel maps the customer's seven buying stages and the seller's five stages. The buyer has seven decision tasks, from problem discovery to commitment and signing; the seller has five, from contact to closing. The axes are many-to-many. Use customer behavioral evidence to judge synchronization, then let your next action serve the customer's current decision task, avoiding stage-jumping.

**Q6: What is the PRTABCD tool?**

A6: PRTABCD is a decision-information scanning tool within PSS5's relationship power. It uses seven categories of information to identify intelligence blind spots: P for past history, R for requirements, T for timing and key milestones, A for Arm, meaning who in the customer organization is embracing you, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, and D for Decision, the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. Each item should record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. It is a seven-category decision-information scan, not a role profile.

**Q7: For a customer in expansion, how does the negotiation landscape change?**

A7: Stronger customer supply capability usually means its bargaining chips and timing window toward upstream suppliers both change. Sellers should prepare a BATNA in advance, meaning the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action you can take if the two sides cannot reach agreement. Preparation should specify executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records, rather than scrambling for reasons only after price pressure arrives.

**Q8: Does this article have reference value for sellers outside optical communications?**

A8: Yes. The event belongs to the optical module industry, but the analysis path is general: for any major supply-side change, first judge your positional relationship with that company, then use value power to translate your proposal into the other side's business numbers, system power to judge the buying stage, and relationship and questioning power to fill in decision information. These four steps hold in another industry.
