Reorganizations are often announced as corporate news, but they create a sales problem before they create a product story. When a company expands its service scope, customers do not automatically understand what the new capability means for them. They ask a quieter question: does this supplier now fit a problem we already have? The source case is a useful example. Keruisi disclosed a major asset restructuring plan that would add packaging shells for lasers, optical communications and hybrid integrated circuits, plus heat sinks for high-power fiber laser chips, through share issuance and cash, with matching funds. The transaction is described as a major asset restructuring and related-party transaction, not a restructuring listing, and the shares resumed trading. For B2B sales, the important change is not the press release. It is that the company can now promise a different service scope, enter different project types and talk to different functions. The sales team's first job is to translate that expanded scope into a customer investment case that the buyer can calculate.

## The first move: convert capability into an investment case

Customers do not owe a supplier attention because the supplier has a new strategy. They compare options against cost, yield, delivery, reliability and return on investment. The source calls this the value capability: turning a product or service into profit, investment and operating value for the customer. Packaging shells and heat sinks sit upstream of customer product performance and reliability. If a seller says only “we have added this business,” the buyer hears a supplier introduction. If the seller shows where the capability affects rework, de-rating, delivery delays, maintenance, downtime or certification cost, the buyer can start to compare. The source recommends a profit improvement plan, or PIP, that answers three practical questions.

### Three questions a profit improvement plan must answer

- Which customer product is affected by heat dissipation or reliability problems in the packaging shell or heat sink, and where do rework, de-rating or delivery delays appear?
- If the customer replaces or adds a supplier, how will yield, maintenance, downtime and certification investment change?
- Who inside the customer will verify the result, with which data, over what cycle, and under what release conditions?

A useful discipline is to write the PIP as a customer document, not a supplier brochure. Name the cost or revenue line, the profit protected, and the evidence the customer will accept. If the data is missing, mark it as missing and ask for it; do not turn an assumption into a customer fact.

## Rescan the decision chain after the service boundary moves

When the service scope changes, the customer-side map changes too. A contact who used to cover purchasing may need to bring in process, quality, R&D or finance. The source places this work in the relationship capability and uses PRTABCD to scan seven types of decision information: past, requirement, timing, arm, budget, competition and alternatives, and decision. Two points are easy to get wrong. A is Arm, the person or group embracing you inside the customer organization. D is Decision, a multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single leader. For every item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source and the next question. Keep unknown as unknown instead of filling the gap with a guess.

The five capabilities can be summarized as follows:

| Capability | Sales question | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Value | What customer profit, investment or operating result changes? | A cost, yield or delivery line the customer can calculate |
| Relationship | Who influences and decides, and who is embracing us? | A mapped decision chain with known and unknown items |
| Questioning | Where is the customer affected, and how would they verify improvement? | Customer data, impact estimates and agreed validation |
| System | Is the customer actually advancing? | Customer behavior such as data sharing, reviews or trials |
| Negotiation | What will we exchange for each concession? | Clear BATNA, limits and conditions recorded |

## Ask impact questions instead of interest questions

With a new capability, sellers often ask, “Are you interested?” That question invites “let us see.” The source's questioning capability asks sellers to design questions around background, difficulty, impact and need-return so customers see the problem for themselves. A practical sequence is: ask how the customer currently manages packaging and heat dissipation; ask which exceptions most affect delivery; ask what rework and line-stop losses one exception creates; then ask how the customer would want to verify a reduction in that loss. Keep the questions tied to customer business numbers. Do not present a seller's hypothesis as a customer fact.

## Use a dual-view funnel to separate activity from progress

Reorganization news can make a team excited, but customer behavior decides whether a project is moving. The source's system capability combines a five-stage sales view with a seven-stage customer purchasing view. The seller looks at contact, requirement, solution, negotiation and close. The buyer looks at problem discovery, value judgment, confirmation that a solution exists, confirmation of responsibility, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The two axes are many-to-many, not a simple one-to-one checklist. Progress should be judged by customer behavior evidence: sharing data, arranging a process review, naming a budget owner or agreeing to a trial. Visits and seller confidence cannot replace that evidence.

## Negotiate a trade, not a discount

A resumption of trading is a capital-market event, not a customer order. The source's negotiation capability asks for clarity on power, time, information, limits and alternatives before concessions are designed. If a customer uses the restructuring news to push for a lower price, first confirm the customer's BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. What can the customer actually do if no deal is reached? What are your own limits, authority and records? Price can move, but it should move with conditions: scope, payment terms, delivery pace, validation responsibility or a later expansion commitment. Write each concession as an exchange so the agreement remains sustainable.

## A practical checklist for the next account review

- Turn each new capability into three pieces of customer-verifiable evidence: performance data, validation method and responsibility boundary.
- Complete a PRTABCD review and write every unknown as a next question.
- Test the PIP against the customer's own cost, yield, delivery and certification data.
- Replace “are you interested” with questions about where the customer is affected and how they will verify improvement.
- Record customer behavior evidence before moving a project into solution, negotiation or close.
- Check every proposed concession against BATNA, authority and an explicit exchange.
- Assign a next customer action and an internal owner; do not end with “feels good.”

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the direct sales lesson from the Keruisi restructuring news?**

A1: A change in business boundary creates new customer problems and new comparison criteria. Sales should explain the added capability in terms of customer cost, yield, delivery and investment return. Customers do not have to understand a supplier's strategy; they judge whether the capability improves their operating result. Prepare a customer investment case before a technical conversation.

**Q2: What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?**

A2: A is Arm, the person or group embracing you inside the customer organization. D is Decision, a multi-person, multi-department decision chain. Neither should be replaced by a job title. Record known and unknown information, sources and next questions.

**Q3: Why rescan the decision chain after a restructuring?**

A3: A changed service boundary can change the customer's evaluation criteria. A purchasing contact may need process, quality, R&D or finance to join the judgment. Record each role's concerns, influence and next action. Maintaining only one contact can turn “someone replied” into a false sign of progress.

**Q4: How can questioning avoid “let us see”?**

A4: Move from “do you want it” to “where are you affected.” Use situation questions about current packaging and heat management, problem questions about delivery exceptions, impact questions about rework and line-stop losses, and need-return questions about how the customer would verify improvement. Keep questions tied to customer facts.

**Q5: How does the dual-view funnel judge real progress?**

A5: It places the five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages on one map. Real progress requires customer behavior evidence, such as data sharing, a review, a named budget owner or a trial. Seller actions alone do not justify moving a stage forward.

**Q6: How should sales respond when a customer uses the trading resumption to press for price?**

A6: Separate the capital-market event from the order. Clarify power, time, information, limits and alternatives, confirm the customer's BATNA, and check your own authority. A price concession should be exchanged for scope, payment, delivery pace or validation responsibility.

## Methodology and source note

This guide is based on the source titled 'Keruisi's Reorganization into Optical Communications: How Key-Account Sales Can Explain Value' and its sections on value, relationship, questioning, system and negotiation. The source describes a company restructuring disclosure and uses it as a sales-training case; it does not provide customer results or endorsements, and none are added here.
