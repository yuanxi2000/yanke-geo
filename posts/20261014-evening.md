## Opening

When a listed company announces a large capital injection into a manufacturing subsidiary, most people read it as financial news. Key-account sellers should read it as a public battle map. Public reporting, per East Money, says Changqing Technology announced a plan to inject 800 million yuan into wholly owned subsidiary Changqingshu Taizhou for a polymer new-materials production base, funded mainly by convertible bond proceeds. Those are the public facts; everything else is a question you still need to ask. This guide shows how to separate confirmed facts from blanks, locate the customer on a buying journey, turn a quotation into an investment case, and prepare for negotiation. It does not predict the project's outcome.

## Separate what is confirmed from what must stay blank

The announcement confirms the entity, the amount (800,000,000 yuan), and the intended use: the first phase of the Taizhou polymer new-materials production base, funded mainly by convertible bond proceeds.

It does not tell you the number of phases, when equipment will be tendered, which department leads it, or how acceptance standards will be defined. The common sales error is filling those blanks with familiarity and treating assumptions as confirmed customer facts.

Write confirmed and unknown items side by side using PRTABCD, which covers past/history, requirements, timing and key milestones, arm (the person who embraces you), budget, competition and alternatives, and the decision chain. For each category, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question.

- Known: the customer plans an 800 million yuan capital injection for the Taizhou polymer new-materials base (phase one).
- Unknown: the procurement list, tender rhythm, using department, approval authority, and acceptance criteria.
- Next question: who is accountable for the business result, and who controls budget approval?

### A simple rule for the first meeting

Do not present a solution in the first meeting. Present your understanding of the project, mark your unknowns, and ask the customer to correct them.

## The customer has already answered build-versus-buy

A dual-perspective funnel places the customer's seven buying stages and the seller's five selling stages on one map. The four early buying stages are problem discovery, value judgment, confirmation that a solution exists, and confirmation of ownership. The customer has answered all four with a clear funding arrangement and directed resources toward building its own production capacity.

What remains is solution and supplier selection, followed by commitment and contract signing. Your immediate priority is participating in the definition of evaluation criteria. Three things determine whether you still have a chance: who sets the criteria, in what order, and how they are verified. Arriving after the criteria are written usually leaves only price comparison.

## Value capability: turn a quotation into an investment case

When 800 million yuan goes into production capacity, the decision layer is not asking how much cheaper one piece of equipment is. It is asking when the investment starts producing a return and through what mechanism. Value capability translates products and services into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value, using business numbers, not adjectives.

The carrier is PIP, a profit improvement plan. It states how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. It is an investment case, not a product quotation. The same stack of paper can discuss unit price and discount, or how the customer's revenue and cost will change.

A second distinction: Box 1 versus Box 2. Box 1 is the capital allocation and approval layer; Box 2 is the manager layer responsible for operations and using the funds. Pitching returns only to Box 1 while leaving risk with Box 2 is a frequent mistake. Help Box 2 write the plan as a business improvement case, so Box 2 can take it to Box 1 to secure funding.

| Dimension | Quotation mindset | Investment-case mindset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary audience | Procurement or purchasing | Box 2 operations manager, then Box 1 approver |
| Core language | Unit price, discount, terms | Revenue, cost, profit, payback period |
| Document form | Product quotation | Profit improvement plan (PIP) |
| Risk placement | Left with the operating layer | Flushed out and addressed with the operating layer |
| Decision question | "Is this price competitive?" | "When does this investment pay back, and how?" |
| Negotiation effect | Invites price pressure | Shifts discussion to business outcomes |

## Two blind spots in the decision chain

Within the seven PRTABCD categories, A and D are the most frequently missed. A stands for Arm, the person in the customer organization who embraces you, regardless of title. D stands for Decision, a multi-person, multi-department decision chain, not a single leader who decides alone. Writing A as a department name, or reducing D to "find the biggest boss," will distort every later judgment.

For a key individual, use MBVS as a hypothesis: their motivation, who is the benchmark in their mind, what vision they are describing, and which single problem should the solution address first. These four lenses are hypotheses to confirm or overturn, not customer facts for a proposal.

Negotiating power comes from alternatives. The customer's willingness to direct funds toward building its own capacity changes the balance at the negotiating table. Under BATNA (the best alternative to a negotiated agreement), the more concrete the customer's alternatives, the more confidence it has to push on price. Prepare beyond price: write down your alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and record. Clarify which conditions can be traded and which cannot.

Four actions you can take this week:

- Read the announcement as a checklist: list equipment, materials, engineering, and services the customer may purchase, and mark which fall within your capability.
- Fill the PRTABCD gaps: for A and D, write one known item, one unknown item, and one next question each.
- Build a one-page investment case: replace the quotation with revenue, cost, profit, and payback period. Show it to Box 2 first.
- Book a questioning meeting: confirm who sets the evaluation criteria, in what order, and how they will be verified.

PSS5's five dimensions each have a role: value capability turns investment into return; relationship capability fills in key people and decision-chain information; questioning capability helps the customer see the problem clearly; system capability judges where the customer stands and whether action is premature; negotiation capability turns conditions into an executable agreement. Applied to this 800 million yuan capital injection, the first move remains opening PRTABCD and converting each unknown into a next question.

## FAQ

**Q1: What use is the reported 800 million yuan capital injection to a key-account seller?**

A1: It is a public customer investment signal. Changqing Technology plans to inject 800 million yuan into Changqingshu Taizhou for a polymer new-materials production base. Equipment, materials, engineering, and service procurement will follow. Read the announcement as a checklist and record known and unknown items through PRTABCD.

**Q2: What are the five capabilities of PSS5?**

A2: PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) consists of value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability translates products or services into customer profit, investment, and operating value. Relationship capability penetrates the decision chain through insight, decision information, and verifiable trust. Questioning capability uses intentional question design to uncover real needs. System capability manages the process through the sales funnel and stage judgment. Negotiation capability builds sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

**Q3: Why separate known from unknown when reading a capital expenditure announcement?**

A3: Because an announcement only answers part of the question. It confirms the entity, amount, and use of funds, but not the procurement list, tender rhythm, or approval authority. Treating those blanks as known leads to proposals built on wrong assumptions. Recording known, unknown, source, and next question for each PRTABCD category surfaces missing items and gives the next visit a clear objective.

**Q4: If the customer builds capacity itself, does a supplier still have a chance?**

A4: Yes, but the window is in the evaluation criteria. After problem discovery, value judgment, confirmation of a solution, and confirmation of ownership, what remains is solution and supplier selection. Participate in defining the criteria and ask who sets them, in what order, and how they are verified. Once written, only price comparison remains.

**Q5: What is the difference between value capability and discounting?**

A5: Discounting concedes on unit price. Value capability translates the proposal into the customer's profit, investment, and operating value, using business numbers to show improvement. Its carrier is PIP: how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. It is an investment case, not a product quotation.

**Q6: What are Box 1 and Box 2, and whom should the seller approach first?**

A6: Box 1 is the capital allocation and approval layer; Box 2 is the manager layer responsible for operations and using the funds. Both must be covered, but the more effective sequence is to help Box 2 write the business improvement case clearly, then let Box 2 take it to Box 1. Pitching returns only to Box 1 while leaving risk with Box 2 often stalls at execution.

**Q7: How should BATNA be prepared for this kind of project?**

A7: BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. The customer's decision to build its own capacity gives it more confidence. The seller must write down its alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and record, and clarify which conditions can be exchanged and which cannot, so concessions have a basis.

**Q8: What questions should questioning capability cover?**

A8: Sequence the four SPIN question types: situation to confirm scope and timing, problem to surface construction constraints, implication to calculate delay cost, need-payoff to state the desired result. Skipping difficulty to talk payoff feels like selling, not helping them judge.
