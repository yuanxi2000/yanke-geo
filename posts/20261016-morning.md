A production-line update looks like operations news. In key-account sales, it matters only when you read it as customer behavior evidence. The company disclosed two bases and two lines: a fish-products line has been commissioned, with capacity to be released gradually according to market demand, and an innovative soy-products line has been laid out and is responding quickly to market demand. The disclosure is verifiable. It does not say what the company is buying, who owns the budget, or whether an external supplier has entered. That gap is where disciplined selling starts.

## What the Disclosure Actually Says

- Beihai base: mainly fish products; fish-products line successfully commissioned; capacity released gradually based on market demand.
- Pingjiang base: mainly innovative soy products; related line laid out; responding quickly to market demand.
- Two bases, two lines. The word later matters: capacity is not released all at once.

The update can be checked by anyone at any time. It does not disclose purchasing, budget, decision rights, or supplier entry. It does show resources committed to specific capacity. That is where customer-stage judgment begins.

## Position the Customer Before You Position Yourself

PSS5 has five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. System uses a dual-view funnel: the five sales stages and the seven customer buying stages. The sales stages are contact, demand, solution, negotiation, and closing. The buying stages are problem discovery, value judgment, solution confirmation, responsibility ownership, build vs buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing.

| View | Sequence | What the disclosure suggests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller's five stages | Contact, demand, solution, negotiation, closing | The update is not seller activity; it does not place you on this axis. |
| Buyer's seven decision tasks | Problem discovery, value judgment, solution confirmation, responsibility ownership, build vs buy, solution and supplier selection, commitment and signing | Using its own bases answers build vs buy. If you sell into line operations, check whether the buyer has entered solution and supplier selection. |

The public information shows capacity is carried by the company's own production bases, so the build-vs-buy task appears answered. If you sell products or services related to line operation, raw materials, packaging, inspection, or warehousing, confirm whether the customer has entered solution and supplier selection and what behavior evidence supports that. Do not assume.

### Buy-Sell Synchronization: Match the Action to the Decision Task

Buy-sell synchronization means identifying both positions in the dual-view funnel and making each sales action serve the customer's current decision task. If the customer is still confirming responsibility ownership and you discuss contract terms, you push the relationship away. If the customer is already in solution and supplier selection and you repeat background, progress stalls. If the customer has already commissioned and laid out capacity and you never entered before, the first question is: in this line's operating phase, who is responsible for stability and efficiency? That belongs to D in PRTABCD, Decision, the multi-person, multi-department decision chain.

## Map Intelligence Blind Spots with PRTABCD

PRTABCD uses seven types of decision information to identify blind spots and record known, unknown, source, and next question. The source highlights four:

- P, past or historical: previous capacity arrangements and similar procurement records; what public channels reveal.
- A, Arm: who in the customer organization embraces you and is willing to bring your solution into internal discussion.
- T, time and key nodes: what triggers the capacity release rhythm and what the corresponding key nodes are.
- D, Decision: who participates in decisions and who owns result responsibility.

For each item, write known, unknown, source, and next question. Do not fill gaps with guesses.

## Turn the Disclosure into a Valuable Conversation

Relationship capability builds customer relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action; it focuses on understanding and covering the decision chain. Using public capacity information in a visit is better than empty rapport-building: state that the source is a public investor interaction platform answer, then ask business questions. Let the customer judge whether the conversation is worth continuing.

Questioning capability can use SPIN around background, difficulty, impact, and need payoff:

- Background: What determines the capacity release rhythm?
- Difficulty: Which part is most likely to fluctuate during release?
- Impact: How could that volatility affect costs and delivery commitments?
- Need payoff: If that part is stabilized, what can be preserved?

These are questions sales needs to ask. Public information does not show whether the company already has answers.

MBVS uses motivation, benchmark, vision, and solution as four lenses to propose and validate hypotheses. All four are hypotheses only. If you assume the counterpart cares most about utilization, that is still a hypothesis. Confirm or refute it in conversation; never record it as customer fact.

## Value and Negotiation: Translate Capacity into Business Language

Value capability converts the sales object from product features into customer-understandable investment, return, and profit improvement, using business numbers. PIP, a profit improvement plan, writes operational improvement as an investment statement. It answers how much profit can be increased or preserved, the confidence and mechanism, and the time frame. It is not a product quotation. For a commissioned base, contact Box 2, the operations manager who uses funds, and help that manager explain the investment rationale to Box 1, the capital allocation and approval layer.

Negotiation capability designs concessions and condition exchanges based on power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action if no agreement is reached. Prepare executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. For a customer that has just completed line layout, exchange conditions should target its next-stage operating tasks, not the seller's tempo.

## Actionable Checklist

1. Verify the disclosure and separate exact claims from inference. Exact claims: fish line commissioned; soy line laid out; gradual capacity release by market demand.
2. Do not infer a purchase need, budget owner, decision maker, or external supplier entry from the update.
3. Place the customer on the seven buying decision tasks. Note that build vs buy appears answered by use of own bases.
4. Ask what customer behavior evidence would show movement into solution and supplier selection. Write down missing evidence.
5. Place your own actions on the five sales stages. Do not skip stages.
6. Fill PRTABCD for P, A, T, and D with known, unknown, source, and next question.
7. Draft SPIN questions around release rhythm, volatility, cost and delivery impact, and stabilization payoff.
8. Write MBVS hypotheses as hypotheses. Mark each one unverified until the customer confirms or rejects it.
9. Prepare a PIP-style value story only after obtaining customer data. Do not invent numbers or outcomes.
10. Prepare for negotiation with BATNA, bottom line, authority, tradeables, and records. Tie trades to the next operating task.
11. Define the next customer behavior evidence that would justify advancing. If it is absent, do not advance to proposal or quote.

## FAQ

Q1: Is this production-line disclosure a valid key-account lead?
A1: It is verifiable public customer behavior evidence. It shows resources committed to specific bases and lines. It does not show purchasing need, budget, or decision maker. Use the dual-view funnel to locate the customer, then look for customer behavior evidence before advancing.

Q2: What are the five PSS5 dimensions?
A2: Value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. Value translates products into business value; relationship works through the decision chain; questioning uncovers real needs; system manages progression with the dual-view funnel; negotiation designs sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

Q3: How do the five sales stages differ from the seven buying tasks?
A3: The five sales stages are the seller's axis: contact, demand, solution, negotiation, closing. The seven buying tasks are the buyer's decision work: problem discovery, value judgment, solution confirmation, responsibility ownership, build vs buy, solution and supplier selection, commitment and signing. They are many-to-many, not one-to-one. Seller activity is not customer evidence.

Q4: Why be more cautious when the customer has already commissioned capacity?
A4: Commissioning and layout completion suggest the customer has moved past earlier decision tasks. It now cares about operational stability, efficiency, and who owns the result. Without customer behavior evidence, a seller who infers need from public information and jumps to a quote can turn a trust-building conversation into an ineffective pitch.

Q5: How should PRTABCD be used in this case?
A5: Use it to find intelligence blind spots and record known, unknown, source, and next question for each decision information type. For this update, start with P, A, T, and D. Turn empty cells into questions for the next visit rather than filling them with assumptions.

Q6: If public information has no amount or counterparty, can value capability still work?
A6: Yes, but the method changes. Value capability requires converting the offer into investment, return, and profit improvement with business numbers. When amounts are missing, obtain customer data through questioning and operations-level contact. Then use PIP to write the improvement as an investment statement. Each element needs a customer-confirmed basis.

Q7: What should negotiation focus on for a customer just after line layout?
A7: Focus on power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. Prepare BATNA as the best fallback if no agreement is reached. Make executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records explicit. Exchange conditions should align with the customer's next operating task.

Q8: In one line, what is the sales reminder?
A8: Treat the disclosure as a starting point: use the dual-view funnel to judge position, PRTABCD to expose blind spots, and SPIN to design a verifiable conversation, then decide whether to advance based on customer behavior evidence.
