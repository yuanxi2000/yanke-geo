A large engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) opportunity can attract attention before its decision process is understood. Before discussing price, establish three things: who actively supports the proposed solution, who allocates the money, and who can commit the customer. Record identifiable people and evidence wherever possible. Leave the remaining entries explicitly unknown.

The reported Guinea alumina project provides a useful starting point for this discipline. Its public description identifies the project parties and proposed contracting model, while leaving important commercial questions unanswered. For suppliers and partners considering such an opportunity, the practical task is to turn those gaps into a structured investigation.

## Establish the boundary between facts and assumptions

According to the supplied source's account of Eastmoney reporting, Aluminum Corporation of China's Guinea alumina project was proposed to be undertaken by China Aluminum International under an EPC arrangement. The report described it as Aluminum Corporation of China's first overseas alumina project and noted the integrated design and construction advantages of the proposed model.

Those statements establish a limited factual foundation. They do not identify the customer's decision-makers, budget source, approval route or project milestones. The proposed arrangement should also retain its provisional wording; it should not become a confirmed contract in an internal briefing.

Begin the opportunity record with two categories: reported facts and unresolved questions. Do not infer an individual's authority from a company name, or assume that a publicly announced project already has an accessible budget.

## Use PRTABCD to organize decision information

Within PSS5, relationship capability combines professional competence, decision information and credible action. Its focus is understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. Familiarity alone cannot establish who supports a proposal or how approval works.

PRTABCD provides seven categories for scanning that chain. For every category, record what is known, what remains unknown, the information source and the next question.

- **P — Past:** Which comparable projects has the customer undertaken? Which previous lesson does the organization repeatedly mention?
- **R — Requirements:** Is capacity expansion, cost reduction or supply security the documented objective? Distinguish a written requirement from an untested interpretation.
- **T — Time:** What milestones govern construction, commissioning and acceptance? Who can change them?
- **A — Arm:** Who inside the customer organization actively embraces and advances your proposal? What effort has that person made to move it forward?
- **B — Budget:** Which funding category would finance the project, and which approval level controls access to it?
- **C — Competition:** Who else is developing a solution? What criteria will the customer use to eliminate alternatives?
- **D — Decision:** Which people and departments must agree, and who can veto the proposal?

A completed scan is an evidence record, not a collection of confident guesses. An empty entry gives the next conversation a purpose. If a contact cannot answer, the next useful question may be who can.

### Keep the supporter separate from the decision chain

In this tool, A means Arm: the person embracing your proposal inside the customer's organization. It does not mean bargaining leverage or departmental approval authority. Look for evidence of active support, including what the person has done to advance the proposal.

D means Decision and covers the participating people and departments. Do not reduce it to one senior leader. A supporter can help a proposal advance, while the wider chain determines where it will be examined and potentially rejected.

These entries serve different purposes. Knowing a supporter creates an opening for further inquiry; it does not prove that every approval requirement has been covered.

## Connect operating needs with funding approval

The source distinguishes two organizational layers. Box 1 allocates and approves funds. Box 2 consists of managers responsible for operations and for using those funds. Identify both before deciding who should present the business case.

| Dimension | Box 1: Funding and approval | Box 2: Operations and fund use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Responsibility | Allocate and approve money | Manage operations and use the money |
| Discussion focus | A case that supports approval | The operating improvement required |
| Preparation | Approval route and reporting requirements | Capacity, unit cost and supply certainty |

The sales task is to help Box 2 express operating improvements in language Box 1 can evaluate. Additional capacity, unit cost and supply certainty are relevant categories; their actual values must come from verified project information.

For a cross-border opportunity, establish who occupies each layer, where they work and how they report. Then prepare an operating explanation for the managers and a concise approval case for the funding layer. A one-page brief is useful only when its assumptions and evidence are clear.

## Use questions to test stakeholder hypotheses

PSS5 questioning capability moves through background, difficulty, impact and need-payoff questions. First establish the situation and constraints. Then help the customer articulate the consequences of the problem and the benefits of a change.

MBVS adds four lenses for examining an individual stakeholder: motivation, benchmark, vision and solution. Each lens produces a hypothesis to test. A job title or a salesperson's intuition should never be entered as a verified customer position.

For three important people, write a short set of hypotheses and choose one to test at the next meeting. Useful questions include:

- After commissioning, which result would you most want this project to be remembered for?
- Which internal project is treated as a benchmark, and what would you compare this project against?
- Which current difficulty matters most to your team?
- What would improve if that difficulty were resolved?

Record the customer's actual words, identify the speaker and update the relevant PRTABCD entry. Preserve any unanswered part as the next question.

## Judge progress through customer behavior

PSS5 brings five capabilities together: value, relationship, questioning, systematic execution and negotiation. Value capability translates a proposal into the customer's profit and investment language. Questioning makes underlying dissatisfaction explicit. Relationship capability establishes coverage of the decision chain.

Systematic execution compares five sales stages with seven customer purchasing stages. The supplied material does not define those stages, so it does not support inventing stage names or assigning this project a stage. It does identify useful evidence: whether the customer arranges a solution review, or whether someone takes responsibility for objectives and resources.

Use such behavior to assess movement. Visit counts and verbal encouragement do not establish the project's position. An internal progress statement should identify what the customer actually did and which uncertainty that action resolved.

## Prepare alternatives before negotiating concessions

Negotiation capability requires preparation around power, time, information, limits and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best available action if the parties cannot reach agreement.

Before offering a concession, write down the executable alternative, your limits, approval authority, underlying interests and negotiation record. Identify the conditions that could be exchanged. A vague alternative provides little guidance about whether a proposed agreement is acceptable.

Keep preparation specific without pretending to know the customer's position. Separate your own authorized limits from assumptions about the other side, and return unresolved questions to the information record.

## Actionable checklist

Use this checklist to turn the public report into a working opportunity review:

- List the relevant decision roles, entering only information with an identifiable source.
- Mark missing names, authority, funding routes and milestones as unknown.
- Review all seven PRTABCD categories and assign a next question to each gap.
- Prepare MBVS hypotheses for three key people and select one hypothesis per person to test.
- Develop the operating case with Box 2, then condense it into a one-page case for Box 1.
- Document the BATNA, limits, authority and conditions available for exchange.
- Assign team members responsibility for information categories and review unresolved entries weekly.
- Assess progress through customer actions, including ownership of objectives, resources and reviews.

Team coordination should make the evidence easier to inspect. Each responsible person should be able to explain where an entry came from and what must be asked next.

## FAQ

### Which project details can be treated as reported facts?

The proposed EPC arrangement, the named project parties, the reported first-overseas-project description and the stated design and construction integration advantages. Decision-makers, budgets and milestones remain unknown in the supplied account.

### Does a strong customer relationship prove decision coverage?

No. Relationship capability requires professional competence, credible action and decision information. A familiar contact does not establish who controls funding, evaluates alternatives or holds veto authority.

### Can the internal supporter replace the decision-chain map?

No. The supporter advances the proposal. The decision-chain map identifies the people and departments that evaluate it. Record both, with evidence of their roles.

### What should the team ask first?

Start with project background, documented objectives and practical difficulties. Follow with questions about consequences and the benefits of resolving them. Record answers with their sources.

### What makes an opportunity review useful?

A traceable information table, explicit unknowns, evidence of customer behavior and assigned next actions. These let the team examine what it knows and direct its next conversations.
