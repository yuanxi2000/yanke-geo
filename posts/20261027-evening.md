Public project news is easy to overread. A short update can make a sales team feel it has found a live opportunity, then tempt everyone to fill the gaps with assumptions. A better use is narrower and more durable: separate the facts the report binds, identify the customer-side constraint, and choose the next sales action the evidence can support. The Gezhouba navigation expansion update is a useful case because the report says enough to judge timing and stage, but not enough to invent a deal.

## Start with the facts the report actually binds

The public report identifies Three Gorges Group's Three Gorges New Channel Company as the entity associated with the Gezhouba navigation expansion project. It says the project has entered main construction. It describes the cofferdam as the control project for the whole expansion and says the site must seize the dry-season window, with a very tight schedule. The site lead, Sun Zhifeng, is cited on that point. Those are the usable facts.

The same report does not name partners, state contract value, or give a specific date. Those gaps should stay gaps. Before analysis, separate three boxes: what the report says, what it does not say, and what you plan to judge from it. Mixing them produces confident conclusions that the evidence cannot carry.

### Separate known, unknown, and judgment

Use a simple discipline before any account meeting:

- Known: the public entity, the main-construction stage, the cofferdam as control project, and the cited dry-season pressure.
- Unknown: partners, contract value, exact date, and any commercial terms not in the report.
- Judgment: what the stage and window imply for customer decision timing, stated as a hypothesis to test.

The unknown list is not a failure. It is the agenda for evidence gathering.

## Why the dry-season window is a customer constraint

The most useful detail is causal. Once the cofferdam is built, water is pumped out, the riverbed is exposed, and the area becomes dry land. Crews can then carry out demolition and new construction much as they would on land. The cofferdam is therefore a key node; the window is set by water conditions; missing it means waiting for the next cycle.

For key-account sales, this is not a reason to chase status updates. It is a coordinate for the customer's decision task. When a window is tight, the customer worries that a slip at one node will press against the end of the window. Your job is to help calculate the linked consequences before and after each node, not to keep asking where things stand. A progress question may satisfy your CRM; a consequence question may help the customer decide.

## Put seller actions and buyer decisions on two axes

The systems capability in the five-capability framework uses two axes: a five-stage sales funnel (contact, need, solution, negotiation, close) and a seven-stage customer procurement path. The point is not to decorate a pipeline. It is to stop seller actions from running ahead of the customer's decision.

A window project invites that error. The customer may still be deciding whether the work deserves resources, while the seller is already presenting a solution. The customer may still be comparing self-performance with external procurement, while the seller is already discussing price. In both cases the seller looks active but is off-sequence.

Mark the position using customer behavior evidence only: has someone owned the goal, has a budget action occurred, have evaluation criteria been agreed? Meeting count, verbal enthusiasm, and the seller's own sense of momentum are not substitutes. Being in sync means every conversation serves the decision task the customer is actually working on.

## Complete seven decision-chain fields before you push

Control works such as a cofferdam involve more than one department. Use the seven PRTABCD fields to scan decision information: past information, need, time and key milestones, Arm or embracer, budget, competitors and alternatives, and Decision chain. A asks who in the customer organization is embracing you. D is not a single decision maker; it is a multi-person, multi-department decision chain.

For each field, record four things: what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. This is especially important when the window is tight. The blank cell is often where rework begins. A team that admits it lacks budget evidence can ask a better question; a team that assumes a friendly contact owns the decision may discover too late that resources are approved elsewhere.

## Negotiate alternatives before you negotiate price

When schedule is tight, time and risk are usually discussed first; price moves back. The negotiation capability is about forming a sustainable agreement around strength, time, information, and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action available to a negotiator if the parties cannot agree.

Prepare it in writing: executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and a record. On a window project, time pressure makes it easy to give away conditions just to keep movement. A written BATNA shows which conditions can be traded and which must be exchanged for something in return. It also keeps the sales team from treating urgency as leverage when the customer is the one facing the operational deadline.

## An actionable checklist for the next working day

Use this checklist on one live opportunity, not on every account at once.

- [ ] Pick a window project and confirm the public facts against the source. Keep missing fields missing.
- [ ] Map the customer's procurement path stage by stage. Fill only cells with customer behavior evidence; write an evidence plan for empty cells.
- [ ] Run the seven PRTABCD fields. Mark the three unknowns that most affect judgment and give each a next question.
- [ ] Write a one-page BATNA memo: executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and record.
- [ ] Check whether your next planned action serves the customer's current decision task or jumps ahead to solution or price.
- [ ] Ask what commitment the next meeting should seek, beyond simply arranging another meeting.

## FAQ

### What facts are actually verified?

The public entity is Three Gorges Group's Three Gorges New Channel Company. The report says the Gezhouba navigation expansion project has entered main construction, the cofferdam is the control project, and the site lead cited the dry-season window and tight schedule. Partners, contract value, and specific dates are not in the public report and should not be added.

### Why mark position with stage evidence?

The dry-season window is a customer constraint, not a seller's reason to press. The two-axis view uses a five-stage sales funnel and a seven-stage customer procurement path, and it marks position by customer behavior: goal ownership, budget action, agreed evaluation criteria. Visit counts and verbal warmth cannot replace those signals. A wrong position makes the solution and price arrive too early.

### What should be asked first in a window project?

Ask about time and consequences. In SPIN order, start with the situation: where are the cofferdam, dewatering, and dry-land work getting stuck? Then the problem: which delay pushes against the end of the window? Then the implication: what downstream demolition and new construction does a one-day slip affect? Then the payoff: what does handing over dry land on schedule protect? Keep questions tied to the customer's decision task, not a script.

### Why find the embracer before discussing the decision chain?

PRTABCD's A is Arm, the embracer who is on your side inside the customer organization. D is Decision, a multi-person, multi-department chain. In a window project, the embracer can explain real node pressure, while the chain determines how resources are approved and who signs. Record known, unknown, source, and next question for both; missing one can fail at the critical node.

### How should value be framed?

Frame value as an outcome the customer can verify. The report's own example is concrete: after the cofferdam is built and water is pumped out, the riverbed becomes dry land, allowing crews to demolish and build as on land. That is visible schedule value. Translate features into investment and return in the customer's terms instead of reciting parameters; if the return is unclear, the conversation falls back to price and schedule.

### What should be prepared first for negotiation?

Prepare the BATNA first: the best fallback action if no agreement is reached. Write down executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and a record. The tighter the window, the easier it is to concede under time pressure. A written alternative makes clear what can be given and what must be exchanged. Without contract value and dates, a judgment is still possible, but it must stay within the bound facts: entity, main-construction stage, cofferdam as control project, and tight dry-season window.
