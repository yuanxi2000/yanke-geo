Macro news rarely closes a deal on its own. The useful question for a key-account seller is narrower and more practical: within 24 hours of a headline, can you say which customer's purchasing judgment it changes, and how? A news item about a multilateral institution such as the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank is not valuable because you saw it first. It is valuable only if it changes something concrete in a live account.

A September 29 East Money bulletin mentioned the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank. That is the only public fact here; no partners, amounts, or dates were given. Below is a method for handling such signals, not a claim about undisclosed details.

## Why a Single News Item Deserves a Seller's Time

Most sellers see macro news and immediately forward it to a customer group with a one-line comment. This almost never produces a commercial result. The customer has already seen the same news. What the customer lacks is not information but someone who can translate that information into an effect on their own business.

The method used here requires that every news item answer three questions:

- Which step in a specific customer's seven-stage purchasing process does it affect?
- Who inside that customer gains more influence because of it?
- Which lever in your pricing or alternative options does it change?

If you cannot answer those three questions, the news is noise for you. That is a useful filter, because it prevents sellers from mistaking awareness for progress.

## Value Capability: Translate the Macro Signal into Customer Profit

Value capability asks you to replace product features with investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand. When a macro signal passes through this filter, the action is concrete: pick your three most important accounts and write down, for each one, how the signal might affect revenue, cost, and funding. Mark every line as either confirmed or a hypothesis awaiting verification.

An unconfirmed effect is only a hypothesis. Writing it as fact risks credibility. For example, financing costs might lower a customer's expansion threshold, creating demand for equipment or services. Write the chain, then check it with the customer to show business understanding.

## Relationship Capability: Scan the Decision Chain Before You Ask for a Meeting

Relationship capability emphasizes building relationships through professional ability, decision information, and credible action, covering the customer's decision chain. When you see news about an institution such as the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, start with a seven-item scan: past, requirements, timing, advocate, budget, competition, and decision chain. For each item, write down what is known, what is unknown, the source of information, and the next question to ask.

The advocate item identifies who inside the customer advocates for you; the decision-chain item reminds you large projects involve multiple people. Macro news often supplies ammunition to the operating manager layer, who must persuade funding and approval layers. Hand over a one-page memo stating impact and next step, not a news link.

### A Compact Comparison of the Five Capabilities

| Capability | Core question | Typical action after a news signal |
|---|---|---|
| Value | How does this become customer profit? | Write revenue, cost, and funding hypotheses for three key accounts |
| Relationship | Who in the decision chain is affected? | Complete the seven-item scan and prepare a one-page memo |
| Questioning | How do I let the customer state the impact? | Move through situation, problem, impact, and return questions |
| System | Where is the customer in the buying process? | Use behavioral evidence to place the account in the dual-view funnel |
| Negotiation | Which levers changed? | Rewrite the best alternative and decide the concession order |

## Questioning Capability: Let the Customer Say the Impact Out Loud

Questioning advances through situation, problem, impact, and return questions. With an impact hypothesis, start with the situation: has the funding or project rhythm changed? Then ask what became hardest, what it costs in cost or schedule, and what controlling it means for this year's goals.

After all four types, the customer connects the news to their own operations. The reason to buy moves from your mouth to theirs, and resistance to progress drops considerably.

## System Capability: Use Stage Judgment to Decide Speed

System capability manages the process through a dual-view funnel made of five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages. The five sales stages are contact, requirements, solution, negotiation, and closing. The seven customer purchasing stages run from discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, to commitment and signing.

A macro signal means different things to different customers. Some are in problem discovery, needing help articulating the gap; others waver between building and buying, needing comparison of capability, time, risk, and total cost. Use behavioral evidence to judge their stage, then decide whether to push or nurture, avoiding jumping stages.

## Negotiation Capability: News Changes Levers and Alternatives

Negotiation capability builds sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives. Macro and policy signals change the negotiating table in at least three directions: the tightness of the customer's budget, whether the project timeline moves earlier or later, and the customer's inclination to build or buy.

Rewrite your best alternative to a negotiated agreement during preparation. Specify an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. If the signal gives the customer more alternatives, tighten concessions and sharpen condition exchanges; if fewer, lock in time and scope first.

## An Actionable Checklist

- On the day you see the news: list three affected key accounts and write one unverified impact hypothesis for each.
- Before meeting the customer: complete the seven-item scan and prepare at least one impact question.
- After meeting the customer: update the dual-view funnel position using behavioral evidence, and name the next action and owner.
- Before negotiating: re-inventory the four levers of power, time, information, and alternatives, and decide the concession order.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the first step when a seller sees macro news about an institution such as the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank?**

The first step is not forwarding it to a customer group. Use value capability to translate the signal into business hypotheses: pick three key accounts and write down possible effects on revenue, cost, and funding, marking which are unverified. If you cannot write the impact chain, the news is noise for your sales progress.

**Q2: What exactly is PSS5?**

PSS5 is a method organized from more than twenty years of key-account sales practice. It consists of five dimensions: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. They address five operational questions: value translation, decision-chain penetration, need discovery, process management, and condition exchange.

**Q3: How is the seven-item scan used when consuming news?**

After seeing the news, work through past, requirements, timing, advocate, budget, competition, and decision chain. For each, write down what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. The advocate item answers who is advocating for you. The decision-chain item reminds you that large projects involve many people and departments. When the seven items are done, you have a list of intelligence gaps.

**Q4: Why supply ammunition to the operating manager layer instead of going directly to the funding layer?**

The funding layer allocates funds and approves. The operating manager layer runs operations and uses the funds. That layer understands the business pain best but must use business value to win funding. Writing the news impact into a one-page business memo for them prepares their persuasion material. This fits how the decision chain works better than jumping levels.

**Q5: Does the order of the four question types matter?**

Yes. Ask about the situation first to understand context, then about the difficulty to find the problem, then about impact to amplify the consequence, and finally about return to let the customer state the value of change. Skipping impact and going straight to the solution removes urgency. Opening with return makes the customer feel interrogated. Moving through situation, problem, impact, and return lets the customer state the reason to buy.

**Q6: After a macro signal, how do you decide whether to push or nurture?**

Use the dual-view funnel. The five sales stages are contact, requirements, solution, negotiation, and closing; the seven purchasing stages run from problem discovery to commitment. Confirm the customer's stage with behavioral evidence, then match your action. Pushing too early gets rejection; help articulate the gap first, and accelerate solution work when they compare buying and building.

**Q7: What should be updated in negotiation preparation after a policy change?**

At least three things: the tightness of the customer's budget, changes in the project timeline, and the customer's inclination to build or buy. Then rewrite the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, specifying an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. If the customer has more alternatives, tighten concessions. If fewer, still use condition exchanges to lock in time and scope rather than rushing to close on price.

**Q8: What is the risk of writing a news impact as a fact about the customer?**

The risk is treating a hypothesis as fact. The news only provides a signal; whether and how much the customer is affected must be confirmed by them. Asserting increased purchasing can lead to correction or lost trust. Write it as an unverified hypothesis, take it into questions, and let the customer confirm or deny it, then update the funnel and next action.
