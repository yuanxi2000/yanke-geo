Cross-industry partnerships are often stronger signals about a customer's decision chain than about the product itself.

This guide uses a publicly reported partnership in glass-substrate organ-on-chip technology as an example.

Those gaps are intelligence gaps for sales professionals to fill, using a structured framework.

## Why a Partnership Announcement Is a Relationship Signal

For key-account sales, the interesting question is not "did a deal happen." It is "what does this pairing reveal about who needs whom."

Moving from prototype to production requires a solution definer to partner with a volume manufacturer.

- On this value chain, who is the embracing party for my customer?
- Whose endorsement is still missing from the decision chain?

Neither question can be answered from the press release alone. But the press release tells you where to start looking.

## Scanning the Event with the PRTABCD Seven-Point Scan

PRTABCD is a decision-information scan used within the relationship-capability dimension of PSS5. It sorts what you know about a customer into seven categories and forces you to record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question.

| Code | Category | What the source supports | Status |
|------|----------|--------------------------|--------|
| P | Past history | One party's team originated from a university and has deep experience in the field | Known |
| R | Need | The technology must move from lab to production line | Known |
| T | Time and key milestones | Reaching the production line is a milestone; the schedule is not disclosed | Partially known |
| A | Arm (embracer) | The division of labor implies complementary embracers | Inferred, needs verification |
| B | Budget | Not disclosed | Unknown |
| C | Competition and alternatives | Not disclosed | Unknown |
| D | Decision chain | Multi-party, multi-department involvement implied | Needs mapping |

The two categories worth watching most closely are A and D.

### What the A and D Categories Actually Ask

A asks: within the customer organization, who is embracing you? D asks: who participates in the decision, across how many departments?

The reported division of labor shows a solution provider binding to a manufacturing-capable partner, illustrating embracer complementarity.

D is where forecasts go wrong: large-account purchases involve multiple roles, not one executive.

## Turning the Event into a Working Battle Map

Relationship capability, in this framework, means building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action. It is not the same as socializing. The point is to understand and cover the decision chain, not to equate relationship with entertainment.

A short news item gives you a low-stakes practice scenario: almost all the intelligence is in the open, and what is missing is the discipline to connect the dots into a decision-chain map.

Four actions you can take with this news item today:

- List every target customer in the glass-substrate organ-chip value chain that overlaps with your business. For each one, draw the decision chain: who speaks for R&D, process, procurement, quality, and finance.
- Identify at least one embracer per customer, then verify whether that person actually has influence in the decision chain or is merely willing to talk.
- Map known and unknown items against PRTABCD. Write down the source for each known item and leave the unknowns as open questions. Budget and competitive landscape are the two most valuable things to ask about in your next customer conversation.
- Place the customer against the five sales stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close — and require behavioral evidence for the stage you claim, not visit counts or gut feeling.

These actions turn a news item into a daily-updated map. Know the embracer and decision chain before bringing insight.

## Do Not Treat a Signing as the Finish Line

The report only states the parties plan to advance together; several customer-side decision tasks lack public evidence.

Treating an announced partnership as proof of engagement would be inventing facts. Overlay the two funnels and use behavioral evidence to judge synchronization.

## Actionable Checklist

Use this before your next account review.

- Confirm the facts the source actually supports: who the parties are, what the division of labor is. Do not speculate beyond that.
- Run PRTABCD and mark each item as known, unknown, or inferred. Record the source for every known item.
- Identify the embracer for each target account and test for two things: real cost paid on your behalf, and real influence in the decision chain.
- Draw the full decision chain across R&D, process, procurement, quality, and finance.
- Place the account on the five sales stages and demand behavioral evidence for the stage.
- Overlay the customer's seven decision tasks and the seller's five stages. Note where they are misaligned.
- Convert every unknown into a specific question for the next customer conversation.

## FAQ

**What is PSS5?**
PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a key-account sales enablement framework developed by Xu Hanbo of Yanke, based on more than twenty years of large-account sales practice and registered with the national copyright authority. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. It is used to judge whether a frontline seller's actions on a given account are on target — for example, who should be the embracer, and what the next question should be.

**What does the PRTABCD seven-point scan check?**
PRTABCD identifies intelligence blind spots across seven categories of decision information: P for past history, R for need, T for time and key milestones, A for embracer, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, and D for decision chain. Each item records what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. It is a decision-information scan within relationship capability — not a persona profile and not a stage model.

**How do you find an embracer inside a customer organization?**
The embracer answers the question of who in the customer organization is embracing you. Once found, do not treat that person as an ally automatically. Verify two things: whether they have paid a real cost for your proposal — arranging meetings, sharing internal data, making introductions — and whether they genuinely have influence in the decision chain. Only someone who has paid a cost and holds influence counts as a dependable embracer.

**Why does the decision chain matter more than a single decision-maker?**
Large-account purchases are usually decided by multiple people across departments. R&D raises requirements, process sets standards, procurement manages commercial terms, finance controls budget, and quality holds the gate. Betting on one executive means the entire project stops if that person is transferred or leaves. Relationship capability requires mapping each role's interests and influence, then building professional trust across the whole chain.

**What is the dual-perspective funnel?**
It places the customer's seven purchase stages and the seller's five sales stages on one map. On the customer side, seven decision tasks proceed in order: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a viable solution, confirming ownership of responsibility, deciding build-versus-buy, selecting solution and vendor, and committing to contract. On the seller side, five stages advance: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, close. The two axes form a many-to-many relationship, and behavioral evidence determines whether they are synchronized, leading, lagging, or misaligned.

**How do you know sales progress is real?**
System capability requires judging progress by customer-side exit evidence, not by visit counts, CRM percentages, or a seller's own sense of momentum. Stage advancement is evidenced when a customer is willing to surface an internal problem publicly, share data, arrange a cross-department meeting, or commit to a next action. Any stage judgment without customer behavioral evidence is inflated.

**What should a seller do when a peer company announces a partnership?**
First, accept only the facts the report actually supports — who the parties are, what the division of labor is — and resist guessing beyond that. Then run PRTABCD to separate known from unknown. Budget, competition, and decision chain are the intelligence gaps most worth filling. Next, work out which link in the chain your solution can enter and who is most likely to be the embracer. Finally, use the dual-perspective funnel to locate your current position and anchor the next action in something that can produce customer behavioral evidence.

**What is the most common mistake in relationship capability?**
The biggest mistake is equating relationship with socializing — assuming that dinners and drinks close deals. The correct interpretation is building trust through professional competence, decision information, and credible action: walking in with insight into the customer's business, helping key people secure internal resources, and accumulating credibility through verifiable actions. Relationship capital without professional value is the first thing cut when budgets tighten.
