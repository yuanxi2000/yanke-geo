A public announcement can be a useful sales intelligence entry point, but it is not the same thing as a buying signal. On October 14, a Dongfang Caifu financial evening news item reported that Wuhan Tianyuan plans to sign a cooperation agreement to invest in a high-purity indium and indium phosphide polycrystalline materials production base. That is enough to register as a lead. It is not enough to assume a purchase budget, timetable, or supplier choice exists. The practical question is how to read the early signal without turning it into a forecast.

## What the Public Item Does and Does Not Tell You

The confirmed event facts are narrow: the subject is Wuhan Tianyuan; the source is the Dongfang Caifu financial evening news item from October 14; the action described is a plan to sign a cooperation agreement and invest in a production base. The word plans matters. In announcement language, a planned step often corresponds to internal responsibility and risk that the customer has not fully confirmed. If a seller reads planned as closed, the next visit will be misaligned: the seller may talk delivery cadence while the customer is still deciding whether the project deserves resources. Treat the item as an intelligence entry point that needs verification.

## Locate the Signal in the Customer's Buying Process

The seven-stage customer buying process describes seven decision tasks: problem recognition, value judgment, confirmation that a solution exists, confirmation of responsibility ownership, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. A public plan to invest usually shows activity only in the early tasks. Later tasks require customer behavior evidence: an internal review, a request for a validation method, or a person taking ownership of a target and result. Seller activity, such as number of visits, proposals sent, or CRM stage percentages, cannot replace that evidence.

### Why Seller and Buyer Views May Not Match

The PSS5 system uses a dual-view funnel: one axis is the seller's five stages, which are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close; the other axis is the customer's seven buying stages. The two axes have different task granularity, and their relationship is many-to-many. They are not one-to-one, and no third stage model should be invented. For the Wuhan Tianyuan item, the seller may still be at contact while the buyer is already judging value. When positions differ, do not push the stage forward. Fill the evidence gap for the customer's current step.

## Use PRTABCD to Turn Unknowns into Questions

PRTABCD scans seven categories of decision information to expose intelligence blind spots: Past or history, Requirement, Time and key milestones, Arm, Budget, Competition and alternatives, and Decision chain. For each category, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. For this public item, Arm and Decision deserve first attention. Arm asks who in the customer organization is embracing you. Decision refers to the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. Clarifying those two is more useful than collecting more similar news.

## Value Capability: Turn the Solution into an Investment Case

Value capability requires translating product features into investment, benefit, and profit improvement that the customer can understand, supported by business numbers. For a planned investment project, prepare a profit improvement plan, or PIP. It answers how much profit can be added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame. A PIP is an investment case, not a price quote. Before writing it, monetize the impact: identify the customer's operating areas affected by the solution, quantify the improvement, and convert it into revenue, cost, profit, payback period, or opportunity cost. For approval audiences, distinguish Box 1, the capital allocation and approval layer, from Box 2, the operating managers who use the funds. Help Box 2 make the business value case to Box 1.

## Relationship and Questioning: Bring Hypotheses, Return with Evidence

MBVS examines the same key person through four lenses: motivation, benchmark, vision, and solution. These are hypotheses to test, not role labels and not customer facts. SPIN uses four question types to advance: situation or background questions, problem or difficulty questions, implication questions, and need-payoff questions. Confirm the project organization and timing first. Use difficulty and implication questions so the customer sees what may be lost by not acting. Use need-payoff questions so the customer states the benefits of change. The goal is to return from the conversation with evidence, not with internal assumptions written as conclusions.

## Negotiation: Prepare Alternatives Before Terms

Negotiation capability revolves around power, time, information, and alternatives. BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action available if the parties cannot agree. Prepare five elements in writing: an executable alternative, a bottom line, authority, interests, and a record. When an investment is still early, rushing to price usually leaves the seller without leverage. A more stable order is to complete positioning and evidence first. If the customer suddenly goes quiet, use a three-part closing diagnostic to identify a false stall and translate the silence into the next verifiable progress item.

## From Public Intent to Managed Opportunity: Four Steps

1. Register. Record the Wuhan Tianyuan item as a lead. Write down the source and known facts, and leave unknown fields blank.
2. Position. Use the seven-stage buying process to judge where the customer stands. Write the customer behavior evidence that proves that step.
3. Fill blind spots. Go through PRTABCD and mark known, unknown, source, and next question for each item. Prioritize Arm and Decision.
4. Pre-set. Prepare a PIP and a BATNA. Define how value is calculated and how terms might be exchanged. Then decide whether to advance to the next stage.

## FAQ

Q1. What is PSS5 and what do its five dimensions manage?
A1. PSS5 is a five-capability framework for professional key-account sales: value, relationship, questioning, system, and negotiation. They address value translation, decision-chain penetration, needs discovery, process management, and agreement. In complex projects, missing one capability creates risk.

Q2. Why is the Wuhan Tianyuan public item worth a seller's attention?
A2. It shows a public investment-related action by that company, which can be a registrable lead for sellers of equipment, materials, engineering, or park services. It remains intelligence to verify, not proof that budget, timing, or supplier selection are settled.

Q3. Are the seven buying stages and five sales stages one-to-one?
A3. No. The buying stages describe seven customer decision tasks; the sales stages are five seller actions: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. They have different granularity and are many-to-many.

Q4. What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?
A4. A is Arm, asking who in the customer organization is embracing you. D is Decision, the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. PRTABCD is a scan for intelligence blind spots, so each item records known, unknown, source, and next question.

Q5. How is MBVS different from labeling a key person?
A5. MBVS proposes and tests hypotheses about one person through motivation, benchmark, vision, and solution. It is not four role types. Until verified by questions, the four items are hypotheses only.

Q6. What should BATNA preparation make clear?
A6. BATNA is the best alternative if no agreement is reached. Preparation should state an executable alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and record in reviewable writing. If the alternative is not truly executable, concessions have no boundary.

Q7. If the customer's investment is early, what should sales do first?
A7. Work on information before price. Register the lead, position it in the buying stages, scan PRTABCD, and prioritize Arm and Decision. When evidence is insufficient, advance information, not stage. Discuss solution and terms after customer behavior evidence appears.

Q8. How is a PIP different from a quote?
A8. A PIP writes the solution's effect on the customer's business as an investment case: profit added or protected, confidence and mechanism, and time frame. A quote answers what is bought, at what price, and when delivered. A PIP belongs in business-value discussions; a quote belongs in the commercial stage.
