Urban renewal in Shenyang gives key-account sellers a practical mirror. The former Shenzhong Group No.2 Metalworking Workshop site became 1905 Cultural and Creative Park. Seven historic buildings and one rail spur at Dongmao Warehouse were preserved and converted into Shidai Wencang City Reading Room. The Fourth Grain Depot became Grain Warehouse Cultural and Creative Industrial Park. Different sites, same sales question: why should a customer sign for an investment? The answer is rarely the asset itself. It is the operating value the asset can release.

## From Legacy Asset to Operating Value

These projects put money into visible bricks and mortar, but the return appears in less visible operations. The same is true in key-account sales. A proposal that only describes features gives an approval layer nothing to repeat. Value capability means moving the sales conversation from product functions to an investment, return and profit improvement the customer can understand. In action, talk less about what we built and more about how the customer will earn more, spend less, or see the first confirmed revenue sooner. The Shenyang examples work because old industrial spaces are recast as small theaters, live-music venues, art spaces and city reading rooms. The customer does not approve the building as a building; the customer approves a reopened operation and its business imagination. Your proposal needs the same translation.

## The Three-Line Account: Make Value Checkable

### The Three-Line Account

Before any investment discussion, put the account in front of the customer. These three lines are a draft sheet you can carry into every visit:

- Revenue line: Where will new revenue come from after the upgrade or launch? Who pays? What are the rough price and frequency?
- Cost line: What is the one-time investment, ongoing maintenance, energy, labor and depreciation? Which items change with scale?
- Time line: How long from investment to the first confirmed revenue? How long an empty period can the customer tolerate?

The numbers do not need decimal precision, but the customer must confirm the definitions. A benefit the seller calculates alone will not be taken into a meeting. A number the customer writes down has a chance of entering the budget. Record the customer's exact words and definitions: who said it, in what situation, and who will verify it next time. Blank fields are not failures; they are the agenda for the next conversation.

## Decision Chain: Funders and Users Are Not at the Same Table

Existing-asset renovation, park operation and public cultural space projects often involve two groups. Box 1 controls capital allocation and approval. Box 2 operates the asset and uses the funds. A common sales mistake is to build rapport only with the user side and never help that side prepare an operating-value case that can enter the approval meeting. Relationship capability is not entertainment. It is the use of professional insight, decision information and verifiable action to penetrate the decision chain.

PRTABCD is a seven-item scan you can use as a checklist: past information, requirement, time and key milestones, adopter or champion, budget, competitors and alternatives, and decision chain. A is always the champion: who in the customer organization is embracing you? D is always the decision chain: multiple people and departments participate, not one leader. For each item, write what is known, what is unknown, the information source and the next question. Blank spaces become the topics for the next visit.

| Decision group | What it controls | What it needs from sales | Common risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Box 1: funding and approval | Capital allocation and approval | A business case with investment, return, time and risk | The seller never gets past user-side enthusiasm |
| Box 2: operations and use | Daily operation and use of funds | Practical operating assumptions, user evidence and an implementation path | The user supports the proposal but cannot carry it into approval |

## Stage Judgment: Use Customer Behavior as Evidence

The customer procurement process has seven stages: problem discovery, value judgment, solution confirmation, responsibility ownership, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The sales process has five stages: contact, needs, proposal, negotiation and close. The two axes are many-to-many and should not be mechanically aligned. System capability requires judging progress from customer behavior evidence. Has anyone claimed the goal and resources? Has a build-versus-buy comparison started? Have evaluation criteria been written down? Has budget and terms confirmation been scheduled? Visit counts and the seller's own feeling are not evidence. Put the observed evidence against both axes; the real position becomes clearer, and the risk of skipping stages becomes visible earlier.

## Negotiation: Prepare Alternatives Before Price

Before price and terms are discussed, negotiation capability requires clarity on four things: power, time, information, bottom line and alternatives. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action a negotiator can take if no deal is reached. Prepare an executable alternative, your bottom line, your authority, both sides' interests and a record of the conversation. Without an alternative in hand, every concession becomes one-way leakage. Write the BATNA and bottom line before quoting, then decide which conditions can be traded and which cannot be given away.

## What to Do Today

Use this checklist on one active account:

- Rewrite the project's value statement as a three-line account, leaving the numbers blank for the customer to fill.
- Scan the account with PRTABCD, mark every unknown item and attach one next question to each.
- Confirm whether your champion can carry the proposal into the approval meeting. List the missing materials and fill them.
- Before quoting, write your BATNA and bottom line. Separate tradeable conditions from protected ones.
- For each stage, collect customer behavior evidence. Do not count meetings, meals or verbal promises as progress.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the most direct lesson from these Shenyang projects for key-account sales?**

A1: Turn existing assets into incremental value. The Shenyang projects make old assets produce value again. In sales, the customer is not buying a feature list; the customer is buying an improvement in revenue, cost or time after the investment. Explain those improvements clearly enough for the proposal to enter the next meeting.

**Q2: What should value capability convert into business numbers?**

A2: At least three things: the revenue the customer will add, the cost the customer will reduce, and the time from investment to the first confirmed revenue. Let the customer confirm the definitions and amounts. The seller's job is to ask the right questions and record the definitions. Customer-written numbers can enter a budget; seller-calculated benefits often stay in a presentation.

**Q3: What if the customer will not provide cost and revenue data?**

A3: Lower the granularity. Start with rough figures that are public or internally accepted: annual volume for a business line, how many people a step needs, or how long a rework takes. Then ask the customer to confirm whether the order of magnitude is right. The seller does not need to force a full disclosure, but should record who said what, when, and who will verify it next time. The blank item is the next question.

**Q4: How do champion and decision chain differ in practice?**

A4: The champion answers who in the customer organization is embracing you; this is A in PRTABCD. The decision chain is D: a multi-person, multi-department decision process, not a single leader. A person can support you and still not be able to sign. Keep two lists and write known, unknown, source and next question for each, so a supporter is not mistaken for a signer.

**Q5: How do you know a project is moving forward rather than the seller feeling good?**

A5: Look only at customer behavior evidence. Has someone claimed the goal, resources and outcome responsibility? Has a build-versus-buy comparison started? Have evaluation criteria been written down? Has budget and terms confirmation been arranged? Visits, meals and verbal promises do not count. Put the evidence against the seven customer stages and five sales stages; the position becomes clear and stage-skipping risk surfaces earlier.

**Q6: What should be written for BATNA before quoting?**

A6: Write five items: executable alternative actions, your bottom line, your authority, both sides' interests and a record of the communication. BATNA is the best alternative if no deal is reached. The more specific it is, the more conditional your concessions can be. If you cannot write it, your leverage is not clear, and entering price discussion is likely to put you on the defensive.

**Q7: What are common pitfalls in existing-asset and park-operation projects?**

A7: One is talking deeply only with the user side without helping it prepare an operating-value case for the approval meeting. Another is skipping stages while the customer is still confirming responsibility ownership. Funders and users often sit in two layers: Box 1 manages funds and approval, while Box 2 operates and spends. Sales must translate Box 2's operating account into an input-output case Box 1 can check.
