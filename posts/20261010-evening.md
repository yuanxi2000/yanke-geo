# Making Value Verifiable: A Key-Account Sales Guide Inspired by a 1.5 Billion RMB Grocery Brand

Key-account sales conversations often stall on a vague sentence: the customer says they "cannot see the value." The usual response is more adjectives, more features, more reassurance. A more durable approach is to make value checkable. A recent public example from China's grocery sector shows what that looks like when the numbers are put on the table.

East Money's "Future Business Briefing" reported that Dingdong Maicai's Youdouzhi disclosed cumulative sales of 1.5 billion RMB, roughly 20% year-over-year growth, 50,000 mu of soybean planting agreements, an expected 10,000-ton output, and an operating plant in Ningbo.

For consumers, this is a reason to buy and repurchase. For anyone selling to large accounts, it is a mirror. If you cannot place a comparable set of checkable numbers next to your proposal, the value conversation is not finished. The whole craft of value selling is turning "I think it is good" into "here are the numbers."

This guide translates that lesson into a practical workflow for key-account sellers, using the five-capability framework known as PSS5 as a reference point.

## What the Facts Actually Demonstrate

The Youdouzhi figures matter less as a business story than as a demonstration of evidence structure. Four questions are answered, and each answer is backed by an observable fact rather than an assertion.

- Sales volume: cumulative sales of 1.5 billion RMB.
- Growth rate: approximately 20% year-over-year.
- Supply base: 50,000 mu of joint planting agreements with upstream partners.
- Production capacity: a Ningbo plant already in operation, with expected output around 10,000 tons.

Notice that the growth claim does not stand alone. It sits next to a supply arrangement and a physical facility. A skeptical buyer can ask follow-up questions and expect concrete answers. That is the property sales teams should aim for: each value claim has a checkable basis behind it.

### Turning product language into customer operating numbers

PSS5 defines value capability () as translating the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, expressed in operating numbers. In practice that means answering three things: how much profit the solution can add or protect, by what mechanism, and within what time frame.

The Youdouzhi facts fit this definition cleanly. Instead of saying "our product is good," the brand puts forward a cumulative revenue figure, a growth rate, a planting agreement, and a commissioned factory. A customer may not remember specifications, but they remember how much goes in, which operating numbers improve, and why they should believe it.

## Three Steps to Make Value Real on a Key Account

You do not need to overcomplicate this. Mirroring the Youdouzhi fact chain, a key-account seller can work through three steps.

### Step 1: Do the math (monetization)

Identify affected customer operating areas, quantify improvement, and convert it into financial language: revenue, cost, profit, payback, or opportunity cost. Write it as a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP) stating added or protected profit, the mechanism and confidence, and the time horizon.

### Step 2: Attach evidence

Every value claim should carry a checkable basis, just as the Youdouzhi sales figure stands alongside planting agreements and an operating plant. If you claim cost reduction or efficiency gains, back it with pilot results, observed customer behavior changes, or acceptance criteria both sides have agreed on.

### Step 3: Prevent value erosion

Write value into stage goals and acceptance criteria so the customer keeps seeing evidence across the seven-stage buying process, rather than discovering it late when negotiations begin. When you trade concessions, exchange them for something in return, and use negotiation capability to protect the value you have already calculated.

## Timing Matters: Speak at the Value-Judgment Stage

The customer's seven-stage purchasing process begins with problem discovery, then value judgment, solution existence, ownership, and build-versus-buy. Value selling belongs at the second stage: early enough to shape urgency, before the budget frame and competitor shortlist are fixed.

One layer allocates funds; another operates them. Sellers should help the operating manager make a business-value case upward. Verifiable facts like Youdouzhi's are the strongest material for that upward request.

## From a Single Deal to a Wider Footprint

Youdouzhi did not stop at selling goods. Planting agreements manage the supply source, the Ningbo plant manages production, and the front end shows 1.5 billion RMB in cumulative sales. Mapped to key-account sales, this is a migration strategy: after one successful delivery, expand customer value and share through supplements, upgrades, or integration. On a failure, do not mechanically expand.

Value capability governs not only whether this deal closes, but where the next one comes from.

## Value Capability Compared With the Other Four Capabilities

The five capabilities of PSS5 work together. Value capability answers whether the deal is worth it; the other four address whether it can actually be done.

| Capability | Core question | Typical tool or focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | Is it worth it? | Monetization, PIP, operating numbers |
| Relationship | Who decides and who supports? | PRTABCD decision-information scan, finding advocates |
| Questioning | What is the real need? | SPIN: situation, problem, implication, need-payoff |
| System | Are we in sync with the buyer? | Dual-view funnel, five sales stages against seven buying stages |
| Negotiation | How do we protect the value? | Power, time, information, alternatives, condition exchange |

Questioning capability uses SPIN-style questions to surface real needs and anchor the value calculation in the customer's pain. Relationship capability scans decision information to find advocates and map a multi-person decision chain. System capability aligns the sales stages with the buying stages. Negotiation capability designs condition exchanges around power, time, information, and alternatives.

## Actionable Checklist

- Write down the customer operating areas your solution touches.
- Quantify the improvement and convert it into revenue, cost, profit, payback, or opportunity cost.
- Draft a PIP: profit added or protected, mechanism and confidence, time frame.
- Attach at least one checkable piece of evidence to each value claim.
- Confirm the timing: has the customer reached the value-judgment stage?
- Identify the operating manager who will carry your case upward.
- Prepare material that manager can use in a budget request.
- Build acceptance criteria and stage goals that lock in the value.
- Identify a BATNA before entering negotiation.
- Exchange every concession for a condition.
- After delivery, plan the supplement, upgrade, or integration path.
- Avoid mechanical expansion after a failed delivery.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does a grocery brand's sales figure have to do with key-account selling?**

The public facts form a complete value-evidence chain: cumulative sales above 1.5 billion RMB, roughly 20% year-over-year growth, 50,000 mu of joint soybean planting, and an operating Ningbo plant. The transferable lesson is that value should be presented through numbers and facts the customer can check, not adjectives. This is the ability PSS5 value capability trains: translating products into customer investment, return, and profit improvement, expressed in operating numbers.

**Q2: What exactly is value capability under PSS5?**

By the Yanke definition, it is converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, expressed in operating numbers. It answers three questions: how much profit can be added or protected, by what mechanism and with what confidence, and in what time frame. Common tools are monetization and the PIP, which frames a proposal as an investment case rather than a product quote.

**Q3: What is a PIP, and how is it different from a quotation?**

PIP stands for Profit Improvement Proposal. It describes the operational improvement as an investment case: how much profit can be added or protected, the mechanism and confidence, and the time horizon. A quotation answers what you charge. A PIP answers why the investment is worth making.

**Q4: The customer says they cannot see the value. What is the first step?**

Start with monetization: identify affected operating areas, quantify improvement, and translate it into revenue, cost, profit, payback, or opportunity cost. Attach checkable evidence such as pilot results. Then check timing: value judgment is the second buying stage, so the case must appear before resource commitment.

**Q5: What are the two internal layers, and how does value selling serve them?**

One layer allocates funds; another operates them. The operating manager must persuade the approving executive. Sellers should help build a business-value case: how much goes in, which operating numbers improve, and how risk is controlled. Verifiable facts such as supply agreements and an operating plant are most persuasive.

**Q6: What does the 50,000 mu planting agreement teach about promising value?**

It shows that long-term value needs upstream coordination. The agreement writes planting scale and expected output into a contract rather than leaving them as verbal promises. In key-account selling, when your promised value depends on third-party resources, write their role, contribution, and delivery standards into the proposal so the customer sees concrete arrangements rather than intentions.

**Q7: How do the other four PSS5 capabilities work with value capability?**

Questioning uses SPIN's four types to surface real needs so value lands on the customer's pain. Relationship scans decision information to find advocates and map the chain. System aligns sales and buying stages in a dual-view funnel. Negotiation designs condition exchanges around power, time, information, and alternatives. Value answers whether it is worth it; the other four address whether it can be done.

**Q8: How do you stop carefully calculated value from being cut during negotiation?**

Turn value into something both sides recognize before negotiation. Write improvement numbers into the solution's success criteria. Confirm acceptance through customer behavior, such as running a pilot or assigning a counterpart. Prepare a BATNA in advance, specifying the alternative, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Exchange every concession for a condition rather than discounting quietly.
