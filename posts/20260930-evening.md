A regional policy document is rarely just news to forward. For key-account sellers, it can work as a lead map: behind each measure sits a group of enterprises that will soon re-evaluate where their money goes, plus a decision chain to navigate. This guide reads one such document — 24 measures for upgrading Guangzhou's comprehensive bonded zones — and converts it into sales judgment using PSS5.

## Read the Event Accurately First

According to Southern Finance Omnimedia reporting, the Guangzhou Municipal Commerce Bureau issued a work plan to implement General Administration of Customs measures on capacity expansion and quality improvement of comprehensive bonded zones. The plan rolls out 24 specific measures across four key areas, aiming to leverage integrated domestic and international market advantages and push bonded zones from traditional bonded processing and logistics toward a higher-end, more diversified industrial ecosystem.

The document does not sign contracts; the enterprises and decision chains behind it do. For sellers in park operations, bonded logistics, supply chain services, equipment, and digitalization, this signals that customer-side purchasing motivation is being reactivated. Upgrade projects previously hard to approve now have a presentable justification for initiation and a smoother approval path.

## Translating Policy into Battlefield Judgment with PSS5

PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) breaks key-account selling into five dimensions: Value, Relationship, Questioning, System, and Negotiation. Facing a regional policy, ask a few questions per dimension; the answers become next week's action list.

### Value: What Business Problem Is the Policy Trying to Solve?

The document points toward a higher-end, diversified industrial ecosystem. Customers are not buying equipment for its own sake but the returns from industrial upgrading. Translate your proposal into the customer's language of profit, investment, and payback — not a feature list.

### Relationship: Who in the Decision Chain Will Change Attitude?

District-level platform companies, park operators, and leaders of resident manufacturing enterprises each have different concerns and need different coverage approaches.

### Questioning: Has the Customer Seen the Gap?

Use intentional questions to let the customer calculate the cost of maintaining the status quo.

### System: Where Does This Opportunity Sit?

On the five sales stages and seven customer buying stages, most early-policy opportunities sit between contact and needs. Pushing a proposal ahead of the customer's stage gets you left on read.

### Negotiation: What Does the Window Mean for Both Sides?

A window means time pressure for the customer — and for competitors. Think through power, time, information, and alternatives in advance so the conversation does not collapse into discounting alone.

## Relationship: Scanning Decision Information with PRTABCD

In the first weeks after a policy is issued, information gaps become order gaps. The PRTABCD scan lists intelligence blind spots:

- P — past history information
- R — needs
- T — timing and key milestones
- A — adopter (who inside the customer is embracing you)
- B — budget
- C — competitors and alternatives
- D — decision chain

In a policy scenario, T and D are most valuable: which districts move first, who leads, which budget channel is used, when results must be visible. For each item, record what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. Once complete, you know who to approach first.

## System: Putting the Policy Window into a Dual-Perspective Funnel

The seven customer buying stages run from discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, deciding build or buy, selecting solution and supplier, to commitment and signing. Each step must be proven with customer behavioral evidence.

The policy document helps the customer complete half of the first two steps in advance: the gap is written into an official document, and the direction of value is pointed out.

- Whether someone proactively asks about policy details related to your business — a sign of a valid entry point.
- Whether the customer invests internal time to confirm problem and value, such as internal discussions or pulling you into meetings.
- Whether a person has emerged internally to claim the goal and resources. No owner, no progress.

If the customer is still at stage one and you hand over a proposal and quote, that is advancing out of step; the document usually circulates and goes quiet.

## Questioning and Negotiation: Turning the Window into an Agreement

On questioning, advance through the four SPIN categories: situation questions to understand the gap between current business and policy direction; problem questions to surface bottlenecks in higher-end transformation; implication questions to let the customer calculate the cost of not changing; and need-payoff questions to let them state the benefits themselves. A calculation the customer does themselves is worth more than a hundred explanations from you.

On negotiation, the policy window gives the customer a time motive and gives you leverage.

| Lens | What it tracks | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Customer buying stages | The customer's decision tasks | Internal discussions, owner assigned, budget action |
| Sales stages | The seller's axis | Contact made, needs confirmed, proposal requested |

The two are a many-to-many relationship, not a mechanical one-to-one mapping. Position must be judged by customer behavior, not visit counts or the seller's feelings.

## Actionable Checklist for This Week

- Write a one-page value statement in profit language for each key account, answering how much profit this investment can add or protect.
- Book one conversation with a candidate adopter to test your assumptions.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS5?**

A1: PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a key-account sales enablement methodology independently developed by Xu Hanbo, a partner at Yanke, based on more than twenty years of key-account sales practice, and registered with the National Copyright Administration. Its five dimensions are Value, Relationship, Questioning, System, and Negotiation.

**Q2: What practical use does a policy news item have?**

A2: Policy gives customers a justification for project initiation and a resource channel. The 24 measures write higher-end, diversified industrial development into official direction, making internal upgrade projects easier to get budget and approval support. Run a PRTABCD intelligence round rather than just forwarding the news.

**Q3: How does Value land during a policy window?**

A3: Convert the sales object from product features into investment, returns, and profit improvement explained with business numbers. Senior leaders care how much profit the investment can add or protect, the mechanism, and the time frame. A profit-growth proposal backed by policy direction makes internal budget advocacy smoother than a feature-and-specification list.

**Q4: What does the PRTABCD scan cover?**

A4: Seven decision-information types: P past history, R needs, T timing and key milestones, A adopter, B budget, C competitors and alternatives, D decision chain. Each item records what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. It is a scanning tool within Relationship, not a role profile.

**Q5: What does adopter mean?**

A5: Adopter is the A item: who in the customer organization is embracing you — willing to speak for you internally, guide you, and provide decision information. An adopter is a supporter, not an approver or bargaining chip. Early in the window there is often none; build first-layer trust through professional communication, find an inside person to verify assumptions, then expand outward.

**Q6: How do the seven buying stages relate to the five sales stages?**

A6: The seven buying stages are customer-side decision tasks; the five sales stages are the seller-side advancement axis. Placed on one chart they form a dual-perspective funnel — a many-to-many relationship that cannot be mechanically mapped one-to-one. Judge position with customer behavioral evidence, not visit counts or self-perception.

**Q7: How should sales rhythm be arranged?**

A7: Match actions to the customer's decision tasks. Early on, customers are in problem-discovery and value-judgment stages; your task is contact and needs confirmation — gather intelligence, approach key people, help calculate the gap, not rush a proposal. Once an internal owner and budget action appear, move into solution and selection. Competitors also move during the window, so aim for the next real progress.

**Q8: How should BATNA be prepared?**

A8: BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. In a policy-window negotiation the customer faces time pressure and you have more leverage, but preparation must be concrete: your executable alternative if no deal is reached, your bottom line, authority, real interest differences, and a record of every concession and condition exchange.
