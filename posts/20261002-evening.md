A procurement organization rarely publishes its own dependency problems. When it does, sales teams should read carefully. According to a report by East Money, Anthropic disclosed in its prospectus that Broadcom serves simultaneously as a hardware supplier and a financing partner, creating a potential conflict of interest. The same filing warns that Broadcom's decisions on pricing and hardware could affect Anthropic's ability to procure enough computing infrastructure, and that the conflict could affect its access to the compute its research work requires.

Translated into the language of key-account sales: a buyer has publicly admitted that a critical supplier also sits on the other side of the negotiating table, deciding both what gets delivered and, in part, where the buyer's money goes. The buyer names two consequences it may face — not getting enough compute, and having price and hardware decisions influenced by the counterparty. It is unusual for a party to write down its own dependency this plainly. That is what makes it useful.

## Reading the Dual Role Through Negotiation Power

PSS5's negotiation power is about designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarified power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives, in order to reach a sustainable agreement. Three of those dimensions — power, information, and alternatives — map cleanly onto Anthropic's situation.

### Power: who can afford to walk away

Power in a negotiation is not volume. It is the gap between the cost the other side pays to leave you and the quality of your own alternatives. Anthropic needs Broadcom's hardware and capital. Broadcom's dependence on this particular customer is a more replaceable commercial relationship. The power gap shows up in the wording of the filing: Anthropic can flag the risk, but it cannot change the structure. For key-account sellers, the lesson is to measure power by exit cost, not by confidence.

### Information: who sets the price

The line about pricing and hardware decisions affecting procurement capacity tells you where price leadership sits. The supplier holds it. The buyer receives information after the fact. A party on the wrong side of an information asymmetry will have its concession space probed step by step. Many B2B sellers lose in front of customers precisely because the customer understands the seller's cost structure and delivery pressure better than the seller does.

### Alternatives: the quality of the BATNA

BATNA — the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — refers to the best course of action available if the parties cannot reach agreement. In practice, preparing one means specifying an executable alternative, a bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Anthropic's difficulty is that compute suppliers are highly concentrated and switching costs are extremely high, which compresses the quality of its BATNA. Broadcom, with more alternative customers, negotiates from a stronger position.

## Scanning a Customer in This Kind of Structure

If you sell into a similar configuration — or simply want more leverage in front of a strong customer — the PRTABCD scan from relationship capability can surface intelligence blind spots. PRTABCD uses seven categories of decision information to identify blind spots, and for each one records what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question to ask. It is not a role profile organized around six buyer identities. A is fixed as Arm — the embracer — answering who in the customer organization is embracing you. D is Decision, the multi-person, multi-department decision chain.

- Past and Requirement: the counterparty has publicly acknowledged compute shortfalls and procurement constraints. That is requirement evidence stated by the customer, far more reliable than a seller's guess.
- Time and Budget: funding arrangements and expansion pace determine when the counterparty must decide. Without both, any sense of pace is speculation.
- Arm (embracer): who inside the organization will vouch for working with you determines whether your information reaches the decision chain.
- Competition and alternatives: assess your own BATNA quality before discussing concessions. Without a credible alternative, any tough posture is performance.
- Decision chain: when a supplier is also a funder, the chain gains people with both purchasing authority and capital influence. Understand each one's interests before expecting room for exchange.

## What Key-Account Sales Teams Can Take Away

Three practical reminders follow from this case.

1. Audit your dependency structure. For the customer or supplier you rely on most, how many roles does that party play in your business? The more roles, the fewer your bargaining chips. Prepare alternatives early.
2. Put risk into formal documents. Anthropic chose to state the risk in a prospectus — turning a verbal contest into a written record. Key commitments and risks in key-account relationships should also be documented, creating evidence for later conditional exchanges.
3. Replace unilateral concessions with conditional exchange. The weaker party can often trade for time, information, or scope rather than discounting to preserve the relationship.

## Actionable Checklist

- List your top customers and suppliers, and note every role each one plays in your business.
- For each critical dependency, write down your executable alternative, your bottom line, your authority, your interests, and where the record lives.
- Identify what you know versus what you are assuming about the counterparty's cost structure, delivery pressure, and decision timing.
- Confirm who inside the account is willing to vouch for you, and who holds purchasing authority alongside capital influence.
- Before any concession, define what you receive in return — payment terms, order scope, or a longer commitment.
- Convert verbal commitments into written records so later exchanges rest on evidence.
- Re-run the scan on a fixed cadence, not only when a deal stalls.

## FAQ

Q1: What potential conflict of interest did Anthropic disclose?

According to East Money, Anthropic acknowledged in its prospectus that Broadcom acts as both hardware supplier and financing partner, creating a potential conflict of interest. This could affect Anthropic's ability to obtain the compute its research work requires, and Broadcom's pricing and hardware decisions could affect its ability to procure sufficient computing infrastructure. In short, the supplier and the funder are the same party, and the buyer has publicly flagged the risk this structure creates.

Q2: Why should key-account sales teams care?

Because it puts an unequal dependency relationship on the record. The buyer needs the other side's hardware and capital; the supplier holds greater influence over pricing and supply. Key-account selling is a constant negotiation over power, information, and alternatives, and this case offers a real sample written down by the party involved — useful for checking your own position with a critical account.

Q3: What does negotiation power include in PSS5?

PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, used in sales training, capability assessment, and practical coaching. Negotiation power is its fifth dimension. It is defined as designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarified power, time, information, bottom lines, and alternatives, in order to reach a sustainable agreement. It is not a price-cutting technique; it is the discipline of calculating the balance of power, time pressure, information gaps, and fallback options before acting.

Q4: What is BATNA, and how does it apply here?

BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — the best course of action if the parties cannot agree. In preparation, it means specifying an executable alternative, a bottom line, authority, interests, and records. In Anthropic's position, concentrated compute suppliers and high switching costs weaken the quality of its alternative, while the supplier has more options and therefore more confidence. Any party should assess its BATNA before talks begin.

Q5: Can PRTABCD help analyze this kind of customer?

Yes. PRTABCD is a decision-information scanning tool within PSS5's relationship capability. It uses seven categories of decision information to identify intelligence blind spots, recording for each what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question. A is the embracer — who in the customer organization is embracing you. D is the decision chain — the multi-person, multi-department decision process. In a structure where a supplier is also a funder, scanning item by item quickly reveals gaps in time, budget, and decision-chain intelligence.

Q6: What can the weaker party still do?

Being weaker does not mean only accepting terms. Options include improving BATNA quality, even if only by calculating switching costs clearly; locking counterparty commitments into written records to preserve evidence for later exchange; and substituting conditional exchange for unilateral concession — trading payment cycles, order scope, or long-term commitment for comparable movement on price and delivery. Anthropic putting the risk into a prospectus is itself an act of turning a verbal contest into a formal record.

Q7: What does it mean for a buyer when a supplier is also a funder?

Two risks are amplified at once. First, supply security: the filing states the conflict could affect access to required compute. Second, price leadership: Broadcom's pricing and hardware decisions could affect procurement capacity. When the counterparty's commercial interest comes from both selling goods and earning a return on investment, the buyer faces both motives in every round, with fewer reliable information sources and alternatives.

Q8: How can a sales team avoid falling into similar dependency?

Do two things. First, audit dependency regularly: list your largest customers or suppliers and check how many roles each plays in your business — more roles means fewer chips. Second, write key commitments and risk ownership into cooperation documents so verbal consensus becomes a checkable record. Combined with a dual-perspective funnel that judges progress from customer behavioral evidence rather than feeling, passive situations can be identified earlier.
