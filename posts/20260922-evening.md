When a supplier moves from "we can build one" to "we can deliver at a predictable batch cadence," the customer's buying logic shifts. Risk assessment gives way to scheduling commitment. That shift is the practical core of value-language selling, and a recent public report about a Chinese launch vehicle offers a clean industrial example of how it works.

According to a report in East Money's *Investment Morning Brief*, the Lijian-1 rocket, developed by CAS Space, has orders booked into the second half of 2027, with annual production capacity reaching 30 units. The same report notes that a pulsating, takt-based production line has matured and been put into operation, breaking with the earlier pattern of small-batch, customized, experimental production in the private launch sector.

For a sales professional, this fact translates into one sentence: delivery certainty has become a commercial message that can be promised, not just a factory-floor matter. Customers never buy the rocket itself; they buy the promise of getting their assets into orbit on schedule.

## Reading the News Through Value Language

PSS⁵ (Professional Sales Star 5) is a systematic key-account sales enablement methodology developed by Xu Hanbo. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. The definition of value capability is precise: convert the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explain that value with business numbers.

The Lijian-1 case is an industrial version of that expression. It does not pile up performance parameters such as thrust or configuration. Instead, it gives scheduling and delivery information that the customer's decision chain can quote directly.

For a customer planning to build a satellite constellation, launch windows and constellation progress translate directly into money. Every launch delay pushes back the commercial return of an entire batch of satellites. When capacity is stated as 30 units per year and orders are booked into the second half of 2027, the customer has citable evidence when fighting internally for budget and scheduling. Features help the customer understand; capacity helps the customer sign. That is the weight of value language in a real transaction.

### Why Delivery Certainty Becomes a Purchasing Reason

For constellation customers, a delayed launch means the commercial return of the whole system shifts backward, and the loss is calculated across the entire constellation. The supplier's batch delivery capability and order schedule therefore directly determine the customer's investment rhythm and risk level. When this information is verifiable, the customer's buying logic moves from worrying about whether the supplier can deliver at all toward scheduling production and committing to a contract.

## Three Moves You Can Carry Over

1. **Translate feature descriptions into the customer's scheduling language.** Talk about delivery cadence, batch size, and committable time windows, not a list of performance parameters.
2. **Support the value proposition with verifiable numbers.** Customers need citable, definite information for internal reporting. Luxury adjectives do not persuade.
3. **Move delivery certainty forward into the needs and solution stages.** Under the PSS⁵ five sales stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing — capacity and scheduling topics should be on the table early. Do not save them for the negotiation table as a concession chip.

## A Common Misalignment and Its Correction

Many B2B teams write the value of a large project as a technical white paper. The result is that the customer's Box 2 — the manager layer responsible for operations and for using the funds — cannot take it to persuade Box 1, the capital allocation and approval layer. What Box 2 actually needs is business language: how delivery certainty affects construction progress, and how progress affects the revenue curve and payback period.

Once that translation is complete, the proposal moves from a cost item to an investment item — in other words, a qualified Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP). A PIP states clearly how much profit can be added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame the result will be achieved.

## Comparison: Feature Language vs. Value Language

| Dimension | Feature language | Value language |
|---|---|---|
| What is described | Thrust, configuration, technical parameters | Delivery cadence, batch size, committable time window |
| Who can use it | Technical evaluators | Box 2 operations managers and Box 1 approvers |
| Effect on the deal | Helps the customer understand | Helps the customer sign |
| Internal use | Hard to quote in budget meetings | Can be cited directly in budget and scheduling requests |
| Stage to raise it | Often saved for negotiation | Raised in needs and solution stages |

## Actionable Checklist

- Identify the business outcomes your customer cares about, such as construction progress, revenue start time, and cost curve.
- Find the specific impact your solution has on each outcome.
- Quantify that impact into wording the customer can quote in internal reports: delivery cadence, batch size, committable time window.
- Have the customer's Box 2 test the wording. It only qualifies if it can be quoted as-is.
- Bring capacity and scheduling topics into the needs and solution stages, not the negotiation stage.
- Convert the final proposal into a PIP that answers three questions: how much profit is added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame.
- Keep every claim verifiable. Do not dress up uncertain capacity as a promise.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does the Lijian-1 case teach key-account sales?**

The lesson is in the method of value expression. CAS Space used two verifiable pieces of information — 30 units of annual capacity and orders booked into the second half of 2027 — to prove delivery certainty to the market. Key-account sales should likewise translate solution features into scheduling, progress, and return language the customer can quote directly, rather than staying at the technical description level.

**Q2: What does value capability mean in PSS⁵?**

In Yanke's terminology, value capability means converting the sales object from product features into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. It requires the seller to speak from the customer's business perspective, using financial language such as profit, cost, and payback period, instead of listing product performance parameters.

**Q3: Why does delivery certainty become a purchasing reason?**

For constellation customers, a launch delay means the commercial return of the entire system shifts backward, with losses calculated across the whole constellation. The supplier's batch delivery capability and order schedule therefore directly determine the customer's investment rhythm and risk level. When this information is verifiable, the buying logic shifts from fear of non-delivery toward scheduled production and contractual commitment.

**Q4: What roles do Box 1 and Box 2 play in value expression?**

Box 1 refers to the capital allocation and approval layer; Box 2 refers to the manager layer responsible for operations and for using the funds. Sales must help Box 2 use business value to win funding from Box 1. Box 2 cares about operational progress and delivery cadence; Box 1 cares about return on investment and risk. A proposal must provide both languages to complete the customer's internal approval process.

**Q5: What is a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP)?**

A PIP writes the proposal's improvement to the customer's business as an investment statement. It answers three questions: how much profit can be added or protected, what the confidence and mechanism are, and over what time frame the result will be achieved. It is not a product quotation; it is a financial document that helps the customer's internal project approval.

**Q6: At what stage should capacity topics be raised?**

Under the PSS⁵ five sales stages — contact, needs, solution, negotiation, closing — delivery certainty and scheduling capability should be on the table in the needs and solution stages. At that point the customer is judging whether the problem is worth investing in and whether the solution path is feasible; supply certainty is an important input. Raising it only at the negotiation stage reduces it to a concession chip and sharply discounts its value.

**Q7: How do you translate technical parameters into usable business language?**

Three steps: first, confirm the business outcomes the customer cares about, such as construction progress, revenue start time, and cost curve; second, identify your solution's specific impact on each outcome; third, quantify the impact into wording the customer can quote in internal reports, such as delivery cadence, batch size, and committable time window. After translation, have the customer's Box 2 test it. It only qualifies if it can be quoted as-is.
