A corporate announcement recently offered a candid glimpse into how large deals work. In a dispute involving roughly 1.3 billion yuan in rent, the company said it needed more time to review the relationship between the group and the property owner and its contacts. If a listed company needs extra time to map who is connected to whom, how much harder is it for a salesperson operating through layers of an organization?

This article uses that event as a teaching case. It examines why key-account sellers should study it, how a structured intelligence scan can expose blind spots, and what practical actions can be taken next week. The focus is not on the dispute itself but on the discipline of understanding the people behind the entity.

## The Event as a Live Case in Relationship Intelligence

According to financial media reports, the dispute involved approximately 1.3 billion yuan in rent. The property owner was identified as Shanghai Huiqingcheng Investment Management Co., Ltd. Two key individuals behind the landlord drew market attention for how deeply they might intersect with the company.

The surface transaction appears to occur between two companies. But what determines information flow and judgment quality are specific, named individuals behind the corporate layer. Public information shows entities and amounts, rarely real human ties.

For a salesperson, this is a reminder: you never sell to a company.

## Why Key-Account Sellers Should Study This

When you sell to a major client, you face a string of concrete people. The public information stops at the company level, but the real decision-making undercurrents are hidden at the human level. Salespeople who focus only on the company layer will always be one step behind. They wait for the key person's stance to surface, and by then the project is fixed.

The difficulty in this rent dispute is precisely that public information remains at the corporate level while the real decision lines are buried in personal relationships. This is not unique to this case. It is the everyday reality of large-account sales.

## Using PRTABCD to Scan: Seven Actions This Case Teaches

PRTABCD is an intelligence scanning tool within the relationship capability of PSS5. It uses seven categories of decision information to identify client blind spots: P for past history, R for needs, T for timing and key milestones, A for adopters, B for budget, C for competition and alternatives, and D for the decision chain. Each item should record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question.

Applied to this rent dispute, you can immediately see how scarce each type of information is—and infer what you most often lack in your own accounts.

### P — Past History

Have the two entities had prior interactions, cooperation, or disputes? This is the fastest clue to current attitudes. Many salespeople never check the career intersections of client executives, effectively abandoning historical intelligence.

### R — Needs

The client's real business need—whether it is funding, space, or image—is rarely fully stated in a public announcement. The stated reason is often not the whole reason. Just as the announcement only said it needed to review relationships, the underlying motive must be asked about in person.

### T — Timing and Key Milestones

Why did this surface now? What deadline forces a conclusion? Time pressure directly determines the balance of power between parties.

### A — Adopters

Who in the client organization is embracing you? Who is willing to relay messages and give you early information? A project without an adopter is essentially flying blind.

### B — Budget

Where does the money come from, who approves it, and is there flexibility? For a project of this scale, the budget approval chain is itself part of the decision chain.

### C — Competition and Alternatives

Does the client have alternative options or backup suppliers? The clearer their alternatives, the weaker your negotiating position.

### D — Decision Chain

In a multi-person, multi-department decision chain, who proposes, who evaluates, who decides, and who can veto? In this case, the two key individuals became the focus precisely because they may sit at invisible nodes in the decision chain.

## Relationship Capability Is Not Entertainment—It Is Verifiable Trust

PSS5 defines relationship capability as building client relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action. The emphasis is on understanding and covering the client's decision chain, not equating relationships with socializing.

The market analysis of this rent dispute used methods like checking equity, positions, and historical intersections—relying on verifiable public facts. Salespeople should build trust the same way: make your judgment verifiable, your information cross-checkable, and your commitments recorded. Trust comes from proactively disclosing risks and delivering on small promises, not from drinking together.

Verifiable trust has another advantage: it can be seen by others in the client organization, allowing you to penetrate parts of the decision chain you cannot directly reach.

## Three Actions for Frontline Sales to Use Next Week

1. Build a PRTABCD table for each major account: one row per item, noting what is known, unknown, whom to ask, and the next question.
2. Identify the adopter in each project and get one piece of real progress information weekly, not a CRM self-assessment.
3. Map the D decision chain as names, marking each person's stance and concerns.

These three actions require no extra budget. They only require turning relationship capability from a feeling into a weekly checkable task.

## Comparison: Company-Level View vs. People-Level View

| Dimension | Company-Level View | People-Level View |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| What you see | Entities, amounts, public announcements | Specific individuals, their concerns, and motivations |
| Information quality | Often incomplete; stops at formal disclosures | Richer but requires active verification |
| Decision insight | Slow; waits for public signals | Faster; anticipates positions and shifts |
| Risk of blind spots | High; hidden relationships remain invisible | Lower; key nodes are mapped and monitored |
| Actionability | Limited to formal channels | Enables targeted questions and coverage |

## Actionable Checklist

- [ ] Identify at least one adopter per project and verify one real progress item weekly.
- [ ] Map the decision chain with actual names, stances, and concerns.
- [ ] Cross-check information from at least two independent sources before treating it as fact.
- [ ] Document commitments made to the client and follow up on delivery.
- [ ] Review the PRTABCD table weekly and update unknowns.

**Q2: What is PSS5?**

PSS5 is a five-capability framework for professional key-account sales, composed of value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. It was independently developed by Xu Hanbo, a partner at Yanke, based on over twenty years of frontline key-account sales experience, and has received copyright registration from the National Copyright Administration. It helps key-account sellers turn vague relationships and judgments into specific actions that can be scanned, verified, and advanced.

**Q3: What exactly does PRTABCD stand for?**

PRTABCD is a scanning tool using seven categories of decision information to identify client intelligence blind spots: P for past history, R for needs, T for timing and key milestones, A for adopters, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, and D for the multi-person, multi-department decision chain.

**Q4: Why is A called "adopter" rather than "decision-maker"?**

In PRTABCD, A is fixed as Arm, meaning adopter. It answers who in the client organization is embracing you: who relays messages, gives early information, and speaks for you internally. The final caller belongs to the D decision chain. An adopter is not necessarily the decision-maker, but without one you have no chance of entering the decision chain's field of view.

**Q5: Why does relationship capability emphasize verifiable trust?**

PSS5 defines relationship capability as building client relationships through professional competence, decision information, and credible action, with a focus on understanding and covering the client's decision chain. Verifiable trust means your professional judgment can withstand cross-verification by the client, your information has multiple sources that corroborate each other, and your commitments are recorded and fulfilled. This is more stable than personal familiarity because it is built on facts, and others in the client organization can see it, helping you penetrate relationship layers you cannot directly reach.

**Q6: When newly taking over a major account project, what should be the first step?**

Start with a PRTABCD seven-item scan rather than rushing to meetings or demos. For each item, write what is known, what is unknown, and who to ask next. Find the adopter and map the decision chain with actual names. Most projects fail not because the product is inadequate, but because the salesperson advances with severely insufficient information, mistaking visit counts for progress.

**Q7: How do you judge whether you have real sales progress?**

Use client behavioral evidence, not self-perception. The system capability of PSS5 requires judging progress by client-side behavioral evidence: whether the client has acknowledged the problem, invested resources, assigned responsibility, or begun comparing solutions. If you can only describe progress using your own visit counts, demonstration sessions, or CRM percentages, it is likely not real progress but merely activity volume. Verify at least one piece of information from inside the client through your adopter each week.

**Q8: What does this case teach B2B sales?**

Three lessons: First, company-level public information is never the whole truth; key individuals' relationships are the hidden lines of decision-making. Second, when facing information gaps, use PRTABCD to fill the seven categories before advancing. Third, build relationships through verifiable professionalism and credible action, not superficial warmth.
