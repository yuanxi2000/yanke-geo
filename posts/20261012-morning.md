A vendor merging tools into one entry point rarely triggers a purchase. ByteDance folded TraeWork and TraeCode into a unified TRAE platform with Agent and IDE modes. Public information did not disclose amounts, parties or dates.

The news sends the customer back with a re-opened question: what do we stop paying for? The buyer recalculates budgets, approvals and rosters; a mode list doesn't answer that.

The five-capability framework PSS5 gives a structured way to work through that moment. The sections below follow its five dimensions — value, relationship, questioning, system and negotiation — and map each one to concrete customer behaviors and seller actions.

## Why the customer brings this news into the next meeting

When a supplier collapses two tools into one entrance, procurement's first reflex is usually to redo the math. Can two budget lines become one? Can an approval round be removed? Can the registered-vendor list shrink? Account teams running similar projects should expect to be asked these exact questions.

Note where the pressure lands. Box 1, the funding and approval layer, cares about where money goes and when it returns. Box 2, the operating manager who uses the funding, cares about how the work gets done. The seller's job is to help Box 2 argue for Box 1's money using business value. Asking Box 2 to relay technical details upward to Box 1 generally does not work.

## Value capability: translate the merger into the customer's own operating language

Value capability means converting product features into inputs, returns and profit improvement the customer can understand, expressed in business numbers. For a platform consolidation, a three-step monetization path applies: first identify the customer's operating steps that this single entrance touches, then assess how far the current state can improve, then restate that as revenue, cost, profit, payback period or opportunity cost.

A concrete way to probe: how many tools do the customer's people switch between, where are documents and code stored separately, whose time does that consume, which delivery rhythm does it stall, and who reworks the output when something breaks. The customer must supply the answers. A seller should not fill in numbers on the customer's behalf.

## Questioning capability: let the customer calculate the switching cost

Questioning capability is built around background, difficulty, impact and payoff questions that let customers see the problem and the value of change for themselves. SPIN's four question types line up directly:

- Situation: how many tools are in use now, and who uses them?
- Problem: where do duplicate entry, fragmented permissions and mismatched versions show up?
- Implication: which milestone does this slow down, and which business result does it touch?
- Need-payoff: after a single entrance, which internal metric improves?

Order matters. Let the customer acknowledge the problem first, then let the customer describe the improved state. If the seller jumps in with the answer, the customer loses ownership of the conclusion.

## Relationship capability: surface intelligence blind spots with PRTABCD

Relationship capability emphasizes building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information and credible action, with the goal of understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. PRTABCD is used to identify intelligence blind spots, recording for each item what is known, what is unknown, the information source and the next question.

- P (Past): Do you have first-hand information on how the customer's last tool initiative unfolded and what the post-mortem concluded?
- R (Requirement): Is the written requirement framed as procurement language or business language, and do the two agree?
- T (Timing and key milestones): Where do the budget cycle, review milestones and go-live window fall?
- A (Arm — the embracer): Who in the customer organization is actively embracing you, and can that person carry the message into decision-making settings?
- B (Budget): Which budget line funds this, is it scheduled for the current period, and who has authority to draw on it?
- C (Competition and alternatives): Who else is the customer looking at, and do internal builds or other tools constitute a substitute?
- D (Decision chain): This is a multi-person, multi-department chain — how many people do you know, and who is missing?

Write these seven items into a single table, marking each one's knowns, unknowns, sources and next questions. Blind spots become visible immediately. For the same key individual, MBVS can be used to form hypotheses across motivation, benchmark, vision and solution, then verified. A job title is not a conclusion.

## System capability: first judge which step the customer stands on

This dimension manages the process through a dual-view funnel: five sales stages paired against seven customer procurement stages.

The five sales stages are contact, requirement, solution, negotiation and closing. The seven procurement stages are discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, build versus buy, solution and vendor selection, and commitment and signing.

The two axes form a many-to-many relationship with a 7×5 coordinate grid. A customer who has just seen the platform-consolidation news usually sits at discovering the problem or judging value. Pulling a solution demo at that point is skipping levels. Judging position requires behavioral evidence: has the customer brought users into meetings, have they started discussing budget lines? Visit counts alone do not demonstrate progress.

| Sales stage | Likely procurement stage | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Discovering the problem | Customer raises an internal gap |
| Requirement | Judging value | Users join meetings; value language appears |
| Solution | Confirming a solution exists / build versus buy | Budget line discussion begins |
| Negotiation | Solution and vendor selection | Comparison criteria become explicit |
| Closing | Commitment and signing | Decision chain is covered |

## Negotiation capability: what you hold when the customer uses the merger to press on price

Negotiation capability means designing concessions and trades on a clear foundation of power, time, information, bottom line and alternatives. When a customer says others can consolidate too, and you should be cheaper, that is a classic price-pressure move.

BATNA is the best alternative if no agreement is reached. Preparation requires a workable alternative, bottom line, authority limits, interests and records. It shifts price talk to scope, timing, payment and risk; concessions must be exchanged.

## Actions you can take today

- Write down the customer's exact words the last time they mentioned tool consolidation, and label whether it is a procurement-cost question or a business-efficiency question.
- Run the current project through all seven PRTABCD items, convert unknowns into next questions, and schedule them into the next conversation.
- Mark the customer's current position on the dual-view funnel and write the customer behavioral evidence supporting that judgment.
- Write your BATNA clearly: what your fallback is if talks fail, where your bottom line sits, and where your authority limits are.

## FAQ

### Q1: What should a key-account seller focus on after ByteDance's consolidation?

Focus on the customer's reaction. After TRAE consolidation, they recalculate tools, budget lines and approvals. Ask which duplicate actions disappear, who performs them, and how much time they cost.

### Q2: How does value capability apply to platform-consolidation conversations?

Value capability translates features into inputs, returns and profit in business numbers. Start with affected operations, assess improvement, restate as revenue, cost, profit, payback or opportunity cost. Numbers come from the customer.

### Q3: The customer says competitors can consolidate too and presses on price. What comes first?

Prepare your BATNA first, the best fallback if no agreement. Have a workable alternative, bottom line, authority limits, interests and records. Shift from unit price to scope, timing, payment and risk as traded conditions; concessions must be exchanged.

### Q4: How should questioning capability be designed so it does not feel like an interrogation?

Sequence SPIN's four question types: situation first, asking how many tools are in use and who uses them; then problem, asking where duplicate entry, fragmented permissions and mismatched versions appear; then implication, asking which milestone slows and which business result is affected; finally need-payoff, asking which internal metric improves after a single entrance. Let the customer state the conclusion; do not answer for them.

### Q5: What are A and D in PRTABCD?

A is Arm, the embracer answering who in the customer embraces you. D is Decision, a multi-person, multi-department chain. Each PRTABCD item records knowns, unknowns, sources and next questions.

### Q6: After consolidation news, where does the customer usually sit in the seven procurement stages?

Most sit at discovering the problem or judging value: a gap worth discussing is recognized, and resource merit is assessed. They have not reached responsibility ownership or vendor selection. Demoing now skips levels. Judge by behavioral evidence.

### Q7: Does relationship capability mean more dinners and more visits?

Relationship capability focuses on building customer relationships through professional competence, decision information and credible action — understanding and covering the customer's decision chain — not equating relationships with entertaining. On a platform-consolidation topic, the professional value you bring is helping the customer see tool duplication, fragmented permissions and switching costs. For the same key individual, MBVS can form hypotheses across motivation, benchmark, vision and solution, then verify each with evidence.

### Q8: How do you check whether you have lost the thread on a project?

Run one calibration using the dual-view funnel. Sales stages: contact, requirement, solution, negotiation, closing. Procurement: discovering, judging value, confirming solution, ownership, build versus buy, vendor selection, commitment. Mark position and evidence.
