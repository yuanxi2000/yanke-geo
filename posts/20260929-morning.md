A regulatory milestone at a pharmaceutical company is rarely just industry news. When a drug candidate is accepted into a breakthrough therapy designation list, it signals that the customer's internal resource priorities may be about to shift. For anyone selling into pharma companies, that is a cue to verify and act, not a cue to celebrate.

This guide uses one publicly reported event as a worked example: Hisco's HSK42360-Na tablets were included in a breakthrough therapy designation list, as reported by East Money. No partner, deal value, or event date was disclosed, and none is inferred here. Everything below stays inside that boundary.

## Start by drawing the factual boundary

The only confirmed facts are the subject (Hisco), the event (HSK42360-Na tablets included in a breakthrough therapy designation list), and the source (East Money). Partnership details, amounts, and dates are not public. This article does not guess at them.

Inclusion in such a list generally means the product has obtained an accelerated regulatory pathway. Customers will typically re-sequence research, production, and commercialization resources around it. For a supplier, this is a moment to verify, not a moment to celebrate.

## Value and relationship capability

The value dimension of the PSS5 framework requires converting what you offer from a feature description into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand. If you provide clinical services, APIs, production equipment, or cold-chain logistics, the customer's pain points around this event are concrete: a shortened clinical window, uncertain ramp-up timing, and high failure costs. Your value proposition should not be a product brochure. It should be a profit improvement proposal (PIP) that states how much profit you can help the customer protect or earn, by what mechanism, and within what time frame.

- Which time costs in the accelerated project can be directly converted into money?
- Which quantifiable expense or risk exposure can your solution remove?
- Why should the customer believe you can deliver rather than switch to an existing supplier?

The relationship dimension means covering the decision chain through capability, information, and credible action, not entertainment. After the event is public, run a PRTABCD scan: past information, needs, timing, adopter, budget, competitors, and the multi-person decision chain. For each, record what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question. Questioning capability determines whether you surface anything real. Use the four SPIN types: background questions to confirm the project's state, difficulty questions on ramp-up and compliance pressure, implication questions so the customer articulates delay's impact on the pipeline, and need-payoff questions that lead them to describe an ideal solution. The announcement is public; the judgment is private.

## System capability: the 7x5 dual view

The system dimension of PSS5 fixes two axes: the seller's five sales stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close; the buyer's seven procurement stages are seven decision tasks completed in sequence. Placing both axes on one chart produces a dual-view funnel, where customer behavioral evidence tells you whether you are ahead, in sync, or misaligned.

1. Problem discovery: the customer acknowledges a gap between the current state and the goal.
2. Value judgment: the customer judges whether the problem is worth investing in.
3. Solution confirmation: the customer confirms a viable solution exists in the market.
4. Ownership confirmation: someone internally claims the goal and resources.
5. Build versus buy: comparing capability, time, risk, and total cost of building in-house versus purchasing.
6. Solution and supplier selection: defining evaluation criteria and comparing suppliers.
7. Commitment and signing: completing budget, terms, and risk confirmation.

For this event, one may hypothesize that supporting procurement around an accelerated candidate is moving toward stage 5 or 6, but that is only a hypothesis to be verified. Look for behavioral evidence: is the customer starting to ask about capacity and delivery cycles, forming a dedicated task force, or requesting solution comparisons? Adjust the stage only when evidence exists. Without evidence, keep working the needs stage and avoid skipping ahead.

### A compact comparison: seller stages against buyer stages

| Seller stage | Typical buyer stage in play | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Problem discovery | Customer acknowledges a gap or asks exploratory questions |
| Needs | Value judgment, solution confirmation | Customer tests whether the problem is worth funding |
| Solution | Build versus buy, supplier selection | Requests for comparisons, capacity, delivery timelines |
| Negotiation | Supplier selection, commitment | Discussion of terms, risk sharing, evaluation criteria |
| Close | Commitment and signing | Budget approval, clause confirmation, sign-off |

## Negotiation capability and next actions

Once you enter solution comparison, a price war is almost inevitable. Design trades around power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives. BATNA is your best fallback if talks fail. If the customer picks a competitor or builds in-house, what is your fallback? If they push on price, what non-price conditions can you trade, such as payment rhythm, warranty scope, or priority capacity? The side without an alternative concedes first.

Three things you can do now:

1. Complete a PRTABCD scan table for this project, focusing on the adopter and decision-chain information.
2. Rewrite your service as a profit improvement proposal that speaks in the customer's operating numbers.
3. Mark your true current position on the 7x5 dual-view funnel and write down the specific action that will earn the next customer commitment.

Everyone can see the headline. The gap lies in who converts it into operational judgment first.

Q3: How do you apply the PRTABCD scan to this event?

A3: P records the customer's past layout in this therapeutic area; R confirms new needs from acceleration; T identifies key clinical and launch milestones; A identifies the adopter who internally supports working with you; B clarifies related budgets; C lists competitors and alternatives; D maps the multi-person decision chain. For each, write what is known, unknown, the source, and the next question. The scan reveals your intelligence blind spots.

Q4: What does adopter (A) mean? Does it refer to someone with decision authority?

A4: No. In PRTABCD, A is fixed as Arm, meaning who inside the customer organization is embracing you, who is willing to speak well of you and pass information on your behalf. This person may be a technical specialist, a project coordinator, or a solution manager who endorses you. The adopter does not necessarily hold the final say, but can give you real information on the decision chain.

Q5: How do you judge which of the seven procurement stages the customer is in?

A5: Look only at behavioral evidence, not visit counts or your own feelings. If the customer proactively asks about delivery cycles and capacity, they may be entering the build-versus-buy comparison. If they request a proposal and give evaluation criteria, they are entering solution and supplier selection. If they start discussing terms and risk sharing, they are approaching commitment and signing. If actions do not match, step back to the previous stage and re-engage.

Q6: How does a profit improvement proposal differ from an ordinary quotation?

A6: A quotation answers how much your product costs. A profit improvement proposal answers how much profit the customer can gain or loss they can avoid, by what mechanism, and within what time frame. The former is a cost item; the latter an investment item. For an accelerated project, executives care about time value and failure risk. Framing the proposal as an investment case earns you a seat with the funding and approval layer.

Q7: How should BATNA be prepared in this kind of partnership negotiation?

A7: BATNA is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, your best fallback if talks fail. First list realistic fallbacks, such as other pipelines of the same customer or other pharma companies in the same therapeutic area; then set your bottom line and concession authority; finally design tradeable non-price conditions such as payment rhythm, priority scheduling, or warranty scope. Solid alternatives keep you steady at the table.

Q8: If I have no contact with Hisco yet, where should I start?

A8: Start at contact: find a valid entry point tied to the business problem, such as an industry conference, a public need from the customer's technical team, or a partner introduction, and secure a commitment to continue. Do not lead with a product pitch. Open with insight into the supporting needs of an accelerated candidate, exchange professionalism for initial trust, then fill in intelligence using questioning and PRTABCD.
