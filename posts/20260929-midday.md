Key-account sales rarely turns on a single clever line at the table. More often, it turns on two quieter disciplines: arranging the agenda before trading conditions, and reading where a customer keeps putting money. Two public headlines illustrate these disciplines well. One reports that China and the United States reached an eight-point consensus. The other reports that Nvidia is increasing its bet on glass substrates. Neither headline, on its own, tells a sales team what to do. But the patterns behind them are worth translating into daily practice.

## Negotiation Power: Align the Agenda, Then Exchange Conditions

The public headline states that an eight-point consensus was reached. The specific contents and follow-up arrangements of those eight points are not provided in the source, so this article does not fill them in. What can be examined is the act itself: results were organized into a numbered list. In large bilateral negotiations, the final stage is often a contest of orderliness — which issues enter the list, in what sequence they are discussed, and which concessions are tied to which conditions.

This matches the requirements of negotiation power in the PSS5 framework: on the basis of clarifying power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives, design concessions and conditional exchanges to pursue a sustainable agreement. Mapped to key-account sales, chaos at the negotiating table usually comes from insufficient preparation. The salesperson arrives with only a price; the customer arrives with a budget, internal processes, internal pressure, and alternative suppliers. Before negotiations begin, at least three things should be written down clearly:

- What result must this meeting bring back, and where is the bottom line?
- What conditions can be offered in exchange, and what does each cost?
- If no deal is reached, is the best alternative to a negotiated agreement (BATNA) executable?

Once these three items are clear, a quote shifts from a statement of position to a conditional exchange. The lesson from the eight-point consensus is not the number eight; it is that both sides were willing to put their differences into a single numbered list and handle them item by item. Key-account negotiation works the same way: discussing one item clearly at a time, with consideration attached to each, is more likely to reach a sustainable agreement than circling around a single price.

### A Compact Comparison: Price-Only vs. Condition-Exchange Negotiation

| Dimension | Price-only approach | Condition-exchange approach |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | Salesperson brings a price | Bottom line, tradeable conditions, and BATNA are written down |
| Agenda | Single issue: price | Multiple items, sequenced and tied to conditions |
| Concession logic | Concede to close the gap | Concede only in exchange for something specific |
| Risk | Margin erosion, unsustainable deal | Clearer trade-offs, more durable agreement |
| Customer perception | Vendor competing on discount | Partner solving a business problem |

## Value Power: Where the Customer Adds, Value Speaks Loudest

The second publicly stated fact is that Nvidia is increasing its bet on glass substrates. Increasing a bet means adding investment in a direction already being pursued. The source does not bind this to amounts, timelines, or project details, so this article does not speculate about them. For key-account sales, the reminder is this: to judge what a customer truly recognizes, look at where it keeps putting money — that is more reliable than listening to what it says. Value power requires converting the sales object from product features into investments, returns, and profit improvements the customer can understand, and explaining value with business numbers.

Reading this through a dual-perspective funnel makes it clearer. The customer purchasing process has seven stages: discovering a problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing and signing. The sales process has five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. Put both on the same map and use customer behavioral evidence to judge position. A customer that is increasing its bet has usually already passed the value-judgment stage and is at confirming a solution exists or further along. If the salesperson is still giving a general product introduction at that point, buyer and seller are misaligned, and no amount of activity will move the stage forward.

## Five Actions You Can Execute This Week

- Build a negotiation preparation sheet for your single most important opportunity, stating the bottom line, tradeable conditions, and BATNA.
- Use the seven customer purchasing stages to position that same opportunity, and note the customer behavioral evidence behind the judgment.
- Rewrite your solution explanation as a one-page investment statement: how much profit can be increased or protected, what the mechanism and confidence level are, and over what time frame.
- Run a PRTABCD check across seven categories of decision information, mark unknowns, and write down the next question for each.
- Review your most recent failed quote: what was actually exchanged, and which bargaining chip was missing?

## FAQ

**Q1: What is PSS5?**

A1: PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a systematic key-account sales methodology independently developed by Xu Hanbo, a partner at Yanke, based on more than twenty years of key-account sales practice, and registered with the National Copyright Administration. It consists of five dimensions: value power, relationship power, questioning power, system power, and negotiation power, covering the complete operating process of key-account sales.

**Q2: How is negotiation power defined within PSS5?**

A2: In PSS5 terminology, negotiation power means designing concessions and conditional exchanges on the basis of clarifying power, time, information, bottom line, and alternatives, in order to pursue a sustainable agreement. It requires the salesperson to complete preparation before sitting at the table: executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records must all be written down clearly, rather than relying on who has the louder voice or who can endure longer.

**Q3: What can key-account sales negotiation learn from news like the eight-point consensus?**

A3: What is worth learning is the method of handling the agenda, not the content itself. Compressing complex differences into a numbered list means both sides first agree on the agenda, then exchange conditions item by item. Key-account negotiation is similar: first align with the customer on which few things will be discussed, each side's bottom line, and tradeable conditions, avoiding repeated entanglement over a single price. Details not stated in the report should not be cited speculatively.

**Q4: How should BATNA be prepared before negotiation?**

A4: BATNA refers to the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best fallback action a negotiator can take when the parties cannot reach agreement. In practice, preparation should clarify executable alternatives, bottom line, authority, interests, and records. In key-account sales, this means answering how the company can still arrange capacity and manpower if this deal is not signed, and writing that answer into the negotiation preparation sheet so there is confidence at the table.

**Q5: What does news like Nvidia increasing its bet on glass substrates suggest about value power?**

A5: The headline publicly states that Nvidia is increasing its bet on glass substrates; increasing a bet means adding investment. The lesson for key-account sales is that the direction a customer keeps funding is where it recognizes value. When building a value proposition, organize language along the direction the customer has already increased its bet, and explain the solution's impact on profit, cost, and investment in business language the customer understands. The source does not bind amounts or details, so they should not be cited.

**Q6: How can the seven customer purchasing stages be used to judge opportunity position?**

A6: The seven stages are discovering a problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership of responsibility, deciding whether to build or buy, selecting a solution and supplier, and committing and signing. Each step must be proven with customer behavioral evidence, not replaced by visit counts or the salesperson's own feelings. Comparing against these seven decision tasks shows where the opportunity truly stands and what action is needed next.

**Q7: What seven categories of information does PRTABCD scan?**

A7: PRTABCD uses seven categories of decision information to identify customer intelligence blind spots: P for past history, R for requirements, T for timing and key milestones, A for advocates — who in the customer organization is embracing you, B for budget, C for competitors and alternatives, and D for the decision chain. Each item should record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. It is a decision-information scanning tool within relationship power.

**Q8: How do you judge whether a key-account opportunity is truly progressing?**

A8: Judge by customer-side behavioral evidence, not by visit counts, CRM percentages, or the salesperson's own feelings. The PSS5 sales funnel is fixed at five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. Each stage requires the customer to take observable action, such as confirming the problem and ownership of responsibility, jointly defining success criteria, or exchanging conditions. Without customer behavior, the stage has not advanced.
