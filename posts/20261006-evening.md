When a supplier redefines what it sells, customers redefine what they are willing to pay for it. A move from single-component supplier to platform company is, at its core, a rewrite of value rather than a product launch.

This guide looks at one publicly reported example of that shift and translates it into practical lessons for key-account sales. It stays close to what was actually disclosed and treats everything else as methodology-level reasoning.

## What Was Publicly Disclosed

According to reporting by East Money, 299 STAR Market companies received institutional research visits in September, and discussion of hard-tech supply-chain gaps moved from whole-machine substitution toward module-level breakthroughs. Within that context, Hengyunchang stated during institutional research that:

- Some models of its fourth-generation Cedar series RF power supply and matcher integrated platform had entered small-batch trial production.
- Its MFC MEMS sensor completed sample upgrades and modifications.
- It added a new R&D project for an AI impedance matching system.
- Its positioning is shifting from a single-component supplier to a platform company for core components of plasma processes.

For a key-account sales reader, the important part is not the technical parameters of any one model. It is that the supplier changed how it introduces itself to customers. Previously it described component performance and reliability; now it describes an integrated platform spanning multiple product lines. For a buyer, those two statements map to different evaluation processes, budget categories, and decision levels.

## Why a Positioning Shift Changes How Customers Do the Math

PSS5 is Yanke's five-capability framework for key-account sales, used in sales training, capability assessment and practical coaching. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability is defined as converting the sales object from product functions into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers.

Read through that lens, the positioning shift breaks into several layers.

### The layers a platform story touches

- **Product layer:** When RF power supply and matcher become an integrated platform, the customer no longer evaluates the substitutability of one component. It evaluates whether several component categories can be supplied and validated under one roof.
- **Process layer:** As the supplier covers more steps, the customer's validation, certification, and ongoing maintenance coordination costs may fall. That is a benefit that can be translated into cost and efficiency language.
- **Risk layer:** Sourcing moves from many dispersed suppliers toward platform concentration. The customer sees both coordination benefits and concentration risk, and both directions enter the customer's calculation.
- **Decision layer:** A platform proposal usually involves more departments, so purchasing evaluation rises from a single team to a cross-functional level.

None of this is automatic. The supplier has to make the translation explicit, or the customer will keep comparing on the old basis.

## Turning a Platform Story into Profit Language

Many technology and manufacturing companies evolve toward platforms while their sales talk stays at the component level: parameters, yield, lead time. These are necessary but not sufficient. The profit improvement plan (PIP) within PSS5 requires writing the improvement a proposal creates for the customer's business as an investment statement: how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and over what time frame. Platform suppliers facing large accounts need this translation ability even more.

A practical three-step approach:

1. Map where each component your platform covers sits on the customer's production line, and identify the steps where multiple suppliers create duplicated investment.
2. Work with the customer to convert reduced duplication into financial language, written as a checkable improvement hypothesis rather than a made-up percentage.
3. Validate the hypothesis in a small pilot, let the customer confirm the benefit with its own data, then expand scope.

The common thread: the customer receives not a quotation but a verifiable statement of business improvement.

One boundary matters here. The public research disclosure covered product and R&D progress only. It did not disclose any customer purchase amounts or cooperation details. All suggestions in this guide are methodology-level reasoning for key-account sales practitioners and should not be read as a judgment about any company's actual business results.

## The Other Four Capabilities Must Move Together

A value upgrade does not automatically produce orders. The five PSS5 dimensions need to advance as a set.

- **Relationship capability** requires understanding and covering the customer's decision chain. A platform proposal touches more departments, so the key-person map has to be redrawn.
- **Questioning capability** requires designing questions around background, difficulty, impact, and need-payoff, helping the customer see the value of platformization for itself.
- **System capability** requires judging progress through a dual-perspective funnel built from five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages, using customer behavioral evidence rather than visit counts.
- **Negotiation capability** requires designing concessions and exchanges when power, time, information, and alternatives are clear. The bargaining room a platform creates must be both pursued and bounded.

When all five move together, the value story can actually land as a contract.

## A Compact Comparison: Component Sale vs. Platform Sale

| Dimension | Component sale | Platform sale |
|---|---|---|
| What is compared | Performance, price, substitutability of one part | Overall coverage, coordination efficiency, risk structure |
| Buyer's evaluation | Single team, component-level criteria | Cross-functional, platform-level criteria |
| Cost logic | Unit price and reliability | Duplicated investment, validation cost, concentration risk |
| Sales language | Parameters, yield, lead time | Investment, return, profit improvement |
| Main risk concern | Delivery and quality | Supply continuity, bargaining position, exit cost |

## Actionable Checklist

- List every component your platform covers and where it sits on the customer's line.
- Identify steps where multiple suppliers cause duplicated validation or coordination effort.
- Convert each duplication into a financial hypothesis the customer can check.
- Write the improvement as an investment statement: profit added or protected, mechanism, time frame.
- Redraw the key-person map for a cross-functional decision.
- Prepare questions across background, difficulty, impact, and need-payoff.
- Track progress by customer behavior, not by number of visits.
- Put concentration risk on the table with supply assurance, phased switching, and exit arrangements.
- Define negotiation boundaries before discussing concessions.
- Run a small pilot so the customer confirms value with its own data.

## FAQ

**Q1: What did the public research disclosure actually contain?**

According to East Money reporting, the company mentioned that some models of its fourth-generation Cedar series RF power supply and matcher integrated platform entered small-batch trial production, that its MFC MEMS sensor completed sample upgrades, that it added an AI impedance matching system R&D project, and that its positioning is shifting from single-component supplier to a platform company for core plasma-process components. The report also noted 299 STAR Market companies received institutional research visits in September. Cooperation details and amounts did not appear in the public information.

**Q2: What does a shift from component supplier to platform company mean for key-account sales?**

The way customers evaluate the supplier changes. A single component is compared on performance, price, and substitutability; a platform proposal is compared on overall coverage, coordination efficiency, and risk structure. Sales must upgrade the value proposition accordingly, translating component parameters into checkable cost, efficiency, and risk improvements. Otherwise the customer keeps comparing on the old framework and platform investment struggles to earn a premium.

**Q3: What does value capability mean in PSS5?**

It means converting the sales object from product functions into an investment, return, and profit improvement the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. It asks sales to answer the question an executive cares about: how much profit does this investment add or protect, through what mechanism, and over what time frame.

**Q4: What are the five dimensions of PSS5?**

Value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability translates the proposal into business value; relationship capability penetrates the decision chain; questioning capability surfaces and amplifies real needs; system capability manages the sales process through a dual-perspective funnel; negotiation capability builds sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives. They work together.

**Q5: What is PIP and why does a platform supplier need it?**

PIP is the profit improvement plan. It requires writing the improvement a proposal creates for the customer's business as an investment statement, answering how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and over what time frame, rather than presenting a product quotation. A platform supplier covers more steps, so improvement points are scattered across cost, efficiency, and risk. Only when written in checkable profit language can the customer's decision layer compare and approve.

**Q6: What customer-side risks does platform positioning raise?**

Customers usually see two sides of the ledger: coordination benefits and concentration risk. As sourcing concentrates with one platform supplier, validation and coordination costs may fall, but dependence on a single supplier rises. Customers will care about supply continuity, bargaining position, and exit cost. Sales should put these concerns on the table and manage them with supply assurance, phased switching, and exit arrangements rather than avoiding them.

**Q7: How can questioning capability validate a platform proposal's real value?**

Use the four SPIN question types: background questions to clarify the customer's current supplier structure and validation flow; difficulty questions to surface duplicated investment caused by multiple suppliers; impact questions to quantify the effect of that duplication on cost and line stability; and need-payoff questions to let the customer state the improvement a unified platform could bring. Value the customer voices is more credible than value the salesperson claims.

**Q8: How do you judge how far a platform value story has advanced inside the customer?**

Use the dual-perspective funnel of system capability. The seven customer purchasing stages are: recognizing a problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming ownership, build-or-buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. The seller side maps to five sales stages. Judge by customer behavioral evidence. For example, willingness to open production-line data for a pilot indicates the customer has entered solution validation. Visits and reports without customer action do not count as progress.
