The source article on Xi'an Yicai offers a sales lesson: a public capacity signal can guide account planning, but it is not proof that procurement has changed. The article links delayed long-order transmission with rising depreciation and says the profit inflection still needs verification. For suppliers, this is a cue for disciplined questions, not a reason to forecast a bigger budget or a faster project.

## What the public signal does and does not prove

According to the source, the verifiable facts are specific:

- Wuhan third factory construction is underway.
- First-phase capacity is expected in June 2027.
- Line capitalization follows accounting rules and occurs gradually with ramp.
- Depreciation growth is expected to be below revenue growth.
- Early new-plant depreciation is expected to be covered by mass-production revenue.
- The company expects 2027 gross margin to keep growing and net profit to turn positive.

These facts support a capacity and depreciation story. They do not prove that the customer has changed budgets, accelerated a project, shortlisted suppliers, or fixed acceptance criteria. The source's judgment that the inflection needs verification is not a customer announcement. Writing 'expected to turn profitable' as 'the customer is buying more' turns analysis into an unsupported fact.

| Public signal | What it supports | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Wuhan third factory is under construction | A capacity project is underway | Treating construction as a purchase order |
| First-phase production is expected in June 2027 | A possible time anchor for scheduling | Assuming the procurement schedule is fixed |
| Capitalization follows the ramp | Depreciation will be staged | Treating accounting timing as a buying trigger |
| 2027 margin and profit are expected to improve | A management expectation to verify | Repeating it as a customer commitment |

### Keep facts, hypotheses, and actions separate

In account notes, label each item. Public facts can be cited. Hypotheses can only become questions. Actions must name the next customer conversation or evidence. This prevents a news analysis from silently becoming a forecast.

## Use value capability to calculate three accounts

Value capability translates product features into the customer's profit, investment, and operating improvement. For a customer in ramp and capitalization, tie the proposal to three variables the customer can check.

- Time account: which purchases must be locked before first-phase production, and what acceptance milestones apply?
- Depreciation account: which capitalization, utilization, maintenance, and energy assumptions affect coverage? The customer fills the numbers.
- Gross-margin account: is expected margin growth driven by price, yield, utilization, or cost structure? List the assumptions; do not decide them for the customer.

Then translate the offer into a customer metric. If the offer affects downtime, connect it to ramp loss. If it affects yield, connect it to unit cost. If it affects delivery stability, connect it to revenue coverage from mass-production lines. The supplier supplies the calculation method and a blank template; it does not invent customer numbers.

## Ask questions that turn the inflection point into choices

Questioning capability can use SPIN's four categories. The aim is to let the customer interpret what the inflection signal means for procurement.

- Situation: Which purchases must be locked before first-phase production?
- Problem: Which ramp-period stoppages would distort depreciation coverage?
- Implication: If long-order transmission lag continues, will approval focus more on unit price or total cost?
- Need-payoff: If a proposal shortens ramp or cuts downtime, what verifiable result would the customer gain?

'Budget not set' and 'acceptance standard not issued' are useful answers. Record them instead of pushing a product narrative.

## Map the decision chain with relationship capability

Relationship capability rests on professional competence, decision information, and credible action, not entertainment. PRTABCD covers past, need, time, arm or adopter, budget, competition and alternatives, and decision chain. A asks who is embracing you. D is the multi-person, multi-department decision chain. For this signal, record who owns production ramp, depreciation and margin assumptions, budget, and possible opposition to new spending. Use four columns: known, unknown, source, next question. Keep unknown blank. Do not make one executive the sole decision maker because the company expects positive net profit.

## Judge progress with system capability

System capability uses a dual-view funnel of five sales stages and seven customer purchasing stages, judged by customer behavior. The news shows capacity and ramp decisions, not procurement negotiation or close. Look for evidence that the customer acknowledges a problem, judges value, confirms a solution, assigns ownership, compares build versus buy, enters solution and supplier selection, and makes a formal commitment. Technical exchange is early. Clear specifications, acceptance criteria, and budget definitions suggest selection. Payment, scope, and risk sharing suggest negotiation. Visits and sales enthusiasm are not evidence.

## Negotiate around the 2027 milestone, not with it

Negotiation capability rests on power, time, information, and alternatives. June 2027 is a public time anchor for delivery, acceptance, and capacity planning, not a reason to raise price. Put delivery, acceptance, payment, and risk sharing on one table. Earlier delivery can trade against capacity reservation and payment terms. Later payment can trade against acceptance timing or change responsibility. A broader scope can trade against resources and priority. Every concession needs an executable return condition. If budget, acceptance, or responsibility is unclear, narrow the commitment.

## Build a one-page battle card

Use four columns: known, unknown, next question, and evidence source. Known can include the Wuhan third factory, June 2027 first-phase production, staged capitalization, expected 2027 margin growth, and expected positive net profit. Unknown can include budget, project owner, supplier shortlist, acceptance criteria, and the specific impact of long-order lag. Next questions must be directly answerable. Keep unsupported cells blank. 'The customer will press price because of depreciation' is a hypothesis. Ask instead: 'After the new line is capitalized, will procurement approval focus more on unit price or total cost?' The answer drives the next action.

## Actionable checklist

- Put only source-supported facts in the known column.
- Mark budget, owner, shortlist, and acceptance as unknown until confirmed.
- Meet the ramp owner; confirm production milestone, acceptance standard, and purchases to lock.
- Bring a blank value model for time, depreciation, and gross margin.
- Ask the customer to fill utilization, downtime loss, and cost assumptions.
- Complete a PRTABCD scan and list decision-chain gaps.
- Test stage progress against customer behavior, not visit count.
- Prepare delivery, payment, scope, and risk trade-offs; record the next question.

## FAQ

Q1: What should sales confirm first after reading that the profit inflection needs verification?

A: Confirm that this is the source's judgment, not a customer procurement announcement. List the known facts, then ask whether the budget covers the work, who owns production, and what acceptance criteria apply. Unanswered items stay unknown.

Q2: How can value capability be used on this signal?

A: Tie the proposal to three checkable variables: whether the first-phase milestone is affected, whether depreciation coverage is more stable, and whether margin improvement has support. The customer supplies the numbers.

Q3: How can questioning avoid becoming leading?

A: Move from facts to difficulties, consequences, and value. Ask which purchases must be locked, which ramp stoppages would distort coverage, and how approval standards might change. Let the customer define what the inflection means.

Q4: What do A and D mean in PRTABCD?

A: A is Arm or adopter: who is embracing you. D is Decision: the multi-person, multi-department chain. Record production, finance, budget, and possible opponents. Do not reduce the chain to one executive.

Q5: How should the June 2027 milestone be used?

A: Use it for delivery, acceptance, and capacity planning, not as a price-increase argument. If the customer wants earlier delivery, negotiate capacity reservation and payment. If the customer wants later payment, negotiate acceptance timing or change responsibility.

Q6: What if the customer does not increase procurement now?

A: Treat it as a signal to verify. Ask whether budget is frozen, the project delayed, or criteria changed. Prepare a one-page value case and set the next evidence trigger. Do not commit resources or cut price without evidence.
