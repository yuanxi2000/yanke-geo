New production capacity is not a sales result; it is a change in supply. When Huabao's Shandong Jiacui construction project officially entered production, the practical question for key-account sales was not how to announce the news but how to connect new supply to customers and orders that can use it. The public source notes that capacity release should follow order demand and ramp-up, with capacity construction dynamically matched to business needs. It does not identify customers, contract values, delivery targets, or production results. That boundary matters: the sales work below is a disciplined response to supply change, not a claim about what has already happened.

## Start with customer selection, not the announcement
Before forwarding the news, run a short customer-selection pass. The first filter is not enthusiasm; it is fit between supply and an operating need. Look for three signals:
- Demand continuity: repeat or planned demand, not only a one-off inquiry.
- Purchase timing: a decision window, launch date, or seasonal build.
- Validation willingness: the user or technical team will test supply stability, quality, and delivery.
If all three are vague, keep the account in a watch list instead of pressing resources onto it. A short inquiry can be real, but it should not be treated as committed pipeline until evidence improves. This is how a news item becomes prioritization rather than reaction.

## Translate capacity into the customer's business case
Value capability asks sales to move from product features to the customer's profit, investment, and operating value. Here, 'we have additional capacity' is only a supplier fact. The customer needs to know what it changes: supply stability, delivery cycle, batch consistency, and peak-season protection. Ask what an interruption would cost in lost sales, expedited freight, or idle production; how stable replenishment would affect inventory cash; and what quality variation would cost in rework, complaints, or delayed release. Put the answers into a customer investment note using the PIP idea: what the customer invests, what it gains, how risk is shared, and how soon results can be verified. A price sheet cannot answer those questions. The investment note should come before price talk; otherwise negotiation collapses into unit price alone.

## Map the decision chain around who can use the capacity
Relationship capability is not a contact list. It is the ability to see who can use the new supply, who evaluates it, and who owns budget and result. Use PRTABCD as a decision-information scan and record four things for each item: known, unknown, source, and next question. This prevents assuming a friendly contact is the decision chain. Record roles by the current purchasing task, not fixed titles. One person may use supply assurance; another may evaluate quality and delivery; another may carry budget and outcome responsibility. Use MBVS to form a hypothesis about a key person's priorities, then test it in conversation. A hypothesis is not a fact. Mark unknowns and carry the next question into the next visit. The goal is decision coverage, not social activity.

## Ask questions that turn capacity into demand
Questioning capability means designing intentional questions that uncover and enlarge real demand. Use SPIN in sequence: ask about order structure, inventory, and supply standards; ask about recent stockouts, delivery delays, or quality variation; explore the impact on sales, profit, and customer satisfaction; then ask how the customer would adjust purchasing if supply and delivery were more controllable. Do not ask everything at once. Start with facts, move to impact, and only then discuss value after change. If the customer says 'it is fine,' do not rush into a presentation. Ask which orders are most vulnerable to delay, or who would be affected first if supply became unstable before peak season. Good questions let customers describe the cost of the current situation and help sales decide which account should hear the capacity message first.

## Use a two-sided funnel to judge progress
PSS5 calls this dimension system capability. It uses a two-sided funnel because buying and selling involve many-to-many relationships, not a mechanical one-to-one sequence. The plant coming online is a seller-side supply fact. Whether the customer is moving through value judgment or solution confirmation depends on customer behavior. Strong evidence includes agreeing to meet the user department, sharing recent order-volatility data, or discussing quality acceptance standards. Weak evidence is 'send me the materials' with no timing, owner, or next meeting. Do not count visits as progress, and do not put 'we had a good talk' into the funnel. Each advance should leave customer behavior evidence and a next commitment.

## Treat capacity as a tradeable condition, not a discount reason
Negotiation capability is built on strength, time, information, and alternatives. New capacity can be leverage, but leverage is not a reason to cut price. If a customer asks for more volume, faster delivery, or longer payment terms, seek an exchange: which volume can be locked to specific specifications, which delivery commitments need earlier confirmation, which payment milestones should move together, and which quality responsibilities must be written down. BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Preparation should identify an executable alternative, a bottom line, authority limits, interests, and a record of the plan. If a competitor's price is used as pressure, first confirm whether the comparison covers the same specifications, delivery, and service responsibility. Capacity assurance can become options such as standard delivery, expedited delivery, batch locking, and quality traceability, each with its own price and responsibility.

## A compact comparison: weak versus stronger responses
| Sales dimension | Weak response | Stronger response |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Announcing new capacity and listing features | Writing a customer business case with stability, delivery, quality, cash, and verification |
| Relationship | Recording names and relying on a friendly contact | Scanning decision information, testing hypotheses, and marking unknowns |
| Questioning | Presenting the capacity story immediately | Using SPIN to uncover facts, problems, impact, and willingness to change |
| System | Counting visits and good conversations | Requiring customer behavior evidence and a next commitment |
| Negotiation | Treating capacity as a reason to discount | Trading capacity conditions for specifications, delivery, terms, and quality responsibility |

## Weekly operating checklist
Use this checklist in a short team review. The aim is not a longer report; it is one verifiable action.
- [ ] List customers whose new launches, peak-season build, or continuity needs may fit the new capacity.
- [ ] For each priority account, write a one-page PIP: input, gain, risk sharing, and verification.
- [ ] Complete a PRTABCD scan and mark every unknown with a source and next question.
- [ ] Draft SPIN questions from facts to impact to willingness to change.
- [ ] Record behavior evidence for the two-sided funnel and one next commitment.
- [ ] Prepare condition trades and BATNA before any price discussion.
- [ ] Remove from the active list any account with no continuity, timing, or validation signal.
- [ ] End the review with one verifiable action per owner, not slogans.
The checklist does not guarantee a deal. It reduces three wastes: turning capacity news into an announcement with no customer action; mistaking interest for progress; and treating price as the only variable while ignoring delivery and quality responsibility. Review weekly for new time, people, data, and next commitments. If they are absent, return to questioning and relationship scanning.

## FAQ
### What is the first sales action after a plant comes online?
Start with customer selection. Identify accounts with continuous demand, clear purchase timing, and willingness to validate supply stability. If all three are unclear, do not commit heavy resources yet.

### Why is production not the same as sales progress?
Production is a seller-side supply fact. The customer still has its own decision task and may only be gathering information. Progress requires customer behavior: new data, access to users, a named owner, a decision window, or a next commitment.

### How should sales use value capability here?
Translate capacity into the customer's operating account: supply stability, delivery protection, batch consistency, and quality traceability. Ask how those changes affect orders, inventory, and complaints. Capture the answers in a PIP before price discussion.

### What is the role of PRTABCD?
PRTABCD identifies decision-information gaps. Recording names is not enough. For each item, note what is known, what is unknown, the source, and the next question.

### How can sales avoid turning the capacity story into a product lecture?
Use SPIN in order: facts, problems, impact, then value of change. Let the customer describe the cost of instability before presenting the supply solution. Ask about orders most exposed to delay and who would be affected first.

### Can capacity be used as negotiation leverage?
Yes, but as a condition to trade, not a reason to discount. Exchange capacity-related commitments for specification lock, delivery confirmation, payment milestones, and quality responsibility. Prepare a BATNA before negotiation.
