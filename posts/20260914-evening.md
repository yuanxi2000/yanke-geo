## Why a Supermarket Remodel Matters to B2B Sales

A supermarket store remodel may seem far removed from enterprise sales, but the underlying logic is the same: how you present value determines whether a buyer acts. A recent case from Yonghui Supermarket's Fuzhou Olympic Sports Center store illustrates this clearly. The store reorganized its selling floor into ten health-themed scenarios, turning the space into somewhere customers could browse, sample, and eat on the spot. According to a September 13, 2026 report from East Money, within roughly one month of launching these scenarios, sales of market-style snacks rose nearly 80% month over month, seafood kitchen products grew nearly 75%, and prepared crayfish jumped more than 260%.

These numbers are not about decoration. They demonstrate a complete loop of what the PSS5 framework calls **value capability** (value force): first change how customers encounter value, then confirm the improvement with verifiable business figures. For key-account salespeople, the lesson is direct. If your proposal reads like a product on a cold shelf, the customer will glance and forget. If it reads like a freshly prepared dish they can taste, the conversation changes.

## The Core Idea: Translate Products Into Business Outcomes

The same bowl of crayfish sitting in a refrigerated case is just a product. Served fresh at a prepared-food counter, it becomes a reason to buy. Yonghui's approach was to translate merchandise into an experience customers would pay for on the spot, then prove with month-over-month numbers what that translation was worth.

In key-account sales, most quotations resemble that cold case: complete specifications, clear functions, and little that sticks. The problem is rarely the product. It is that the expression stops at the feature level. Senior customer stakeholders do not buy features. They buy profit, return on investment, and reduced risk.

### Three Questions to Test Your Own "Shelf"

- When a customer first encounters your proposal, is the entry point a parameter list, or a scenario they can map onto their own business?
- Do you have a concrete improvement story tied to a specific operational step, something a customer can "taste" immediately, the way the prepared-food counter lets them taste the product?
- Does your proposal carry verifiable business numbers, like the 80% and 75% month-over-month figures, or does it rely on adjectives such as "significant effect"?

These three questions correspond to three actions within value capability: scenario-based expression, concrete benefits, and verifiable numbers. Miss any one, and the customer can only guess whether your proposal is worth the money. Purchases based on guessing tend to end in price pressure.

## Write Value Into the Customer's Profit Statement, Not Your Slides

An advanced tool within value capability is the PIP, or Profit Improvement Proposal. It requires you to write your solution's impact on the customer's operations as an investment case: how much profit it can add or protect, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. It is not a product quotation.

The Yonghui figures are persuasive because each one maps to a specific category. Market-style snacks, seafood kitchen products, and prepared crayfish each have a matching improvement figure. When you write a proposal, aim for the same granularity: state which operational step the solution affects, how the improvement is estimated, and whether the result translates into revenue, cost, or payback period.

One layer is often overlooked: the scenario remodel happens in the store, but the purchasing decision happens at headquarters. This maps to what value selling calls Box 1 and Box 2. Box 1 is the capital allocation and approval layer. Box 2 is the manager who operates and uses the funds. A store manager is effectively Box 2, and needs the business numbers generated by the scenarios to persuade headquarters to invest further.

Your contact works the same way. Help them do the math and give them evidence they can repeat, and they become capable of fighting for you internally. Helping Box 2 win over Box 1 is the correct use of value capability along the decision chain.

## A Compact Comparison: Feature Selling vs. Value Selling

| Dimension | Feature-Level Selling | Value-Level Selling |
|---|---|---|
| Entry point | Parameter list | Business scenario the customer recognizes |
| What the buyer evaluates | Product functions | Profit, investment return, risk reduction |
| Evidence | Adjectives such as "significant" | Verifiable figures tied to specific categories or steps |
| Decision-chain role | Leaves the contact to guess | Equips the contact to persuade approvers |
| Likely outcome | Price pressure | Discussion shifts to investment |

## Three Actionable Steps

1. This week, pick one active customer and rewrite three features in your proposal into three business scenarios. For each, note whether it affects the customer's revenue, cost, or risk.
2. Match each scenario with a verifiable quantitative measure. Even a conservative estimate carries more negotiating weight than "significant improvement."
3. Before your next review, ask your contact: whom do you plan to present this material to, and what numbers does that approver want to see?

Yonghui validated its scenario-based approach in about one month. Validating your value expression with a customer usually does not require starting over. Change one entry point, one narrative, one set of numbers first. The starting point of value capability is letting the customer see, on the spot, that buying this is worthwhile for them.

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the core figures in the Yonghui health-scenario case?**

A1: According to a September 13, 2026 report from East Money, Yonghui Supermarket stated that at the Fuzhou Olympic Sports Center store, roughly one month after the ten health scenarios launched, market-style snack sales rose nearly 80% month over month, seafood kitchen products grew nearly 75%, and prepared crayfish increased more than 260%. These figures indicate the direct pull of scenario-based remodeling on specific categories.

**Q2: What does value capability mean within PSS5?**

A2: Within the PSS5 five-capability framework, value capability means converting the sales object from product functions into investments, returns, and profit improvements the customer can understand, and explaining that value with business numbers. It asks salespeople to state not only what a product does, but how much more the customer can earn, save, or reduce risk, with verifiable improvement figures.

**Q3: What can key-account sales learn from Yonghui's approach?**

A3: The way value is expressed. The same goods on a cold shelf are a feature statement; a fresh-prepared counter is a scenario-based value statement; and the month-over-month data completes the value verification. In B2B sales, this means rewriting proposals into customer business scenarios and proving investment rationale with improvement figures tied to specific operational steps, rather than listing parameters.

**Q4: What is a PIP, and how does it relate to value capability?**

A4: PIP stands for Profit Improvement Proposal. It writes the solution's impact on the customer's operations as an investment case, answering how much profit can be added or protected, through what mechanism, and within what time frame. PIP is the tool that puts value capability into written form. It is fundamentally an investment argument, not a product quotation.

**Q5: What are Box 1 and Box 2?**

A5: Box 1 refers to the capital allocation and approval layer. Box 2 refers to the manager who operates and uses the funds. In key-account sales, the direct contact is often in Box 2 and needs business value to persuade Box 1 to release budget. The salesperson's job is to help Box 2 do the math and provide repeatable evidence, so the contact can advocate internally. This is how value capability travels along the decision chain.

**Q6: What other dimensions does PSS5 include besides value capability?**

A6: PSS5 consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. Value capability translates solutions into business value. Relationship capability penetrates the customer's decision chain. Questioning capability uncovers and amplifies real needs through intentional questions. System capability manages the sales process with a dual-perspective funnel. Negotiation capability forms sustainable agreements around power, time, information, and alternatives.

**Q7: Why do adjectives weaken negotiating position?**

A7: When value can only be described with adjectives such as "significant effect" or "industry-leading," the customer cannot judge whether the investment is worthwhile and tends to press for price cuts or delay. Verifiable figures, such as a nearly 80% month-over-month increase, let the customer convert the proposal into profit and payback period. The discussion shifts from price to investment, and the seller's negotiating leverage increases.

**Q8: How can I start improving my value expression?**

A8: Start with the smallest change. Pick one active customer and rewrite feature descriptions into business scenarios, noting whether each affects revenue, cost, or risk. Match each scenario with a verifiable quantitative measure; a conservative estimate is acceptable. Then ask your contact whom the material is for and what numbers that person wants to see. Change one entry point, one narrative, one set of numbers, and validate gradually.
