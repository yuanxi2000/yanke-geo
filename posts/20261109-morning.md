A market headline can suggest a useful customer conversation. It does not, by itself, establish a buying project. For key-account sales teams, the practical challenge is to turn a topic into questions about business value, organizational support, purchasing decisions, and negotiation conditions without filling gaps with assumptions.

## Establish the facts before judging the opportunity

Begin with one working record that separates what is known, what remains unknown, the information source, and the next question. Leave unsupported fact fields blank. A plausible interpretation should remain a hypothesis until evidence supports it.

## Translate the topic into customer business value

Value capability means explaining an offering through investment, benefits, and potential profit improvement in terms the customer recognizes. Start with the customer's operating priorities: revenue, cost, turnover, cash flow, or opportunity cost.

A market-entry discussion becomes useful when the customer can connect it to one of those priorities. Prepare three questions:

- Which business measure would this investment affect?
- Who is accountable for that measure?
- What evidence would show that improvement had occurred?

These answers guide which materials to prepare, who should validate the value hypothesis, and whom to involve next. They also distinguish a topic that attracts interest from a problem the customer considers worth addressing.

The source provides no Samsung investment amount, so it cannot support a return calculation. Record the proposed value as a hypothesis for joint verification. The objective is for the customer to explain why an investment deserves consideration; a quotation is only one supporting document.

## Map support and the full decision chain

Relationship capability rests on professional competence, decision information, and credible action. Its practical focus is understanding and covering the customer's decision chain.

Keep internal support and decision participation distinct. In PRTABCD, A identifies the person embracing your involvement. D describes how multiple people and departments participate in the decision. One supportive contact does not establish organizational commitment.

Record who:

- Volunteers useful information.
- Helps arrange introductions.
- Controls budget.
- Can veto a proposal.
- Is responsible for results after implementation.

You do not need to complete the map in one visit. Add one or two verified details with each interaction, recording the source and next question. If a role assignment exists only in the salesperson's interpretation, keep it marked as unknown.

Useful relationship evidence includes access to decision information, meetings with relevant participants, and willingness to validate value together. Polite conversation alone does not establish these commitments.

## Use questions to clarify an acknowledged need

Questioning capability uses deliberate questions to uncover and develop real needs. SPIN provides four categories: situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff.

### A practical SPIN sequence

For a discussion about entering a market, use the following sequence as a guide:

1. Situation: How do you currently enter a new market?
2. Problem: Which step is most likely to stall?
3. Implication: What business results are affected when that happens?
4. Need-payoff: What would become possible if that obstacle were resolved?

Follow the customer's answers instead of reading through a questionnaire. Questions about a previous comparable decision can reveal who initiated it, who reviewed it, and who used the eventual solution. Return any timing, role, or budget clues to the PRTABCD record for verification.

Judge the conversation by the customer's response. Has the customer acknowledged a problem, described its consequences, and shown willingness to discuss the benefit of change? A longer question list cannot substitute for those reactions.

## Track customer purchasing work alongside sales activity

Process capability uses two perspectives: the salesperson's stages and the customer's purchasing tasks.

The five sales stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. The seven customer purchasing stages are:

1. Recognizing a problem.
2. Assessing value.
3. Confirming that a solution exists.
4. Confirming ownership and responsibility.
5. Deciding whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Selecting a solution and supplier.
7. Committing and signing.

These perspectives have different levels of detail and a many-to-many relationship. Do not force a one-to-one mapping between them. A sales action should respond to the purchasing work the customer actually needs to complete.

Look for behavior: an acknowledged problem, someone accepting responsibility, a build-versus-buy comparison, stated selection criteria, or confirmation of budget, terms, and risks. Visits, CRM percentages, and salesperson confidence cannot replace that evidence.

At each review, ask: Which purchasing task has the customer completed, what proves it, and what task comes next? If the customer is still assessing value, that unfinished task should inform the next sales action.

## Prepare alternatives before exchanging concessions

Negotiation capability brings together power, time, information, and alternatives to support a sustainable agreement. Begin with BATNA: the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, meaning the best available action if agreement cannot be reached.

Preparation should identify executable alternatives, limits, authority, interests, and the information that must be recorded. Follow three steps:

1. Write down your executable alternative and your limits.
2. List the conditions the customer may care about: price, scope, timing, payment, and risk.
3. Plan what equivalent condition you would seek for each concession.

Before offering a number, clarify the pressure behind the request. Is the customer trying to address a budget constraint, a deadline, or exposure to risk? That answer helps determine which conditions to exchange.

The missing Samsung amount and date remain missing here. They cannot become invented anchors for pricing or concessions. Keep proposed exchanges tied to confirmed information and record the conditions discussed.

## Make the account review actionable

Bring the five capabilities into one working sheet: business measures, internal support and decision participation, SPIN questions, purchasing evidence, and negotiation exchanges. Use it consistently between meetings.

An actionable review checklist:

- Separate confirmed public facts from unknown fields.
- Keep sources and next questions beside each PRTABCD category.
- Identify the business measure the customer wants to improve.
- Confirm who owns that measure and how improvement would be judged.
- Distinguish active support from participation in the decision chain.
- Check whether the customer has acknowledged the problem and its effects.
- Identify the purchasing task completed and its supporting evidence.
- Prepare executable alternatives and conditional concessions.
- Assign who goes next, what they will ask, and which materials they need.

After each visit, update only what the evidence supports. For unknown items, write the next question. During team review, examine customer purchasing progress first, then assess whether sales actions match it.

## FAQ

### Does the headline establish a Samsung sales opportunity?

No. It provides a market-entry discussion topic. Without a counterparty, amount, or event date, the supplied material cannot establish a project stage, budget, or decision chain. Use it to frame questions for verification.

### What should happen when the value case lacks numbers?

Keep it as an unverified hypothesis. Ask which business measure matters, who owns it, and what evidence would demonstrate improvement. Do not calculate returns using invented investment figures.

### Is a helpful contact enough to demonstrate relationship progress?

Record the specific help provided, such as information or an introduction. Continue mapping budget control, veto roles, and responsibility for outcomes. Support from one person does not establish the entire decision chain.

### Can a CRM stage prove progress?

A stage label cannot replace customer behavior. Review which purchasing task was completed and the evidence supporting that judgment, then choose the next sales action.

### How should a team negotiate with incomplete information?

Prepare its BATNA, limits, authority, and possible exchanges. Clarify whether the customer's concern involves budget, timing, or risk before designing concessions, and record agreed conditions.
