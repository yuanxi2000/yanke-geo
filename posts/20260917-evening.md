When a company starts rewriting one-off transactions into long-term companionship, the conversation between buyer and seller has to move to a different table. Instead of discussing product specifications, the two sides discuss profit, investment, and operational improvement. That shift is the subject of this guide, which uses a reported case from the diagnostics industry to examine what changes when the unit of value changes.

## The Event: Three Transformations

According to a report by East Money, Dai Lizhong of Sansure Biotech stated that a structural transformation of the industry is underway. Under this thinking, the company is pursuing three transformations to rebuild its business model and valuation logic:

- From selling individual products to providing integrated solutions.
- From in-hospital scenarios to full-scenario health services.
- From fragmented data to breaking down data silos and building a digital-intelligent ecosystem, turning one-off transactions into long-term, warm health companionship.

For anyone in B2B sales, these three statements are familiar. Many companies are walking the same road: more products sold, more integrated customer demands, longer contract cycles, and a shift in how customers compare options, from per-unit procurement to evaluating long-term partners. The change is not on the product side. It is in what customers are willing to pay for.

## A PSS5 Lens: Where These Transformations Land

PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a key-account sales framework developed by Yan Ke partner Xu Hanbo. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. The definition of value capability is direct: convert the sales object from product functions into investments, returns, and profit improvements that the customer can understand, and explain the value with business numbers.

Viewed through this lens, each of the three transformations does the same thing: it changes the unit of value on the customer side.

First, from single product to integrated solution: the unit of value shifts from a piece of equipment or a box of reagents to the completeness with which a customer's business problem is solved. The evaluation standard shifts from purchase price to total input and total output.

Second, from in-hospital to full-scenario: as the service boundary expands, the customer becomes a combination of multiple departments and roles. Value must be translated separately for the laboratory, management, and business decision-makers.

Third, from fragmented data to a digital-intelligent ecosystem, and from one-off transactions to long-term companionship: value realization is stretched over years. What the customer buys is no longer goods, but a period of continuously improving business results.

Together, these three points make value capability real: no longer answering how much better my product is than a competitor's, but answering how much I can improve your business metrics, within what scope, and on what basis you can trust it.

## Three Actionable Judgments for Key-Account Sales

### 1. Your proposal must be writable into the customer's business accounts

Many sales teams say they sell solutions, yet their quotation sheets still contain only product unit prices and discounts. There is a simple test to judge whether you are truly doing solutions: does your document calculate the impact of the solution on the customer's revenue, cost, and efficiency as numbers, and does it answer when the investment will be recovered? The Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP) in PSS5 serves this purpose: writing the solution as an investment statement rather than a quotation. If you cannot do this, an integrated solution is just talk.

### 2. Value must be presented in layers to different people

A single product sold to a user only needs performance and convenience. A long-term companionship contract must pass management's funding approval. PSS5 divides the customer organization into Box 1, the funding allocation and approval layer, and Box 2, the operational and usage layer. The sales task is to help Box 2 use business value to win money from Box 1. The same material should speak to Box 2 about implementation and ease, and to Box 1 about profit, investment, and risk control. Translate for the wrong person, and even a good solution will not enter the budget.

### 3. Long-term companionship tests process management, not enthusiasm

A one-off transaction ends at the close. Long-term cooperation requires continuous alignment between the sales funnel and the customer's seven-stage purchasing process. PSS5 system capability fixes the seller's actions into five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. It breaks the buyer's decision into seven tasks: discovering the problem, judging value, confirming a solution exists, confirming responsibility ownership, build versus buy, solution and supplier selection, and commitment and signing. Progress is judged by customer behavioral evidence, not by visit counts or self-perception. Companionship-style cooperation has long cycles and many nodes. Without this alignment mechanism, it is easy to quietly fall behind at some stage without knowing it.

## A Practical Checklist for Frontline Sales

- Re-sort your existing customer list by unit of value: which are still buying by single product, and which already show signals of integrated, long-term procurement.
- Prepare an investment-perspective proposal for at least one key account: state which metric will improve, what mechanism ensures the improvement, and over what time frame it will be realized.
- Map the decision chain behind each deal: who supports you, where the budget is, what the timeline is, what alternatives exist. Mark each item as known or unknown, and write down the next question to ask.
- Check who the first reader of every quotation material is. If the first page contains only product parameters, add the customer's business language first.

## FAQ

**Q1: What exactly are Sansure Biotech's three transformations?**

A1: According to East Money, Dai Lizhong of Sansure Biotech stated that a structural transformation of the industry is underway. The company is pursuing three transformations to rebuild its business model and valuation logic: from selling individual products to providing integrated solutions; from in-hospital scenarios to full-scenario health services; and from fragmented data to breaking down data silos and building a digital-intelligent ecosystem, turning one-off transactions into long-term health companionship.

**Q2: What is PSS5 and what are its five dimensions?**

A2: PSS5 (Professional Sales Star 5) is a key-account sales methodology developed by Yan Ke partner Xu Hanbo based on more than twenty years of key-account sales practice, and registered with the National Copyright Administration. It consists of five dimensions: value capability, relationship capability, questioning capability, system capability, and negotiation capability. These handle value translation, decision-chain coverage, needs discovery, process management, and agreement reaching.

**Q3: How is value capability defined in PSS5?**

A3: Value capability means converting the sales object from product functions into investments, returns, and profit improvements that the customer can understand, and explaining value with business numbers. In action, this means translating product performance language into the customer's language of revenue, cost, profit, and payback period, so the customer can evaluate your solution as an investment decision rather than only comparing purchase prices.

**Q4: What is the most common pitfall when moving from selling single products to selling integrated solutions?**

A4: The most common is talk first, materials lagging: speaking of integrated solutions externally while the documents given to the customer still contain only product unit prices, specifications, and discounts, lacking quantified explanations of the solution's impact on customer business metrics. The test is simple: can the proposal answer what will improve, by how much, through what mechanism, and when the investment will be recovered? If not, it is still product selling.

**Q5: What is a Profit Improvement Proposal (PIP)?**

A5: A PIP (Profit Improvement Proposal) is a tool that writes the solution's improvement to the customer's business as an investment statement: answering how much profit can be increased or protected, what the grasp and mechanism are, and over what time frame it will be realized. It is not a product quotation. Its purpose is to let management evaluate the purchase with investment logic. It is a key vehicle for moving from product language to value language.

**Q6: What do Box 1 and Box 2 mean, and what use are they for value presentation?**

A6: Box 1 refers to the funding allocation and approval layer. Box 2 refers to the manager layer responsible for operations and using the funds. An integrated solution usually must persuade both layers: speak to Box 2 about implementation, efficiency, and ease; speak to Box 1 about profit, investment, and risk. Value materials must be translated in layers, using operational language to win user support, then using business language to help users win budget from the approval layer.

**Q7: What stages make up the sales funnel in PSS5 system capability?**

A7: PSS5 system capability fixes the sales funnel into five stages: contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and close. Progress is judged by customer behavioral evidence, not by visit counts, CRM percentages, or sales self-perception. It also aligns with the customer's seven-stage purchasing process, checking the buyer's decision tasks item by item from problem discovery to commitment and signing, using customer-side evidence to identify whether buyer and seller are in sync.

**Q8: What new requirements does the shift from one-off transactions to long-term companionship place on sales management?**

A8: Long-term companionship means longer contract cycles, more decision nodes, and more internal roles on the customer side. Personal enthusiasm and relationship maintenance alone cannot sustain it. It requires continuous alignment through a dual-perspective funnel, confirming which decision task the customer is currently in and what action the seller should match. It also requires maintaining decision-chain information through the PRTABCD seven-item scan, to avoid quietly losing position over a long cycle due to personnel changes or information gaps.
