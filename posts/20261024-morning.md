## Apply the five capabilities as checks, not slogans

PSS5 works here as a checking order, not a presentation script. Each dimension turns a broad claim into a question that can be verified in the account.

- Value capability: Translate capacity planning, delivery stability, and cost actions into the customer's investment, return, and profit improvement. Because the source gives no benefit figures, the seller can only frame value hypotheses. The customer supplies the numbers, or the field stays blank.
- Relationship capability: Use the decision chain to check who cares about capacity, who cares about cost, and who cares about the technology route. Record knowns, unknowns, information sources, and the next question with PRTABCD. Treat A as the embracer and D as the decision chain. Do not mistake one contact for the whole buying group.
- Questioning capability: Use SPIN to turn industry argument into situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions. Ask about the customer's own capacity utilization, inventory, investment rhythm, and risk tolerance before discussing a solution. Do not make an industry judgment on the customer's behalf.
- System capability: Read the account through the sales five-stage view and the customer purchasing seven-stage view. The seller advances from contact toward closing; the buyer moves from problem recognition to commitment and signing. The two axes are many-to-many, not a forced one-to-one map. Use customer behavior evidence to locate the account. If the customer is still judging value, a quote is premature and will distort the forecast. The purchasing seven-stage check asks whether the customer acknowledges a gap, judges the investment worthwhile, confirms a solution exists, assigns ownership, compares build versus buy, enters solution selection, and completes commitment and signing.
- Negotiation capability: Put capacity release, delivery, price, payment, and risk into a conditions exchange. Prepare the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, the bottom line, authority, interests, and records. The source has no amount or contract information, so do not promise returns or delivery dates.

### From news item to three evidence-collection actions

1. Draw the decision chain. Use the PRTABCD seven-item scan. Write P, R, T, A, B, C, and D one by one. Mark every unknown and write a specific next question. Do not turn A into customer leverage, and do not reduce D to a single signer.
2. Match the customer purchasing seven stages. Check whether the customer acknowledges a gap, judges the investment worth making, confirms that a solution exists, has someone own the responsibility, compares build versus buy, enters solution selection, and completes commitment and signing. Each step needs a customer behavior evidence item, not a visit count.
3. Prepare a one-page value note. Map cost-reduction actions to cost items the customer can check. Map capacity timing to delivery risk. Map the technology route to uncertainty in investment payback. If there is no number, write to be verified; do not invent percentages or timelines.

## Risk boundaries

The easiest way to misuse this source is to treat an earnings-call explanation as an operating result. When a seller cites such material, state first what the source can support, then discuss what it may mean for the customer. The same discipline applies to Putailai: the source can support analysis of capacity and cost capability, but it cannot support announcing customers, orders, or mass production.

## FAQ

**Q1. What should a seller check first in this earnings-call information?**
Check the fact layer first: what was an explanation topic, what was a disclosed action, and what is only a sales inference. The source supports capacity planning and cost-reduction actions. It does not support orders, mass production, or profit figures. Separate those layers before asking questions.

**Q2. How should value capability handle cost reduction?**
Do not tell the customer how much it will save. Ask how its cost is composed, which parts are affected by capacity, graphitization, and power-feeding curves, and which cost items it can verify. Frame the rest as value hypotheses to be tested.

**Q3. Why start with the decision chain instead of a friendly contact?**
Because the earnings call discusses capacity, cost, and technology route at the same time, interest inside the customer may be spread across roles. Relationship capability uses professional insight, decision information, and credible action across the chain. A single contact is not the whole decision chain.

**Q4. How should questioning handle the debate about batteries repeating the solar cycle?**
Do not answer the grand industry question for the customer. Break it into answerable questions: where capacity and demand diverge, whether inventory and utilization are under pressure, how payback is calculated, and what is lost if nothing changes. Use SPIN to let the customer articulate the reason to change.

**Q5. What do the two system axes show?**
The sales five-stage view and the customer purchasing seven-stage view show where the seller is and where the buyer is. They do not map one-to-one. Use customer behavior evidence. If the customer is still evaluating value, jumping to a proposal or quote misreads real progress.

**Q6. What must be ready before negotiation?**
Prepare the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, the bottom line, authority, interests, and records. Design conditional exchanges around capacity release, delivery, price, payment, and risk. Do not trade a single price cut for a signature, and do not invent commitments the source cannot support.
