A strategic cooperation announcement gives a key-account team a reason to investigate. The useful work begins with the decisions that follow: which projects are in scope, who owns the objectives, what evidence will establish value, and what the customer is prepared to do next.

Poly Property Group's reported agreement to explore jointly developing charging and battery-swapping facilities in property settings provides a practical case. The announced direction covers existing and new projects across residential communities, commercial complexes, office buildings, and industrial parks. This guide uses PSS5 to turn that direction into customer questions, evidence requirements, and proposed actions. Project-level conclusions remain subject to verification.

| Four property settings | Intended areas for deployment | Site selection and pilot conditions |

Describe proposed next steps as recommendations. A suggested pilot is not an approved pilot; a discussion about budget is not budget authorization.

## Use the five capabilities to organize the work

PSS5 brings together five capabilities: value, relationships, questioning, systematic sales management, and negotiation. Each addresses a different part of the account task.

Value connects the offer to the customer's financial and operating priorities. Relationships establish decision-chain coverage through insight, information, and verifiable trust. Questioning uncovers needs through purposeful inquiry. Systematic management compares seller activity with customer procurement progress. Negotiation turns interests and constraints into workable agreements.

Apply the capabilities together. A value hypothesis needs an owner; that owner needs questions they can answer; the answers need to inform stage assessment and negotiation preparation.

## Define value before claiming benefits

Charging and battery-swapping facilities may raise questions about asset operations, customer experience, and resource utilization. These are possible areas of value to investigate. The announcement does not demonstrate improvement in any of them.

For each property setting, ask the customer to define the operating issue and the evidence they would accept. Residential communities, commercial complexes, office buildings, and industrial parks should each receive a separate discussion, because the relevant conditions and responsibilities still need confirmation.

A useful value record includes:

- The customer objective under discussion.
- The proposed benefit, clearly marked as a hypothesis.
- The measure the customer agrees to use.
- The person responsible for confirming the measure.
- The proposed verification action and its conditions.

Where operating figures are unavailable, document the hypothesis and validation plan. Leave the benefit unquantified until the customer supplies or confirms a basis for calculation.

## Map relationships with PRTABCD

Relationship coverage requires understanding how decisions move across people and departments. The source recommends a seven-part PRTABCD scan:

- **P — Past information:** Relevant account history and existing knowledge.
- **R — Requirements:** Customer needs that have been identified or remain uncertain.
- **T — Time:** Timing, milestones, and decision points.
- **A — Arm:** Who embraces or actively supports the proposed cooperation.
- **B — Budget:** What is known about budget and its basis.
- **C — Competition:** Competitors and alternative approaches.
- **D — Decision:** How multiple participants and departments contribute to decisions.

For every entry, record what is known, what is unknown, the information source, and the next question. Distinguish potential sponsors, users, and approvers at different organizational levels without assuming those roles have already been identified.

Support from one contact leaves other questions open. Establish who owns the target, who can explain budget requirements, and who participates in selection.

## Ask questions that advance customer decisions

SPIN provides four question categories: situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff. Use them to investigate the customer's decision tasks in a sensible sequence.

### Move from site conditions to a verification commitment

Start with situation questions: Which property setting would the customer consider first? What charging arrangements already exist? What conditions constrain site selection in existing or new projects?

Then explore problems: Where are there unresolved issues in location choice, operations and maintenance, or revenue allocation? Who needs to resolve each issue?

Use implication questions to let the customer explain consequences: What would delaying resolution mean for the project? How would uncertainty over responsibilities affect the next decision? Avoid supplying consequences the customer has not confirmed.

Finally, ask need-payoff questions: What would a useful pilot establish? Who would confirm facility utilization, operating responsibilities, and the basis for revenue allocation? What verification action is the customer willing to undertake?

Record the answers. If an answer is unavailable, mark it unknown and agree who will confirm it and when. An unresolved question can define the next useful conversation.

## Assess progress through two connected funnels

PSS5 compares five sales stages with seven customer procurement stages. The sales stages are contact, needs, proposal, negotiation, and closing.

The customer procurement stages are:

1. Recognizing a problem.
2. Assessing its value.
3. Confirming that a solution exists.
4. Establishing ownership and responsibility.
5. Deciding whether to build internally or buy externally.
6. Selecting a solution and supplier.
7. Making a commitment and signing.

Record both perspectives for each project. A group-level agreement can represent a customer commitment at that level, while individual projects still require separate assessment.

Stage judgments should reference customer behavior. Relevant evidence includes someone accepting ownership of an objective, providing the budget basis, arranging a pilot, participating in selection, or confirming terms. Identify the person and action in the record.

Visit counts, verbal enthusiasm, and the announcement itself cannot substitute for these project-level signals. If the evidence does not establish a stage, retain the uncertainty and specify what would resolve it.

## Prepare negotiation around conditions and alternatives

The negotiation capability considers power, time, information, and alternatives. After a strategic agreement, project discussions may involve scope, exclusivity, construction timing, operating responsibilities, payment, and risk allocation. These are possible negotiation subjects, not confirmed terms of this cooperation.

Prepare a BATNA: the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, meaning the best available action if agreement cannot be reached. The alternative must be executable.

Before negotiating, document the available alternatives, limits, decision authority, interests, and relevant records. Identify which conditions can be exchanged and who can authorize those exchanges. Keep price within this broader preparation.

Write agreed conditions in terms that can be checked later. The purpose is a sustainable agreement with clear responsibilities and commitments.

## Actionable account checklist

Use these four actions to structure the next account review:

- [ ] **Build the PRTABCD evidence record.** For all seven categories, capture known information, unknowns, sources, and the next question.
- [ ] **Assess each project through both funnels.** Record its sales stage and customer procurement stage, with supporting customer behavior.
- [ ] **Prepare SPIN questions for the four property settings.** Clarify pilot conditions, responsibilities, and how benefits would be measured.
- [ ] **Prepare negotiation alternatives.** Record the BATNA, limits, authority, and conditions available for exchange.

For every open item, identify the next confirmation owner and timing. Make the record specific enough that another team member can distinguish evidence of progress from a proposed action or courteous conversation.

## FAQ

### Does a strategic agreement count as a sales result?

It establishes a cooperation commitment at its stated level. It does not automatically establish the budget, scope, responsibilities, or procurement commitment of each underlying project. Assess those separately.

### What should the team do when financial data is missing?

Record the proposed operating value as a hypothesis. Ask the customer to confirm the measurement basis, verification method, and responsible person before stating a quantified benefit.

### Which evidence should change a project's stage?

Use customer actions relevant to the decision: ownership of objectives, budget clarification, pilot arrangements, selection participation, or confirmation of terms. Explain how the action supports the stage recorded.

### What if the customer cannot answer a key question?

Keep the answer marked unknown. Agree on the person and time for confirmation, and carry the question into the next interaction. Do not convert an unanswered question into an account fact.
