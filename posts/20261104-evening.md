A public project update can tell a key-account salesperson where to investigate next. It cannot, by itself, establish a purchase order, an available budget, or a supplier relationship. The useful move is to translate the reported milestone into questions about the customer's current decisions, then identify the next action that would verify progress.

The supplied source describes Tianlu Technology reporting that construction of its TAC film project facilities was nearing completion and equipment move-in was approaching its end. That is an observable project milestone. This guide uses it to show how to separate evidence from assumptions, investigate customer needs, and keep sales-stage judgments tied to customer behavior.

## Separate the reported facts from sales assumptions

The source says the company discussed facility construction and equipment move-in during an investor relations activity. It does not provide procurement amounts, counterparties, contract terms, or subsequent capacity, order, or revenue arrangements.

Keep those boundaries visible in the account record:

- Record the reported construction and equipment milestones as known information.
- Mark budget, supplier involvement, commercial terms, and future production arrangements as unverified.
- Turn each information gap into a question for an appropriate customer role.

Do not fill the gaps with a plausible commercial story. Equipment arriving on site does not establish an opportunity for your offering or prove that a particular supplier has won business.

A useful account note makes uncertainty specific: what is missing, who might confirm it, and what evidence would resolve it.

## Read the milestone through two separate lenses

Equipment move-in nearing completion suggests that attention on site is shifting toward installation and commissioning. Whether this leads into subsequent production arrangements still requires later public information or customer behavior to confirm.

The customer and the seller have different tasks. Within the source's seven-stage purchasing perspective, customer attention may move among confirming that a solution exists, assigning responsibility, deciding whether to build internally or buy externally, and selecting solutions or suppliers. The update does not establish which decisions are complete.

The seller's five stages are contact, needs, solution, negotiation, and closing. These do not map mechanically onto the customer's purchasing stages. Several customer decisions may remain open during one sales stage, and one customer decision may require several sales activities.

Ask two questions separately: what must the customer decide next, and what customer behavior supports the sales stage currently recorded?

## Use five capabilities to organize the investigation

PSS5 provides five capability lenses for examining the next move. Use them together around the same customer decision.

| Capability | Working question | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | Why does this issue matter now? | A customer-accepted explanation of the benefit |
| Relationships | Who supports the work, and who participates in decisions? | An identified supporter and decision chain |
| Questioning | What difficulty needs investigation? | Customer answers about problems, consequences, and desired results |
| System | What supports the current sales stage? | Observable customer actions and commitments |
| Negotiation | What could each side exchange or do instead? | Alternatives, authority, limits, and exchange conditions |

### Turn equipment progress into business questions

Installation and commissioning can bring production timing, output, cost, cash flow, and delivery responsibility into focus. These are subjects to investigate, not confirmed problems at Tianlu Technology.

Start with questions the customer can answer concretely:

- Which commissioning activity has the greatest influence on the intended production start?
- If that activity slips, which operating objective would be affected?
- Who is accountable for the production-start result?

Use the answers to develop a benefit statement the customer can verify. Product specifications alone do not explain why the customer should act. Equally, an attractive benefit statement needs customer confirmation; the public milestone supplies no basis for invented savings or output figures.

## Find the supporter and map the decision chain

The source's PRTABCD information structure covers seven areas: past or historical information, requirements, timing and milestones, an internal supporter, budget, competitors and alternatives, and the decision chain.

Populate only what is known. A public construction update may inform timing while leaving budget and decision responsibilities unresolved.

Two distinctions matter. A, labeled Arm in the source, concerns the person inside the customer organization who supports your involvement. It is not a bargaining chip. D, labeled Decision, concerns the chain of people and departments participating in decisions. It is not simply a single executive's name.

A supportive contact does not establish the whole decision chain. Ask who defines completion criteria, who coordinates subsequent production arrangements, and who participates in any later procurement decision. Record confirmed responsibilities and leave unresolved roles visible.

## Let questions follow the customer's answers

SPIN organizes discovery into situation, problem, implication, and need-payoff questions. Around commissioning, a practical sequence is:

1. Situation: How are installation and commissioning divided into stages?
2. Problem: Which stage is most vulnerable to an unmet prerequisite?
3. Implication: If that stage is delayed, how would it affect subsequent production arrangements or internal coordination?
4. Need-payoff: What evidence would let you judge commissioning complete?

These are prompts for a conversation, not a questionnaire to deliver all at once. Follow the customer's answer before moving deeper. If the customer identifies a dependency, clarify that dependency before asking about consequences.

Record the customer's wording, the remaining uncertainty, and any agreed follow-up. The purpose is to establish a need and a way to verify it.

## Advance the sales stage when customer behavior supports it

A project site can progress while your sales opportunity remains unverified. The milestone alone does not justify moving an opportunity into negotiation or closing.

Look for behavior such as the customer confirming a problem, arranging participation by relevant people, defining verification criteria, discussing terms, or making a formal commitment. Match the recorded stage to the evidence actually available.

For each next step, specify the customer action you need to observe. An agreed discussion with the person responsible for commissioning criteria is more concrete than a note to reconnect later. After the interaction, record what happened and reassess the stage.

## Prepare negotiation boundaries before offering concessions

The source provides no amount, terms, or counterparty. At this point, negotiation preparation is more appropriate than assuming a price concession is needed.

Clarify executable alternatives, limits, authority, interests, and records. What could each side do if the customer does not proceed? Which conditions could be exchanged? Which require authorization, and who can approve them? Distinguish decision authority from influence.

BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the best available action a negotiator can take if agreement cannot be reached. Prepare your own alternative and investigate the customer's rather than assuming either.

This preparation establishes boundaries for a later proposal. A discount does not establish that the customer has completed a purchasing decision.

## An actionable account-review checklist

Use a three-column working record: known facts, information to verify, and next question. Keep it focused on the next customer decision.

- [ ] Capture only checkable public milestones and attributable customer statements as facts.
- [ ] Identify each information gap, the role to ask, and the verification method.
- [ ] State the customer's likely next decision as a hypothesis requiring confirmation.
- [ ] Prepare a question about commissioning completion criteria.
- [ ] Identify responsibility for subsequent production arrangements.
- [ ] Clarify the internal supporter and the wider procurement decision chain.
- [ ] Seek customer confirmation of the proposed benefit.
- [ ] Check whether observed behavior supports the recorded sales stage.
- [ ] Prepare alternatives, authority limits, and possible exchanges.
- [ ] Record the customer's answer and the next agreed action or commitment.

Managers can review the same record across all five capabilities. Empty fields indicate unresolved work. Completed records should contain verifiable evidence; the presence of text alone does not demonstrate progress.

## FAQ

### Does equipment move-in prove that an order exists?

No. The reported update contains no procurement amount, contract terms, or counterparty. Treat it as a project-stage clue and verify any commercial opportunity separately.

### What does the milestone help a salesperson investigate?

It suggests questions about installation, commissioning, responsibility, verification criteria, and supplier coordination. It does not establish that any particular purchasing decision has been completed.

### Which capability should come first?

Start by identifying the customer's current decision task and checking your sales-stage evidence. Then use questions, value analysis, relationship mapping, and negotiation preparation together to investigate what remains unresolved.

### Is an internal supporter the decision-maker?

Not necessarily. Support for your involvement and authority within a multi-person decision chain are separate matters. Verify both instead of treating one contact as the entire buying organization.

### What should a useful next question produce?

It should clarify a decision and lead toward observable customer behavior, such as arranging relevant participation or agreeing verification criteria. Record the actual response, including any absence of commitment.

### What can managers conclude from a complete review?

They can assess whether the team has evidence supporting further account work. The review does not itself prove an order, a commercial relationship, or a future outcome.
